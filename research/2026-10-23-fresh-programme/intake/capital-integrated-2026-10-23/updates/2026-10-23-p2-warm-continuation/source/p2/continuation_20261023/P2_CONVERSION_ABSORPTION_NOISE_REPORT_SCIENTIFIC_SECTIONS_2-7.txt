2. WHAT THE GAAS CALCULATION DID AND DID NOT PROVE

The completed work derived a sharp two-mode hot-converter threshold after
charging cold same-frequency absorption, conversion work and absorbed pump
power. In its material normalization, the relevant scalar penalty has the
structure

 H0 = epsilon0 v D_h epsilon_c'' epsilon_p'' Omega /
      [2 chi2^2 hbar omega_c omega_h Delta_n],
 Q_c/P_p,abs <= Hcrit(d)/H0.

Here v is pump volume per participating pair, D_h is the energy-normalization
factor, Delta_n is the useful occupation difference and d measures the
unwanted lower-frequency hot occupation contrast. The exact old derivation,
range conditions, proof and finite-cycle qualifications remain in the
completed package, rather than being rewritten to fit a new agenda.

For the mixed-source GaAs example, H0=1.58014303e7 and the optimistic
two-mode absorbed-pump ratio bound is 8.80941524e-10. That number combines
inputs which were not jointly measured in one sample. It is a reference
scenario, not a newly measured GaAs performance limit. The triad calculation
and more favorable spatial patterns do not erase the material/pump gate.

An invalid inference would be: finite chi2 necessarily requires enough
linear absorption at the signal and pump frequencies to impose this severe
ratio on every material or converter. The old bound assumed specified
loss-response data; it did not derive a universal material law supplying
those losses. Published geometry-independent optical bounds likewise
condition their finite bounds on a specified absorptive susceptibility [S1].

The correct distinction is between internal heat production, useful or
unwanted radiation into explicit ports, and extinction of an incident
frequency channel. Frequency conversion can remove photons from one
frequency by sending them into another port. That is not automatically
internal material heating.

3. AN EXPLICIT MICROSCOPE FOR THE LOSS/NOISE RELATION

The first construction uses a coordinate x with

 V(x)=m omega0^2 x^2/2 + m a x^3/3 + m b4 x^4/4,
 a^2 < 3 b4 omega0^2,

coupled to an oscillator bath through positive completed squares. The
inequality gives a strictly positive global curvature floor and prevents
the usual unbounded cubic-potential defect. A nonnegative bath spectral
density J(nu) supplies both the causal self-energy and fluctuation force.

For the harmonic reference, with z in the upper half plane,

 D(z)=omega0^2-z^2
      -(2z^2/pi m) integral J(nu)/[nu(nu^2-z^2)] dnu.

On the positive real axis Im D=-b(omega), with b=J/m. For number density
n and charge q, in the stated real-peak field convention,

 chi1(omega) = n q^2/[epsilon0 m D(omega)],
 chi2(omega_h;omega_c,Omega)
   = -n a q^3/[epsilon0 m^2 D_h D_c D_p]

at leading nonvanishing anharmonic order. The fluctuation force spectrum
is fixed by the same b and temperature. It cannot be chosen independently
to improve cooling.

Let X_p=q E_p/(m D_p). The joint-response identity is

 |chi2 E_p|^2/(epsilon_c'' epsilon_h'')
     = a^2 |X_p|^2/(b_c b_h),

and the irreversible pump heat density is

 p_abs = n m Omega b_p |X_p|^2/2.

Thus, if a target material conversion ratio R requires the left side to
be at least R, nonzero microscopic spectral floors imply

 p_abs >= [n m Omega/(2a^2)] b_c b_h b_p R.             (M)

This is the useful narrower bound. It ties conversion and heating through
one Hamiltonian and one bath, instead of treating chi2 and every loss as
free knobs. Geometry, local-field limits, finite pump displacement and
response saturation can make the practical requirement stronger. Equation
(M) supplies no positive universal floor unless the three spectral losses
and other resource constraints have actually been established.

For an explicit causal example, the bath has a compact nonzero band from
4 to 6 in declared dimensionless units, with peak J=.4, omega0=5, a=.5 and
b4=.05. The operating frequencies are .2, 1 and 1.2, with 1.2=1+.2. Damping
at the dressed resonance remains about .400; it is not tuned to zero.
The same response has zero linear bath absorption at the three operating
frequencies and leading-order chi2=-3.54361535e-5. Independent Kramers-Kronig
integration reproduces the real response and the oscillator-strength sum
rule. The global classical potential remains stable.

That numerical Lorentz construction does not prove absence of finite-
temperature Raman absorption in the full anharmonic system. We therefore
did not use it as the sole exact counterexample.

4. EXACT GAPPED COUNTEREXAMPLE, AND ITS LIMITS

The second construction has an exactly diagonalizable matter-plus-bath
Hamiltonian

 H0 = Delta |e><e|
      + integral hbar nu [b_nu^dagger+sigma_z f_nu*]
                         [b_nu+sigma_z f_nu] dnu,
 D_dipole=d sigma_x+e sigma_z.

Choose a nonzero square-integrable bath displacement f supported in a
strictly positive frequency band. The completed squares make H0 bounded
below. Its ground state has an exact excitation gap
min(Delta,hbar nu_min). The asymmetric dipole gives a nonzero second-order
response. No phenomenological damping constant is independently reduced.

At zero temperature, the off-diagonal dipole's absorption measure consists
of transitions at Delta plus nonnegative bath-excitation energies. Hence
the internal linear absorption and finite-frequency equilibrium dipole
noise vanish below Delta/hbar. The static quadratic response coefficient is

 beta_static=6 e d^2 integral dmu(E)/(Delta+E)^2,

where mu is the normalized positive Franck-Condon energy measure. It is
strictly nonzero for e,d nonzero. The exact sum-frequency response is also
a positive spectral integral when all three operating frequencies lie
below the gap; its explicit kernel and field convention are preserved in
the microscopic proof note.

In the declared example Delta=5, bath support 6 to 8, d=.8, e=.3 and total
displacement strength S=.4. At frequencies .2,1,1.2, the exact material
linear absorption and zero-temperature dipole noise are zero while the
sum-frequency coefficient is approximately .03599975159. Numerical
quadrature converges across three resolutions, and an independent
density-matrix calculation for an explicit bath oscillator checks the
spectral formula. These numbers are synthetic dimensionless constructions,
not candidate material specifications or acquired response measurements.

This result is sufficient to reject a universal pointwise internal-loss
floor based only on causality, passivity and FDT. It is not sufficient to
claim zero total extinction for a finite radiating object. After coupling
to electromagnetic ports, radiation reaction contributes to the dressed
response. Elastic radiation, converted radiation and incoming thermal
photons belong in the explicit port ledger; none is discarded.

There is a useful exact finite-temperature qualification. In the Gibbs
independent-boson model, let S_abs=integral 4|f_nu|^2 n_B(nu)dnu and let
p_e=1/[1+exp(Delta/k_BT)]. S_abs is a dimensionless Poisson intensity in
the energy-transfer distribution, not a rate per second. Subgap absorption
from the electronic ground sector requires at least one thermally absorbed
bath quantum. Its integrated positive-frequency dipole spectral weight
therefore obeys

 W_subgap <= d^2[(1-p_e)(1-exp(-S_abs))+p_e]
          <= d^2(S_abs+p_e),
 S_abs <= S/[exp(hbar nu_min/k_BT)-1].                 (G)

With S_DD^>(omega)=integral exp(i omega t)<D(t)D(0)>dt,
W_subgap=integral_0^(Delta/hbar) S_DD^> dω/(2pi), excluding the DC atom.
FDT converts this to the corresponding integrated absorption bound.
Equation(G) does not imply a pointwise finite-temperature transparency
window, a finite-temperature chi2 value, or a favorable room-temperature
device. It makes the thermal limitation explicit and shows where a real
spectral/temperature investigation would enter.

The diagonal bath coupling does not by itself thermalize electronic
populations. Finite classical pumping can access multiphoton excitation;
finite-temperature Raman channels may remain. Fixed volume, oscillator
strength, field, throughput, reset, and source-efficiency budgets were not
all imposed. A practical construction must satisfy them before claiming
useful performance. This failed attempt at an unrestricted finite-T
no-loss claim is retained rather than silently promoted to a theorem.

5. THE BROADER NOISE CONSTRAINT THAT DOES SURVIVE

A number-preserving frequency converter can be a unitary beam splitter
for its two signal bands. It need not add amplifier-style vacuum photon
population. Existing coherent Josephson conversion is relevant prior art
for that statement [S2]. Thermal transport is nevertheless noisy because
both reservoirs emit and because thermal bosons bunch.

For one independent two-port temporal mode with transmission T and thermal
occupations n_c,n_h, count the increase q in the upper-band photon number.
The exact two-measurement moments are

 mean(q)=T(n_c-n_h),
 Var(q)=T[n_c(1+n_h)+n_h(1+n_c)] + T^2(n_c-n_h)^2.

Set A=hbar omega_h/(k_BT_h)-hbar omega_c/(k_BT_c). In a narrow band with
constant occupations and positive net photon current J, arbitrary spectral
transmission gives

 V/J >= coth(A/2),
 V=lim_(tau->infinity) Var[N(tau)]/tau.                (N)

This uses a variance-per-time convention, not a PSD with an unexplained
factor of two. For variable band occupations the same pointwise moments
and Cauchy-Schwarz give the scoped entropy uncertainty bound

 V (Sdot_generation/k_B)/J^2 >= 2

for independent passive two-terminal bosonic channels, including bands
with opposite current signs. It is not asserted for arbitrary interacting
or feedback-controlled quantum networks. The underlying bosonic full
counting statistics are established physics [S3]; novelty of this scoped
application is UNKNOWN.

For an ideal refrigerator, Q_c=hbar omega_c J and W_conv=hbar Omega J.
Thus COP_ideal=omega_c/Omega, and

 A=[hbar Omega/(k_BT_h)](1-COP_ideal/COP_Carnot).

The relative current-noise factor diverges approaching reversible
operation. This does not re-create the GaAs absorption penalty. It
separates a real universal thermal fluctuation mechanism from an
unjustified universal internal-heating claim.

The accompanying code verifies the moment formula against exact unitary
mixing in complete fixed-photon-number Fock blocks. At n_c=.4,n_h=.2 and
T=.63, the analytic mean is .126 and variance .494676. A 32-sector truncation
matches the variance to about 4.1e-15, with negligible omitted probability.
This independent check resolves the sign of the bosonic excess-noise term.

A weak-conversion quantized-pump extension adds another warning. The two
thermal signal modes act on the pump as a virtual bath with

 n_v=n_h(1+n_c)/(n_c-n_h),
 T_v=hbar Omega/(k_B A).

Reverse conversion can create a pump photon from vacuum. A stiff-pump
approximation needs sufficient pump occupation and controlled depletion;
it does not make the control state an inexhaustible pure work resource.
For a freely evolving quantum pump, entropy production also includes its
state-entropy change: Sdot_total/k_B=Sdot_p/k_B+A J_p. The steady classical-
pump expression A J cannot be used alone for that transient. Counting
upper-band signal photons gives semiclassical conversion-work statistics,
not a number measurement on the coherent pump that would dephase it.
Reset changes both energy and entropy, and its best work bound is DeltaF
relative to a specified reset bath. No arbitrary positive reset penalty
is inserted without that interface. The exact scope and rates are in
TRANSPORT_NOISE_BOUND.txt.

The additional QUANTUM_PUMP_CYCLE_BOUND.txt closes one idealized finite
stroke explicitly. A pure coherent pump of occupation N0 freely evolves
for u=exp(-gamma tau), transferring mean M=(N0-n_v)(1-u) photons and
acquiring thermal noise n_v(1-u). Resetting it to its initial state with
the hot reservoir requires at least

 W_cycle >= hbar Omega M+k_BT_h s[n_v(1-u)],
 s(n)=(n+1)ln(n+1)-n ln n.

The conversion energy is already the first term. If a physical coherent
capacity N_max is supplied, this becomes

 W_cycle >= hbar Omega M
       +k_BT_h s[n_v M/(N_max-n_v)].

This is a conditional conversion/noise/control-resource bound, with
weak-coupling, isolation and reset assumptions stated in full. N_max bounds
the initial mean occupation, not a hard photon cutoff; tail errors and the
required duration versus bath correlation times must be checked. No physical
pump capacity or reachable reversible reset is inferred for the cited
broadband flux-driven device. Continuous controllers need their own
accounting; they are not required to follow this pure-pump reset policy.

There is also a necessary finite-capacity distance from reversibility:
M<N_max-n_v implies A>ln[1+1/(N_max-M)]. Equivalently,

 COP_conversion/COP_Carnot
   <1-[k_BT_h/(hbar Omega)]ln[1+1/(N_max-M)].

This strictly finite-duration corollary is specific to the freely depleting
coherent pump. It does not constrain every continuously replenished drive.
The actual cycle COP must still include the reset work above.

6. A SINGLE-RECORD JOSEPHSON COMPARISON

Lecocq et al. [S4] report one flux-pumped superconducting converter with
mode frequencies 4.155, 5.756 and 7.915 GHz; linewidths kappa/(2pi) of
29.8, 29.5 and 59.3 MHz; and external coupling fractions .96, .96 and .97.
The lower two modes are converted at normalized pump setting |beta|~.5.
The source's approximately one added-noise photon concerns amplification,
not this difference-frequency conversion operation. It gives no calibrated
absorbed pump power for the refrigeration calculation.

All numbers in the calculation use that single device. No favorable
Josephson pump efficiency from another experiment is substituted.
The proposed reservoir assignment is 80 mK at the lower external input
and 100 mK for the chip/internal baths and upper input. The source's stage
was 20 mK. Retaining its rates at the proposed temperatures is explicitly
conditional, not a measured temperature dependence.

Using angular linewidths and C=4g^2/(kappa_a kappa_b), the exact local
Markov two-mode calculation, with all four baths included, gives

 Q_c(C)=[6.26649995 C/(1+C)-1.33504140] aW.

At C=1, g/(2pi)=14.8248103 MHz and

 cold external heat extraction       +1.798208579 aW
 lower internal hot-bath heat         +1.465593479 aW
 upper external hot-bath heat         -4.340550388 aW
 upper internal hot-bath heat         -0.180856266 aW
 net conversion work                 +1.257604596 aW

Heat is positive out of each bath. The first law closes, and the gross
Q_c/W_conv is 1.429868009, below Carnot 4. This is a model result, not a
measured refrigerator. Integration of the complete four-port scattering
spectrum agrees with the covariance currents; the result is not peak
conversion multiplied by an assumed rectangular bandwidth.

The fixed-rate baseline requires C>.27071938 for positive extraction.
At C=1, any additional cold-directed pump heat and excess noise must fit
within 1.7982 aW. For wall COP>=1, even if that extra cold load is zero,
all costs beyond net conversion work must fit within .540604 aW. Absolute
pump, recovery, flux-control and cryogenic wall costs are UNKNOWN. It
would be unjustified to infer them from the measured signal losses.

The output thermal-noise channels are tied to the same loss fractions.
At the proposed temperatures, the full four-port calculation gives cold
count variance/mean 13.9164 and normally ordered internal added noise of
.00937436 photons referred to the lower input on resonance. The latter
excludes the independent upper external input and any unmeasured driven
noise. It is not an experimental noise calibration. The ideal two-port
version at the same frequencies would obey V/J>=7.45679; that ideal bound
is not directly substituted into the four-port case.

7. ATTEMPTED INTERFACE, FALSIFICATION, AND THE STRONGER TOPOLOGY

The 2017 device uses one broadband measurement line. Separate temperatures
for its two bands require a physical frequency-selective thermal network.
We attempted the simplest constructive interface: two parallel series-RLC
branches, each terminating in 50 ohms, centered at the two mode frequencies,
with filter Q=20. Each branch is passive and its thermal noise is fixed by
the dissipative admittance. Center-frequency cross-contamination is only
about .5616%, which initially looks acceptable.

That center-only check misses a thermal short. With the device port open,
the two branch impedances form a hot–cold loop. Their reactances cancel
at f*=sqrt(f_a f_b)=4.890417 GHz. The exact direct transmission is

 T_hc(f)=4/{4+Q^2[f/f_a-f_a/f+f/f_b-f_b/f]^2}.

It reaches unity between the desired bands. Integrating

 integral_0^infinity h f T_hc(f)[n(f,100mK)-n(f,80mK)]df

gives 59.0615 aW, about 32.8 times the proposed converter extraction.
The bandwidth is about 241.3 MHz. This failed construction is preserved
with exact code and the general loading expression in
JOSEPHSON_INTERFACE_AUDIT.txt. A real device admittance can shift or load
the resonance, so 59.0615 aW is not claimed as an assembled-device
measurement or as its exact loaded prediction.

The conclusion is consequential: reservoir temperatures cannot be assigned
only at desired resonances while ignoring the network connecting them.
The first explicit passive interface fails its full-frequency heat test.

A stronger architecture is already represented by the distinct signal and
idler spatial ports in Abdo et al.'s Josephson ring converter [S2]. In the
ideal symmetric ring, the quadratic modes separate and the leading mixing
term is cubic in the signal, idler and pump coordinates. Without the pump,
there is no intended linear cross-admittance between the two signal ports.
That topology removes the specific parallel-RLC cancellation loop. It does
not prove broadband thermal isolation, zero hybrid loss, or a small pump
bill. None of its numerical parameters is mixed into the 2017 fixture.

The next device question is therefore a bounded passive-network and pump-
reference-plane problem, not another arbitrary intrinsic-loss sweep.
