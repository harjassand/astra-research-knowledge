ASTRA-FRESH-20261023 / P2 — bounded finite-pump cycle result
2026-10-23. Private analytic derivation; no executed experiment.

RESULT

Even when internal material absorption is absent, a finite coherent pump
used for thermal conversion can acquire entropy. In a specified weak-
conversion model, restoring that pump after a finite stroke has a
free-energy lower work bound. It is a broader conversion/noise/control
constraint, but it is conditional on the stated dynamics and reset bath.
It is not a universal material absorption coefficient.

This result specializes, rather than replaces, the original immutable
thermal_mechanism/FINITE_CYCLE_AUDIT.txt. That earlier audit remains the
general warning that steady-state COP does not fix finite-cycle COP.

1. PHYSICAL ASSUMPTIONS

Two rapidly relaxing signal modes at frequencies omega_c,omega_h are held
by independent thermal reservoirs T_c,T_h. Let Omega=omega_h-omega_c>0,
n_c=n(omega_c,T_c),n_h=n(omega_h,T_h),Delta=n_c-n_h>0. They interact with
a quantized pump p through the weak three-wave effective Hamiltonian

 H_int=hbar g0(a b^dagger p+a^dagger b p^dagger).

Assume g0 sqrt(N_p)<<kappa_c,kappa_h throughout the stroke, fixed thermal
signal occupations, resonant RWA, and no additional pump loss channel.
Adiabatic elimination is then the leading Born-Markov description. The
full physical Hamiltonian must have a stable completion; an unrestricted
cubic Hamiltonian is not being treated as globally bounded.

At reset, the pump is isolated from the signal conversion dynamics and
restored using a work source plus one explicitly designated thermal bath
T0. Interaction-energy/correlation changes are neglected in the stated
weak-coupling limit. If switching them is appreciable, their exact work
and heat must be added before using the bound for a physical device.
The free-energy bound is necessary; it does not provide a realizable
finite-speed, reversible preparation of a pure coherent state. A phase
reference and its reset costs are not supplied for free.

2. REDUCED DYNAMICS

Set G=4g0^2/(kappa_c+kappa_h). The pump rates are

 Gamma_-=G n_c(1+n_h),  Gamma_+=G n_h(1+n_c),
 gamma=Gamma_--Gamma_+=G Delta,
 n_v=Gamma_+/gamma=n_h(1+n_c)/Delta.

Define A=ln(Gamma_-/Gamma_+)
        =hbar omega_h/(k_BT_h)-hbar omega_c/(k_BT_c)>0.
The pump's stationary virtual thermal occupation is n_v=(exp A-1)^-1.
The initial pump is the pure coherent state |alpha0>, with N0=|alpha0|^2.
For u=exp(-gamma tau), its freely evolved state at stroke duration tau
is a displaced thermal state with

 coherent occupation = N0 u,
 incoherent occupation = n_v(1-u),
 total occupation = N0 u+n_v(1-u).

Its entropy is S_p=k_B s(n_v(1-u)), where

 s(n)=(n+1)ln(n+1)-n ln n,  s(0)=0.

The integrated net upconversion count is

 M=int_0^tau[Gamma_- <N>-Gamma_+(<N>+1)]dt
  =(N0-n_v)(1-u).

Consequently the stroke extracts Q_c=hbar omega_c M from the cold bath,
rejects hbar omega_h M to the hot bath, and reduces the pump energy by
hbar Omega M. Positive refrigeration in this stroke requires N0>n_v.
For N0=0 the direction reverses: the thermal modes populate the pump.
There is no second-law violation; for this transient,

 Sigma_stroke/k_B=A M+s(n_v(1-u))>=0.

Omitting the pump-state entropy would give the wrong sign for some
initial states. Counting signal upconversion is also distinct from a
projective number measurement on the initially coherent pump, which would
alter the initial state.

3. RESET AND CLOSED-CYCLE WORK

Restoring rho_p(tau) to |alpha0><alpha0| with thermal bath T0 requires

 W_reset >= F_T0(initial)-F_T0(final)
         = hbar Omega M+k_BT0 s(n_v(1-u)).              (P1)

The first term already restores the energy used for conversion. It must
NOT be added a second time as a separate drive-energy bill. The second
term accounts for restoring the pump's low entropy. At ideal reversible
reset the bath receives k_BT0 s(n_v(1-u)) of heat. Actual controller,
phase-reference, finite-time or switching costs require their own ledger.

If reset rejects only to the same hot reservoir (T0=T_h), and no cold
load is incurred during reset, a necessary cycle COP upper bound is

 COP_cycle <= hbar omega_c M /
   [hbar Omega M+k_BT_h s(n_v(1-u))].                  (P2)

This is below the conversion-only frequency ratio for any positive
thermal pump noise. It does not claim that equality is attainable.
For a third reset bath, the resource is a three-bath operation and must
be compared with the corresponding full thermodynamic budget.

4. EXPLICIT CONTROL-ENERGY CAPACITY FORM

For a prescribed mean transferred photon count M>0, eliminate duration:

 n_noise=n_v M/(N0-n_v),  0<M<N0-n_v.

If the initial mean coherent pump occupation has a certified upper bound
N0<=N_max, with N_max>n_v+M, monotonicity of s gives

 W_cycle >= hbar Omega M
       +k_BT_h s[n_v M/(N_max-n_v)],                  (P3)

under the isolation, weak-coupling and reset assumptions above. This is a
resource-bound form of the conversion/noise connection. Increasing
initial pump occupation can reduce entropy cost per useful converted
photon, but the stored coherent energy is then hbar Omega N0 and the
weak-conversion/dynamic-range constraints still apply. It is not free.

N_max is a bound on the initial mean coherent occupation, not a strict
photon-number cutoff. Coherent and displaced-thermal states have tails.
A hardware dynamic-range claim must specify a tolerated tail probability
or error and check the whole stroke against that criterion. The duration
required at the endpoint is

 tau(M,N_max)=-ln[1-M/(N_max-n_v)]/gamma.

It must be long compared with signal correlation and switching times for
this eliminated Markov model to apply. If it is not, the endpoint bound
remains a formal necessary inequality of the semigroup, not a realizable
optimum. A valid restricted control set can only impose a stronger cost.

The same finite-stroke condition gives a useful necessary distance from
reversibility. Because n_v=(exp A-1)^-1 and N_max-M>n_v,

 A > ln[1+1/(N_max-M)].                              (P4)

For the ideal signal pair, A=(hbar Omega/k_BT_h)
  [1-COP_conversion/COP_Carnot], so

 COP_conversion/COP_Carnot
   < 1-[k_BT_h/(hbar Omega)] ln[1+1/(N_max-M)].       (P5)

These are necessary conditions for this freely depleting finite coherent
pump to transfer the prescribed positive M in finite time. They do not
apply unchanged to a continuously replenished pump or another controller.
They bound the conversion-only frequency ratio; actual cycle COP also
includes (P1)-(P3). The right-hand side must be positive for the proposed
refrigerating frequency ratio to be feasible at all.

Without a measured or designed N_max, g0 and control/reset interface,
(P3) does not supply a numerical device COP. In particular the selected
2017 broadband flux-pumped converter cannot be assigned an arbitrary
resonant-pump N_max. Its actual drive interface must be modeled first.

For the ideal pair at4.155/5.756 GHz with proposed80/100 mK baths,
n_v=3.2283941389 and A=.2698374418. These only specify the virtual-bath
factor; they do not identify the pump's physical capacity or reset power.
No new freely assigned material loss or pump damping was introduced.

5. LIMITS THAT EXPLAIN THE RESULT

In the formal reduced-semigroup limit at fixed finite N0 and tau->0,
the entropy behaves as
s(n_noise)~n_noise[1-ln(n_noise)]. Repeatedly restoring a pure pump after
arbitrarily short strokes is therefore not equivalent to a free
continuous classical drive. The asymptotic reset entropy per transferred
photon grows logarithmically as the stroke becomes short. This describes
that particular reset policy, not every possible continuous controller.
The arbitrarily short-stroke limit lies outside the assumed separation
from signal correlation and switching times; it is not a controlled
prediction of the underlying circuit at arbitrarily high reset frequency.

At fixed M and N0->infinity, n_noise->0 and the entropy overhead bound
vanishes. But the stored coherent resource diverges, and for fixed g0
the weak-conversion assumption eventually fails. Thus no universal
nonzero overhead independent of control capacity follows.

A pump maintained continuously in a displaced stationary thermal state
need not be reset to purity after every short interval. Its steady energy
flow and noise must be evaluated with its actual pump ports. The present
finite pure-pump cycle is a useful bounded construction, not an imposed
requirement for all converters.
