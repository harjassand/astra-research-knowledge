STRONGEST SCOPED RESULT

Causality, passivity, and equilibrium fluctuation–dissipation relations alone do
not impose a strictly positive pointwise floor on intrinsic linear absorption
or equilibrium dipole noise at three operating frequencies merely because the
second-order conversion coefficient there is nonzero. An exactly diagonalizable,
bounded-below matter-plus-bath Hamiltonian below supplies a counterexample. Its
internal bath coupling is fixed and nonzero in a specified frequency band; the
construction does not take a phenomenological damping constant to zero.

This does NOT establish a practical room-temperature converter, zero total
electromagnetic extinction, or zero heating at arbitrary finite coherent pump
power. It does establish that the earlier adverse GaAs scenario cannot be
promoted to a theorem following from those three principles alone. Whether that
GaAs loss is dominated by defects, intrinsic multiphonon physics, or another
sample-dependent mechanism remains UNKNOWN without matched material data.

A complementary lossy anharmonic-oscillator model gives an exact algebraic
conversion-to-absorption identity at its stated perturbative order. Once actual
spectral floors and an anharmonicity ceiling are supplied, it yields a nonzero
pump-heating floor. The same bath supplies the noise. This is a conditional
constraint suitable for material screening; it is not a universal material bound.

1. FIELD, RESPONSE, AND SPECTRAL CONVENTIONS

All frequencies are angular frequencies. Except where ℏ is displayed, sections
2–4 use ℏ=1. The dipole observable is denoted D; an ensemble of independent,
aligned copies at number density n has polarization P=n<D>.

For a static field, define

  <D>(E) = D0 + α_static E + β_static E² + O(E³).

Thus β_static is HALF the second derivative of <D> with respect to E at zero.
For two distinct positive-frequency real-peak phasors,

  E(t) = Re[Ec exp(-iωc t) + Ep exp(-iΩt)],
  ωh = ωc + Ω,

define the sum-frequency output as

  <D>(t)|ωh = Re[β_sum(ωh;ωc,Ω) Ec Ep exp(-iωh t)].

The kernel below computes β_sum in this convention; its joint zero-frequency
limit equals β_static. It is not the coefficient of Ec Ep in a convention where
Ec and Ep are positive-frequency Fourier half-amplitudes. The numerical density-
matrix response contains the corresponding factor 1/2 explicitly.

The unsymmetrized equilibrium spectrum and retarded polarizability are

  S_DD^>(ω) = ∫dt exp(iωt) <D(t)D(0)>,
  α(ω) = (i/ℏ) ∫_0^∞dt exp(iωt) <[D(t),D(0)]>.

At a Gibbs temperature T,

  α''(ω) = [1-exp(-ℏω/kBT)] S_DD^>(ω)/(2ℏ),   ω>0.

These are spectra of matter and its specified internal bath, before adding
propagating electromagnetic ports. A conserved permanent dipole produces a DC
spectral atom; all positive-frequency statements below exclude that atom.

2. EXACT GAPPED INDEPENDENT-BOSON HAMILTONIAN

Let σz have eigenvalues -1 and +1 in states |g> and |e>. Choose Δ>0, and a real,
square-integrable nonzero fν supported on νmin≤ν≤νmax, with νmin>0. Define

  H0 = Δ |e><e|
       + ∫dν ν (bν† + σz fν)(bν + σz fν),
  D = d σx + e σz,
  H_drive(t) = -D E(t).

Every bath integral in sections 2–4 runs over [νmin,νmax]. The model contains no
additional gapless internal bath channels; their absence is an explicit physical
assumption, not a property asserted of an actual ambient solid.

Here e denotes the permanent-dipole parameter, not electric charge. Completed
squares include the counterterm. A controlled bosonic displacement maps H0
unitarily to Δ|e><e|+∫ν bν†bν. This transformation is well defined because f is
square-integrable; compact positive-frequency support also makes its energy
integrals finite. Consequently H0 is bounded below and its exact full-system
ground-state gap is min(Δ,νmin). D is a bounded operator, so a bounded classical
drive does not make the instantaneous Hamiltonian unbounded below.

The bath coupling is not absent. Expansion gives the finite coupling
σz∫νfν(bν+bν†)dν and a positive counterterm. One possible spectral-density
convention is J_IB(ν)=πν²|fν|², positive on its specified band. It is fixed while
the operating frequencies are selected below the spectral threshold.

This is the independent-boson structure used in primary treatments of phonon
dephasing [2]. The gapped bath, permanent dipole, and the response construction
here are declared model assumptions, not claims about the gapless acoustic bath
in that paper. The diagonal bath coupling does not by itself relax the electronic
population. Preparation of a ground state or a Gibbs electronic mixture must be
provided independently in an operating device.

Define the nonnegative displacement spectral measure

  s(ν)dν = 4|fν|²dν,              S = ∫s(ν)dν.

At T=0, the Franck–Condon energy-transfer probability measure is

  μ = exp(-S) Σ_{k=0}^∞ s^{*k}/k!,

where s^{*0} is a unit atom at zero energy. It is a normalized compound-Poisson
measure supported on E≥0. Its zero-phonon atom has weight exp(-S). Every optical
ground-to-excited transition has energy δ=Δ+E≥Δ.

3. EXACT LINEAR TRANSPARENCY WITH NONZERO SECOND-ORDER RESPONSE

For the ground state, the exact intrinsic polarizability is

  α(ω) = d² ∫dμ(E) [1/(δ-ω-i0) + 1/(δ+ω+i0)],   δ=Δ+E.

It is causal, passive, and has no positive-frequency absorption below Δ.
Its real part there is nonzero. Its imaginary part and its equilibrium internal
dipole noise are exactly zero for 0<ω<Δ, by the spectral representation and FDT.
No linewidth has been suppressed by hand.

For ωc>0, Ω>0, and ωh=ωc+Ω<Δ, the exact coefficient in the field convention of
section 1 is

  β_sum = e d² ∫dμ(E) K(δ;ωc,Ω),

  K = [1/(δ-ωh)] [1/(δ-ωc)+1/(δ-Ω)]
      + [1/(δ+ωh)] [1/(δ+ωc)+1/(δ+Ω)]
      + [1/ωh] [1/(δ-ωc)-1/(δ+ωc)
                 +1/(δ-Ω)-1/(δ+Ω)].

All three terms are strictly positive throughout this domain. Therefore e≠0
and d≠0 give a finite nonzero real β_sum. Independent aligned copies have
χ^(2)=nβ_sum/ε0, subject to the stated absence of inter-copy/local-field effects.

One derivation inserts the complete displaced-bath energy basis into the
second-order retarded density-matrix response. There must be two off-diagonal
dipole insertions and one diagonal insertion. The diagonal dipole is constant
within each electronic sector, so the bath dependence reduces to the single
positive Franck–Condon measure. The two coherence terms and the population term
give the three terms of K. The accompanying code independently evaluates the
full second-order density-matrix expression for a finite explicit bath mode and
compares it with this spectral formula.

Taking ωc,Ω→0 with positive fixed ratio gives

  β_static = 6 e d² ∫dμ(E)/(Δ+E)².

For f=0, direct ground-energy perturbation gives
Eg(E)=eE-d²E²/Δ-2ed²E³/Δ²+O(E⁴),
so -∂Eg/∂E gives β_static=6ed²/Δ². More explicitly, with the original D and an
irrelevant constant chosen so Eg(0)=0,

  Eg(E)=Δ/2 - sqrt[(Δ/2-eE)²+d²E²],
  <D>=-e + (2d²/Δ)E + (6ed²/Δ²)E² + O(E³).

The construction rules out a positive pointwise inequality of the form
α''(ωj)≥c|β_sum|², or a corresponding positive equilibrium dipole-noise floor,
with c>0 derived solely from causality, passivity, and FDT across this class of
Hamiltonians. It does not evade an integrated oscillator-strength sum rule by
claiming that an actual electronic material has arbitrary d or density. A finite-
level dipole model does not by itself impose all canonical-charge sum rules;
those would be additional physical constraints on a material realization.

4. FINITE-TEMPERATURE INTEGRATED LEAKAGE LEMMA

Restore ℏ in this section. Assume the actual stationary state is the Gibbs state
of H0: pe=[1+exp(Δ/kBT)]^(-1), pg=1-pe, and thermal conditional bath states.
This assumption is not supplied by the purely diagonal coupling itself.

The exact thermal Franck–Condon energy change can be represented as independent
emission and annihilation compound-Poisson processes. Its characteristic function
is

  φ_T(t)=exp{∫dν s(ν)[(nB(ν)+1)(exp(-iνt)-1)
                              +nB(ν)(exp(+iνt)-1)]},
  nB(ν)=[exp(ℏν/kBT)-1]^(-1).

Thus the mean number of annihilation events in this probability distribution is

  S_abs=∫s(ν)nB(ν)dν ≤ S/[exp(ℏνmin/kBT)-1].

S_abs is a dimensionless Poisson intensity, NOT a rate per unit time. The energy
change distribution obeys detailed balance, μ_T(-E)=exp(-E/kBT)μ_T(E).

Define the positive subgap unsymmetrized dipole spectral weight

  W_< = ∫_(0,Δ/ℏ) S_DD^>(ω) dω/(2π).

A ground-to-excited transition in that interval needs negative net bath energy
change and therefore at least one annihilation event. Its total probability is
at most 1-exp(-S_abs). The excited-to-ground contribution is no larger than the
entire initial excited-state weight pe. The diagonal dipole contributes only at
ω=0 and is excluded. Hence the exact bound is

  W_< ≤ d²[pg(1-exp(-S_abs))+pe] ≤ d²(S_abs+pe).

Using equilibrium FDT and 0<1-exp(-ℏω/kBT)<1 gives

  ∫_(0,Δ/ℏ) α''(ω)dω
       ≤ (πd²/ℏ)[pg(1-exp(-S_abs))+pe]
       ≤ (πd²/ℏ)[S/(exp(ℏνmin/kBT)-1)+pe].

This is an INTEGRATED upper bound, not a pointwise zero-loss theorem or an upper
bound on the height of an arbitrarily narrow spectral line. It excludes the DC
atom, does not compute finite-T β_sum, and does not establish that the chosen
model parameters are realizable at 300 K. Thermally annihilated phonons and Raman
energy differences can produce subgap absorption at any nonzero temperature.

5. ELECTROMAGNETIC AND FINITE-PUMP BOUNDARIES

The α above is matter plus its specified INTERNAL bath before propagating-field
dressing. A finite dipole coupled to radiation has radiative reaction and obeys
the optical theorem [3]. In vacuum, with SI electric polarizability α,

  σ_ext = k Im(α)/ε0,
  σ_scat = k⁴|α|²/(6π ε0²),
  σ_abs = σ_ext-σ_scat ≥0.

In the lossless-material case the extinction can be entirely scattering. Thus
Im(α_dressed)≥k³|α_dressed|²/(6π ε0) is not a mandatory intrinsic heat-absorption
floor. Elastic and converted radiation must remain accounted output ports, or
explicitly identified unused-radiation losses; they carry their incident vacuum
and thermal fluctuations. No zero-total-extinction claim is made here.

The exact ground-state χ^(2) is a weak-field response statement. A finite coherent
pump has an unbounded photon-number distribution; high-order multiphoton
excitation can reach the material gap. Finite pulses can also have spectral tails
in absorbing bands. Neither effect is covered by the three target-frequency
linear absorption values. For a real weak pulse with Fourier transform E~(ω),
the linear absorbed energy density is

  E_abs/V=(ε0/π)∫_0^∞dω ωχ''(ω)|E~(ω)|².

Duration, envelope, allowable spectrum, switching/reset, source efficiency, and
all-order pump stability therefore remain operating constraints. A fixed-duration
positive heat bound would require those constraints; it cannot be inferred from
a single pointwise χ^(2) value.

A finite-number incoming field state whose total energy is below a material-plus-
bath excitation threshold cannot leave a real material/bath excitation by energy
conservation, provided the threshold survives a stable, consistently regularized
field coupling. Virtual frequency conversion may still occur. This conditional
scattering observation is not a derivation of a practical finite-power device or
a complete ultraviolet electromagnetic Hamiltonian.

The familiar no-added-noise idealization applies only to the passive,
photon-number-conserving two-mode frequency-conversion rotating-wave model.
It does not eliminate input thermal transport fluctuations, describe arbitrary
amplification, or prove that a finite quantum pump is classical and noiseless.
Existing coherent Josephson conversion experiments provide a separate microwave
record [4]; they do not measure ambient thermal refrigeration or complete-cycle
pump efficiency.

6. SAME-RESPONSE LOSSY LORENTZ CONSTRUCTION

For a coordinate x, use the fully stable Hamiltonian

  H=p²/(2m)+m[ω0²x²/2+a x³/3+b4 x⁴/4]
    +Σν[Pν²/(2mν)+(mνν²/2)(Qν-cνx/(mνν²))²]-q x E(t).

Choose b4>0 and a²<3b4ω0². Then

  V''(x)/m ≥ ω0²-a²/(3b4)>0.

The quartic term and completed bath squares prevent the instability of a naked
cubic potential or omitted counterterm. Define the nonnegative bath spectrum

  J(ν)=(π/2)Σν cν²/(mνν) δ(ν-ν_mode).

Eliminating this same bath gives a causal memory kernel
μ(t)=(2/π)∫J(ν)cos(νt)dν/ν. The exact harmonic-reference denominator, analytic for
Im z>0, is

  D_L(z)=ω0²-z²-(2z²/(πm))∫_0^∞ J(ν)dν/[ν(ν²-z²)].

On the positive real axis D_L(ω)=d_r(ω)-i b(ω), with b(ω)=J(ω)/m. The principal-
value real shift and absorptive part derive from the same J. This construction
is consistent with the passive oscillator-bath framework in [1].

Set A=nq²/(ε0m), B=nq³a/(ε0m²). At leading order in cubic anharmonicity and weak
fields, in the same real-peak sum-frequency convention,

  χ1(ω)=A/D_L(ω),
  χ^(2)(ωh;ωc,Ω)=-B/[D_h D_c D_p].

The harmonic reference response is exact. The χ^(2) formula is perturbative in
anharmonicity; it is not an exact finite-T quantum response of the quartic model.
Thermal and zero-point renormalization and nonlinear Raman processes require
additional treatment in a material prediction.

The eliminated bath's two-sided symmetrized force spectrum is

  S_FF^sym(ω)=ℏ J(|ω|) coth[ℏ|ω|/(2kBT)].

For one oscillator, the dipole spectrum obeys
S_dd^sym=ℏ coth[ℏω/(2kBT)] Im[ q²/(mD_L) ]. Thus noise and absorption were not
chosen independently. Linearizing about pump displacement Xp=(q/m)Ep/D_p gives
the two-frequency rotating-wave response matrix

  [[D_c, aXp*], [aXp, D_h]] [δxc,δxh]^T = [Fc,Fh]^T/m.

Its output noise follows by multiplying the same bath noise matrix by this
matrix's inverse and adjoint. Omitted Floquet sidebands are an approximation to
be checked; they are not free noiseless ports.

7. CONDITIONAL HEAT FLOOR FROM SPECIFIED SPECTRAL FLOORS

Let b_j=J(ωj)/m>0, ε_j''=A b_j/|D_j|², and define the dimensionless material
conversion-to-signal-loss ratio R=|χ^(2)Ep|²/(ε_c'' ε_h''). Direct cancellation gives

  R = a² |Xp|²/(b_c b_h),
  p_abs ≡ P_abs/V = (nmΩ b_p/2)|Xp|²,
  |χ^(2)|²/(ε_c'' ε_h'' ε_p'')=ε0 a²/(nm b_c b_h b_p).

Therefore achieving R≥R0 requires

  p_abs ≥ [nmΩ/(2a²)] b_c b_h b_p R0.

If actual spectral floors b_j≥b_j,min and |a|≤a_max are known, substitute them
to obtain a genuinely positive lower floor. Geometry, contact leakage, unwanted
mixing modes, source efficiency, finite-time switching, and reset can only add
requirements to this particular material screening calculation; they were not
optimized away. In the parent's hot-side thermal-noise gate R>d_th, substitute
R0=d_th for the necessary, but not sufficient, local material condition.

Without supplied spectral floors, the product b_c b_h b_p can be zero in a
transparent interval. Causality fixes a dispersive relation over frequency and
sum rules fix integrated response; neither supplies a positive pointwise J.
Stability a²<3b4ω0² also does not supply a finite a_max if b4 is left unbounded.
The perturbative construction requires |aXp|/ω0²≪1 and b4|Xp|²/ω0²≪1, plus
corresponding control of thermal and quantum fluctuations. These restrictions
must be included before translating the algebra into device performance.
