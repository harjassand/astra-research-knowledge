THERMAL TRANSPORT NOISE IS NOT A MATERIAL ABSORPTION FLOOR
Private working result. 2026-10-23. No physical experiment executed.

STATUS AND PURPOSE

This note proves a conditional noise/irreversibility bound that survives an
ideal lossless frequency converter. It does not assert a universal lower
bound on linear material absorption, and does not add a fictitious half
photon of heat to a number-preserving converter. The bosonic transport
statistics are established physics; novelty of this particular scoped
presentation/application is UNKNOWN. The accompanying calculation checks
the moment formula against a finite-Fock two-measurement distribution and
applies the full multiport variance to one published Josephson device tuple.

1. ONE PHYSICALLY COMPATIBLE SIGNAL TRANSFORMATION

Take two narrowband propagating bosonic inputs a,b at omega_c and omega_h,
with omega_h=omega_c+Omega. A coherent difference-frequency pump produces
the rotating-wave Hamiltonian H_int=hbar(g a b^dagger + g* a^dagger b).
This interaction preserves signal photon number. With no unobserved loss
ports its scattering transformation is a two-by-two unitary beam splitter.
The energy increment hbar Omega per net upconverted photon is work drawn
from the pump. This is a passive transformation in signal photon number,
not a phase-preserving amplifier. There is no obligatory added vacuum
photon population.

The physical assumptions are explicit: a stiff coherent pump, stable
number-preserving RWA, no unresolved sideband or pair-creation channel,
stationary independent thermal input modes, and all ports accounted for.
Lossless here describes the signal transformation, not the wall-power cost
of producing/maintaining/recovering the pump. Finite signal coupling and
good signal conversion do not establish a small pump bill.

2. DIRECT TWO-MEASUREMENT PROOF OF THE THERMAL NOISE

For one independent temporal/spectral mode let transmission be 0<=T<=1,
and input mean occupations be n_c,n_h. Measure the upper photon number
before and after conversion; let q be its increase. The initial thermal
state commutes with the first number measurement, so the first two moments
can be calculated with K=N_h,out-N_h,in. Up to irrelevant phases,

 K = T(N_c-N_h) + sqrt[T(1-T)](a^dagger b+b^dagger a).

Thermal factorization, Var(N)=n(1+n), and zero input coherence give

 E[q] = T Delta,                                  Delta=n_c-n_h,
 Var(q) = T[n_c(1+n_h)+n_h(1+n_c)] + T^2 Delta^2.       (1)

The first term includes forward and backward transfer. The second is
bosonic excess fluctuation; it is nonnegative. Equation (1) also follows
from the bosonic per-mode cumulant generator

 -log{1-T[a(exp(i chi)-1)+b(exp(-i chi)-1)]},
 a=n_c(1+n_h), b=n_h(1+n_c).

This generator is consistent with established harmonic bosonic transport
FCS; our short moment proof does not depend on a convention for negative
frequency integration or on a convention for symmetrized noise spectra.
We use V=lim_{tau->infinity} Var[N(tau)]/tau, with no extra factor of two.

For thermal reservoirs define the dimensionless forward affinity

 A = hbar omega_h/(k_B T_h) - hbar omega_c/(k_B T_c).

Then a/b=exp(A), and Delta>0 exactly when A>0. In a band where the
occupations/affinity are effectively constant, arbitrary T(delta) gives

 J = integral T Delta d(delta)/(2 pi),
 V = integral {T(a+b)+T^2 Delta^2} d(delta)/(2 pi),

 V/J = coth(A/2) + Delta [integral T^2 / integral T]
     >= coth(A/2),                                      (2)

provided J>0. This is a thermal CURRENT-noise floor. It remains when
intrinsic absorption and normally ordered converter-added noise vanish.
It does not state that a converted coherent input must acquire this noise;
the premise is two thermal inputs.

3. BAND-DEPENDENT GENERALIZATION AND THERMODYNAMICS

For frequency-dependent occupations and affinity A(delta), apply (1)
pointwise. Write j=T Delta and v=T(a+b)+T^2 Delta^2. Then

 v >= j coth(A/2) >= 2j/A,

where j and A have the same sign. The zero-affinity limit is understood
continuously. The entropy production rate divided by k_B is

 Sigma_dot/k_B = integral j A d(delta)/(2 pi) >= 0.

Here the maintained classical pump is treated as a work source with no
state-entropy change in the reduced steady scattering description. This
expression is not the full entropy production of a transient quantum pump.

Cauchy-Schwarz gives J^2 <= V integral(j^2/v), and therefore

 V (Sigma_dot/k_B) / J^2 >= 2.                           (3)

This statement allows both signs of j within the spectrum; it assumes
independent passive two-terminal channels and thermal incident states.
It is not asserted for arbitrary interacting or feedback-controlled
quantum networks. Extra loss ports require the appropriate complete
multiport counting problem, not insertion of their loss into (2).

In the narrowband ideal refrigerator,

 Q_c=hbar omega_c J, W_conv=hbar Omega J,
 COP_ideal=omega_c/Omega,
 A=[hbar Omega/(k_B T_h)](1-COP_ideal/COP_Carnot),
 COP_Carnot=T_c/(T_h-T_c).

As the reversible limit is approached, A->0 and the relative count-noise
factor diverges. Fixed finite channels also have J->0. This is a real
noise/irreversibility constraint; it does not reproduce the GaAs
absorption penalty and cannot by itself bound wall-power COP.

4. INTERNAL LOSS AND FDT: THE CORRECT SIGNAL NOISE LEDGER

For modes with kappa_j=kappa_je+kappa_ji and eta_j=kappa_je/kappa_j,
let C=4|g|^2/(kappa_c kappa_h). On resonance the external conversion is

 T_ext = 4 eta_c eta_h C/(1+C)^2.

The output at h receives internal bath contributions, input-referred to
the desired c input, of

 n_add,int = [(1-eta_c)/eta_c] n_ci
           + [(1-eta_h)/(eta_c C)] n_hi.                 (4)

The independently incident external h field is another input and must be
included in a thermal-current ledger. It is not silently identified with
device-added noise. With vacuum loss baths the normal-ordered population
in (4) vanishes; attenuation still requires vacuum fluctuations to
preserve commutators. A symmetrized input-referred attenuation noise
(1-T_ext)/(2 T_ext), with every unused input in vacuum, is not dissipated
heat and is not a universal amplifier-style half-quantum requirement.

More generally b_out=S b_ext+L b_int, where
 S S^dagger+L L^dagger=I.
Thermal output covariance is S N_ext S^dagger+L N_int L^dagger. The
absorption/noise relation requires knowing bath occupations. Pump-induced
non-equilibrium noise is not guaranteed by equilibrium FDT or by a
low-temperature unpumped linewidth measurement.

5. QUANTIZED PUMP: A FURTHER LIMITED RESULT, NOT A HIDDEN FREE RESOURCE

Within the weak-conversion limit g0 sqrt(N_p)<<kappa_c,kappa_h, eliminate
the fast thermal signal modes from

 H_int=hbar g0(a b^dagger p + a^dagger b p^dagger).

For resonant Lorentzian Markov modes the pump master equation has rates

 Gamma_minus = G n_c(1+n_h),
 Gamma_plus  = G n_h(1+n_c),
 G=4 g0^2/(kappa_c+kappa_h).

The effective pump damping is gamma=Gamma_minus-Gamma_plus=G Delta.
Its virtual thermal occupation is

 n_v=Gamma_plus/gamma=n_h(1+n_c)/(n_c-n_h),
 T_v=hbar Omega/(k_B A).

For an initially coherent pump and no maintaining drive,

 alpha(t)=alpha(0) exp(-gamma t/2),
 n_incoherent(t)=n_v[1-exp(-gamma t)],
 J_p(t)=gamma[<p^dagger p>-n_v].

Reverse conversion can create a pump photon even from pump vacuum. A
classical-pump approximation that omits it needs N_p large compared with
n_v and a controlled depletion interval. This supplies a concrete finite
control backaction channel even for an otherwise lossless material.
It is not a constant positive absorption coefficient, and does not imply
an extra steady-state energy debit beyond a consistently evaluated pump
energy flow. For a maintained coherent amplitude, the stationary pump
can carry both coherent energy and this thermal component.

Resetting such a pump changes energy and entropy. Relative to a specified
reset bath T0, an ideal lower work bound is Delta F_T0, not k_B T0 Delta S
alone. Depending on the state and bath, reset can recover some work.
Without a specified recovery/reset circuit, its charge is UNKNOWN.
For the freely evolving quantum pump, the complete entropy production is

 Sigma_dot/k_B = (1/k_B) dS_p/dt + A J_p >= 0,

within the stated weak-coupling model. In particular a vacuum initial pump
has J_p=-Gamma_plus; its entropy increases. Applying A J_p alone to this
transient would give a false second-law violation. Equations (2)-(3) are
not transferred to that transient without including the changed resource.

The effective cubic Hamiltonian is used only in its weak, low-occupation
regime; a real Josephson cosine or other stable microscopic Hamiltonian
provides the completion. No unrestricted large-occupation stability claim
is made for an isolated cubic polynomial.

6. REPRODUCIBLE CHECKS AND THE JOSEPHSON EXAMPLE

Run: OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=1 python3 transport_noise.py
Inputs: josephson_inputs.json, from Lecocq et al. (2017), one device.
Results: transport_noise_results.json.

The independent Fock-space calculation uses complete fixed-total-number
blocks and exact matrix exponentials. At n_c=.4, n_h=.2, T=.63 the analytic
mean is .126 and variance .494676. Increasing the retained total-number
cutoff from20 to32 reduces the variance discrepancy from3.71e-9 to4.05e-15;
the final omitted probability is at floating-point roundoff. This checks
the sign and bosonic bunching term in (1), not merely a restatement of it.

At the published frequencies 4.155 and5.756 GHz, but PROPOSED baths80 and
100 mK, an ideal pair has A=.26983744 and V/J>=7.4567883. Its weak-pump
virtual bath would have n_v=3.2283941 and T_v=.28474878 K. These ideal
reference values are not a measured-device noise prediction.

Keeping the measured linewidths and eta=.96 at those proposed temperatures,
the full four-port scattering calculation gives at C=1:
 Q_c=1.79820858e-18 W; W_conv=1.25760460e-18 W;
 cold count mean=653150.115/s; variance rate=9089515.735/s;
 cold Fano=13.91642675; normal-ordered internal input-referred noise=.00937436.
The ideal two-port bound (2) is NOT applied directly to this four-port case.
The multiport variance uses K_c=P_c-S^dagger P_c S and
 Var_rate=integral Tr[K_c N K_c(I+N)] d(delta)/(2 pi).
All loss ports are included; no rectangular passband approximation is used.
The code also counts upper-band signal photons. Multiplying this count by
hbar Omega gives semiclassical conversion-work fluctuations. It is not a
two-projective-measurement experiment on the coherent pump itself: a first
pump-number measurement would dephase that pump and change the setup.

These integrations are exact within the local Markov/RWA model with
occupations frozen at resonance centers, not a full frequency-dependent
circuit simulation. They omit a detector, technical pump noise, rate
changes with temperature, and other modes/sidebands. A calculated intrinsic
averaging time therefore does not certify an achievable measurement.

DECISION

Retain a conditional microscopic loss/noise bound and this thermal
transport bound. Reject the inference that FDT alone forces the severe
GaAs pump-loss number onto every converter. Conversely, do not infer a
good full-cycle refrigerator from a lossless susceptibility or low signal
insertion loss. The decisive Josephson interface remains net pump power,
where it thermalizes, pump-induced noise, and full reset/control costs.
