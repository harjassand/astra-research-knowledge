# Primary-source trail and novelty boundary

Accessed 26 October 2026. Sources were sought through public web search and
public pages; no paid retrieval service, Astra archive preload or other trial
was used. The common public starting packet was read in full. Its existing
thermal-network benchmark was treated as a warning against declaring an
ordinary fitting advantage. This project concerns controlled inverse network
identification, not that supplied benchmark.

1. E. B. Curtis, D. Ingerman and J. A. Morrow, *Circular planar graphs and
   resistor networks*, Linear Algebra and its Applications 283 (1998), 115-150.
   Primary publisher record:
   https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0024379598100873
   DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/S0024-3795(98)10087-3
   The indexed publisher abstract was available; opening the full publisher
   page returned 403. It establishes that resistor-network conductance recovery
   and algebraic response-matrix methods are long established for critical
   circular planar networks. Those results are not claimed here as inventions.
   Their fixed boundary response matrix differs from our assumed controllable
   internal shunts. No claim that this source proves our finite design is made.

2. E. B. Curtis and J. A. Morrow, *Determining the Resistors in a Network*,
   SIAM Journal on Applied Mathematics 50 (1990), 918-930.
   https://epubs.siam.org/doi/10.1137/0150055
   Publisher abstract was retrieved through search. This is another established
   boundary-reconstruction algorithm, for rectangular networks. Its existence
   rules out treating inverse resistor identification itself as a discovery.

3. E. Blåsten, H. Isozaki, M. Lassas and J. Lu, *Gel'fand's inverse problem for
   the graph Laplacian*, arXiv:2101.10026v3, revised 3 March 2023.
   https://arxiv.org/abs/2101.10026v3
   https://arxiv.org/html/2101.10026
   The abstract and a full HTML response were retrieved; a later open timed
   out. Known weighted-graph recovery from boundary spectral data requires
   geometric hypotheses. This provides context for not assuming topology is
   generally identifiable from an unperturbed single boundary response.

4. D. Manik et al., *Network susceptibilities: Theory and applications*,
   Physical Review E 95, 012319 (2017).
   https://journals.aps.org/pre/abstract/10.1103/PhysRevE.95.012319
   https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevE.95.012319
   The official abstract, reference list and indexed sections were inspected.
   Inverse or pseudoinverse network response to local vertex/edge changes and
   finite-rank update methods are pre-existing. Reference 42 credits Woodbury's
   *Inverting Modified Matrices* (1950). PROOF.md derives the rank-one and
   rank-two consequences explicitly instead of asserting those identities are
   new. The source concerns a different dynamical class; its physical examples
   are not evidence for a hidden thermal actuator.

5. S. Demko, W. F. Moss and P. W. Smith, *Decay Rates for Inverses of Band
   Matrices*, Mathematics of Computation 43 (1984), 491-499.
   https://doi.org/10.1090/S0025-5718-1984-0758197-9
   https://www.ams.org/mcom/1984-43-168/S0025-5718-1984-0758197-9/S0025-5718-1984-0758197-9.pdf
   The indexed primary PDF abstract and publisher bibliographic record were
   retrieved; direct PDF opening returned 403. Exponential inverse-entry decay
   is established mathematics. Our path estimate has an elementary complete
   proof and is not represented as the first such decay result.

6. N. Mandache, *Exponential instability in an inverse problem for the
   Schrödinger equation*, Inverse Problems 17 (2001), 1435-1444.
   https://doi.org/10.1088/0266-5611/17/5/313
   Author-paper PDF, indexed full-text/abstract:
   https://citeseerx.ist.psu.edu/document?doi=0ddf0fc95c813d30b489bd2d61949e70ee2d5c03&repid=rep1&type=pdf
   This established severe inverse-problem instability and extensions to
   electrical impedance tomography. The finite static one-port theorem here
   is not that continuum theorem and is proved independently.

7. W. W. Piegorsch and G. Casella, *Erratum: Inverting a Sum of Matrices*,
   SIAM Review 32 (1990), 470.
   https://epubs.siam.org/doi/pdf/10.1137/1032080
   The indexed primary erratum displays the classical inverse update formula
   and its antecedents. Matrix update algebra is not a novelty claim.

8. Keithley/Tektronix, *Optimizing Low Current Measurements with the 4200A-SCS
   Parameter Analyzer*, official application note.
   https://www.tek.com/en/documents/application-note/optimizing-low-current-measurements-4200a-scs-parameter-analyzer
   Official page retrieved. Its leakage-current/guarding section describes
   driving a guard near the measured potential to suppress cable/fixture
   leakage. This makes leakage suppression an established comparator concept.
   It does NOT implement opening every internal leak of an unknown network.

9. Keithley/Tektronix, *A Guide to Signal Switching in Automated Test Systems*.
   https://download.tek.com/document/SwitchingHandbook.pdf
   Indexed primary handbook sections were inspected. Switched measurement
   circuits and guarding are ordinary components; switch transients, leakage
   and settling must be accounted for. No experiment with those components
   was performed in this trial.

Searches additionally covered single-port network identification with loads,
resistor tomography with shunts, inverse-M matrix reconstruction, local
perturbation network reconstruction and diagonal loading. They did not provide
a verified prior statement of the exact finite design or its Blackwell
comparison. That limited retrieval is NOT a novelty proof. The candidate
contribution is the precise combined derivation in PROOF.md; historical novelty
is unresolved. The present evidence does not justify publication priority or
the mission-scale advance label.
