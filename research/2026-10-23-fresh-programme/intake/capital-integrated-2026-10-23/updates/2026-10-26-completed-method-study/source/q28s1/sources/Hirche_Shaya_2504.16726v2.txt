 Partial orders and contraction for BISO channels

  Abstract 
    
 A fundamental question in information theory is to quantify the loss of information under a noisy channel. Partial orders and contraction coefficients are typical tools to that end, however, they are often also challenging to evaluate. For the special class of binary input symmetric output (BISO) channels, Geng et al. showed that among channels with the same capacity, the binary symmetric channel (BSC) and binary erasure channel (BEC) are extremal with respect to the more capable order. Here, we show two main results. First, for channels with the same KL contraction coefficient, the same holds with respect to the less noisy order. Second, for channels with the same Dobrushin coefficient, or equiv. maximum leakage or Doeblin coefficient, the same holds with respect to the degradability order. In the process, we provide a closed-form expression for the contraction coefficients of BISO channels. We also discuss the comparability of BISO channels and extensions to binary channels in general. 
  
   §    §  footnotetext:  The order of authors is chosen alphabetically.   
 
  I   Introduction  

 
 Data processing inequalities state that information measures are monotone under the application of a noisy channel, i.e. information can only decrease along the transmission, qualifying them as distinguishability measures  [ 1 ] . Contraction coefficients quantify by how much the measure decreases after the application of the channel and hence provide a strengthening of the data processing inequality. A closely related concept are channel partial orders, formalizing the idea that one channel can be generally more suitable to transmit information by some measure. An example of this connection was given by Polyanskiy et al.  [ 1 , Proposition 15]  who characterized the Kullback–Leibler (KL) contraction coefficient via the maximal erasure probability \beta such that the erasure channel dominates the given channel with respect to the less noisy partial order  [ 2 ] , 
 

  
  
 \displaystyle\beta(P) 
 \displaystyle=\sup\{\varepsilon:BEC(\varepsilon)\succeq_{\operatorname{l.n.}}P_{Y|X}\} 
  
  (I.1)    
  
  
 \displaystyle\eta_{KL}(P) 
 \displaystyle=1-\beta(P) 
  
  (I.2)    
 
 For technical definitions, we refer to the next section.
Using instead the degradation partial order  [ 3 ]  in the right-hand side of Equation (  I.1  ), leads to the definition of Doeblin coefficents  [ 4 ] .
Yet another partial order is the more capable order  [ 2 ] .
Geng et al.  [ 5 ]  established that among all binary input symmetric output (BISO) channels with the same capacity the binary erasure channel (BEC) and the binary symmetric channel (BSC) form the two extremes with respect to the more capable partial order. This leads to their main result that the inner and outer bounds for the corresponding BISO broadcast channel differ if and only if the two BISO channels are more capable comparable.
In a similar line of work,  [ 6 ]  derived a criterion for the less noisy domination by a symmetric channel which in turn implies a logarithmic Sobolev inequality for the original channel. Beyond those results, partial orders and contraction coefficients have found numerous further applications  [ 7 ,  8 ,  9 ] .
This work identifies the BSC and BEC as extremal for BISO channels of the same contraction coefficient, resp. Doeblin coefficient w.r.t. the less noisy resp. more capable partial order and also comments on general binary channels. 
 
 
 
  II   Preliminaries  

 
 In this work, we mainly consider binary input symmetric output (BISO) channels with input alphabet {\cal X}=\{0,1\} and output alphabet {\cal Y}=\{0,\pm 1,\pm 2,\dots,\pm l\} for some integer l\geq 1, those are the channels for which P_{Y|X}(y|0)=P_{Y|X}(-y|1)\coloneqq p_{y}. We can always assume that the output alphabet {\cal Y} has even number of elements because we can split Y=0 into two outputs, Y=0_{+} and Y=0_{-}, with P_{Y|X}(0_{-}|0)=P_{Y|X}(0_{+}|0)=\frac{p_{0}}{2}. The binary convolution is denoted by a\ast b=a(1-b)+(1-a)b and the binary entropy function by h_{2}(p)=-p\log_{2}(p)-(1-p)\log_{2}(1-p). We denote the Bernoulli distribution with probability P(X=0)=p by Ber(p).
We are interested in ordering channels for which we employ the following partial orders. We say P_{Y|X} is  more capable  than Q_{Y^{\prime}|X}, denoted P\succeq_{\operatorname{m.c.}}Q, if for all P_{X}, 
 

  
  
 \displaystyle I(X:Y)\geq I(X:Y^{\prime}). 
  
  (II.1)    
 
 Here, I(X:Y) denotes the mutual information.
P_{Y|X} is  less noisy  than Q_{Y^{\prime}|X}, denoted P\succeq_{\operatorname{l.n.}}Q, if for all P_{UX}, s.t. U-X-Y (resp. U-X-Y^{\prime}), 
 

  
  
 \displaystyle I(U:Y)\geq I(U:Y^{\prime}). 
  
  (II.2)    
 
 Finally, P_{Y|X} is  degradable  into Q_{Y^{\prime}|X}, denoted P\succeq_{\operatorname{deg}}Q, if there exists a channel D_{Y^{\prime}|Y} such that, 
 

  
  
 \displaystyle Q_{Y^{\prime}|X}=D_{Y^{\prime}|Y}\circ P_{Y|X}. 
  
  (II.3)    
 
 Equivalently, the degradable partial order is characterised via the conditional min-entropy  [ 10 ] , 
 

  
  
 \displaystyle\forall P_{UX},\;H_{\min}(U|Y)\leq H_{\min}(U|Y^{\prime}). 
  
  (II.4)    
 
 We will further group the BISO channels into classes for which we need some information quantifiers. The capacity of a channel P_{Y|X} is given by 
 

  
  
 \displaystyle C(P_{Y|X})=\sup_{P_{X}}I(X:Y). 
  
  (II.5)    
 
 The KL contraction coefficient and the Dobrushin coefficient are respectively defined by, 
 

  
  
 \displaystyle\eta_{KL}(P) 
 \displaystyle=\sup_{P_{X},Q_{X}}\frac{D(P\circ P_{X}\|P\circ Q_{X})}{D(P_{X}\|Q_{X})}, 
  
  (II.6)    
  
  
 \displaystyle\eta_{TV}(P) 
 \displaystyle=\sup_{P_{X},Q_{X}}\frac{TV(P\circ P_{X},P\circ Q_{X})}{TV(P_{X},Q_{X})}, 
  
  (II.7)    
 
 where we denote the distribution on Y from applying P to P_{X} resp. Q_{X} by P\circ P_{X} resp. P\circ Q_{X}. We will use frequently that \eta_{KL}(P)=\eta_{\chi^{2}}(P), where the latter is the contraction coefficient for the \chi^{2}-divergence, see  [ 11 ] . 
 
 
 If we replace the supremum with an infimum we call them expansion coefficients and denote them with \widecheck{\eta}. They quantify the preservation of information after the application of a noisy channel.
The Doeblin coefficient 
 

  
  
 \displaystyle\alpha(P_{Y|X})=\sum_{y\in\mathcal{Y}}\min_{x}P_{Y|X}(y|x) 
  
  (II.8)    
 
 is also characterised by the extremal erasure probability such that the given channel is a degraded version of an erasure channel, 
 

  
  
 \displaystyle\alpha(P_{Y|X})=\sup\{\varepsilon:BEC(\varepsilon)\succeq_{\operatorname{deg}}P_{Y|X}\}. 
  
  (II.9)    
 
 Maximal leakage  [ 12 ]  is the logarithm of the max-Doeblin coefficient introduced in  [ 13 ] , 
 

  
  
 \displaystyle{\cal L}(X\rightarrow Y) 
 \displaystyle=\log\alpha_{\max}(P), 
  
  (II.10)    
  
  
 \displaystyle\alpha_{\max}(P) 
 \displaystyle=\sum_{y\in\mathcal{Y}}\max_{x}P_{Y|X}(y|x). 
  
  (II.11)    
 
 Here, we slightly deviate from the usual definition of maximal leakage by making it a quantity of the channel alone assuming that the input has full support.
In this work, we are mostly focused on binary input channels. For those, many of the above quantities are directly related to each other. 
 
 
   Observation II.1   .  
 
  For any binary channel P_{Y|X}, we have,  
 

  
  
 \displaystyle\eta_{TV}(P) 
 \displaystyle=TV(P_{Y|X=0},P_{Y|X=1})=1-\alpha(P) 
  
  (II.12)    
  
  
  
 \displaystyle=\alpha_{\max}(P)-1=e^{{\cal L}(X\rightarrow Y)}-1=\widecheck{\eta}_{TV}(P). 
  
  (II.13)    
 
 
 
 
 Proof. 
 
  The first equality follows from the equivalent representation of the Dobrushin coefficient that is \eta_{TV}(P)=\sup_{x,x^{\prime}}TV(P_{Y|X=x},P_{Y|X=x^{\prime}}).
Most of the remaining equalities follow either, as noted in  [ 13 ] , from the affinity characterisation of the TV distance TV(P,Q)=1-\sum_{y}\min\{P(y),Q(y)\}=\sum_{y}\max\{P(y),Q(y)\}-1, or by definition. The final equality is a direct calculation.
∎  
 
 
 
 Specializing further on the aforementioned BISO channels, also the KL contraction coefficient simplifies and we can give a closed-form expression. 
 
 
   Lemma II.2   .  
 
  For any BISO channel P_{Y|X} we have  
 

  
  
 \displaystyle\eta_{KL}(P) 
 \displaystyle=\sum_{y>0}\frac{(p_{y}-p_{-y})^{2}}{p_{y}+p_{-y}} 
  
  (II.14)    
 
 
 
 
 Proof. 
 
  We consider the binary input distributions P_{X}=Ber(p) and Q_{X}=Ber(q).
Note that for any binary channel, by direct calculation, the contraction coefficient simplifies to  
 

  
  
 \displaystyle\eta_{KL}(P)=\eta_{\chi^{2}}(P)=\sup_{q}\sum_{y}\frac{(p_{y|x=0}-p_{y|x=1})^{2}(1-q)q}{qp_{y|x=0}+(1-q)p_{y|x=1}}. 
  
  (II.15)    
 
  For a BISO channel, this evaluates to  
 

  
  
  
 \displaystyle\sup_{q}\sum_{y}(p_{y}-p_{-y})^{2}\left(\frac{q(1-q)}{qp_{y}+(1-q)p_{-y}}\right) 
  
  (II.16)    
  
  
  
 \displaystyle=\sup_{q}\sum_{y}\frac{(p_{y}-p_{-y})^{2}}{p_{y}+p_{-y}}\frac{q(1-q)}{q\ast\frac{p_{-y}}{p_{y}+p_{-y}}} 
  
  (II.17)    
  
  
  
 \displaystyle=\sup_{q}\sum_{y>0}\frac{(p_{y}-p_{-y})^{2}}{p_{y}+p_{-y}}\frac{q(1-q)}{q\ast\frac{p_{y}}{p_{y}+p_{-y}}(1-q\ast\frac{p_{y}}{p_{y}+p_{-y}})}. 
  
  (II.18)    
 
  We can exchange the supremum and the sum if every summand is optimized by the same q.
Now we show that f(q)=\frac{q(1-q)}{q\ast\frac{p_{y}}{p_{y}+p_{-y}}(1-q\ast\frac{p_{y}}{p_{y}+p_{-y}})} has its maximum at q=\frac{1}{2} for any p_{y}. Let us denote \delta_{y}=\frac{p_{y}}{p_{y}+p_{-y}}.  
 

  
  
  
 \displaystyle\frac{df(q)}{dq}=\frac{-\delta_{-y}\delta_{y}(2q-1)}{(q\ast\delta_{y})^{2}(q\ast\delta_{-y})^{2}}\overset{!}{=}0 
  
  (II.19)    
  
  
 \displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\, 
 \displaystyle(\delta_{y}\in\{0,1\}\land q\not\in\{0,1\})\lor q=\frac{1}{2}. 
  
  (II.20)    
  
  
 \displaystyle\frac{d^{2}f(q)}{dq^{2}} 
 \displaystyle=-2\delta_{-y}\delta_{y}\left(\frac{1}{(q\ast\delta_{y})^{3}}+\frac{1}{(q\ast\delta_{-y})^{3}}\right), 
  
  (II.21)    
  
  
 \displaystyle\frac{d^{2}f(\tfrac{1}{2})}{dq^{2}} 
 \displaystyle=-32\delta_{-y}\delta_{y}\leq 0. 
  
  (II.22)    
 
  Hence q=\frac{1}{2} is a maximum and the result follows.
∎  
 
 
 
 As we are interested in extrema among channels with common properties, we define the following classes: 
 

  
  
 \displaystyle C_{C} 
 \displaystyle=\{P_{Y|X}\mid C(P)=c\}, 
  
  (II.23)    
  
  
 \displaystyle C_{\eta} 
 \displaystyle=\{P_{Y|X}\mid\eta_{KL}(P)=c\}, 
  
  (II.24)    
  
  
 \displaystyle C_{\alpha} 
 \displaystyle=\{P_{Y|X}\mid\eta_{TV}(P)=c\}. 
  
  (II.25)    
 
 We are now ready to discuss our main results in the following section. 
 
 
 
  III   Main Results  

 
 Let F(C) denote an arbitrary BISO channel belonging to the class C. Abusing notation, we denote by BSC(C) and BEC(C) the binary symmetric channel
and the binary erasure channel belonging to the respective class. 
 
 
   Theorem III.1   .  
 
  For any BISO channel F(C_{\eta}), we have  
 

  
  
 \displaystyle BEC(C_{\eta})\succeq_{\operatorname{l.n.}}F(C_{\eta})\succeq_{\operatorname{l.n.}}BSC(C_{\eta}). 
  
  (III.1)    
 
 
 
 
 Proof. 
 
  By Equation (  I.1  ) we directly have BEC_{\varepsilon}\succeq_{\operatorname{l.n.}}P.  
 
 
  To show F(C_{\eta})\succeq_{\operatorname{l.n.}}BSC(C_{\eta})
we use  [ 6 , Proposition 8] , which states that
W\succeq_{\operatorname{l.n.}}V if and only if  
 

  
  
 \displaystyle F(P_{X})=\chi^{2}(W\circ P_{X}||W\circ Q_{X})-\chi^{2}(V\circ P_{X}||V\circ Q_{X}) 
    
 
  is convex in P_{X} for all Q_{X}.
Taking the second derivative, we get,  
 

  
  
 \displaystyle\frac{1}{2}F^{\prime\prime}(p) 
 \displaystyle=\sum_{y>0}(w_{y}+w_{-y})\frac{\frac{(w_{y}-w_{-y})^{2}}{(w_{y}+w_{-y})^{2}}}{q\ast\frac{w_{y}}{w_{y}+w_{-y}}(1-q\ast\frac{w_{y}}{w_{y}+w_{-y}})} 
    
  
  
  
 \displaystyle-\sum_{y>0}(v_{y}+v_{-y})\frac{\frac{(v_{y}-v_{-y})^{2}}{(v_{y}+v_{-y})^{2}}}{q\ast\frac{v_{y}}{v_{y}+v_{-y}}(1-q\ast\frac{v_{y}}{v_{y}+v_{-y}})} 
  
  (III.2)    
 
  We want to choose V as a BSC. Hence, the above simplifies to,  
 

  
  
 \displaystyle\frac{1}{2}F^{\prime\prime}(p) 
 \displaystyle=\sum_{y>0}(w_{y}+w_{-y})\frac{\frac{(w_{y}-w_{-y})^{2}}{(w_{y}+w_{-y})^{2}}}{q\ast\frac{w_{y}}{w_{y}+w_{-y}}(1-q\ast\frac{w_{y}}{w_{y}+w_{-y}})} 
    
  
  
  
 \displaystyle-\frac{(1-2p)^{2}}{q\ast p(1-q\ast p)} 
  
  (III.3)    
 
  More specifically, we want to choose p such that the BSC has the same contraction coefficient as W. The contraction coefficient of the BSC is,  
 

  
  
 \displaystyle\eta_{\chi^{2}}(BSC(p))=(1-2p)^{2}. 
  
  (III.4)    
 
  Hence p=\frac{1}{2}(1\pm\sqrt{\eta}) and p(1-p)=\frac{1}{4}(1-\eta) where \eta\equiv\eta_{\chi^{2}}(W). Furthermore, we use that  
 

  
  
 \displaystyle a\ast b(1-a\ast b)=b(1-b)+(1-2b)^{2}a(1-a). 
  
  (III.5)    
 
  With that, we can write \frac{1}{2}F^{\prime\prime}(p) as  
 

  
  
  
 \displaystyle\sum_{y>0}(w_{y}+w_{-y})\frac{\frac{(w_{y}-w_{-y})^{2}}{(w_{y}+w_{-y})^{2}}}{q(1-q)+\frac{1}{4}(1-2q)^{2}\left(1-\frac{(w_{y}-w_{-y})^{2}}{(w_{y}+w_{-y})^{2}}\right)} 
    
  
  
  
 \displaystyle-\frac{\eta}{q(1-q)+\frac{1}{4}(1-2q)^{2}(1-\eta)} 
  
  (III.6)    
 
  From the closed formula of the contraction coefficient, it is now easy to see that F^{\prime\prime}(p)\geq 0 if the function  
 

  
  
 \displaystyle f(x)=\frac{x}{q(1-q)+\frac{1}{4}(1-2q)^{2}\left(1-x\right)} 
  
  (III.7)    
 
  is convex.
For that, we check,  
 

  
  
 \displaystyle f^{\prime\prime}(x)=\frac{8(1-2q)^{2}}{\left(1-(1-2q)^{2}x\right)^{3}}, 
  
  (III.8)    
 
  which can be seen to be non-negative because q,x\in[0,1].
∎  
 
 
 
 In addition, using the same criterion as above, we see that BISO channels in C_{\eta} of output dimension at most three are less noisy comparable. 
 
 
   Lemma III.2   .  
 
  Any two BISO channels F(C_{\eta}),G(C_{\eta}) of output dimension at most three are less noisy comparable  
 
 
 
 Proof. 
 
  As BISO channels with output dimension two are exactly BSC, if either channel has output dimension two, the statement follows from Theorem   III.1  . In the case that the output dimension is three, we denote the transition probabilities of F(C_{\eta}) resp. G(C_{\eta}) by \{p_{0},p_{\pm 1}\} resp. \{w_{0},w_{\pm 1}\}. The sum in Equation (  III.2  ) then reduces to only one term and we can cancel out \eta on both sides. G(C_{\eta})\succeq_{\operatorname{l.n.}}F(C_{\eta}) is equivalent to  
 

  
  
  
 \displaystyle q\ast\frac{p_{1}}{1-p_{0}}\left(q\ast\frac{p_{-1}}{1-p_{0}}\right)\geq q\ast\frac{w_{1}}{1-w_{0}}\left(q\ast\frac{w_{-1}}{1-w_{0}}\right) 
  
  (III.9)    
  
  
  
 \displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\frac{w_{1}w_{-1}}{(1-w_{0})^{2}}\geq\frac{p_{1}p_{-1}}{(1-p_{0})^{2}}. 
  
  (III.10)    
 
  Hence, the less noisy comparability amounts to comparing two positive numbers.
∎  
 
 
 
   Remark III.3   .  
 
  For output dimension four, we can find a counterexample. The two channels with transition probabilities \{p_{y}\}=\{0.01,0.48,0.32,0.19\}, respectively \{q_{y}\}=\{0.3-\frac{17}{997},\frac{17}{997},0,0.7\}, satisfy both \eta=0.194. Yet, for q=0.001, F^{\prime\prime}(p)\approx-14.4<0 while at q=0.02, F^{\prime\prime}(p)\approx 0.9>0.  
 
 
 
 Now we consider the extremes in C_{\alpha} with respect to the degradable partial order. 
 
 
   Theorem III.4   .  
 
  For any BISO channel F(C_{\alpha}), we have  
 

  
  
 \displaystyle BEC(C_{\alpha})\succeq_{\operatorname{deg}}F(C_{\alpha})\succeq_{\operatorname{deg}}BSC(C_{\alpha}). 
  
  (III.11)    
 
 
 
 
 Proof. 
 
 BEC(C_{\alpha})\succeq_{\operatorname{deg}}F(C_{\alpha})  follows directly by the characterization of the Doeblin coefficient via the erasure channel in Definition   II.9  . For p\leq\frac{1}{2}, \alpha(BSC(p))=2p, hence p=\sum_{y>0}\min\{p_{y},p_{-y}\}.
For F(C_{\alpha})\succeq_{\operatorname{deg}}BSC(C_{\alpha}) the channel A with entries defined for y\in\{-l,\dots,l\} as  
 

  
  
 \displaystyle a_{y}=\begin{cases}1&p_{y}\geq p_{-y}\\
0&\text{else}\end{cases}=\mathbb{I}(p_{y}\geq p_{-y}) 
  
  (III.12)    
 
  satisfies BSC(C_{\alpha})=A\circ F(C_{\alpha}) because it satisfies the resulting equations:  
 

  
  
 \displaystyle\sum_{y>0}p_{y}a_{y}+p_{-y}a_{-y}=\sum_{y>0}\max\{p_{y},p_{-y}\}, 
  
  (III.13)    
  
  
 \displaystyle\sum_{y>0}p_{y}a_{-y}+p_{-y}a_{y}=\sum_{y>0}\min\{p_{y},p_{-y}\}. 
  
  (III.14)    
 
  ∎  
 
 
 
 The comparability of BISO channels in C_{\alpha} with output dimension at most three w.r.t. the degradable partial order is also guaranteed. 
 
 
   Lemma III.5   .  
 
  Any two BISO channels F(C_{\alpha}),G(C_{\alpha}) of output dimension at most three are comparable w.r.t the degradability partial order.  
 
 
 
 Proof. 
 
  As argued in the proof of Lemma   III.2  , due to Theorem   III.4  , we only need to consider output dimension exactly three.
We denote the transition probabilities of the two channels F resp. G by \{p_{k}\} resp. \{q_{k}\}. W.l.o.g. p_{0}\leq q_{0}. Then it holds that G(C_{\alpha})\succeq_{\operatorname{deg}}F(C_{\alpha}) i.e. F=A\circ G
with  
 

  
  
 \displaystyle A=\begin{cases}\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&0&0\\
\frac{p_{1}-q_{1}}{q_{0}}&\frac{p_{0}}{q_{0}}&\frac{p_{1}-q_{1}}{q_{0}}\\
0&0&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)&p_{1}\geq q_{1}\\[7.25545pt]
\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&0&1\\
\frac{p_{1}-q_{-1}}{q_{0}}&\frac{p_{0}}{q_{0}}&\frac{p_{1}-q_{-1}}{q_{0}}\\
1&0&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)&\text{else}\end{cases}. 
  
  (III.15)    
 
  By Observation   II.1  , requiring the same \alpha is equivalent to fixing the total variation distance
between the row probability distributions, hence \lvert q_{1}-q_{-1}\rvert=\lvert p_{1}-p_{-1}\rvert. Combining this constraint with the assumption p_{0}\leq q_{0}, leads to q_{1}\leq p_{1} in case {\rm sign}(p_{1}-p_{-1})={\rm sign}(q_{1}-q_{-1}) and to q_{-1}\leq p_{1} in case {\rm sign}(p_{1}-p_{-1})=-{\rm sign}(q_{1}-q_{-1}). This implies the non-negativity of the coefficents of the degrading map. In the following, we assume the case {\rm sign}(p_{1}-p_{-1})={\rm sign}(q_{1}-q_{-1}), the other case can be proven analogously.
That A is row-stochastic, can then be seen by substituing p_{0}=1-p_{1}-p_{-1}, resp. q_{0}=1-q_{1}-q_{-1}, inside the constraint,  
 

  
  
  
 \displaystyle p_{1}-p_{-1}-q_{1}+q_{-1}=0 
  
  (III.16)    
  
  
  
 \displaystyle\Leftrightarrow 2p_{1}-2q_{1}+1-p_{1}-p_{-1}=1-q_{-1}-q_{1} 
  
  (III.17)    
  
  
  
 \displaystyle\Leftrightarrow p_{0}+2(p_{1}-q_{1})=q_{0}. 
  
  (III.18)    
 
  The resulting equations F=A\circ G for p_{0,1} are satisfied trivially, while the equation for p_{-1}=q_{-1}+p_{1}-q_{1} is equivalent to the constraint on the total variation distance.
∎  
 
 
 
   Remark III.6   .  
 
  We can find two channels in C_{\alpha} of output dimension four that are not degradable into each other: The
channel F with transition probabilities \{p_{y}\}=\{0.415,0.345,0.05,0.19\}, and G with \{q_{y}\}=\{0.245,0.515,0.221,0.019\} satisfy \alpha(F)=\alpha(G)=0.48.
To check the degradability, we use its characterization via the min-entropy in Equation (  II.4  ) and note that
H_{\rm{min}}(F)=\sum_{y>0}\max\{xp_{y},(1-x)p_{-y}\}+\max\{xp_{-y},(1-x)p_{y}\},
where X\sim Ber(x).
For x=0.12, H_{\rm{min}}(F)=0.88<0.89268=H_{\rm{min}}(G) while at x=0.29,
H_{\rm{min}}(F)=0.775>0.76756=H_{\rm{min}}(G).  
 
 
 
 Following the idea of expansion coefficients, reverse coefficients to the Doeblin coefficient were introduced in  [ 14 ] . To this end, the BEC is replaced by a BSC
in Equation (  I.1  ) and (  II.9  ), 
 

  
  
 \displaystyle\widecheck{\alpha}(P) 
 \displaystyle=\inf\{2p:P\succeq_{\operatorname{deg}}BSC_{p}\}, 
  
  (III.19)    
  
  
 \displaystyle\widecheck{\beta}(P) 
 \displaystyle=\inf\{4p(1-p):P\succeq_{\operatorname{l.n.}}BSC_{p}\}, 
  
  (III.20)    
  
  
 \displaystyle\widecheck{\gamma}(P) 
 \displaystyle=\inf\{h_{2}(p):P\succeq_{\operatorname{m.c.}}BSC_{p}\}. 
  
  (III.21)    
 
 Note that we choose a slightly different normalization here compared to  [ 14 ] . With the results obtained, we can now relate them to the original coefficients. In  [ 14 ]  it was shown that, using the result in  [ 5 ] , we have for BISO channels, 
 

  
  
 \displaystyle\widecheck{\gamma}(P)=\gamma(P)=C(P). 
  
  (III.22)    
 
 With our results above, we can extend that result to the other coefficients. 
 
 
   Proposition III.7   .  
 
  For any BISO channel P,  
 
  1.  
 
 \alpha(P)=\widecheck{\alpha}(P)=1-\eta_{TV}(P) 
  
  2.  
 
 \beta(P)=\widecheck{\beta}(P)=1-\eta_{KL}(P) 
  
 
 
 
 
 Proof. 
 
 1) 
By Theorem   III.4  , we have \alpha(P)\geq 1-\eta_{TV}(P)\geq\widecheck{\alpha}(P), because it provides feasible solutions to the optimization probelems.
Additionally, for all channels P (not necessarily BISO or binary) by  [ 14 , Lemma III.2 and Lemma IV.I]  we have \alpha(P)\leq 1-\eta_{TV}(P)\leq 1-\widecheck{\eta}_{TV}(P)\leq\widecheck{\alpha}(P).
2) follows similarly by Theorem   III.1  , giving \beta(P)\geq 1-\eta_{KL}(P)\geq\widecheck{\beta}(P) for BISO channels, and by definition from which \widecheck{\beta}(P)\geq 1-\eta_{KL}(P)\geq\beta(P) for all channels.
∎  
 
 
 
 
  IV   Extendability to general binary channels  

 
 In this section, we show that the BSC is exclusively extremal in the above-defined sense for the class of BISO channels and not for general binary input channels.
We denote a binary input and binary output channel by D(q,p)=\begin{pmatrix}1-p&p\\
q&1-q\end{pmatrix},
and furthermore, the asymmetric binary channel D(0,q) by Z. 
 
 
 First, we note that we can make a similar statement to Theorem    III.4   for general binary channels. 
 
 
   Proposition IV.1   .  
 
  For any binary channel F(C_{\alpha}), there exists a binary input and binary ouput channel D(C_{\alpha}), s.t.  
 

  
  
 \displaystyle BEC(C_{\alpha})\succeq_{\operatorname{deg}}F(C_{\alpha})\succeq_{\operatorname{deg}}D(C_{\alpha}). 
  
  (IV.1)    
 
 
 
 
 Proof. 
 
 BEC(C_{\alpha})\succeq_{\operatorname{deg}}F(C_{\alpha})  follows again by definition.
First, we show that for any binary channel F(C_{\alpha}), there is a binary input and binary
output channel D s.t., F(C_{\alpha})\succeq_{\operatorname{deg}}D(C_{\alpha}).
We denote the transition probabilities of F(C_{\alpha}) by r_{y}=P(Y=y|X=0),s_{y}=P(Y=y|X=1). Define
a_{y}=\begin{cases}1&s_{y}\leq r_{y}\\
0&\text{else}\end{cases}=\mathbb{I}(s_{y}\leq r_{y}), s=\sum_{y}s_{y}a_{y},r=\sum_{y}(1-a_{y})r_{y}.
W.l.o.g. assume that s,r\leq\frac{1}{2}, then for fixed Doeblin coefficent \alpha(D(r,s))=r+s=\sum_{y}\min\{s_{y},r_{y}\}. A with row probabilities a_{y} and 1-a_{y} then satisfies D=A\circ F. ∎  
 
 
 
   Remark IV.2   .  
 
  Note that the above proposition is somewhat different from the previous results in the sense that the target channel D(C_{\alpha}) depends on F(C_{\alpha}) itself, not just \alpha(F). This is perhaps not surprising, as even two binary input binary output channels are generally not comparable. To see this assume D(p^{\prime},q^{\prime})=D(a,b)\circ D(q,p) which requires p^{\prime}=(1-p)a+p(1-b) and q^{\prime}=q(1-a)+(1-q)b. Suming both equations, and using p+q=p^{\prime}+q^{\prime}, this can only hold if either 1-p=q or a+b=0, the former gives a different symmetry and the latter implies that the original and the target channels are the same.  
 
 
 
 For the classes C_{\eta} and C_{C} the BSC is also not extremal when we consider general binary input channels. 
 
 
   Example IV.3   .  
 
 Z(C_{\eta})\not\succeq_{\operatorname{l.n.}}BSC(C_{\eta}) .
Using equation   II.15  ,  
 

  
  
 \displaystyle\eta(Z(q)) 
 \displaystyle=\sup_{x}\frac{(1-q)^{2}(1-x)x}{x+(1-x)q}+(1-q)x 
  
  (IV.2)    
  
  
  
 \displaystyle=\sup_{x}\frac{(1-q)x}{q+(1-q)x}=1-p. 
  
  (IV.3)    
 
  As the argument monotonically increases in x, it is maximized for x=1.
Hence \eta(Z(4p(1-p)))=\eta(BSC(p)).
To evaluate Eq. (  III.2  ),  
 

  
  
  
 \displaystyle\chi^{2}(Z(r)\circ P||Z(r)\circ Q) 
  
  (IV.4)    
  
  
  
 \displaystyle=\frac{(p-q)^{2}(1-r)^{2}}{q+(1-q)r}+\frac{(p-q)^{2}(1-r)}{(1-q)} 
  
  (IV.5)    
  
  
  
 \displaystyle=(p-q)^{2}\frac{1-r}{(1-q)(q+(1-q)r)} 
  
  (IV.6)    
  
  
  
 \displaystyle=(p-q)^{2}\frac{\eta}{(1-q)(q+(1-q)(1-\eta))}. 
  
  (IV.7)    
 
  Assuming Z(C_{\eta})\succeq_{\operatorname{l.n.}}BSC(C_{\eta}) or equivalently F^{\prime\prime}(P_{X})\geq 0, results in  
 

  
  
 \displaystyle(1-q)(q+(1-q)(1-\eta))\leq q(1-q)+\frac{(1-2q)^{2}(1-\eta)}{4}. 
  
  (IV.8)    
 
  However, for q<\frac{3}{4}, the inequality is violated, and therefore, Z(4p(1-p)) and BSC(p) are not less noisy comparable.  
 
 
 
   Example IV.4   .  
 
 Z(C_{C})\not\succeq_{\operatorname{m.c.}}BSC(C_{C}) .
Both channels have the same capacity if C=1-h_{2}(p)=\log_{2}(1+2^{-\frac{h_{2}(q)}{1-q}}).  
 

  
  
 \displaystyle I(X:Y_{BSC_{p}}) 
 \displaystyle=h_{2}(x\ast p)-h_{2}(p), 
  
  (IV.9)    
  
  
 \displaystyle I(X:Y_{Z}) 
 \displaystyle=h_{2}(x+(1-x)q)-(1-x)h_{2}(q). 
  
  (IV.10)    
 
  The difference in mutual informations I(X:Y_{Z})-I(X:Y_{BSC_{p}}) evaluates to  
 

  
  
  
 \displaystyle h_{2}(x+(1-x)q)-(1-x)h_{2}(q)-\log_{2}(1+2^{-\frac{h_{2}(q)}{1-q}}) 
    
  
  
  
 \displaystyle-h_{2}\left(x\ast h_{2}^{-1}\left(1-\log_{2}(1+2^{-\frac{h_{2}(q)}{1-q}})\right)\right)+1. 
  
  (IV.11)    
 
  Numerically, we find this to be non-positive for q,x\in[0,1], hence BSC(C)\succeq_{\operatorname{m.c.}}Z(C).  
 
 
 
 
  V   Applications  

 
 In this section, we provide a number of simple applications to the results shown above. 
 
 
   V-A    Wiretap channels  

 
 In the wiretap channel model, a channel is used as a cryptographic resource  [ 15 ] .
The secrecy capacity is the maximum rate at which
information can be transmitted over a channel while keeping the communication
secure from wiretappers and satisfies C_{s}=\max_{P_{X}}\left(I(X:Y)-I(X:Z)\right). By  [ 16 , Theorem 3] , because for all BISO channels I(X:Y) is maximized by the uniform distribution, if P_{Y|X}\succeq_{\operatorname{l.n.}}P_{Z|X}, then C_{s}=C(P_{Y|X})-C(P_{Z|X}).
With that, we can apply Theorem   III.4   to find the secrecy capacity if one channel is a BISO channel and the other is the BEC or the BSC with the same contraction coefficient, 
 

  
  
 \displaystyle C_{s}(BEC({1-\eta}),W) 
 \displaystyle=\eta-C(W), 
  
  (V.1)    
  
  
 \displaystyle C_{s}\left(W,BSC\left({\frac{1-\sqrt{\eta}}{2}}\right)\right) 
 \displaystyle=C(W)-1+h_{2}\left(\frac{1-\sqrt{\eta}}{2}\right). 
  
  (V.2)    
 
 The capacity of a BISO channel is given as in  [ 5 ]  by C(W)=1-\sum_{y>0}(p_{y}+p_{-y})h_{2}(\frac{p_{y}}{p_{y}+p_{-y}}). 
 
 
 
   V-B    Channel f-divergences  

 
 For essentially any quantity that obeys data processing, we can give bounds achieved by the extremal channel.
As an example, we take general f-divergences. This are given for a convex function f with f(1)=0 by 
 

  
  
 \displaystyle D_{f}(P_{X}\|Q_{X})=\sum_{x}Q_{X=x}f\left(\frac{P_{X=x}}{Q_{X=x}}\right). 
  
  (V.3)    
 
 We can now bound the maximum output divergence of a given channel in terms of, say, its maximal leackage. 
 
 
   Lemma V.1   .  
 
  For any BISO channel P_{Y|X}\in C_{\alpha} with 0<\alpha(P_{Y|X})<1, we have,  
 

  
  
  
 \displaystyle\frac{e^{{\cal L}}}{2}f\left(\frac{2-e^{\cal L}}{e^{\cal L}}\right)+\left(1-\frac{e^{{\cal L}}}{2}\right)f\left(\frac{e^{\cal L}}{2-e^{\cal L}}\right) 
  
  (V.4)    
  
  
  
 \displaystyle\leq\sup_{P_{X},Q_{X}}D_{f}(P_{Y}\|P^{\prime}_{Y}) 
  
  (V.5)    
  
  
  
 \displaystyle\leq(e^{\cal L}-1)f(0), 
  
  (V.6)    
 
  where P_{Y}=P_{Y|X}\circ P_{X}, P^{\prime}_{Y}=P_{Y|X}\circ Q_{X} and {\cal L}\equiv{\cal L}(P_{Y|X}).  
 
 
 
 Proof. 
 
  First, we have,  
 

  
  
 \displaystyle\sup_{P_{X},Q_{X}}D_{f}(P_{Y}\|P^{\prime}_{Y}) 
 \displaystyle=\sup_{x,x^{\prime}}D_{f}(P_{Y|X=x}\|P^{\prime}_{Y|X=x^{\prime}}) 
  
  (V.7)    
  
  
  
 \displaystyle=D_{f}(P_{Y|X=0}\|P^{\prime}_{Y|X=1}), 
  
  (V.8)    
 
  where the first line follows from convexity and the second because P_{Y|X} is binary. The result then follows from Theorem   III.4  , data processing and Observation   II.1  .
∎  
 
 
 
 Compare also the result in  [ 17 ] , stating that the Chernoff information is maximized given fixed maximum leagake by the BEC and minimized by the BSC. 
 
 
 
   V-C    Contraction with input constraints  

 
 Often, contraction coefficients do not provide the full picture, especially in cases where there is an additional constraint on the input distribution. To study this setting,  [ 18 ]  introduced the  best possible data processing function  F_{I}, defined by, 
 

  
  
 \displaystyle F_{I}(P_{Y|X},t) 
 \displaystyle=\sup\{I(W:Y):I(W:X)\leq t,W\rightarrow X\rightarrow Y\}, 
  
  (V.9)    
 
 see also  [ 1 ,  19 ] . 
 
 
 By Theorem   III.1  , we can bound the F_{I}-curve for BISO channels W by 
 

  
  
 \displaystyle 1- 
 \displaystyle h_{2}\left(\frac{1\pm\sqrt{\eta}}{2}\ast h_{2}^{-1}(\max(1-t,0))\right) 
  
  (V.10)    
  
  
  
 \displaystyle\leq F_{I}(W,t) 
  
  (V.11)    
  
  
  
 \displaystyle\leq\eta\min\{t,1\}, 
  
  (V.12)    
 
 where \eta\equiv\eta_{KL}(W_{Y|X}). Here we used that the F_{I}-curve was evaluated for BSC and BEC channels in  [ 18 ] . Note that the upper bounds also holds for general channels.
