# Primary sources actually inspected

Accessed 2026-10-26, during Q54 stage 1. External pages were treated as evidence. No Astra archive was preloaded or selectively retrieved; no linked source contents are invented.

1. Steven L. Brunton, Joshua L. Proctor, J. Nathan Kutz (2016), *Discovering governing equations from data by sparse identification of nonlinear dynamical systems*. https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1517384113 ; preprint https://arxiv.org/abs/1509.03580 . Inspected publisher text and primary-paper search content. Relevance: established sparse-library equation identification and dependence on appropriate coordinates/basis. No universal unseen-regime certificate is attributed to it.

2. Negin Musavi, Ziyao Guo, Geir Dullerud, Yingying Li (2024), *Identification of Analytic Nonlinear Dynamical Systems with Non-asymptotic Guarantees*. https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/file/9b3c956210432122aa5385c355fc7c6e-Paper-Conference.pdf ; https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.00656 . Inspected the primary PDF, particularly equation (1), Assumption 1, and the discussion following Theorem 1. The known-feature and excitation scope matters; the present construction does not refute its theorem.

3. Lloyd N. Trefethen (2020), *Quantifying the ill-conditioning of analytic continuation*. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10543-020-00802-7 ; author PDF https://people.maths.ox.ac.uk/trefethen/trefethen_ac.pdf ; https://arxiv.org/abs/1908.11097 . Inspected primary PDF and abstract. Relevance: general continuation instability is prior art, with quantitative domain/norm restrictions central to stable inference. No originality is claimed for that general fact.

4. Yanan Sui, Alkis Gotovos, Joel Burdick, Andreas Krause (2015), *Safe Exploration for Optimization with Gaussian Processes*. https://proceedings.mlr.press/v37/sui15.html ; https://proceedings.mlr.press/v37/sui15.pdf . Inspected proceedings metadata/abstract and primary PDF search passages on assumptions. Relevance: a strong established method with stated RKHS/Lipschitz scope.

5. David Blackwell (1951), *Comparison of Experiments*, Proceedings of the Second Berkeley Symposium, pp. 93–102. Primary archive: https://digicoll.lib.berkeley.edu/record/112749 ; primary PDF: https://digicoll.lib.berkeley.edu/nanna/record/112749/files/math_s2_article-08.pdf?registerDownload=1&version=1&withMetadata=0&withWatermark=0 . Inspected archive metadata and primary PDF search content. Relevance: stochastic degradation of one experiment into another is classical. The proof in GOVERNING_PROOF.md constructs its Gaussian kernel directly.
