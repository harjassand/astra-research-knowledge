Let K,L have the same n-element input alphabet, and m_K,m_L nonzero output rows. Let C_cond be the sharp comparison constant after subtracting the reference relative entropy, and C_eq the restriction to hypotheses with equal reference marginals. The construction proves

\[
\boxed{C_{cond}(K,L)=C_{eq}(K,L),\qquad \dim R\le n+m_K+m_L\ \text{suffices}.}
\]

The coefficient is exactly the supremum of a finite-dimensional quadratic comparison. On an active space of dimension at most `n-1+m_K+m_L`, choose positive definite operators Q_i summing to the identity and vectors u_i summing to zero. For each output define

\[
W_y^K=\sum_i k_{yi}Q_i,\quad v_y^K=\sum_i k_{yi}u_i,\qquad
F_K=\sum_y\langle v_y^K,(W_y^K)^{-1}v_y^K\rangle.
\]

Then the sharp coefficient is `sup F_K/F_L`, with explicit conventions for a zero denominator and infinite coefficient. The finite-reference bound concerns the coefficient and its limiting witnesses; it does **not** claim that an arbitrary original pair of states can be compressed while preserving its full relative entropies.

The governing proof in `finite_reference_proof.md` has three substantive steps. The BKM integral maps every quantum reference-state tangent to positive operators Q_i and vectors a_i. The exact gauge `u_i=a_i-Q_i sum_j a_j` removes changes in the reference marginal for both channels simultaneously. A span of the u_i and the inverse-applied output vectors preserves both quadratic forms exactly and bounds its dimension. Finally, a common vacuum plus active Q_i blocks realizes every compressed witness by faithful, equal-marginal states. A Schur-complement formula gives an explicit positive step size, and a logarithmic convergence bound records its precision cost. This completes the proof over all finite references rather than inferring a theorem from random fixtures.

For rational channel entries, the finite representation is a real-algebraic quantified problem. Using established real quantifier elimination, every finite sharp coefficient is real algebraic, and comparison to a rational threshold is decidable in principle.
