# Primary evidence inspected

Retrieved on 26 October 2026. These are antecedents and evidence boundaries, not endorsements of Q95's theorem. All local numerical data are synthetic. No Astra archive was loaded and no source from another trial was read.

1. **Anna Stankiewicz, Monika Bojanowska and Paweł Drozd (2023), “On Recovery of a Non-Negative Relaxation Spectrum Model from the Stress Relaxation Test Data.”** [DOI 10.3390/polym15163464](https://doi.org/10.3390/polym15163464), [primary full text at PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10457811/). The indexed primary text was inspected for the positive-spectrum representation, inverse-problem statement, and identification scheme. Nonnegative spectral fitting and data-dependent choice of basis scale/model size are established operations. They are competent ordinary incumbents, not a new Q95 capability. The paper's numerical examples do not certify the absence of modes outside the fitted range. Direct PMC and publisher fetches sometimes returned captcha/rate-limit responses; this was not represented as a new full-text retrieval.

2. **Henry J. Brown and Yury Grabovsky (2024), “On feasibility of extrapolation of completely monotone functions.”** [arXiv:2401.15178v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.15178v1), [full HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.15178v1). The introduction, problem definition, Theorem 2.1, and local-extremal discussion were inspected. Quantitative worst-case passive-response extrapolation and positive-measure extremal constructions already exist. This rules out treating generic inverse-Laplace instability or a positivity-constrained extrapolation envelope as Q95's invention. Q95 changes the question to bounded active feedback and an equilibrium functional; it does not claim to improve this paper's extrapolation bound.

3. **Piotr Bania (2019), “Bayesian Input Design for Linear Dynamical Model Discrimination.”** [DOI 10.3390/e21040351](https://doi.org/10.3390/e21040351), [primary PDF](https://mdpi-res.com/d_attachment/entropy/entropy-21-00351/article_deploy/entropy-21-00351.pdf), [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7514835/). The primary PDF and indexed text give a family of linear Gaussian models, optimal deterministic design, and binary-model analysis. Optimizing an input to separate constitutive hypotheses is established. Its energy constraint differs from Q95's peak-strain constraint; no numerical superiority over its algorithm is claimed. Q95's actual comparator is the exact Gaussian likelihood-ratio test on the globally dominating step input.

4. **David Blackwell (1953), “Equivalent Comparisons of Experiments.”** [DOI 10.1214/aoms/1177729032](https://doi.org/10.1214/aoms/1177729032), [publisher link](https://projecteuclid.org/journals/annals-of-mathematical-statistics/volume-24/issue-2/Equivalent-Comparisons-of-Experiments/10.1214/aoms/1177729032.full). The publisher metadata/link was checked; the page exposed an iframe rather than readable article text. The proof in `PROOF.md` explicitly constructs the parameter-independent simulator, so it does not require an uninspected proposition from this source. Comparison by stochastic garbling is classical and is not claimed as new.

5. **Xinyu Song (2026), “Pre-Disclosure Experiment Menus: Oracle-Relative Risk and Joint Sample–Menu Asymptotics.”** [arXiv:2608.22905v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.22905v1), [full HTML, Section 2](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.22905v1#S2). Theorem 2.1 and its recursive simulation proof were inspected. It explicitly states the adaptive-collapse implication of a greatest Blackwell experiment for a finite menu. This is an additional antecedent for the *logic* of the Q95 argument, not independent validation or priority evidence for its constitutive-law corollary. It is a preprint. Its later asymptotic results are not used.

6. **Richard J. Sheridan, Stefan Zauscher and L. Catherine Brinson (2024), “BOTTS: broadband optimized time–temperature superposition for vastly accelerated viscoelastic data acquisition.”** [DOI 10.1039/D4SM00798K](https://doi.org/10.1039/D4SM00798K), [primary article](https://pubs.rsc.org/en/content/articlehtml/2024/sm/d4sm00798k), [publisher abstract](https://pubs.rsc.org/en/content/articlelanding/2024/sm/d4sm00798k). The publisher-indexed abstract and sections on broadband DMA and BOTTS were read. The demonstrated operation is collecting broadband chirp responses at multiple temperatures and assembling compatible master curves with noise-aware shifts. The paper reports approximately fivefold acquisition acceleration on its model thermoset comparison. That is source-reported performance, not Q95 data. It is a strong physical incumbent for master-curve acquisition in the supported regime. Its output is not a uniform certificate that an arbitrary material has no slower hidden mode. Direct article fetches returned 403 on some attempts; only the retrieved primary passages are relied on.

7. **D. G. Fesko and N. W. Tschoegl (1971), “Time-temperature superposition in thermorheologically complex materials.”** [DOI 10.1002/polc.5070350106](https://doi.org/10.1002/polc.5070350106), [primary abstract](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/polc.5070350106). The primary abstract was read. It identifies multicomponent cases where one temperature-only horizontal shift cannot superpose the mechanical response, and its model requires constituent response information. Thus unknown compatibility cannot be supplied by simply assuming that all hidden modes share an observed shift factor.

The searches also returned much older relaxation-spectrum regularization/minimax work and existing optimal-input design. Those hits make a sweeping novelty claim less credible; no claim of novelty is inferred from the absence of an exact keyword match. Uninspected secondary sources were not used as governing evidence.
