# Stage 2 scientific notes, including failures

The first construction remains preserved unchanged. The second stage did not inspect siblings or private experiment material and did not use delegation, programme messages, model APIs, paid services, physical equipment or external writes.

## The strongest competing explanation was partly right

The stage1 exact n-to-error formula is only a fixed-budget statement. The strongest conventional sequential method does better in expected readings while using the same force sensor, candidate knowledge, safe endpoint and noise. Treating the fixed-n threshold as a universal full-cycle acquisition cost would have been wrong. The ordinary SPRT approximately halves the mean cost in the tested weak-signal case. Its tail is explicitly saved; an expectation is not a hard resource guarantee. A change-of-measure lower bound extends the exponential obstruction to every finite-expected-cost adaptive safe-query method.

The explanatory alternative that the obstruction came from a law sitting on the lower stiffness limit also fails: a symmetric interior pair stays a fixed margin from both declared bounds and retains the exponential signature loss. This stress is proved for every positive transition width, not inferred from a few numerical widths.

A different exact passive analytic pair shows that endpoint optimality cannot be generalized from the original ordered pair to the full analytic class: it has zero endpoint difference and an informative interior difference. This was not a failure of the explicitly pair-scoped stage1 theorem, but it prevents overextending the ordinary comparator's design to all unknown materials.

## Positive pivot attempted and completed at its actual scope

I attempted to replace exact constitutive-law acquisition with a task-first certificate: keep every response law compatible with measured force intervals and known stiffness bounds, then ask whether all such laws put the target force below a specified ceiling. The resulting asymmetric-cone envelopes have a governing optimality proof and match an independently formulated linear program. Gaussian-smoothed piecewise-linear near-extremizers construct actual real-analytic laws approaching both target bounds while preserving all data intervals. The full real-domain derivative guarantee comes from convolution, not the grid diagnostics.

This is a useful positive mathematical operation, but nonlinear set-membership / Lipschitz interpolation is established literature. The proposed representation equals the strongest ordinary incumbent. It does not yield a novel advantage. More than half the test decisions remain UNKNOWN, as demanded by the compatible-law uncertainty. No hidden transition law has been acquired.

## Withheld execution and scope

The predeclared protocol uses five families, 200 independently generated cases and four fixed force ceilings each. The certificate routine and ordinary LP receive only safe measurement intervals and m,L; family labels, generator parameters and target values are withheld from both algorithms and used only for validation. There is no training/tuning stage or selected favorable subset. All 200 cases, all 3,400 scalar calibration readings, all 800 task decisions and all 400 analytic near-extremizers are saved. This is a reproducible model holdout, not a blinded external evaluator or a measured physical dataset.

A single record's 99% simultaneous calibration interval misses at least one true safe force in one of 200 records. That record is retained. Its target interval still contains the target in this run. The formal confidence is per record, and zero observed incorrect certificates is not a proof of 100% coverage. The analytic-envelope theorem, not the holdout fraction, establishes the claimed class-wide result.
