2. A separate exact finite-resource limitation of the mask task

For the exact kernel q_(alpha,gamma)=theta_(alpha,gamma)/lambda on the
FULL tested alphabets, ordinary matrix rank gives an additional boundary.
Since L is invertible,
  rank(L^(tensor t))=2^t.
For a finite bipartite ancillary state rho and effects E_alpha,B_gamma,
write q=Tr[rho(E_alpha tensor B_gamma)]. Expanding E in a basis of the
local operator space expresses q as a sum of at most D_A^2 rank-one
matrices. The Bob expansion gives the other bound. Therefore
  rank(q)<=min(D_A^2,D_B^2),
so an exact finite kernel has min(D_A,D_B)>=2^(t/2), where dimensions
count ALL finite local registers. This remains true for mixed rho.

If shared CLASSICAL randomness is counted separately, suppose it has
K supported branches and every branch's local quantum dimension is at
most D on each side. Each branch contributes a matrix of rank at most
D^2, so
  2^t<=rank(theta)<=K D^2.                                  (RANK)
Writing b=log2 K and q=log2 D gives b+2q>=t. Here b is worst-case
support/cardinality cost, not Shannon entropy or expected random bits.
For a purely classical finite exact decomposition, D=1 gives K>=2^t.
If the purported classical label is used coherently instead of as a
classical mixture, it is part of the quantum dimension and must be
counted there. Free/unbounded classical labels do not imply a positive
lower bound on quantum qubits alone.

These rank bounds concern exact finite kernel representations. They
are not robust approximation lower bounds and are not a count of
exponentially many qubits: log2 dimension gives only a linear qubit
tradeoff. Infinite commuting resources have no finite D to insert.
