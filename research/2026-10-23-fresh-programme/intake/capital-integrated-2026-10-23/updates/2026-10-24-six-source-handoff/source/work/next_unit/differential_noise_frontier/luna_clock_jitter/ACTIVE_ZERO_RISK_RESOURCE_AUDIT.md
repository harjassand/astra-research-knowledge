# Resource audit: the proposed zero-risk extraction jump

**Scope.** This audits only the proposed extension
\(L_c=\sqrt{\lambda}\,|s\rangle(\langle a|+i\langle e|)\) against the finite-dimensional, bounded coherent-controller class in `ACTIVE_ZERO_RISK_SUPPORT_OPENING.txt`. It is not a physical counterexample or a claim that no finite instrument can approximate the extension. The opening is broad inherited context, not a blind-start claim. The initial narrow challenge is frozen in `ACTIVE_ZERO_RISK_RESOURCE_CHALLENGE.txt`.

## Why the formal jump changes the theorem

In the \((a,e)\) sector,
\[
 L_c^\dagger L_c=\lambda\begin{pmatrix}1&i\\-i&1\end{pmatrix}
 =\lambda(P_a+P_e+i|a\rangle\langle e|-i|e\rangle\langle a|).
\]
The cross terms are present only because the two input amplitudes feed the *same indistinguishable output channel*. The no-click evolution has \(H_{\rm eff}=gV-iL_c^\dagger L_c/2\). Its amplitudes obey
\[
 \dot c_e=-\frac{\lambda}{2}c_e-i(g-\lambda/2)c_a,
 \qquad
 \dot c_a=-\frac{\lambda}{2}c_a-i(g+\lambda/2)c_e.
\]
At \(\lambda=2g\), an initial \(c_e=0\) remains in the no-click \(a\) direction; clicks transfer that amplitude to \(|s\rangle\). Thus the proposed Lindblad extension really can evade the *formal coherent-only* support proof at a matched \(g\). The opening’s theorem does not cover this extra generator.

If the two origins have distinguishable records or independent reservoirs, the channels are instead \(L_a=\sqrt\lambda|s_a\rangle\langle a|\) and \(L_e=i\sqrt\lambda|s_e\rangle\langle e|\). Then \(L_a^\dagger L_a+L_e^\dagger L_e=\lambda(P_a+P_e)\): the cross term and directional cancellation disappear. Erasing which-origin information coherently after separate ports is itself a common-output/recombination operation that needs specification.

## Port and controller check

The jump has a known \(a\)-input amplitude as well as an \(e\)-input amplitude. A known \(b\leftrightarrow e\) port alone supplies no such \(a\)-to-output path. Using the unknown \(a\leftrightarrow e\) signal to create that path makes the proposed jump depend on \(g\), rather than implementing the fixed \(L_c\); adding a known \(a\)-to-output path is a new port. A fixed \(\lambda\) can of course match one nominated value \(g_*=\lambda/2\); that does not supply the missing jump port or make mid-run readout/reset part of the coherent generator.

There is a useful exact boundary here. If the controller, including any coherent syndrome memory, remains part of the joint finite-dimensional state and evolves only under the allowed bounded Hamiltonian with \(P_eH_cP_a=0\), then the supplied zero-risk support proof still applies. A Stinespring dilation does not evade it merely because an intermediate subsystem is called a meter: with no trace/reset, it is just another coherent controller. Reading and resetting/discarding a meter during sensing changes the joint generator and is the extra nonunitary resource. A final measurement after decoupling cannot retroactively alter the time-integrated \(e\)-occupation.

## What a finite coherent dilation can do

The Markov-rate claim must not be confused with the implementability of one finite channel. Let
\[
 |v\rangle=(|a\rangle-i|e\rangle)/\sqrt2,
 \qquad L_c=\sqrt{2\lambda}\,|s\rangle\langle v|.
\]
With a qubit meter initially \(|0\rangle\), a single pulse generated by
\[
 H_{\rm dil}=\Omega\bigl(|s,1\rangle\langle v,0|+|v,0\rangle\langle s,1|\bigr)
\]
has no direct \(a\)-to-\(e\) matrix element. Its norm is \(|\Omega|\), and a pulse of angle \(\theta=\Omega u\) gives, after measuring the meter,
\[
 K_0=I+(\cos\theta-1)|v\rangle\langle v|,
 \qquad K_1=-i\sin\theta |s\rangle\langle v|.
\]
Choosing \(\cos\theta=e^{-\lambda\Delta t}\) implements exactly the finite amplitude-damping channel \(e^{\Delta t\mathcal D[L_c]}\) when considered by itself. Thus a bounded, finite-dimensional *one-step* dilation is possible if the hardware supplies a phase-coherent shared coupling from both \(a,e\) inputs to the same safe-meter output. This is an actual finite-instrument construction, not a continuous Markov implementation.

Its resource classification is conditional on the port graph. The displayed pulse requires an \(a/e\)-to-safe-meter coupling in the bright superposition \(|v\rangle\). A contract that grants only the known \(b\leftrightarrow e\) port does not grant that coupling; it is a new shared extraction port. If the original “otherwise arbitrary ancilla couplings” clause really includes this port, the pulse’s coherent part can fit the Hamiltonian class, but measuring/resetting the meter during sensing is still an added intervention. If the meter is retained coherently, it remains covered by the zero-risk theorem and is not the dissipator \(\mathcal D[L_c]\).

## Finite cadence versus a Markov jump

A fresh-ancilla dilation of one short step \(\Delta t\) of the proposed channel needs a jump-branch amplitude
\[
 K_1=\sqrt{\lambda\Delta t}\,|s\rangle(\langle a|+i\langle e|)+O(\Delta t^{3/2}).
\]
The amplitude is order \(\sqrt{\Delta t}\), not order \(\Delta t\). If the joint interaction Hamiltonian has norm at most \(B\), its unitary over a pulse window \(u\) satisfies \(\|U(u)-I\|\le Bu\). Therefore a pulse producing that branch must have, up to the chosen operator-norm normalization,
\[
 u\gtrsim \frac{\sqrt{\lambda\Delta t}}{B}.
\]
If it must fit inside each step, \(\Delta t\gtrsim\lambda/B^2\). At fixed finite \(B\), the pulse duty fraction lower bound \(u/\Delta t\gtrsim\sqrt{\lambda/\Delta t}/B\) diverges as \(\Delta t\to0\). This is optimistic: the C1 ramp required by the derivative bound \(\|\dot H_c\|\le B^2\) can only add latency. So a bounded finite controller cannot realize the arbitrarily fine collision/reset limit that yields the ideal Markov generator. Reusing a finite ancilla additionally requires a specified reset/readout latency; using fresh ancillas instead requires dimension growing with the number of steps. A finite-step instrument can be meaningful, but its cadence, coherent coupling, reset, and residual death during each nonzero interval must be counted. It is not the free continuous jump assumed by the algebraic extension.

This is a resource scaling, not a universal lower bound for every possible hardware design: another physical implementation may have different port geometry. To count one, it must exhibit a bounded implementation of the *same coherent common output*, with the \(a\)-input route, relative phase, bandwidth, controller dimension, and reset cost explicit. None is supplied by specifying \(L_c\) alone.

## Disposition

Under the original independent-ancilla finite-\(d\), bounded-\(B\)/bandwidth, known-\(b\)-\(e\)-port contract, the ideal continuous jump is **not an in-class counterexample as stated**. A finite coherent dilation of one channel step exists, but it needs the shared \(a/e\)-to-output port; and making it a dissipative, repeatedly refreshed process needs readout/reset or a common bath. If the broad Hamiltonian clause includes the shared port, the one-step pulse itself is allowed, yet its coherent evolution is still subject to the exact support proof; a mid-run measurement/reset plus finite dead intervals is a different protocol and must be analyzed with its actual schedule. The zero-risk theorem therefore remains valid for the stipulated coherent generator. Whether finite-cadence interventions yield useful *small but nonzero* risk is a separate quantitative question not answered here.

No primary-source search was needed: the conclusion follows from the displayed Lindblad/Kraus algebra and the stipulated control class. The finite dilation is a mathematical coherent-control construction; it does not claim that the specified physical apparatus contains the required port.
