# Passive-reference dissipation: bounded source and control audit

24 October 2026. Three primary controls reviewed; this is not a broad priority search.

## Scope and provenance

I read the lead-owned `PASSIVE_REFERENCE_DISSIPATION_OPENING.txt` and froze `PASSIVE_REFERENCE_CONTROL_CHALLENGE.txt` before opening sources. The model, fixed-reference column identity, bounds (A)–(D), and proposed route to an all-controller result are supplied by the lead. This audit checks the proposed sublemma against standard QFI flow and interferometric coherence bounds, and identifies its operational limits. It does not prove the complete all-controller obstruction.

## The fixed-reference estimate and its closest prior

The norm-to-fringe step is elementary: for a fixed output vector `f`,
`|Re⟨f,v_g0−v_g1⟩| ≤ ||f|| ||v_g0−v_g1||`. This is the familiar fact that an interferometer's fringe visibility is limited by coherence between its two paths. Englert’s primary two-path inequality formalizes the complementary relation between fringe visibility and which-way information stored in a detector. That is the right conceptual incumbent for a pinned reference column, but it contains no signal-coupling parameter, a-specific exposure ledger, or absorbing specimen-loss probability. [Englert, *Fringe Visibility and Which-Way Information: An Inequality*, PRL 77, 2154 (1996)](https://journals.aps.org/prl/abstract/10.1103/PhysRevLett.77.2154).

I checked the constants in the lead’s fixed-reference route. With reference eigenvalue `Z|r0⟩=−|r0⟩`, the off-diagonal column obeys `v̇_g=(-iH_g−D)v_g`, where `D=(κ/2)(Z+I)^2+(γ/2)P_e`. Thus `D≥2κP_a+(κ+γ)P_e/2` and `d||v_g||²/dt=−2⟨v_g,Dv_g⟩`, giving `∫||P_a v_g||²≤1/(4κ)`. Because the fixed reference population is `1/2`, positivity gives `||P_e v_g||²≤2 Tr(P_eρ_g)`; for an absorbing dead state, `ρ_dead(T)=γ∫Tr(P_eρ_g)dt`, hence `∫||P_e v_g||²≤2ρ_dead(T)/γ`.

For `w=v_g0−v_g1`, common Hermitian controls and `D` cancel from the difference equation except for the dissipative form, leaving a cross term proportional to `Δg Im⟨v_g0,Vv_g1⟩`. Since `V` joins only `a` and `e`, Cauchy–Schwarz with the two integral ledgers yields the stated `(C)` coefficient, `||w(T)||²≤|Δg|√(2/(κγ))(√ρ_0+√ρ_1)`. Therefore (C) is not an immediate corollary of a generic two-path inequality; it is a short, model-specific combination of fixed-reference Lindblad contraction, `a`-dephasing, and actual absorbing-loss accounting. The inequalities are internally consistent under the stated Markovian/absorbing model.

## Wan–Lasenby is broader in control and different in objective

Wan and Lasenby derive the SLD quantum-Fisher-information flow identity

`Ḟ = 2i Tr(ρ[∂_g H,L]) − Σ_j Tr(ρ[L_j,L]†[L_j,L])`,

where `L` is the symmetric logarithmic derivative. Their framework permits time-dependent Markovian generators; the controlled system can include ancillas and arbitrary parameter-independent probe–ancilla interactions. They also represent intermediate measurement and feedback by deferred measurement, so their bounds encompass adaptive strategies and ideal fast gates, provided the Markovian description remains applicable. [Wan and Lasenby, *Bounds on adaptive quantum metrology under Markovian noise*, PR Research 4, 033092 (2022)](https://journals.aps.org/prresearch/pdf/10.1103/PhysRevResearch.4.033092).

That result does not immediately reproduce (C). Its object is QFI of the complete accessible state, including any measurable population or record information. It has no fixed reference state, no invariant off-diagonal column, and no risk-conditioned integral of `P_e`. Treating the `dead` flag as part of the accessible system makes its event statistics part of the QFI; Wan–Lasenby do not impose a cost for that event. Conversely, (C) controls only the particular reference-coherence vector. It does not bound QFI or distinguishability of the full output law.

The HNLS theorem for QEC metrology makes the active-control boundary concrete. Zhou et al. show Heisenberg scaling under HNLS with noiseless ancillas and fast, accurate error correction. [Zhou et al., *Achieving the Heisenberg limit in quantum metrology using quantum error correction*, Nature Communications 9, 78 (2018)](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-017-02510-3). For the lead model, `V=|e⟩⟨a|+h.c.` is outside the Lindblad span generated by the diagonal dephasing jump and `|dead⟩⟨e|` loss jump, so the formal HNLS criterion appears to hold. That theorem is an incumbent for active reference/code strategies; it is not by itself a finite-cadence or low-death protocol.

## Exact escape boundary

The reference-column lemma includes any common `H_c` that leaves `r0` invariant and has zero reference energy, even if `H_c` encodes the sensing branch into noiseless ancillas, performs syndrome processing there, or transfers amplitude among safe modes. Such operations cannot evade (C) for the fixed-reference fringe: they remain in its hypotheses. This does not mean that all useful information is a fringe. Parameter-dependent live populations, a terminal-death/survival flag, or an ancilla syndrome can remain informative after the particular off-diagonal column becomes small. No step in (C) bounds those diagonal blocks or outcome records. A complete no-go needs an additional bound for the full instrument, including its terminal outcome distribution and any final `e` residue/readout failure.

A different class genuinely exits the lemma: controls that move, split, or entangle the reference branch with sensing/ancilla degrees of freedom, so that there is no fixed invariant `r0` and no closed single column satisfying the displayed `D` equation. This can occur before specimen death and does not require resurrecting the specimen. Active encoding or reference updates may store phase in an ancilla; if the specimen later dies, reading that stored information is allowed, but no further specimen-dependent sensing occurs. A recovery that corrects dephasing only within the sensing branch while keeping `r0` fixed stays inside (C). A recovery that acts on both logical branches may leave the fixed-reference contract.

Standard ideal QEC does not license repair of the terminal `L_loss` event in this physical model. Any useful code for `V` needs nonzero `e` support; if a code had `P_eP=0`, then `PVP=0` and it would erase the signal. For nonzero `e` support, the `|dead⟩⟨e|` error occurs. Abstract QEC recovery can map that error space back into the code, but doing so here would restore a live sample after its stipulated irreversible death. A no-resurrection protocol may instead flag death, stop sensing, and decode information already stored in the ancilla; the death probability must still be charged in the full risk ledger.

Finally, the source’s exact fixed-reference argument does not rely on a finite recovery cadence; it holds for the whole subclass at any common control waveform, even idealized fast controls, so long as the Markovian noise operator and the pinned-reference condition remain fixed. That is logically separate from the finite bounded-cadence cost of a proposed QEC escape. HNLS and asymptotic QFI scaling do not supply that cadence or erase terminal risk.

## Bounded conclusion

The standard literature already supplies (i) coherence/which-way limits for a two-branch interferometer and (ii) broad adaptive QFI-flow bounds with ancillas and ideal controls. The lead’s `(C)` is a direct and internally consistent fixed-reference, risk-conditioned estimate within its explicit contract, not an all-output distinguishability result. It remains open whether the diagonal/syndrome/active-reference channels admit a comparable risk bound under finite controls. The three-source review does not support a novelty claim from absence, nor does it validate the physical interface.
