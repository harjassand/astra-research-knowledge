Main result and remaining gate

For the BG coordinate-block game, a strategy of loss delta can be
replaced by an actual strategy that always uses ONE fixed common label,
always satisfies the local coordinate constraints, and has loss at most

             (sqrt(delta)+sqrt(8e))^2
          <= (9+4sqrt(2)) delta,                         (L1)

where e is the original probability of unequal labels and e<=delta.
The new shared resource is chosen before the questions. All fresh-input
measurements are local. The operation works both for finite tensor
strategies and for commuting strategies in a common representation.
No same-side commutation across different questions is assumed.

Consequently an unrestricted base gap for a typical BG graph is,
up to this universal factor, the gap of a precise private relation-code
problem below. We do NOT prove that gap. A fixed-query entropy bound
does not close it, and summing that bound over alternative Bob queries
is false. The original low-information selection problem remains open
for unrestricted tensor/commuting resources.

1. Actual game and literal models
----------------------------------
Fix a finite bipartite graph Gamma on [k]^r by [k]^r. Questions x,y are
independent uniform [k]. Alice outputs (s,l), Bob (t,j), with s,t in
[k]^r and l,j in [r]. A win requires

       l=j,  s_l=x,  t_l=y,  (s,t) in E(Gamma).           (L2)

For each l let v_l be the value in the SAME model after fixing the
output label to l and requiring coordinate validity on every input.
Thus Alice's complete measurement on x outputs only s with s_l=x,
and Bob's complete measurement on y outputs only t with t_l=y.
Write v_fix=max_l v_l, and C=9+4sqrt(2).

The robust operation proves, at the level of supremal values,

       (1-v_fix)/C <= 1-v_general <= 1-v_fix.             (L3)

The right inequality is just inclusion of the fixed-label strategies.
For the left, apply the operation to every original strategy and then
take its value to the supremum. No existence of an optimal finite
tensor strategy is required. Commuting strategies may be written as
unit vectors eta with two commuting local algebras; no tensor or
tracial representation is inferred. Mixed finite tensor states can be
purified into a local ancillary space, or treated by convex mixtures.

The independent product question law is used in the reference-row
choice in Section 3. No correlated-question version is claimed here.

2. One common local Naimark amplification, including the co model
------------------------------------------------------------------
We may make every fine POVM projective on one common enlarged local
space while leaving the original shared vector embedded in a fixed
input-independent way. Here is an explicit construction that does not
assume an operator-algebra isometry has an unproved unitary completion.

For one local m-outcome POVM (E_a), let
 V:H -> H^m,  V xi=(sqrt(E_a) xi)_a.
Then V*V=I. On H direct-sum H^m, with codomain ordered as H^m
direct-sum H, the block operator

        U = [ V      I-VV* ]
            [ 0        V* ]                              (L4)

is unitary: V*(I-VV*)=0, and I-VV* is a projection. Embed the original
H in the first input summand. Conjugate the m coordinate projections
in the first output summand by U; assign the final output summand to
one default original outcome. These are a PVM with compression E_a.
Use the same ancillary size, embedding and output labels for all
questions, while allowing U to depend on the player's own question.

All entries of U belong to the original local von Neumann algebra
and its finite matrix amplifications. Perform the analogous dilation
on a SEPARATE Bob ancilla. Alice and Bob's enlarged algebras still
commute, and the joint fine table on the embedded vector is unchanged.
This also gives the usual finite tensor dilation when H is finite.

Henceforth E_(s,l)^x and F_(t,j)^y denote actual local fine PVMs,
with common unit vector eta. Put
 P_l^x=sum_s E_(s,l)^x,  Q_l^y=sum_t F_(t,l)^y.
These are coarse PVMs. Different x settings on Alice's side need not
commute; neither do different Bob y settings. Only cross-party
commutation is used below.

3. An input-independent shared-label resource
---------------------------------------------
Let delta_xy be the original losing probability on (x,y), and
 e_xy=Pr[l_A!=l_B | x,y]. Then e_xy<=delta_xy. Define
 delta=E_(x,y) delta_xy,  e=E_(x,y) e_xy.

In one MATHEMATICAL label register define the isometries on eta

       V_x eta=sum_l |l> P_l^x eta,
       W_y eta=sum_l |l> Q_l^y eta.

Because the coarse projections are complete and commute across sides,

 ||V_x eta-W_y eta||^2
 =sum_l <eta,(P_l^x+Q_l^y-2P_l^x Q_l^y)eta>
 =2 e_xy.                                                (L5)

Choose a fixed positive-prior x0 with E_y e_(x0,y)<=e. Squared triangle
inequality, followed by averaging the independent x and y, gives

 E_x ||V_x eta-V_x0 eta||^2
 <=2 E_(x,y)||V_x eta-W_y eta||^2
    +2 E_y||W_y eta-V_x0 eta||^2
 <=8e.                                                   (L6)

As a mathematical embedding, duplicate the comparison label into two
registers. Choose the following ACTUAL NEW shared resource before either
question, as allowed in a value comparison:

       zeta0=sum_l |l>_A |l>_B P_l^x0 eta.               (L7)

It has norm one. Both parties can measure their label registers first.
This produces a common classical l with
 pi_l=||P_l^x0 eta||^2
and normalized branch resource eta_l=P_l^x0 eta/sqrt(pi_l).
Zero-weight branches are omitted. Equivalently the initial resource
is the dephased direct sum of these states, with shared flag l.
The label is not the realized output of any fresh-input measurement.
It is an offline strategy parameter drawn from a fixed resource.
This is NOT an LOCC conversion of a particular supplied resource eta:
Alice cannot copy her projected label into Bob's register without
communication. The existential value construction selects new branch
resources before play. The dephased mixture of those branches, rather
than the coherent flag vector in isolation, is the resource used for
the Schmidt-number statement below.

For comparison ONLY, define the normalized, x-dependent vector
       zeta_x=sum_l |l>_A |l>_B P_l^x eta.
It is not supplied online. Equations (L5)-(L6) imply
       E_x ||zeta0-zeta_x||^2<=8e.                       (L8)

4. Local fine measurements, defaults, and the loss bound
---------------------------------------------------------
On resource (L7), each party first reads the common l and then runs
the ORIGINAL fine PVM for its own question. Alice may obtain (s,h).
If h=l and s_l=x, she returns (s,l). Otherwise she returns any fixed
valid (s_default(x,l),l). Bob uses the identical rule with (t,j), y.
This is a complete local postprocessing. It uses neither the other
fresh question nor the other party's realized measurement record.
The common-label, coordinate-valid strategy is therefore legal on
EVERY input, including inputs that had zero original winning mass.

For fixed (x,y), let M_xy be the losing effect of this SAME controlled
measurement and default rule on the extended space. It satisfies
0<=M_xy<=I. The parties' fine PVMs and the local flag projections
commute across sides; no same-side cross-setting product is taken.

On the ideal comparison vector zeta_x, Alice's original fine PVM
refines P_l^x. Every original winning fine event had matching labels
l, valid coordinates, and an edge of Gamma. It therefore occurs with
the same probability on zeta_x and is unchanged by either default.
For an original fine winning pair E=E_(s,l)^x,F=F_(t,l)^y,

 <P_l^x eta, E F P_l^x eta>=<eta,E F eta>,                (L9)

because E P_l^x=E and F cross-commutes. The new rule may also turn
some former losses into wins; it cannot erase any original win.
Consequently
       <zeta_x,M_xy zeta_x><=delta_xy.                  (L10)

Take mathematical direct sums over the original product prior on x,y
and apply the triangle inequality to the losing vectors. These direct
sum labels are used only in the proof and are not online information.
Using ||sqrt(M_xy)||<=1 and (L8)-(L10),

 sqrt(delta_candidate)
 <=sqrt(E_(x,y)<zeta_x,M_xy zeta_x>)
     +sqrt(E_(x,y)||zeta0-zeta_x||^2)
 <=sqrt(delta)+sqrt(8e).                                (L11)

This yields (L1). The candidate table is an offline mixture of the
fixed-l branch tables. At least one branch has loss at most the
mixture's loss. Selecting that branch as the new initial resource
proves (L3). No postselection is performed during the one-round game.

The branch vector P_l^x0 eta is obtained by a local projection after
local isometric dilation. In a finite pure-state strategy its Schmidt
rank does not increase. Thus the construction also respects a bound
on Schmidt number, allowing free private ancillas and shared classical
randomness; one can select a high-value pure component first and
compress the final branch to its Schmidt supports. A literal bound on
the displayed ancillary Hilbert-space dimension is not claimed.

5. What the fixed-label problem says about actual Bob information
-----------------------------------------------------------------
Fix any l and a coordinate-valid strategy, perfect or not. Alice's
input X is uniform [k], her output is S with S_l=X, and Bob's actual
premeasurement system/algebra is B. The complete Alice measurement
gives normal positive Bob functionals tau_(s|x) with

         sum_s tau_(s|x)=phi_B                           (L12)

for every x. Thus X and B are independent before Bob's measurement.
Write p(s|x)=tau_(s|x)(I), and rho_(s|x)=tau_(s|x)/p(s|x)
on positive-weight rows. Since X is a function of S,

 chi(S:B)=I(S:B|X)
 = (1/k)sum_(x,s) p(s|x) D(rho_(s|x)||phi_B)
 <=H(S|X)<=(r-1)ln k.                                  (L13)

For normal von Neumann algebras these are finite-cq relative-entropy
identities, not entropy differences of arbitrary infinite states.
The order phi_B>=p(s|x)rho_(s|x) bounds each summand divergence by
-ln p(s|x), so the upper bound is finite. This uses the standard normal-
functional domination/DPI facts already pinned in the normal-state
entropy source notes; no finite trace representation is asserted.

Let P be S's unconditional law. For t in [k]^r write
 N(t)={s:(s,t) in E(Gamma)} and set

 m_y(P)=max_(t:t_l=y) P[N(t)],
 c(P)=(1/k)sum_y m_y(P).                                 (L14)

A CLASSICAL strategy can use Alice's same conditional distribution
P(S|X) and Bob's best deterministic t_y in (L14). It wins with
probability c(P), so c(P)<=v_class(G_Gamma).

For each y the actual Bob measurement has classical output T_y. Let
 a_y=Pr[(S,T_y) in E(Gamma)] and let
 z_y=(P times P_(T_y))[E(Gamma)]<=m_y(P).
Under the actual joint law of S,Y,T, the edge event has probability
w=E_y a_y. Under the product-reference law P times mu_Y times
P_(T|Y), it has probability z=E_y z_y<=c(P). Measurement DPI and
then binary-event DPI give

 chi(S:B)>=I(S:T|Y)>=d_binary(w||z)
            >= w ln(1/c(P))-h_binary(w).                (L15)

If w>=c(P), the middle lower bound may also be replaced by
d_binary(w||c(P)). For a perfect fixed-label strategy in particular,

         chi(S:B)>=ln(1/c(P))
                  >=ln(1/v_class(G_Gamma)).             (L16)

Positive-success cases have z>0; zero-weight rows are simply omitted.
If the classical value is zero then the graph supports no accepted
fine pair, so a positive quantum payoff is impossible as well.

This is an actual, dimension-independent necessary condition, but it
does not settle the typical BG gate. Its supplied classical upper
bound yields only an Omega(ln k) lower bound, whereas (L13) permits
order r ln k. The gap between those scales is load-bearing.

6. Why a query-by-query amplification is invalid
-------------------------------------------------
The tempting replacement of (L15) by a SUM over alternative y
measurements is false for actual quantum systems. Here is the full
finite calculation, rather than an appeal to counterfactual language.

Let n be even and S=(S_1,...,S_n) uniform in F_2^n. Encode
  |psi_s>=(1/sqrt(n))sum_i (-1)^(s_i)|i>.
For any perfect matching y on [n], Bob measures the orthonormal basis
 (|i>+|j>)/sqrt(2), (|i>-|j>)/sqrt(2), one pair per matching edge.
The two amplitudes are
 [(-1)^(s_i)+(-1)^(s_j)]/sqrt(2n),
 [(-1)^(s_i)-(-1)^(s_j)]/sqrt(2n).
Exactly one survives. Output T_y=(edge,parity) therefore gives a
uniform matching edge and the exact bit S_i xor S_j. Consequently

       I(S:T_y)=ln2 for EVERY y,
       rho_bar=I_n/n,  chi(S:B)=ln n.                    (L17)

Already n/2 distinct cyclic matchings between two equal halves of
[n] give sum_y I(S:T_y)=(n/2)ln2, unbounded relative to ln n.
Using all perfect matchings gives an even larger violation. Each
individual measurement obeys DPI. Their outcomes are not one jointly
available record, so the summed inequality is not a chain rule.

This failure survives a genuine privacy condition: conditioning one
fixed bit X=S_1 leaves the average encoded state I_n/n, because every
off-diagonal phase still averages to zero. Hence I(X:B)=0, as well
as I(X:T_y)=0 for every fixed query. Privacy of one input does not
make the different relational readouts simultaneously accessible.

This is the conventional Hidden Matching encoding. It falsifies the
proposed GENERAL information amplification, not a random-Gamma bound
and not the existence of some useful low-information selected history.

There is also a matched perfect-game scale check inside the previously
proved adjusted-exponent BG template. Its actual MS^m strategy has
k=3^m, always l=1, and resource Phi_(4^m). For every fixed Alice
input tuple the joint row PVM has 4^m rank-one outcomes. Conditional
Bob states are pure, average I/(4^m), and S records the input and
outcome injectively. Thus

       chi(S:B)=H(S|X)=m ln4=O(ln k),                   (L18)

while that graph's classical value is at most k^(-c/2). This does
NOT describe a typical unplanted graph or the original .2/.1 exponents.
It demonstrates that the coarse graph template and classical gap do
not supply the missing r-fold quantum information amplification.

7. Consequence for the original selection programme
-----------------------------------------------------
The typical ORIGINAL BG random-graph source already supplies, after
any nonempty tested C, zero selected live loss and L/|J| tending to
zero. The separate frozen finite-dimension theorem obstructs COMPLETE
extraction with its stated growing Schmidt-number bound. It does not
establish a positive unrestricted entangled/commuting base gap.

The present operation removes one possible excuse for that remaining
base-gap question: near-perfect strategies cannot rely essentially on
an input-dependent common choice of l. They would yield a high-value
always-valid one-label quantum relation code, with actual private Bob
states satisfying (L12)-(L13). A substantive next theorem would have
to control that code for a typical UNPLANTED random graph by an
operator-sensitive property, not by summing the separate query Holevo
bounds or by reusing only the small-rectangle classical argument.
No such theorem, generic cheap repair, or unrestricted no-go is proved.
