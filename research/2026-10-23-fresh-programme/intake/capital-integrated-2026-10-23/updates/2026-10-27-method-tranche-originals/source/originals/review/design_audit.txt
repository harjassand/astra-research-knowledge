ASTRA META-RESEARCH PILOT: DESIGN AUDIT AND BLIND-SCORING NOTES
Date: 2026-10-25
Role: independent methodological critic
Status: early methodological advice plus later material review and label-blind scoring.

Materials boundary
At the time of the first three checks, experiment/ contained no files. I have not
inspected any other investigator's output, scientific branch, or retriever state.
The first-pass threats below were written against the design brief. A subsequent
review of the frozen materials is recorded in the addendum at the end. No worker
responses or other investigator outputs were accessed in either stage.

The proposed study is a useful bounded feasibility and elicitation pilot: three
source-derived research-allocation vignettes, two prompt packages, two independent
starts per cell, a fixed model/reasoning setting, no tools, and a common short
response schema. It can reveal whether these two prompt packages elicit different
answers on these three cases. It cannot establish better science, a uniquely
effective research philosophy, held-out generalization, or downstream discovery
value. The following eight threats deserve attention before spawning.

1. QUESTION BEGGING THROUGH THE OUTCOME DEFINITION — HIGH PRIORITY

Risk: Arm B requests mechanism/input/output/access/cost decomposition, a minimal
missing capability, and an informed incumbent. If the rubric awards points for
those very items, the pilot rewards compliance with B's instructions. Likewise,
if every "good" allocation means moving away from a finite result toward empirical
acquisition, the conclusion is embedded in the tasks and scoring. Recognizing an
acquisition gap is not automatically the right use of the next unit of effort.

Before launch: Score evidence-grounded decision validity, assumption fidelity,
test discrimination, and justified claim scope. Do not score checklist labels or
mechanism vocabulary as outcomes. Write vignette-specific anchors that admit more
than one defensible allocation. Explicitly allow continuation of a finite kernel
or a restricted theorem when it serves the stated decision horizon. An obstruction
can justify stopping one route without requiring a particular new research agenda.
Pre-list conditions under which B would be unhelpful, overcautious, or wrong.

Interpretation: A B advantage under a vocabulary-matched rubric would show superior
elicitation of prescribed content, not superior research allocation. Keep that
distinction visible even if the content itself is useful.

2. BASELINE COMPETENCE AND ASYMMETRIC TASK REQUIREMENTS — HIGH PRIORITY

Risk: "Generic expert" can be made artificially weak by omitting ordinary expert
practice. If only B is told to compare a strong alternative, charge relevant
resources, or respect the actual objective, a win partly reflects unequal task
requirements. Conversely, putting B's full procedure into A removes the intended
intervention. These are different issues and should not be conflated.

Before launch: Both arms should share the objective, evidence, resource boundary,
required decision/falsifier/preserve-stop fields, factual constraints, and ordinary
expectations of expert judgment. A can be asked to consider credible alternatives,
relevant uncertainty and cost, using its best method. B's explicit capability-map
procedure remains the manipulation. Freeze the exact wording of both arms before
seeing any answer. Let the critic inspect A for an obvious missing competence.

Interpretation: This compares two whole prompt packages. It does not isolate which
B component mattered or establish that B is distinct from competent scientific
practice. Do not call the comparison a test of B against the strongest possible
prompt unless that much stronger baseline-development claim is justified.

3. SOURCE SELECTION AND ANSWER LEAKAGE — HIGH PRIORITY

Risk: The investigators know the histories from which these cases were derived.
Choosing three cases that motivated B, then compressing them using B's concepts,
can make the answers unusually recoverable by B. Source titles, retrospective
judgments, and words such as "only a kernel," "missing acquisition," or "local
obstruction" can reveal the intended conclusion. Even equal leakage to both arms
can make the task too easy or turn the study into a comprehension check.

Before launch: Distinguish source facts from historical interpretation in a
private provenance record. Give both arms the same neutral factual vignette;
retain decision-relevant facts while removing source verdicts and framework
branding. State the concrete research objective, available resources, and what
would make an allocation useful. Include at least one genuine boundary condition
where blanket application of B would not improve the answer, if such a condition
can be supported by the chosen source facts. Do not manufacture one.

Interpretation: Label the tasks development-selected and source-derived. They
are not a random sample, held-out validation, or novel unknown decisions. If a
case already states the decisive limitation, describe the result as elicitation
under supplied evidence rather than discovery of that limitation.

4. AMBIENT CONTEXT AND THE MEANING OF "INDEPENDENT"

Risk: A fresh worker or fork with no parent history can still receive common
system instructions, developer instructions, memory summaries, tools, or shared
paths. Those may teach the same Astra narrative being tested. "No tools" removes
an information channel only if it is respected or enforced; it does not remove
already supplied context. Same-model workers are also not independent scientific
validators, regardless of isolated starts.

Before launch: Record the actual launch configuration and the context inherited
by workers, as far as observable. Use the same neutral wrapper for both arms and
avoid parent study hypotheses, expected verdicts, historical source conclusions,
other workers' outputs, and evaluator material in worker context. Disable tools
where supported; otherwise prohibit tool use and retain tool-call logs. If clean
context isolation cannot be established, use CONTEXT_SHARED or ISOLATION_UNKNOWN.
Do not delay the bounded pilot merely to invent an unverified isolation claim.

Interpretation: Say "independently initiated model runs" when accurate. The
experimental units of scientific scope remain three related vignettes. Agreement
across two starts is a small stability observation, not independent validation.

5. RUBRIC SUBJECTIVITY, ARM INFERENCE, AND POST-HOC FLEXIBILITY

Risk: Anonymous IDs do not fully blind a scorer when B's language or response
structure reveals its arm. The same model family may share the investigators'
preferences. Vague terms such as "deep," "minimal," or "strongest" leave room to
prefer an appealing narrative. Reweighting criteria after viewing answers or
rescuing one arm with a charitable interpretation can determine the result.

Before launch: Use identical response fields and neutral anonymous IDs. Freeze
task-specific factual/error anchors, primary criteria, weights if any, tie rules,
and handling of missing fields before collecting answers. Each criterion should
separate correctness from presentation. Score the raw responses, preserving hard
errors even when the recommendation sounds prudent. Ideally obtain two independent
initial scores before adjudication; retain disagreements. Ask scorers to guess arm
and confidence after scoring so the blindness limitation can be assessed. Do not
rewrite answers to hide their provenance through discretionary normalization.

Interpretation: Call scoring label-blind unless condition blindness is supported.
Report criterion-level judgments, substantive mistakes, and ties alongside any
aggregate score. A majority preference is not ground truth about future research.

6. INSTRUCTION DOSE, RESOURCE COST, AND BUNDLED TREATMENT

Risk: A common 250-word cap does not equalize prompt length, thinking effort,
latency, token consumption, or cognitive demands. A longer checklist can elicit
more useful detail or crowd out the final decision. Max reasoning is a setting,
not a measured equal budget. Three linked B instructions form one treatment.

Before launch: Match model configuration, word cap, output fields, evidence, tool
policy, and stopping rules. Record input/output tokens, reasoning tokens when
available, latency, and truncation. Preserve unavailable cost fields as UNKNOWN.
Ensure the schema leaves both arms enough space to express the required result;
do not grant B extra answer space after seeing its difficulty. State whether the
study targets answer quality at a fixed operational setting or cost efficiency.

Interpretation: Report any observed quality difference separately from resources.
Without measured costs and actual research outcomes, do not claim cheaper or
faster science. Component attribution requires a later ablation, not speculation
from the present two-arm comparison.

7. SMALL SAMPLE, TASK CLUSTERING, AND EXECUTION SELECTION

Risk: Twelve outputs are not twelve distinct research problems. Two replicates
per cell provide little information about response variability. Run order,
transient service behavior, retries, dropped failures, and selective replacement
can matter disproportionately. Choosing the best answer in each arm would hide
the reliability of the prompting process.

Before launch: Freeze the full 3 x 2 x 2 schedule and randomize or counterbalance
execution order. Record every launch, exact prompt, raw answer, failure, timeout,
tool attempt, and retry. Predefine the replacement rule before any failure occurs;
never replace an answer because it is weak or disagrees with expectation. Keep
attempted-run and valid-completion denominators distinct. Hash the frozen inputs
and keep the ID-to-arm key separate from scoring until scores are locked.

Interpretation: Present all twelve planned outputs and all extra attempts. Report
each task's arm contrast and replicate range or disagreement, then a descriptive
aggregate if useful. Do not use n=12 as independent case-level support, make a
significance claim, or infer broad reliability from two matching responses.

8. SURROGATE SUCCESS AND CONSERVATISM BIAS

Risk: A polished next-step proposal can satisfy a rubric while being impractical,
uninformative, or overly conservative. "Acquire more data" and "preserve the finite
result but stop the broad claim" may be safe generic moves. Repeatedly choosing
them can look calibrated without making a consequential research decision.

Before launch: Require the proposed next action to discriminate live alternatives,
name a feasible observable or derivation, and state how either outcome changes the
allocation. A falsifier must be capable of defeating the actual recommendation,
not merely restate the known gap. Scope preservation should identify what remains
justified and what follows from failure; it should not receive automatic credit
for pessimism. Score invented access, nonexistent comparators, or impossible
observations as factual errors rather than ambitious proposals.

Interpretation: The immediate outcome is quality of a constrained written research
decision under supplied evidence. Downstream value needs a later prospective,
held-out decision study with actual follow-through and observable consequences.
This pilot may justify refining or abandoning the prompt package, not promoting a
scientific or general efficacy claim.

MINIMUM PRE-LAUNCH CHANGES

A. Freeze exact task evidence, both prompt arms, the shared schema, launch policy,
   scoring anchors, all-attempt handling, and anonymous-ID procedure.
B. Remove rubric rewards for B-specific labels; list defensible alternative
   allocations and explicit ways B can lose.
C. Verify A contains ordinary expert competence without copying B's distinctive
   procedure; label the comparison as a bundled prompt-package comparison.
D. Record source selection, supplied conclusions, and observable context leakage;
   do not claim held-out tasks, condition blindness, or isolation by assumption.
E. Lock the narrow conclusion: three development-selected task contrasts and
   process feasibility, with no significance or general research-efficacy claim.

On the design brief alone, these are tractable amendments. The concrete frozen
materials were subsequently checked against the launch conditions below.

MATERIAL-SPECIFIC ADDENDUM AND TIMING CORRECTION

Files reviewed: experiment/preregistration.json, experiment/tasks.json,
experiment/rubric.json, experiment/arm_prompts.json, and
experiment/AMENDMENT_BEFORE_COLLECTION.json. The assignment key, individual
response prompts, and all worker answers were not opened. The shared frozen word

VERDICT: GO for the explicitly development-only diagnostic. No blocking design
flaw was found within that stated scope. This verdict does not certify source
scientific claims, runtime isolation, execution authenticity, or later scoring.

Why launch is methodologically defensible:
- The common instruction requires costs, alternatives, concrete outcome-dependent
  decisions, and careful negative scope. A asks for expert comparison of plausible
  investigations and practical uncertainty resolution; it is not a straw baseline.
- B adds a causal-path/weak-link/incumbent procedure. The contrast is a bundled
  instruction package, as the preregistration says, rather than an isolated theory.
- The four 0-2 rubric dimensions evaluate action, discrimination, fairness and
  scope. The explicit no_reward rule rejects special terms, caution alone, length,
  and matching the investigators' preferred mechanism. T1 permits other useful
  capability-discriminating choices, T2 does not demand microscopic reconstruction,
  and T3 permits a different platform if its test addresses the actual interface.
- The preregistration openly states purposive retrospective cue-rich selection,
  shared ambient context, independent starts, unmeasured inference/monetary costs,
  no rerolls, all attempts, one fixed batch, and descriptive task-level comparison.
  Those declarations substantially address threats that would block stronger claims.

Residual limits and nonblocking advice:
- The tasks are especially cue-rich. T1 supplies both the cheaper controls and
  unmeasured acquisition/future workload; T2 supplies the one-clock counterexample
  and observational assumptions; T3 supplies the ambiguous progression signal and
  missing compatibility/selectivity evidence. Much of the intended bottleneck is
  already in the packet. A ceiling result would be unsurprising, and a B advantage
  would concern elicitation from those supplied facts, not bottleneck discovery.
- The T1 rubric's "Prefer" clause favors prospective evaluation, but the explicit
  permission for other discriminating choices prevents it from being an exclusive
  answer key. Apply that permission substantively when scoring.
- The scoring is label-blind. The response content could still reveal the arm, so
  do not assert successful condition blindness without evidence. The scorer also
  knows the study's framework and has source-aware tasks; that is a declared limit.
- The one assigned prompt-file cat is an acceptable declared common delivery
  exception for this scope. Retain the actual read and tool logs. A single allowed
  read plus instructed boundaries remains shared-filesystem execution, not hard
  isolation. Extra access must be reported rather than silently cleaned up.
- Missing outputs, malformed JSON, cap breaches, and tool-boundary violations must
  remain in the attempt ledger. Do not regenerate a weak or awkward answer. If a
  readable but malformed response is scored, report its format failure separately
  and apply the same decision to both arms.
- Report every response and each task's contrast, including ties and disagreement.
  Do not infer broad research efficacy, novelty, component causality, cost advantage,
  scientific validation, or autonomous capability from the twelve outputs.

The early methodological advice preceded freeze/launch. The material-specific GO
was sent after some pilot launches/responses had already completed, although I
had not viewed responses and had never opened the assignment key at that point.
It was therefore not an enforced prerelease approval gate. The GO applied only
to the declared development diagnostic and must not be reported as approval
obtained before collection. No assignment mapping was accessed during this audit.

LABEL-BLIND SCORING ADDENDUM

I subsequently read experiment/BLINDED_RESPONSES.json against the frozen tasks
and rubric, without opening the assignment key, individual response prompts or
other judges' outputs. Scores were saved in review/auditor_scores.json at
2026-10-25 09:08:15 UTC. Twelve responses received 92 of 96 possible points before
any condition mapping was supplied to this evaluator. This is a descriptive
unmapped total, not an arm comparison.

The scorer previously saw generic arm descriptions during material review and
is aware of the study hypothesis. Anonymized IDs therefore provide label
blindness; successful condition blindness is not demonstrated. The scorer did
not generate responses, but same-model-family agreement is not independent
scientific validation. The principal reported complete collection, <=217 words
per response and exactly one assigned cat per worker; this scorer did not
independently audit the runtime receipts.

Substantive concerns retained in the score file:
- R01 and R05 move from a failed capture discriminant to unqualified abandonment
  of the route or representation. Failure should remain scoped to tested
  substrate/conditions unless a stronger obstruction is actually established.
  Neither response explicitly rejects the valid conditional finite-state proof.
- R04 checks assumptions, then says that if they hold it would investigate
  clock-dependent kinetics or limits of the representation. Clock-dependent
  kinetics would itself violate the unchanged-kinetics premise. Constraint
  violations need premise, measurement and implementation audits; the response
  does not explicitly establish or claim a conditional-theorem falsification.
- R05 handles assay confounds well, but names the display incumbent mainly as a
  fallback rather than operationalizing a capability comparison.
- R10's localization of the current representation as the weakest link is
  stronger than the supplied cost/workload evidence. Its actual evaluation plan
  is useful; this secondary concern did not receive an extra invented penalty.
- R07's setting-specific rejection of an empirically incompatible representation
  and R11's retention of a simpler effective fit at tested clocks were not scored
  as rejection of a theorem or identification of a physical generator.

The results are close to the coarse rubric ceiling. Ties were retained and no
differences were forced. No arm-effect, general-efficacy or scientific-validity
claim follows from these scores; condition aggregation must occur only after all
judges' initial scores are locked.
