RESULT

The deeper limitation of an identical-agent discovery portfolio is that
research value need not be separable over the results agents can currently
produce. A capability may require compatible theory, acquisition, preparation
and readout together. An excellent optimizer of individually checked results
can rationally accumulate the easier components while leaving every valuable
operation incomplete. More independent seeds do not acquire a missing physical
interface or change a shared wrong representation. This is an objective and
feasible-action problem in addition to correlated search.

The constructive result here is a fully specified small Bayesian allocation
counterexample. With identical information and nine unit-cost work calls,
the exact optimizer of resolved gates obtains 3.447 checked gates but 0.447
completed two-gate programmes in expectation. The exact programme optimizer
obtains 2.136 gates but 0.617 programmes. A cheap bottleneck index reaches 0.6173
in this case, but has a substantial failure region. These numbers are SYNTHETIC;
they estimate neither Astra's performance nor the probability or significance
of a civilisation-changing discovery.

2. ANALYTIC MECHANISM

Let a declared programme j require compatible gates G_j. For a simple pair,
U_j(S)=1{T_j in S AND I_j in S}. The marginal value of I_j is zero at S=empty
and one at S={T_j}. Thus the objective has increasing returns across complements;
it is not the diminishing-returns setting in which independently ranking local
outputs is automatically sensible. With T_j known, {I_j} is a minimal cut:
leaving I_j false keeps that programme incomplete regardless of extra T results.
For a larger dependency graph, cuts and alternative sufficient paths matter;
there need not be a unique bottleneck. A new representation can change the
graph itself, as E2's finite certificate does.

The relevant question is which attainable operation or missing evidence would
change the declared programme's status per total remaining cost. It is not
which component currently offers the largest count of accepted facts. This
does not make conditional theorems worthless: for a foundational programme the
theorem itself may be the terminal achievement. The scientific claim and its
significance must determine the gates, rather than a universal engineering
checklist or an unearned mission-level label.

Correlated replication: let Z~Bernoulli(q) denote whether the current
representation/interface is viable. Conditional on Z=1, each attempt succeeds
with probability p; conditional on Z=0, none does. Independent random seeds
then give P(any success after n attempts)=q[1-(1-p)^n], bounded above by q.
The IID-marginal fiction instead gives 1-(1-qp)^n. At q=.35, p=.7, n=10,
these are .349998 and .939818. Pairwise outcome correlation is
p(1-q)/(1-qp)=.602649. This calculation concerns a stipulated common latent
obstruction, not a measured Astra correlation. Different agendas, observations
or mechanism classes can change which latent obstruction is shared; merely
renaming identical workers need not do so. If an indispensable action is absent
from every worker's legal/physical interface, its acquisition probability in
that action set is zero, regardless of the seed count.

Maturation/option value: work can create a reusable partial state before a
checked result appears. In the model below, two paid preparation calls have
this property. They remain available after suspension. A continuation should
depend on remaining cost, posterior feasibility and alternative openings,
not sunk cost alone. After f failed tests the posterior is
q_f=q(1-p)^f/[1-q+q(1-p)^f]. In the base case it is .35, .13907, .04622 after
zero, one and two failures. A crude comparison of one more mature test with
the average fresh setup-and-test rate favors continuation when q_f>=q/(m+1).
At m=2 the threshold is .11667: the first failure need not imply abandonment,
while two failures can justify another direction. This is an illustrative
local comparison, not a universal stopping theorem. Correlated failures can
lower the value of persistence; retained progress can raise it. Both matter.

There are three initially symmetric candidate programmes and nine paid
one-credit calls. Theory T succeeds with probability .9 per call. Interface I
needs two deterministic preparation calls, then succeeds with probability .7
per attempt if its candidate-specific latent Z=1; q=.35. Distinct candidates'
Z values are independent, an optimistic assumption about diversification.
Only T AND I completes a programme. Preparation persists, every failure is
observed, and every policy updates its posterior. Policies know the true model
but never see Z. No policy is given a successful construction, free history,
different observations or extra inference. Costs of the allocator itself are
not modeled, so the exact optimizer is a benchmark rather than a free real tool.

Three policies:
  GATE: exact finite-horizon Bayesian optimizer of expected resolved T+I gates.
        Ties favor completed programmes. This is a competent, fully adaptive
        optimizer of a plausible scoped-output metric, not a random or forgetful
        straw baseline. It never counts duplicate resolution of a gate.
  PROGRAMME: exact optimizer of expected completed programmes, ties favor gates.
        This is the strong mission-aligned benchmark in this stipulated model.
  INDEX: choose the candidate with posterior feasibility divided by remaining
        conditional expected completion cost; investigate I before paying for
        T, then finish T after I succeeds. It accounts for maturation, failed
        attempts and horizon feasibility; uses GATE fallback for partial work
        when no completion is possible. This cheap rule is not claimed optimal.

4. OBSERVATION DOES NOT IDENTIFY AN ALLOCATION INTERVENTION

Warm/protected histories, chosen questions, models, source access and execution
continuity all affect both allocation and outcomes. Selecting only completed
packages additionally favors faster/easier tasks. A strict numeric illustration:
let U~Bernoulli(.5) be task tractability; Y(1)=Y(0)=U, so protected allocation
has zero causal effect. Give protected allocation with probability .8 when U=1
and .2 when U=0. Then observed success is .8 versus .2, despite both do-effects
being .5. This is an analytic confounding example, not a fitted explanation of
Dots or another branch. E1 and E5 already disclose the relevant historical
differences. Counting warm successes cannot establish protection's effect;
blindness of outcome checking also does not randomize assignment.

5. TESTABLE HYPOTHESES AND A FEASIBLE NEXT EXPERIMENT

H1: On genuine scoped research targets with a nominated unresolved complement,
an owner-preserving allocation that reserves work for resolving that complement
will improve passage of ALL predeclared scientific gates per equal assigned
budget relative to ordinary autonomous allocation, even if intermediate checked
outputs decrease. The competent control must already receive the same capability
goal, incumbent comparisons, source content, autonomy and usable prior history.
The effect may be zero or adverse; the synthetic result supplies no effect size.

H2: For attempts sharing a nominated representation/interface obstruction,
failure outcomes will cluster more than an IID marginal-success model predicts.
After charging preparation and history, additional same-method seeds will have
less marginal completed-target yield than that IID model predicts. This can
fail if seed diversity actually explores distinct causal routes; source-topic
similarity alone is not enough to declare a common obstruction.
