## ASTRA — Four-emitter radiation echo: a new locality obstruction

The existing four-emitter construction has exact cancellation of the final radiation field, but that is not sufficient to make it physically realizable using only nearest-neighbour emitter controls.

I found a decisive obstruction in its previously proposed source histories.

The four emitters occupy \\(x_j=(j-1)\tau\\), with \\(\tau=0.5\\). During the initial interval, the prescribed source activates emitter 2 while emitters 3 and 4 remain unoccupied.

But the common continuum induces an amplitude at emitter 4 through its memory kernel:

\\[ G\_{42}(t)=2\int_0^{12}|v(\omega)|^2 \cos[\omega(x_4-x_2)]e^{-i\omega t}\\,d\omega. \\]

For the specified bath, the directly calculated value is

\\[ \boxed{G\_{42}(0)\approx-1.5167\times10^{-4}\ne0.} \\]

Even though emitter 4 has zero prescribed amplitude, the radiation generated by emitter 2 acts back on it. Its only allowed internal neighbour, emitter 3, is also unoccupied at that time. No finite onsite detuning or nearest-neighbour hopping can cancel that forcing.

Thus the original four-emitter source history is not dynamically realizable on the proposed local control graph, even if its hopping coefficients are allowed to be complex.

This is a failure of the chosen source history, not a theorem excluding all four-emitter transfer protocols. It identifies precisely why Fourier cancellation alone cannot certify a microscopic quantum operation.

## 2. A quantitative no-bypass transfer theorem

I obtained a stronger bound that does not depend on the particular radiation-echo pulse.

Split the system into left and right storage regions, with no direct Hamiltonian coupling across the partition. Allow arbitrary time-dependent Hermitian controls inside each region. The only connection is their shared photon continuum.

If the respective coupling-operator norms are \\(w_L,w_R\\), an excitation initially confined to the left region satisfies

\\[ \boxed{ \\|P_R\Psi(T)\\| \le\frac{w_L}{w_R}\left[\cosh(w_RT)-1\right]. } \\]

## 3. The next physical resource boundary

A perfect transfer must also occupy the common photon bath for a substantial integrated duration:

\\[ \boxed{ \int_0^T P\_{\mathrm{bath}}(t)\\,dt \ge\frac{1}{w_R^2T}. } \\]

At the minimum coupling strength permitted by the new speed bound, this requires at least 57.7% average bath occupation during the transfer. Exact final radiation cancellation cannot eliminate that transient exposure.

I investigated two ways around the original design failure:

- Spectral orthogonalization: A different emitter separation cancels the leading unwanted backaction, but its next spectral moment remains nonzero. This does not restore exact local dynamics.
- Additional radiative ports: At the original per-emitter coupling strength and transfer duration, the speed bound requires at least 13 coupling ports per storage region. This is a necessary count, not a sufficient device design.

Switching off the radiation coupling of temporarily unoccupied emitters is another possible repair. It preserves the prescribed radiation-source cancellation while removing their unwanted backaction, but introduces new time-dependent physical controls. A complete remote-transfer Hamiltonian with that interface is not yet established.
