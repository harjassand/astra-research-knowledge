ACTUAL NEW-SOLUTION PRECISION: ADAPTIVE-INPUT LOWER BOUND
24 October 2026. Same operator/noise interface as the positive procedure.

Purpose and status

The completed global-preconditioner lower bound does not by itself imply that
a particular new solution is hard. Indeed these matrices have only two distinct
eigenvalues, and exact arithmetic CG solves them in two exact actions. This note
instead bounds precision cost for the actual solution A_v^{-1}b with the stated
noisy matvec interface, allowing arbitrary adaptive inputs and arbitrary retained
state/postprocessing. Its ingredients are classical Gaussian testing/Assouad
arguments, not a new lower-bound method or an optimal source-acquisition theorem.

Interface

Let b be known and unit, delta>0, beta=delta/(1+delta). For unit v perpendicular
to b, put A_v=I+(delta/2)(b+v)(b+v)^T. Then
 x_v=A_v^{-1}b=(1-beta/2)b-(beta/2)v.
A query (q,m), selected from the past transcript and algorithm randomness,
returns Y=A_v q+N(0,sigma^2 I/m). Its charged energy is m||q||^2. Assume the
expected total energy is <=B under EACH candidate v. Finite adaptive sequences
are covered directly. A random initial history is allowed provided its law is
the same for all candidate values of the NEW v; it may already reveal its full
allowed task subspace. The lower bound grants unlimited free computation and
storage, so charging those separately cannot invalidate it.

Gaussian adaptive transcript identity

For two candidate unit vectors v,w, the chain rule and conditional Gaussian
density ratio give
 KL(P_v||P_w)= E_v sum_j m_j ||(A_v-A_w)q_j||^2/(2sigma^2).
The algorithm's conditional query kernel is the same under the two worlds and
adds no likelihood term. Since u_v=(b+v)/sqrt(2) is unit,
 ||A_v-A_w||op^2=delta^2[1-(u_v^T u_w)^2]
               <=(delta^2/2)||v-w||^2.
Consequently
 KL(P_v||P_w)<=delta^2 ||v-w||^2 B/(4sigma^2).       (1)
Exposure averaging and a single high-amplitude input cannot evade (1): both
increase charged energy by their squared signal amplification.

Dimension term, even when the shared subspace is known exactly

Let the allowed known space for v have dimension q>=2 and choose orthonormal
v0,e1,...,ep within it, p=q-1. For theta in {-1,+1}^p and 0<a<=1, set
 v_theta=sqrt(1-a^2)v0+(a/sqrt(p))sum theta_j e_j.
All vectors are unit and belong to the SAME known space. Adjacent cube vectors
have squared distance 4a^2/p, so (1) gives their transcript KL at most
 kappa=delta^2 a^2 B/(sigma^2 p).                    (2)

From any solution estimate xhat decode theta_j by the sign of -e_j^T xhat
(fix ties once). A wrong sign costs at least beta^2 a^2/(4p) in squared solution
error. Hence on the event ||xhat-x_theta||<=epsilon,
 Hamming(thetahat,theta)<=4p epsilon^2/(beta^2 a^2).
If this success probability is >=1-alpha for every theta, then averaging over
the fair cube gives
 EHamming/p <= alpha+(1-alpha)4epsilon^2/(beta^2 a^2). (3)

For each bit, pair adjacent cube vertices. The sum of their testing errors is
at least 1-TV. Pinsker and (2), followed by averaging over pairs, give
 EHamming/p >= (1/2)(1-sqrt(kappa/2)).               (4)
This is the elementary Assouad argument; no independent/nonadaptive query
restriction is used. Combining (3)-(4) proves, for every admissible a,

 B >= [2sigma^2 p/(delta^2 a^2)]
       [1-2alpha-8(1-alpha)epsilon^2/(beta^2 a^2)]_+^2. (5)

For alpha<1/2, choose
 a^2=24(1-alpha)epsilon^2/[(1-2alpha)beta^2],
provided this is <=1. It maximizes the displayed bound over a^2 in the relevant
range, and gives

 B >= sigma^2(q-1)(1-2alpha)^3 /
       [27(1-alpha)(1+delta)^2 epsilon^2].          (6)

Thus even arbitrary adaptive noisy solver inputs cannot eliminate the
new-task dimension/precision cost. The coefficient is deliberately explicit
and nonasymptotic, but loose; it is not a sharp numerical threshold claim.

Confidence term

For q>=2 and epsilon<beta/2 choose two unit vectors at distance h satisfying
4epsilon/beta<h<=2. Their exact solutions are separated by more than 2epsilon,
so a uniformly successful estimator yields a test with both error probabilities
<=alpha. Data processing of KL to this binary decision gives
 KL(P_v||P_w)>=kl(1-alpha||alpha)
             =(1-2alpha)log((1-alpha)/alpha).
Use (1), then take h down to 4epsilon/beta, to obtain

 B >= sigma^2 kl(1-alpha||alpha) /
                      [4(1+delta)^2 epsilon^2].    (7)

Taking the maximum of (6) and (7) gives the same dimension-plus-log-confidence
order as projected averaging when q>=2 and epsilon is within the stated range.
The conclusion uses max(a,b)>= (a+b)/2, not a claim that the two testing
constructions share one least-favorable distribution.

How it connects to acquired capability

Before any source information, a single future v can point anywhere in b-perp,
so q=n-1 is an allowed hard family even under an unknown low-rank shared-space
promise (one may choose the unknown space to contain that v). After paid source
experience has acquired a certified r-dimensional space, new v still has q=r
continuous degrees of freedom up to its unit constraint. Bound (6) then has
r-1 instead of n-2. The proposed upper bound has r Gaussian noise coordinates,
an acquired subspace-bias term, and log-confidence costs. This establishes why
the dimension reduction changes required NEW-OPERATOR precision measurements,
instead of only comparing a weak full-space estimator to PCA. It does not prove
source acquisition optimal, establish advantage over established meta-PCA, or
excuse the cost of obtaining the source geometry.

Necessary exception: q=1

For a known one-dimensional allowed space, unit v has only two values +/-v0.
Sign estimation from the scalar projected response chooses the exact solution
on the correct-sign event. Gaussian tails give success >=1-alpha with
 m>=8sigma^2 log(1/alpha)/delta^2, independent of an arbitrarily small requested
epsilon. There is no continuous 1/epsilon^2 dimension lower bound in this case.
This is why (6)-(7) explicitly require q>=2. Known unit norm also permits sphere
projection and adaptive matched probing to improve constants; no claim that the
raw projected mean is the best finite-sample estimator is made.

Noise convention and scope

Noise variance sigma^2 is PER OUTPUT COORDINATE and input energy is charged.
Changing to fixed total output-noise energy sigma_n^2=s^2/n changes the
dimension scaling. Full output coordinate readout, source storage, eigensolver
work and input precision are additional resources, not hidden free benefits.
The result is for this exact rank-one task family, not a physical PDE class.

Executable algebra check

new_task_precision_check.py checks the rational six-dimensional cube family:
all exact inverses/solutions, all adjacent-projector spectral identities and
KL norm constants, and the sign/Hamming error inequality on an exhaustive
rational grid of estimates. It checks the optimization in (5) symbolically
through rational derivative identities and evaluates (6)-(7). It cannot
empirically enumerate all adaptive algorithms; the transcript proof covers them.
