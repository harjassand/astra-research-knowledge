## ASTRA — New physics continuation

The strongest result is a passive-resonator construction for a conditional photon gate, together with two mathematical limits on how far its frequency response can be flattened.

### 1. Wider-band coherent operation with finite interactions

I reconstructed the full two-photon-port Hamiltonian, including a 10% coupling-strength imbalance between the intended photon channel and an unwanted channel. Adding a positive-energy side resonator to each emitter permits simultaneous cancellation of the selected resonance's first two frequency derivatives.

The interval is 2.29 times wider than the previous \\(0.035B\\) certificate. These bounds include photons leaving through the unwanted output port and hold independently of the memory's storage capacity.

The cost is substantial: the required minimum memory-conditioned emitter-frequency shift increases from \\(260B\\) to \\(1100B\\). This is a mathematical improvement in bandwidth, not a demonstrated practical advantage.

### 2. Two limits imposed by passive dynamics

For the specified finite-band spectral density and fixed static hopping controls, the analysis establishes:

- An in-band auxiliary resonance is necessary. Passive side modes entirely outside the photon band cannot simultaneously cancel the first and second resonance derivatives. At least one auxiliary frequency must lie between \\(1.5B\\) and \\(2B\\).
- Fourth-order flattening is impossible with scalar passive side resonators. Even with arbitrarily many positive-residue side modes, cancelling the first two derivatives leaves a strictly positive third derivative.

The second statement follows from Cauchy–Schwarz applied to the auxiliary resolvent poles. It identifies a limitation of the allowed physical controls, rather than a failed numerical optimization.

### 3. Stronger construction: one shared resonator cancels both phase distortion and loss conversion

The scalar-resonator limitation suggests a different mechanism. I added one positive-energy auxiliary resonator coupled coherently to both emitters, rather than independently to each emitter.

### 4. Exact compatibility with an entangled many-body memory

I also tested whether the photon gate requires independent, noninteracting memory bits.

It does not. Define the memory excitation number \\(K\\) and a two-body graph-Laplacian interaction

\\[ T=\sum\_{(i,j)}J\_{ij} (|10\rangle-|01\rangle)\_{ij} (\langle10|-\langle01|)\_{ij}, \qquad J\_{ij}\ge0. \\]

This interaction is positive, conserves \\(K\\), and annihilates the symmetric Dicke states. Consequently, genuinely entangled memory eigenstates coexist with the exact spectral-capacity relation

\\[ \boxed{\inf\operatorname{spec}H_N=-B\min(N,m).} \\]

Because the emitter couples to the conserved collective charge, its conditional photon operation does not disturb the internal memory state beyond the memory's own free evolution.

An independent exact-matrix audit checked 30 particle sectors through seven memory sites. The largest Dicke-state eigenvector residual was \\(2.5\times10^{-16}\\).

The assumption matters: arbitrary site-dependent emitter shifts cannot automatically be combined with exchange interactions, because they generally fail to commute.

### 5. A decisive physical vulnerability: mismatched propagation delays

The abstract continuum couplings can be interpreted using an idealized pair of chiral waveguides with position-dependent coupling phases. This makes the required interference geometry more concrete.

However, the design assumes that the two waveguide channels have precisely matched propagation delays.

Replacing one delay by \\(\tau+\delta\tau\\), without recalibrating the controls, gives the following diagnostic results:

| Delay mismatch \\(\delta\tau\\) | Maximum calculated photon-state error |
| ------------------------------- | ------------------------------------- |
| 0                               | 0.935%                                |
| 0.0005                          | 1.015%                                |
| 0.01                            | 2.632%                                |
| 0.05                            | 9.568%                                |

Delay units are inverse binding energy. These are frequency-grid calculations, not certified manufacturing tolerances.

Thus a seemingly minor geometrical mismatch can defeat the 1% target. A static hopping adjustment cannot restore the entire original frequency-dependent self-energy, although recalibration at selected frequencies remains possible.

This is the strongest reason not to claim a practical quantum gate from the present mathematical construction.
