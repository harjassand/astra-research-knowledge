## 2. New result: the choice of reproductive test changes how much contamination the system can tolerate

I reproduced the earlier three-specialist Poisson calculations and extended them to repeated, independently regenerated lineages.

A previously unresolved question was whether replacing a binary success certificate with actual reproductive counts would improve selection while maintaining its resistance to nutrient borrowing.

It does improve the match to expected reproductive productivity, but exposes an important weakness.

In the original example, public growth favours the metabolite-borrowing programme H, while private regeneration favours the reconstructibly productive programme G.

For one public generation followed by \\(m\\) private generations, the expected relative reproductive weight is

\\[ \frac{W_G}{W_H} = \frac{3.1941}{3.5706} \left(\frac{2.6809}{2.3661}\right)^m. \\]

This assumes that every private generation independently regenerates its specialist organisation and that all offspring, rather than only a threshold-selected subset, can propagate.

A single private generation gives G only a 1.36% advantage in the combined life cycle. That is much weaker discrimination than the original three-propagule binary certificate.

However, repeated private generations amplify the genuine reproductive advantage.

New exact-model calculations

| Private generations | G/H reproductive advantage at zero mixing | Mixing threshold where G loses its advantage |
| ------------------- | ----------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------- |
| 1                   | 1.4%                                      | 1.50%                                        |
| 2                   | 14.8%                                     | 8.15%                                        |
| 3                   | 30.1%                                     | 10.59%                                       |
| 4                   | 47.5%                                     | 11.86%                                       |
| 8                   | 143.0%                                    | 13.86%                                       |

Mixing uses the original model's interpolation between lineage-specific production and the resident population's average nutrient supply. It is not a measured physical leakage percentage.

The limiting mixing threshold for arbitrarily many private generations is approximately 15.96% under this resident-environment comparison. Above it, even private-phase reproduction no longer favours G.

This suggests a stronger organising mechanism than a one-off success gate:

Use repeated reconstructive reproduction to amplify small genuine functional advantages, rather than discarding biological output into pass/fail certificates.

It preserves the quantitative nature of selection, avoids requiring a molecular event counter, and improves tolerance to limited shared-resource contamination. It does not eliminate the need for material privacy, or the substantial time and resources consumed by repeated regeneration.

There is also a stricter carryover result: in the single-private-generation quantitative scheme, just three inherited molecules of each essential nutrient reverse the G/H ordering in the example. More accurate counting would not fix this; the material origin of those nutrients matters.

## 3. New result: a reusable developmental rule can outperform the best fixed organisation without accurately counting metabolites

I constructed a second exact calculation using the same 12-worker system and two environments:

- Environment A: specialist A is six times slower than B and C.
- Environment B: specialist B is six times slower than A and C.

Each inherited genome chooses how to distribute nine additional workers, starting from one indispensable worker of each type.

The strongest fixed policy I found for balanced exposure to these two environments allocates approximately 48.24% of additional workers to A, 48.24% to B and 3.52% to C.

Its expected private reproductive output is approximately 1.9843 propagules per founder in either environment.

Now consider a conditional developmental rule: give 70% of additional workers to whichever specialist is identified as slow, distributing the remainder between the other two roles.

When its context classification is correct, it produces 2.6809 expected propagules. When it applies the opposite context's allocation, it produces only 0.7291.

If context-classification errors occur independently of the ensuing developmental and production randomness, its expected reproductive output is

\\[ W\_{\rm responsive}(\eta) =2.68092-1.95178\eta, \\]

where \\(\eta\\) is the probability of choosing the wrong context.

That is substantially weaker than requiring every productive reaction to generate an accurately attributed reward token.

There are two important qualifications. The context cue must actually distinguish which developmental action is useful, and its error must obey the assumed relationship to subsequent reproductive outcomes. An ordinary monotone metabolite sensor does not automatically satisfy either condition.

This result establishes a quantitative feedback specification, not a completed biochemical implementation.

## 4. Stronger result: even a barely informative context signal can have value

The 64.3% accuracy requirement is not fundamental. It belongs to the particular rule that always assigns 70% of additional workers to the predicted bottleneck.

A genome capable of evolving its response strength can do better.

I optimized the complete three-way branching distribution separately for each level of context-sensor accuracy, allowing weak signals to produce small developmental changes and reliable signals to produce stronger changes.

| Context accuracy | Best expected propagules | Gain over fixed optimum |
| ---------------- | ------------------------ | ----------------------- |
| 50%              | 1.984275                 | 0%                      |
| 51%              | 1.984602                 | 0.0165%                 |
| 55%              | 1.992673                 | 0.423%                  |
| 60%              | 2.020318                 | 1.82%                   |
| 70%              | 2.139478                 | 7.82%                   |
| 90%              | 2.502610                 | 26.12%                  |
| 100%             | 2.708326                 | 36.49%                  |

All values are from exact enumeration of the model's 55 possible additional-worker compositions, with Poisson nutrient arrivals. The optimized response is an ideal model comparator, not a demonstrated evolvable genetic circuit.

There is an analytical explanation.

Let \\(\delta=\Pr(\text{correct context})-\tfrac12\\), and let \\(F^\star\\) denote the best fixed expected reproduction.

Near an uninformative signal, the optimal conditional policy satisfies

\\[ \boxed{ F\_{\rm conditional}^\star-F^\star =3.26928\\,\delta^2+o(\delta^2) } \\]

for this model.

The coefficient comes from the locally calculated slope of reproductive output with respect to changing A-versus-B allocation and the curvature penalty from moving away from the balanced allocation.

Consequence: exact biochemical measurement is not a prerequisite for useful conditional development. An imperfect physical cue may suffice when the genome can evolve how strongly to respond.

However, the advantage becomes extremely small close to chance accuracy. A costly sensor, differentiation delays, mutational load or finite-population drift can overwhelm it.

The meaningful physical requirement is therefore not simply a monotone response to some molecule. It is a positive, sufficiently valuable relationship between a cue, the developmental action it triggers, and the resulting reproductive function, after accounting for biological costs.

## 5. An alternative that removes context sensing entirely

A different strategy is to retain two heritable specialist-development programmes and continuously regenerate a small minority of each through stochastic switching.

The population then contains a repertoire of possible organisations. The environment selects which is successful. No individual founder needs to identify the current context correctly.

This exchanges the problem of accurate sensing for the problem of maintaining useful diversity without excessive reproductive cost.

I examined the strongest two specialised policies from the exact model. Each produces approximately 2.7083 propagules in its favourable environment and 0.6066 in the opposite environment.

Let \\(\mu\\) be the heritable programme-switching probability per offspring. In one environment, reproduction followed by switching has the expected population-transition matrix

\\[ M_A= \begin{pmatrix} 1-\mu&\mu\\\ \mu&1-\mu \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} 2.7083&0\\\ 0&0.6066 \end{pmatrix}. \\]

For equal environmental blocks of \\(L\\) generations, the asymptotic expected-population growth factor per generation is

\\[ g(\mu,L)= \rho\\!\left(M_B^L M_A^L\right)^{1/(2L)}, \\]

where \\(\rho\\) is the spectral radius.

Optimizing \\(\mu\\) produced:

| Generations per context | Best switching probability | Expected growth factor per generation |
| ----------------------- | -------------------------- | ------------------------------------- |
| 5                       | 22.05%                     | 1.817                                 |
| 10                      | 10.59%                     | 2.054                                 |
| 20                      | 5.15%                      | 2.273                                 |
| 50                      | 2.02%                      | 2.478                                 |
| 53                      | 1.91%                      | 2.488                                 |
| 100                     | 1.01%                      | 2.573                                 |

For comparison, the previous context-responsive programme with 90% classification accuracy yields 2.486 expected propagules per generation before additional costs.

In this model, the sensor-free repertoire first overtakes that particular responsive rule at approximately 53 generations per environmental block.

This is not proof that stochastic switching is preferable in real organisms. The calculation uses expected population matrices, assumes stable switching probabilities and excludes finite-population loss of rare programmes. Such repertoire-based strategies are also closely related to established biological bet-hedging; the new contribution here is their quantitative comparison within the same reconstructive-cooperation model.

It reveals a useful architectural choice:

- Rapidly changing environments favour conditional developmental instructions when informative cues are available.
- Persistent environments can make a retained repertoire of specialised instructions competitive, even without context sensing.

Both can encode reusable organisational information, but in different forms. The first retains a conditional rule within a genome; the second retains alternatives across a population. Neither requires a bespoke molecule-by-molecule reward writer.

## 6. A previously hidden constraint: specialist production competes with reproductive capacity

The earlier construction granted each founder 12 workers and then counted however many propagules their chemistry could support.

That separation was analytically useful, but it omitted a real developmental competition: a daughter irreversibly allocated to a specialised, nonreproductive role may no longer be available as an independently propagating founder.

I introduced a simple finite developmental budget \\(B\\). If \\(n\\) daughters become specialist workers, at most \\(B-n\\) others can become new founders.

The number of viable offspring is then

\\[ K_B=\min\\{K\_{\rm metabolic}(n),B-n\\}. \\]

Using the same three-resource Poisson chemistry, while optimizing \\(n\\) independently for G and H, gives:

| Total developmental budget \\(B\\) | Best G reproduction | Best H reproduction | Which wins? |
| ---------------------------------- | ------------------- | ------------------- | ----------- |
| 13                                 | 2.155               | 2.247               | H           |
| 14                                 | 2.358               | 2.298               | G           |
| 16                                 | 2.667               | 2.363               | G           |
| 20                                 | 3.520               | 2.499               | G           |

This is a new synthetic-model extension, not a measured yeast resource budget.

The reversal at \\(B=13\\) is informative. Under tight reproductive capacity, having enough production to cross the immediate reproductive threshold can matter more than maintaining the developmental organisation with the highest uncapped expected production.

It recreates the earlier threshold-selection distortion from a different source: finite reproductive capacity itself can impose an effective threshold, even when no artificial certification gate exists.

Consequently, autonomous cooperative development needs an explicitly retained reproductive branch. Creating highly productive terminal specialists is insufficient if the system cannot also generate new founders carrying the complete developmental programme.

## 7. The weakest sufficient feedback contract

The calculations point to a useful mathematical distinction.

Suppose two developmental programmes generate different amounts of useful product, represented by random variables \\(Y_G\\) and \\(Y_H\\). A biological sensor generates a signal \\(Z\\), with conditional mean

\\[ \mathbb E[Z\mid Y=y]=m(y). \\]

Even if \\(m(y)\\) is strictly increasing, a programme with higher average product output need not have a higher average sensor response.

A particularly relevant result is the standard stochastic-dominance criterion:

\\[ \boxed{ \mathbb E[m(Y_G)]\geq \mathbb E[m(Y_H)] \text{ for every increasing }m } \\]

holds if and only if

\\[ \boxed{ \Pr(Y_G\geq y)\geq\Pr(Y_H\geq y) \quad\text{for every }y, } \\]

under the usual integrability assumptions.

This is why a generic monotone sensor cannot guarantee correct selection of the highest-average-output programme.

The earlier cooperative G versus generalist example already provides a counterexample:

- Cooperative G has higher mean private reproduction: 2.681 versus 2.605.
- The generalist has a higher probability of producing at least three propagules: 58.95% versus 57.27%.

Both outcomes are compatible with a monotonically increasing measurement of function. They simply reward different properties of the output distribution.

The weakest contract therefore depends on what Astra wants the living system to learn.

For maximum expected reproductive function, uncapped offspring representation is a direct physical feedback mechanism, assuming reliable genotype-to-function coupling.

For acquisition of a viable new function, a threshold can be sufficient if passing it is itself the meaningful objective. But it does not imply maximization of average production.

For arbitrary externally valuable functions—such as synthesizing a therapeutic compound—reproduction is not a sufficient feedback signal unless a genuine physical link to the intended product is established.

There is an associated impossibility boundary: if two programmes produce identical distributions of everything the learner can observe, but different quantities of the desired external product, no learning mechanism based only on those observations can discover which programme is better from its experience.

This applies equally to biological DNA writers and external controllers.

### The strongest remaining physical obstruction

The central difficulty is closing the material-credit loop while retaining a renewable reproductive lineage.

If the founder consumes metabolites provided by unrelated cells, growth no longer certifies its own developmental instructions. If all daughters become terminal specialists, the genetic instruction may cease to produce independently viable founders. If the founder can reproduce indefinitely without specialist function, selection has little reason to retain the specialists.

The physical system needs a genuinely reproductive founder population whose continued expansion depends on function reconstructed by its own descendants.

This conjunction is not established by the cited experiments.

There is also a useful conservation constraint:

\\[ Q_i^{\rm own}(T)\geq \nu_iB(T)-R_i(0)-U_i^{\rm ext}(T), \\]

where \\(R_i\\) is inherited nutrient inventory and \\(U_i^{\rm ext}\\) is external import.

If initial reserves and external imports are bounded, increasingly extensive reproduction makes the initial reserves less able to explain success. Continuous external import is different: its contribution can grow with reproduction and cannot simply be averaged away.

## 10. Scientific consequence

The strongest combined result is a separation of three capabilities that earlier constructions tended to treat as one.

Functional discovery: variation and selection can acquire developmental instructions that improve the function directly required for reproduction, without exact molecular reaction-event attribution.

Reusable organisational memory: an inherited conditional programme can carry a rule across changing environments, and an appropriately maintained repertoire can preserve alternative programmes. The exact calculations show how sensing accuracy, environmental persistence and developmental costs determine which architecture is favoured.

General-purpose scientific or technological optimisation: neither reproductive selection nor a monotone biosensor automatically transfers to arbitrary valuable outputs. That requires a separate causal coupling or information channel.

The immediate frontier is therefore a physical one: can a newly founded, self-propagating biological lineage make its own regenerated cooperative function sufficiently responsible for its descendants that selection reliably retains improved developmental instructions?

The analytical results now identify meaningful operating limits for resource mixing, sensor informativeness, developmental investment and reproductive viability. They also show that repeated reconstruction is more robust than a single quantitative test in the specified model, and that weak cues can be useful when response strength is adjustable.

No integrated biological system has yet established all these operations, and these mathematical extensions are not claims of a new biological law or demonstrated invention. The consequential advance is a narrower, better-defined route toward autonomous acquisition of useful living organisation, with explicit conditions under which it succeeds—and equally explicit reasons it can fail.
