# Primary-science comparison and source access

Sources were checked on 25 October 2026. The construction was formed before the first primary comparison; subsequent failures and revisions are retained in the scientific notes. This is a focused bounded search, not a novelty certification or exhaustive corpus survey.

## Evidence that changes the construction

**Recovery after ischemia is a real scientific opportunity, with a much narrower established scope than whole-organism restoration.** Andrijevic et al. (2022) report preservation of tissue integrity and selected cellular/molecular processes following one hour of whole-body warm ischemia in a porcine model. Their OrganEx protocol is a coupled perfusion, cytoprotective-fluid and homeostasis system; it does not isolate an oxygen-dependent rheological mechanism or establish the present device. It is the strong system-level alternative against which a future organ-recovery claim would have to be tested. [Primary paper](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-022-05016-1).

**No-reflow includes physical vessel obstruction/constriction.** O'Farrell et al. (2017) directly investigate rat cardiac capillaries after coronary ischemia/reperfusion and associate blockages with pericyte locations. This supports keeping vascular reopening separate from redistributing flow in an already patent network. A viscosity or feed-valve controller cannot be credited with reopening these beds without an acquired mechanical/biological operation. [Primary paper and full text](https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.29280).

**Perfusion heterogeneity and metabolism matching are established problems.** Jespersen and Østergaard (2012) model capillary transit-time heterogeneity and oxygen extraction; Lücker et al. (2018) study oxygen saturation heterogeneity in capillary networks. Neither reduced raw flow variation nor increased average flow alone is an adequate objective. The present derivation instead tracks supply relative to uptake scale and makes the resistance/extraction spatial profile explicit. These papers do not validate the new constitutive/device assumptions. [Jespersen–Østergaard](https://doi.org/10.1038/jcbfm.2011.153), [Lücker et al.](https://doi.org/10.3389/fphys.2018.01296).

**Oxygen-responsive gel rheology is already demonstrated.** Matsuhira and Sakai (2025) provide the material precedent. Its deoxygenation response has the wrong sign for the first flowing-viscosity proposal. Their Table 4 stress-relaxation times motivate a hypothetical 5 s/600 s mechanical-transmission contrast; this is not a fit of the published deoxy gel to a one-mode Maxwell law. The pO2 measurements equilibrate the material before rheological sweeps, so they do not supply ligand-conversion speed. The strong response is below approximately 1 Torr, requiring a separate threshold invention for a healthy tissue oxygen setpoint. [Primary paper and full text](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.biomac.5c00669).

**The desired rheological sign itself is not a safe novelty claim.** Zupančič Valant et al. (2016) report lower viscosity/yield stress in oxygen-depleted blood in samples from two healthy donors. Their conditions and macroscopic flow simulations do not establish the proposed microvascular controller or a general normal-blood constitutive law, but they defeat any claim that the desired sign is a newly discovered possibility. [Primary paper DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00397-016-0967-y), [author-hosted primary PDF indexed by web retrieval](https://mas.ucy.ac.cy/georgios/pdfiles/RA2016b.pdf).

**Hydrogel valves, chemical-to-mechanical flow control, and passive soft hydraulic regulators are established.** Beebe et al. (2000) demonstrated autonomous hydrogel flow control; Baldi et al. (2003) fabricated and tested chemically sensitive hydrogel microvalves. Paludan et al. (2023) combine experiment and theory for soft-channel elastohydrodynamic autoregulation. The present candidate's narrow distinction is oxygen-conditioned relaxation controlling transmission of an externally powered periodic displacement, with explicit signal routing, metering geometry, and work. The general idea of a responsive hydrogel valve is not original. [Beebe et al.](https://doi.org/10.1038/35007047), [Baldi et al.](https://doi.org/10.1109/JMEMS.2003.818070), [Paludan et al.](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevE.108.035106).

**Oxygen binding can alter transport in both directions.** Keller and Friedlander (1966) investigate facilitated oxygen transport through mobile hemoglobin solutions. This is why the sensor-transport derivation retains both immobile buffering and possible bound-carrier mobility. It would be wrong to assert that a dynamic Hb–PEG gel necessarily has the immobile-site diffusivity used in one illustrative calculation. [Primary paper](https://doi.org/10.1085/jgp.49.4.663).

**An ordinary optical sensor plus conventional flow actuation remains a serious alternative.** Modern upstream oxygen-conditioning platforms already separate oxygen regulation from downstream device design. Bussooa et al. (2026) experimentally characterize a standalone membrane gas-exchange unit, while explicitly leaving mammalian-cell validation to later work. This supports an informed instrumentation comparator; it is not evidence for a distributed mechanical controller or tissue recovery. [Current primary paper](https://doi.org/10.3390/mi17091003).
