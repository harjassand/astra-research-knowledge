9. Finite-matrix proof with the tensor base value throughout

Theorem (internal proof candidate). Let G be a finite two-player game with
arbitrary joint question law mu and a {0,1}-valued predicate. Suppose the
accepting relation at each supported question pair is a union of complete
bipartite blocks whose row supports are pairwise disjoint and whose column
supports are pairwise disjoint. This includes noninjective projection games.
With epsilon=1-omega_*(G), for every integer n>=1,

  omega_*(G^n) <= exp(-2^-75 epsilon^2 n).                 (T)

Here omega_* is the supremum over ordinary FINITE bipartite tensor-product
strategies, separately for the base and repeated game. Rounds use mu^n.
No premise asserts that arbitrary commuting correlations are matrix limits.

Proof. Fix one finite n-round strategy. Purify and Schmidt-compress it once
as in Section 7. All original effects now lie in one M_d, with positive
unit sigma in its normalized tracial L2 space. Alice uses left matrices,
Bob the right matrices obtained by transposing his physical effects.

Fix a proper tested coordinate set C and its actual all-success event E.
Write p=Pr(E)>0, m=n-|C|, alpha=mean live conditional loss, and
tau=log(1/p)/m. We prove the single-game scalar inequality

  epsilon <= alpha+65536[sqrt(alpha)tau^(1/4)+sqrt(tau)].  (I)

(a) Exact tested grouping and entropy. For each fixed tested question
string c, the product tested predicate is again a union of disjoint
complete blocks. Use one label k for each block, with its indicator row
factor f_k and column factor g_k. Conditional averages of the original
coarse effects give

  F_j^k=sum_u f_k(u) F_j^u,   H_j^k=sum_v g_k(v) H_j^v,
  sum_k F_j^k<=I,            sum_k H_j^k<=I.

The label depends on c only. Both tested question strings are in the
sampled history; no factor depends on the opposite fresh live question.
The fine effects are merged with precisely the same indicators. By
bilinearity, summing k preserves p and every event-weighted live
question/answer probability, including the original answer alphabets.

For chi(F,H)=tr(sigma F sigma H), the local entropy lemma of Section 1
and weighted log-sum give -U_j<=p log(G_B/p), -Z_j<=p log(G_A/p),
where G_A,G_B<=1. Total expectation makes the masses stage-independent.
Applying the resolvent Jensen-gap inequality to the usual fixed exposure
Omega_j=(X_C,Y_C,X_<=j,Y_>j) and telescoping gives mean unnormalized
resolvent errors e_u,e_z<=p tau. The inequality is proved by the exact
second-resolvent identity: for B=sum q_j A_j and f(t)=t log t,

  sum_j q_j (F_Aj-F_B)^*(F_Aj-F_B)
       <=sum_j q_j f(A_j)-f(B),
  (F_T h)(s)=T(T+sI)^(-1)h.

Regularizing singular effects and then taking a norm limit proves the
inequality at zero eigenvalues. In finite matrices one can alternatively
work eigenvalue by eigenvalue throughout.

(b) Exact finite features and original fine measurements. Collect the
finitely many eigenvalues of EVERY coarse and averaged effect used in
this fixed strategy and exposure. A common finite span of their scalar
resolvent functions, plus 1_[1,2], represents every needed feature exactly.
The feature coefficients obey sum_l g_l(T)^2=T. Thus the column/row
construction of R in M_q tensor M_q tensor M_d preserves exact norms
and the squared-error bounds; there is no compression error or limit.
The positive unit resource is q(e11 tensor e11 tensor sigma).

Fine effects G_a<=T are transported using their bounded support factors
T^(1/2) C_a T^(1/2)=G_a and the feature polar decomposition. Kernel
defaults complete the POVMs. Alice's transport uses the column polar
factor; Bob's uses the row polar factor in the opposite multiplication
order. Their event-weighted fine probability is exactly chi(G_a,K_b).
Consequently there is one finite matrix module, an ideal diagonal law Q,
unit ideal vectors f_st and local unit descriptions a_s,b_t satisfying

  E_Q||a-b||^2<=16tau,   E_Q||a-f||^2<=4tau,
  delta_A,delta_B <= c sqrt(tau),   c=1+1/sqrt(2).         (H)

Here delta_A,delta_B compare Q to the respective fresh-question/local-
history laws. The underlying referee drift is at most sqrt(tau/2),
because D(P_full(.|E)||mu^n)<=log(1/p), entropy tensorization across
independent rounds, and Pinsker. No product assumption within a round
is used. Zero-mass histories receive complete arbitrary defaults.

(c) One actual sampler for the entire family. All a_s,b_t are matrices;
their left moduli have finite spectra already. Use S_actual without any
spectral approximation. For 0<h<1/2, its smooth logarithmic graph filters
K_A,K_B and common finite diagonal resource T obey

  K_A^*K_A=P_c,   K_B K_B^*=P_d,
  m_st=||P_c T-T P_d||^2<=128 r_st,
  K_A T K_B=e_oo tensor x_st,
  ||x_st-b_t||^2<=40 r_st,
  r_st=h^2+||a_s-b_t||^2/h^2.

The second orientation is essential for Bob's complete right POVM.
All descriptions share one finite label interval and one resource. With
N finite independent copies, each party keeps its first local success.
If A,B,C are the two success and joint-success weights, U=A+B-C, the
exact common-index probability is

  (C/U)[1-(1-U/S)^N],   S=Tr(T^2).

Taking N>=e^(2h) S log(2/xi) makes bad weight beta<=384r_st+xi.
On the common index the actually generated unit vector v_st satisfies
||v_st-a_s||^2<=512r_st. If C=0, beta=1 and the bound is trivial;
no zero vector is normalized. Every other branch remains a legal
unconditional table. The telescoping first-success POVMs are complete
for all inputs, including mismatched histories and timeout defaults.
All amplifications and finite tensor powers remain matrix algebras.

Put F_st=896r_st+xi and F'_st=2F_st+2||a_s-f_st||^2. This dominates
both the squared error ||v_st-f_st||^2 and the bad probability jointly.
For any losing effect 0<=L_st<=I, contraction of sqrt(L_st) then gives

  E_Q actual_loss <= alpha+2sqrt(alpha Fbar')+Fbar',
  Fbar'=E_Q F'_st.                                      (S)

This uses the actual good/bad protocol, not merely mean total variation.

(d) Local histories and the same tensor strategy. One independent finite
shared seed implements H's exponential-race coupling for all finite
local history kernels. The pushforward seed alphabet is finite even if
one initially describes the race by continuous random variables. Alice
and Bob use controlled POVMs at their OWN sampled histories on the ONE
sampler resource fixed in (c). They neither detect agreement nor acquire
the other's fresh question. By the marginal law and mismatch union bound,

  actual game loss <= E_Q actual_loss +delta_A
                                      +2(delta_A+delta_B).

These costs are outside the square root in (S). In particular Q is not
replaced by the mismatched-history law in either vector-error average.
The finite shared seed is the state sum_w sqrt(pi_w)|w,w>. Vectorizing
the matrix left/right POVMs gives ordinary finite local tensor POVMs.
Thus every strategy just constructed has loss at least epsilon itself.

(e) Constants and limits. For 0<tau<1/16 set h=tau^(1/4). From (H),

  E_Q r<=17sqrt(tau),
  Fbar'<=30472sqrt(tau)+2xi.

Insert these into (S) and pay the history charge 5c sqrt(tau). Send only
the finite timeout error xi down to zero. Since 2sqrt(30472)<65536 and
30472+5c<65536, (I) follows. At tau=0 use any h>0, then send h and xi
down successively; each selected protocol is finite. For tau>=1/16
the right side of (I) is at least one, so the inequality is trivial.
The base value need not be attained. These are scalar infima over finite
strategies, with no uniform original dimension or resource bound.

(f) Greedy conclusion. If epsilon=0 then (T) is trivial. Otherwise let
theta be the fixed repeated strategy's all-wins probability. If theta=0
there is nothing to prove. Set L=log(1/theta), gamma=2^-75, and suppose
theta>exp(-gamma epsilon^2 n). Starting with C empty, whenever the
conditional mean live loss exceeds epsilon/2, test a coordinate whose
conditional loss is at least epsilon/2. Each addition drops p by a
factor at most 1-epsilon/2, while p>=theta. Therefore the process stops
at a proper C with

  |C|<=2L/epsilon<n/2,  p>=theta,  alpha<=epsilon/2,
  tau<=2L/n<2gamma epsilon^2<=2^-72 epsilon^2.

Equation (I), with K=65536, would now imply

  1 <=1/2+K(2^-72)^(1/4)/sqrt(2)+K sqrt(2^-72)
     =1/2+1/(4sqrt(2))+1/(16K)<1,

a contradiction. This proves (T) for each finite repeated strategy.
Taking the scalar supremum over those strategies proves the theorem.
No attained supremum, floor estimate, or asymptotic restriction on n
has been assumed. End proof.
