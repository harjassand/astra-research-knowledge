1. Exact finite features introduce no residual error

There are finitely many original questions, histories, tested blocks and
exposure stages. Therefore all original, coarse and conditionally averaged
effects form one finite family of finite matrices. Their spectra have a
finite union. For every nonzero collected eigenvalue lambda, include
f_lambda(s)=lambda/(lambda+s) in the common real auxiliary span; include
1_[1,2] as its first basis vector. At each collected lambda,

  q(lambda)=sum_l |<e_l,f_lambda>|^2=||f_lambda||_2^2=lambda.

Consequently source R's exact-norm renormalization
g_l(lambda)=sqrt(lambda)<e_l,f_lambda>/sqrt(q(lambda))
is simply the original feature coefficient on EVERY effect being used.
At lambda=0 both features vanish. The common basis embedding is an exact
isometry for all the required vector differences; there is no 32 Lambda
compression charge or accuracy slack in this finite-matrix specialization.
The positive first direction also preserves the source's support treatment.

The normalized-trace R resource q(e11 tensor e11 tensor sigma) has norm
one: q^2 cancels the two 1/q corner traces. Its algebra is M_(q^2 d).
The fine-effect support/polar transports remain matrices, are complete on
all inputs, and retain the original answer alphabets. Bob's row/opposite
orientation is retained correctly.

2. Exact matrix descriptions permit zero sampler spectral error

All normalized local descriptions and their left moduli are matrices.

The entire family of possible local guessed histories is included once,
