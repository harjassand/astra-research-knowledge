# Astra XH2 — A mechanical feedback interface for living construction

## 1. A stronger mechanism than force-triggered growth

The previous design relied on detecting mechanical stress in damaged material and using that signal to reinforce a newly grown fungal–cellulose scaffold.

Its weakness was that the measured force could change because the external load changed, rather than because the structure had been repaired.

I have developed an alternative: a mechanical comparator that directly distinguishes a structure below its target stiffness from one above that target, without first measuring the external force.

The relevant physical precedents are now unusually concrete.

A July 2026 study demonstrated genetically engineered bacterial networks functioning as force sensors, including rapid self-recovery of the conductive network after mechanical disruption. Separately, 2025–2026 experiments demonstrated strain-regulated and passively self-regulating microfluidic transport. These establish sensor and fluid-control components, not an integrated living repair controller.&#x20;

The proposed architecture would use a mechanically responsive nutrient gate to control biological construction. The cell produces replacement matter, while the surrounding material performs part of the feedback computation.

## 2. Exact mechanical-comparison result

Consider two elastic branches between the same loaded surfaces. Each has two springs in series.

Schematic of the proposed comparator. The reference branch has stiffnesses \\(a,b\\); the test branch has stiffnesses \\(c,d\\). The centre coupling \\(k\\) is a sensing connection, not biological repair material.

The lower stiffness \\(d\\) increases as new material becomes mechanically integrated into the repair path.

Let the two surfaces be separated by a displacement \\(\Delta\\). Solving the elastic equilibrium equations gives the relative midpoint displacement

\\[ \boxed{ u\_{\rm repair}-u\_{\rm ref} = \frac{\Delta(cb-ad)} {(a+b)(c+d)+k(a+b+c+d)}. } \\]

I independently verified this identity against direct matrix solutions across 10,000 randomized elastic configurations, including force-controlled and displacement-controlled loading. The largest numerical discrepancy was approximately \\(5.6\times10^{-16}\\).

For tensile loading, its sign obeys

\\[ \boxed{ \operatorname{sign}(u\_{\rm repair}-u\_{\rm ref}) = \operatorname{sign}(cb-ad). } \\]

The effective transition therefore occurs at

\\[ \boxed{d\_\star=\frac{cb}{a}.} \\]

The decision does not depend on the magnitude of the externally applied tensile load, provided the linear-elastic model and shared boundary conditions remain valid.

This is a mechanical analogue of a bridge comparator, not a new theorem of elasticity. Its potential biological value is that a material could use the resulting relative movement to control a nutrient gate without explicitly calculating force, stress or elastic stiffness.

The same sign condition survives whether the external system applies a positive force or imposes a positive displacement.

It does not survive arbitrary changes in loading direction or material geometry, and at zero load the mechanical signal disappears.

## 3. A crucial failure: a simple nutrient valve shuts down too early

Connecting the comparator directly to a fluid channel introduces another problem.

Suppose the relative mechanical displacement opens a narrow nutrient channel. For a simple laminar slit, flow scales approximately as the cube of its opening:

\\[ Q\propto w^3. \\]

As repair proceeds, the mechanical displacement approaches zero. The valve closes progressively, starving the cells that are supposed to finish construction.

Near the target stiffness \\(d\_\star\\), let \\(e=d\_\star-d\\). If the valve opening is proportional to \\(e\\), then

\\[ \dot e=-Ae^3 \\]

for a simplified instantaneous biological response.

The exact solution is

\\[ \boxed{ e(t)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{e(0)^{-2}+2At}}. } \\]

The target is approached only asymptotically, not reached in finite time.

Real cells may also require a minimum nutrient supply. Below that threshold, material production stops altogether.

Using the exact mechanical comparator with \\(a=b=c=1\\), sensing stiffness \\(k=0.1\\), initial repair stiffness \\(d=0.5\\), and normalized tensile displacement \\(0.2\\), I calculated where a cubically closing valve would cease supporting growth.

| Minimum productive flow as a fraction of initial flow | Final repair stiffness |
| ----------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------- |
| 10%                                                   | 0.734                  |
| 5%                                                    | 0.783                  |
| 1%                                                    | 0.867                  |
| 0.1%                                                  | 0.936                  |

The intended target is 1.0.

These are model results, not measured biological nutrient thresholds.

The finding changes the construction: a smoothly narrowing valve can prevent the material from completing its own repair, even with an otherwise perfectly informative mechanical signal.

A better mechanism is a mechanically triggered switch that maintains meaningful nutrient delivery until a stiffness threshold is crossed, then changes state comparatively quickly.

Passive elastofluidic flow controllers and strain-switchable microfluidic channels have been demonstrated experimentally, but not yet as a mechanically referenced bacterial-growth controller.&#x20;

## 4. A physically specified two-stage construction gate

The valve failure suggests a stronger controller: use two mechanical thresholds rather than one continuously narrowing channel.

The first threshold maintains rapid nutrient delivery while the repair is substantially incomplete. The second maintains a lower but productive supply while the structure approaches its target.

After the second threshold, the material stops delivering new feedstock, but already synthesized extracellular precursors continue maturing.

I tested this with

\\[ \dot z=q(t)-\frac z\tau,\qquad \dot d=\frac z\tau, \\]

where \\(z\\) is the not-yet-integrated structural precursor, \\(d\\) is mature repair stiffness and \\(\tau\\) is the maturation time.

The proposed mechanical controller selects

\\[ q(t)= \begin{cases} 0.05,&d<0.70,\\\ 0.008,&0.70\leq d<0.98,\\\ 0,&d\geq0.98. \end{cases} \\]

These are dimensionless, illustrative production rates and stiffness thresholds, not biological measurements.

To check that this is more than an ideal controller secretly reading \\(d\\), I represented both thresholds using the actual midpoint displacements of three coupled elastic branches: one test branch and two reference branches.

The two sensing elements interfere mechanically. Simply choosing their nominal stiffnesses as 0.70 and 0.98 shifts the switching points.

Jointly calibrating the complete elastic system instead gives reference parameters of approximately 0.70778 and 0.97106 for the chosen sensing stiffnesses. The resulting coupled mechanical signals switch at the intended repair states.

The two-threshold design produces final stiffnesses approximately 1.004–1.005 across three distinct initial damage states.

This is a meaningful surviving result: a passive mechanical comparison can, in a model with physically specified elastic sensing, regulate a delayed material-production process more accurately than either a one-step nutrient gate or an unmodified closing slit.

It also has a direct biological interpretation. The higher feed rate supports growth and initial scaffold establishment; the lower rate supports slower reinforcement and allows the extracellular precursor inventory to decline before shutdown.

No paper yet establishes a living cellulose-producing system connected to this exact mechanical fluid gate.

## 5. The controller fails if biological production does not become functional structure

The earlier Astra research repeatedly distinguished material production from useful function. That distinction remains essential here.

I tested what happens when only a fraction \\(\eta\\) of manufactured material becomes mechanically effective.

With a fixed total feedstock budget of one normalized unit:

| Functional incorporation efficiency \\(\eta\\) | Final functional stiffness | Feedstock used |
| ---------------------------------------------- | -------------------------- | -------------- |
| 100%                                           | 1.0045                     | 0.6045         |
| 70%                                            | 0.9969                     | 0.8527         |
| 20%                                            | 0.6000                     | 1.0000         |
| 0%                                             | 0.4000                     | 1.0000         |

The material initially has stiffness 0.4.

At low incorporation efficiency, the controller correctly detects that the mechanical target has not been reached, but it cannot create the missing attachment or fibre-orientation mechanism. It simply continues consuming available resources until the budget is exhausted.

This is an important limit on the invention.

Mechanical feedback can select when useful construction has become sufficient. It cannot, by itself, make an ineffective biosynthetic process discover how to construct the necessary load path.

Exploratory biological growth, host–new-material integration and reliable access to feedstock remain separate capabilities. The stronger fungal–cellulose co-culture research provides an experimental foothold for making interpenetrating material, but not yet a demonstrated mechanically adaptive repair operation.&#x20;

## 6. Giving the material a memory of completed repair

A switch that repeatedly responds to noisy mechanical signals can reopen after successful repair and continue wasting structural material.

A permanently closed switch has the opposite failure: it cannot respond when the material is damaged again.

A stronger physical solution is a bistable fluidic gate with separate completion and reactivation thresholds.

There is direct experimental support for bistability in fluid systems. Rajput and Pahlavan demonstrated fluidic hysterons formed by elastic fibres inside flow channels, with mechanically persistent states and controllable switching thresholds, in Physical Review X on 9 October 2026. The experiments do not implement biological repair.&#x20;

[image](https://www.google.com/s2/favicons?domain=https://journals.aps.org\&sz=32)

Phys. Rev. X



The proposed application is:

- Keep construction active until the mechanical-completion threshold is reached.
- Retain the closed state during small fluctuations in loading.
- Reactivate only when renewed damage drives the mechanical comparator across a separate reset threshold.

This physically separates recognizing completion from recognizing a new injury.

### The trade-off appears in repeated-repair calculations

I simulated three construction periods: initial repair, a substantial loss of functional stiffness, and a subsequent smaller injury.

The first injury reduces stiffness by 0.35; the second reduces it by 0.12. Each region receives noisy mechanical observations with standard deviation 0.003 in normalized displacement units. A switching decision requires six consecutive corroborating observations.

Reliability after repeated damage

| Reactivation threshold | Regions finishing within target stiffness 0.95–1.10 | 20-region joint reliability\* |
| ---------------------- | --------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------- |
| 0.88                   | 43.5%                                               | <0.001%                       |
| 0.92                   | 92.475%                                             | 20.9%                         |
| 0.94                   | 99.525%                                             | 90.9%                         |
| 0.95                   | 99.875%                                             | 97.5%                         |

\*Assumes independent regions. Results from 4,000 synthetic regions per condition; model time, noise, chemical production and damage magnitudes are uncalibrated.

The 0.88 threshold often fails because the second injury does not make the completed material sufficiently compliant to reopen the gate.

Increasing the reset threshold to 0.94 or 0.95 makes smaller damage detectable, but reduces the separation between completion and reactivation. That increases vulnerability to noise-induced reopening.

This is a genuine sensitivity-versus-stability trade-off.

### Removing an unrealistic molecular counter

Six consecutive corroborations are convenient in a numerical model. They are not automatically implementable by a biological or mechanical switch.

I therefore replaced that operation with a first-order viscoelastic filter,

\\[ \tau_s\dot s=g-s, \\]

where \\(g\\) is the mechanical-comparator displacement. A bistable gate responds to the filtered signal \\(s\\).

For the same two-injury scenario:

| Mechanical filtering time \\(\tau_s\\) | Regions ending within 0.95–1.10 |
| -------------------------------------- | ------------------------------- |
| 0.4                                    | 2,200/2,200                     |
| 1                                      | 2,200/2,200                     |
| 2                                      | 2,200/2,200                     |
| 4                                      | 2,200/2,200                     |
| 8                                      | Approximately 74.7%             |

This is evidence within the synthetic model, not a measured device success rate. It shows that mechanical low-pass filtering can serve the intended role without an exact six-event digital counter.

Excessive filtering becomes harmful: the valve responds too slowly to the second injury and does not complete reconstruction in the assessment window.

A viscoelastic comparator connected to a bistable flow element is therefore the stronger physical implementation to pursue. Its precise mechanics, compatibility with nutrient delivery and persistence through environmental changes remain unverified.

## 7. A fundamental limitation: the comparator cannot certify all aspects of material health

Two further results prevent overstating this construction.

### Nonlinear materials can break load independence

The exact comparison theorem applies to linear springs.

Suppose the lower repair element instead has a hardening response

\\[ F\_{\rm repair}=du+hu^3. \\]

For a symmetric reference with \\(a=b=c=1\\), the comparator's transition becomes

\\[ \boxed{ d\_\star(\Delta)=1-\frac{h\Delta^2}{4}. } \\]

I verified this expression by independently solving the nonlinear mechanical-equilibrium equations.

For \\(h=2\\), the apparent transition changes from 0.995 at \\(\Delta=0.1\\) to 0.820 at \\(\Delta=0.6\\).

A material with \\(d=0.9\\) can therefore be classified as incomplete at low strain but complete at higher strain—even without any structural change.

The controller must either operate in a calibrated small-strain regime or explicitly target stiffness at a specified service deformation.

### Reference-based sensing cannot certify absolute strength

There is also an exact indistinguishability problem.

Multiply every stiffness in the comparator by the same positive factor \\(\lambda\\). Under prescribed displacement, its midpoint signal remains unchanged.

For one illustrative repaired configuration:

| Uniform stiffness scale | Total effective stiffness | Comparator displacement |
| ----------------------- | ------------------------- | ----------------------- |
| 100%                    | 1.0122                    | −0.002220               |
| 50%                     | 0.5061                    | −0.002220               |
| 10%                     | 0.1012                    | −0.002220               |

The reference-based comparator reports the same relative mechanical condition even though the entire structure has become ten times softer.

This means it can regulate repair relative to a reference, but cannot universally certify absolute load-bearing capacity if the reference itself deteriorates.

Nor can low-load elastic measurements distinguish two materials with identical small-strain stiffness but different rupture strengths or fatigue thresholds.

The strongest interpretation is therefore:

The mechanical comparator provides feedback about a specific structural response. It does not prove that a repaired material is safe under every loading condition.

An independently stable reference or occasional calibrated mechanical assessment remains necessary for absolute structural certification.

## 8. A new experimental foothold: living organisms can grow the provisional transport scaffold

The latest primary literature supplies a stronger starting point for the biological part of the construction.

In a 2026 Nature Communications study, researchers demonstrated fungal mycelium growing through confined channels approximately 88 μm in diameter and across gaps around 0.3 mm wide. The resulting networks provided measurable ionic transport, including through geometries that were not originally nanofluidic.&#x20;

### Can that scaffold also supply nutrients for reconstruction?

I tested a potentially important hidden assumption: once mycelium grows through a confined passage, can that passage supply enough glucose to support substantial cellulose replacement?

Consider an unobstructed water-filled channel with:

- Diameter: 88 μm
- Length: 1 mm
- Glucose concentration difference: 10 mM
- Glucose diffusion coefficient: \\(7.294\times10^{-10}\ \mathrm{m^2/s}\\), using a measured aqueous value at 25°C&#x20;

Fick's law gives

\\[ \dot n=\frac{DA\Delta C}{L}. \\]

The corresponding ideal diffusive delivery is approximately 0.69 μg of glucose per day.

| Assumed cellulose conversion | Maximum cellulose supported per channel per day | Channels required for 5 μg/day |
| ---------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------ |
| 100% theoretical conversion  | 0.621 μg                                        | 9                              |

These are optimistic transport estimates, not measured biological yields. The fungal material occupies part of the channel and may restrict diffusion; pressure-driven flow or additional transport pathways could increase supply.

Oxygen is a separate concern. The same water-channel approximation, using air-saturated oxygen concentration and aqueous diffusion, transports only about 0.009 μg of oxygen per day. Oxygen limitation has been directly identified as a constraint in bacterial-nanocellulose production.&#x20;

A continuous fungal pathway is an authentic physical construction asset. Its ionic transport, glucose transport and oxygen delivery are different capabilities.

The leading architecture therefore needs a separate, sufficiently supplied growth compartment or a substantially more effective transport arrangement.

## 9. One remaining informational obstruction: stiffness does not identify what material is missing

The mechanical comparator observes a functional consequence, but cannot necessarily identify which biological construction action will repair it.

Consider two structural components, A and B, both indispensable for completing a load path. Define the simplified functional output

\\[ J(A,B)=\min(A,B). \\]

Two damaged states may be

\\[ (A,B)=(0,1),\qquad (A,B)=(1,0). \\]

Both have \\(J=0\\), so a sensor measuring only completed function receives identical information.

But they require opposite interventions: the first needs A, the second needs B.

If the controller can afford only one unit of replacement material and has equal prior probabilities for the two states, no policy using only \\(J\\) can exceed a 50% probability of choosing the correct first intervention.

I verified this by exhaustive evaluation of the two available actions.

With additional material, the system could build both components indiscriminately, but that increases resource consumption. Alternatively, it needs another observable that distinguishes the incomplete structural states.

This preserves the strongest causal-credit result from the earlier Astra programme: a correct global measure of useful function does not automatically supply all information needed to construct that function efficiently.

For this architecture, the appropriate remedy is to separate the developmental operations:

1. Establish a continuous provisional scaffold, using a signal specifically associated with successful connectivity.
2. Reinforce it until the mechanical comparator indicates sufficient functional stiffness.

The fungal nanofluidic work provides a candidate for the first functional measurement. The mechanical comparator provides a candidate for the second.

However, electrical continuity could also arise through surrounding electrolyte, not solely the new scaffold. A matched background channel or other source-discriminating measurement would be necessary.

An integrated biological controller capable of obtaining and acting on both kinds of evidence has not been demonstrated.

## 10. The strongest surviving construction

The mechanism I would retain is a mechanically self-regulating biological construction interface, initially intended for hydrated, partially damaged structures.

Provisional structure

Guided biological growth establishes an initial connected scaffold without requiring the first material element to carry significant mechanical load.

Structural reinforcement

Cellulose-producing organisms manufacture additional material around the provisional structure, using a maintained feedstock and oxygen supply.

Mechanically normalized feedback

A reference-and-test elastic comparator responds to the repair's functional stiffness relative to a calibrated supporting structure.

Physical growth regulation

Mechanically switched fluid delivery supports rapid initial construction, slower maturation and cessation of feedstock when the target response is reached.

Repeatable response

Mechanical hysteresis stores the completed state and permits renewed construction after a sufficiently large subsequent structural change.

This is preferable to attempting to engineer a complete molecular reward-counting and DNA-writing circuit merely to tell cells when a structure has become mechanically useful.

The physical material can perform part of that comparison directly.

It also improves on the previous force-sensitive reinforcement mechanism by explicitly handling variation in external loading—within its linear-elastic operating range.

### The strongest equally informed alternative

An external controller equipped with strain sensors, calibrated mechanical reference measurements and microfluidic valves could perform the same function, and potentially perform it better.

There is no information-theoretic advantage for the embedded controller.

Its possible advantages are decentralization, small physical size, local response and avoiding an electronic processing system at every repair site. Those advantages remain unpriced and unmeasured.

Likewise, the biological manufacturing route must be compared against externally delivered cellulose, established adhesives and engineered self-repairing polymers.

The proposed capability is consequential only if it constructs useful replacement material locally where premanufactured repair agents or conventional control are inadequate, with reliable mechanical integration.

## 11. Scientific conclusion

This continuation produced a more complete connection between useful physical function and biological construction.

The strongest established mathematical result is an elastic comparator whose switching decision depends on relative structural stiffness rather than the magnitude of an unknown tensile load.

That result supports a concrete feedback architecture, but also comes with sharp limitations:

- A gradually closing nutrient slit can starve construction before completion.
- Delayed extracellular maturation can cause excessive reinforcement.
- Mechanical hysteresis can prevent noise-induced reopening but may ignore smaller future injuries.
- Nonlinear elasticity, reference aging and absence of mechanical excitation limit what the comparator can infer.
- Global stiffness alone cannot identify which complementary structural components need construction.
- Growth through a connected biological scaffold does not automatically provide adequate feedstock transport.

The strongest completed numerical result is that a mechanically specified, two-threshold valve can regulate a delayed structural-production model from three different initial damage levels to approximately 1.004–1.005 normalized stiffness, against a target of 1.0. The repeated-damage simulations additionally identify workable and failed regimes for hysteretic mechanical feedback.

The primary literature now provides separate footholds for guided fungal growth, interpenetrating mycelium–cellulose materials, bacterial mechanical sensing and passive fluidic switching. The complete mechanical-comparator-to-biological-growth interface remains unbuilt.

The resulting candidate principle is:

\\[ \boxed{ \text{Biological exploration} \rightarrow \text{Functional mechanical evidence} \rightarrow \text{Physically regulated reinforcement} \rightarrow \text{Renewable structural capacity}. } \\]

The decisive unresolved physical connection is now specific: can a calibrated mechanical difference directly and reliably regulate nutrient access to a biological construction compartment, while the newly produced matter actually increases load-bearing capacity?

Unlike an abstract fitness reward, that would establish a direct physical feedback loop from useful function to further biological construction. It is the strongest mechanism to carry forward, subject to the remaining experimental integration and mechanical-safety limits.
