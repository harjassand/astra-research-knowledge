1. EXACT FOUR-VALUE COVARIANCE ACTION

Let g=grad F:R^d->R^d be globally L-Lipschitz, F C^{1,1}; convexity is
unnecessary. Let mu=E g(G), C=Cov(g(G)), solely analysis quantities.
The dimension, a certified upper bound L, target sigma>0, and accuracy
epsilon are supplied problem parameters. L is not fitted from finitely many
values. A known L=0 is handled by one g(0) query and independent sigma noise.

The existing S4 action J_s(c;v), for s>0, is:
 if v=0 return 0;
 u=v/||v||; Zperp=Z0-(u dot Z0)u;
 R=s sqrt(A1^2+A2^2+A3^2);
 J_s(c;v)=||v||[g(c+s Zperp+Ru)-g(c+s Zperp-Ru)]/(2R).
Z0 and the three A_i are fresh independent standard Gaussians. The null R=0
event receives a fixed convention. Two original g values suffice. Writing
H_s(c)=E Dg(c+sG), S4 proves
 E[J_s(c;v)|c,v]=H_s(c)v,   ||J_s(c;v)||<=L||v||.       (1)
For fresh Gaussian V, the action is convex-order dominated by LV. The sample
action need not be linear or a realized symmetric matrix.

Draw T uniformly on (0,1), W~N(0,I_d), set c=sqrt(T)W, s=sqrt(1-T), and use
two fresh actions at the SAME c,s:
 Q=J_s(c;v),       A_g(v)=J_s(c;Q).                    (2)
The outer seeds are independent of the inner seeds. Generate T from three
Gaussian scalars as (1+C1/sqrt(C1^2+C2^2+C3^2))/2. Endpoint/null events have
fixed conventions. No extra random primitive or unbounded loop is needed.

THEOREM 1. Formula (2) uses at most FOUR original g queries, O(d) surrounding
real arithmetic and storage on every execution, and
 E[A_g(v)|v]=Cv,       ||A_g(v)||<=L^2||v||.            (3)
For fresh V~N(0,I_d),
 A_g(V) <=_cx N(0,L^4 I_d),
 E||A_g(V)-CV||^2 <= L^4 d-Tr(C^2) <= L^4 d.          (4)

PROOF. For Brownian B, M_t=E[g(B_1)|B_t] has differential
 dM_t=H_(sqrt(1-t))(B_t)dB_t.
Heat smoothing and Ito isometry, followed by Lipschitz approximation, give
 C=int_0^1 E[H_(sqrt(1-t))(B_t)H_(sqrt(1-t))(B_t)^T]dt.
Conservativity makes H symmetric, so the integrand is E H^2. Fresh-seed
conditional unbiasedness in (2) gives (3). Conditional on T,W, successive
fixed-center convex-order bounds give (4)'s first claim. No center depends
on the action input. Finally
 E||A_g(V)-CV||^2=E||A_g(V)||^2-Tr(C^2)
by conditional unbiasedness. QED.

For M independent actions conditional on the SAME V, put
 Abar_M(V)=M^-1 sum_(l=1)^M A_g^(l)(V).
Then
 E||Abar_M(V)-CV||^2 <= L^4 d/M.                      (5)
The cap is at most 4M original queries on EVERY execution.

2. CONDITIONAL GAUSSIAN VALUE CIRCUITS

Fix an available parameter x, possibly depending on all prior history, and
the known circuit
 H_x(W)=sum_(i=1)^q c_i g(b_i(x)+sum_(l=1)^r beta_il W_l), (6)
with W_l iid standard d-Gaussians, explicit coefficients and centers. A known
affine field can be subtracted at nodes without extra original queries.
Write B_i=beta_i^T tensor I_d, S=sum_i |c_i|||beta_i||, Bword=L S.
Zero beta_i nodes are deterministic and can be omitted from covariance.

The conditional Clark matrix is
 M_t=sum_i c_i H_(sqrt(1-t)||beta_i||)
                  (b_i+B_i sqrt(t)W) B_i.             (7)
Both M_t and M_t^T have two-query actions. Choose i with probability
 p_i=|c_i|||beta_i||/S. On input v the forward action is
 (c_i/p_i) J_si(center_i;B_i v);
on input z the transpose action is
 (c_i/p_i) B_i^T J_si(center_i;z).
Their norm bounds are Bword times the input norm. Use a fresh transpose
action followed by a fresh forward action at the SAME T,W. The resulting
four-query action has mean Cov(H_x(W))z and sends a fresh Gaussian z to a
vector convex-order dominated by N(0,Bword^4 I_d).

The selected indices can differ. The scalar-block covariance structure in
(6) is a stated contract. Arbitrary anisotropic B_iW is not automatically
covered by the isotropic S4 action. The actual deterministic replica circuit
has precisely the scalar-block form.

Costs are O(rd+q) surrounding arithmetic in the direct implementation and
O(rd+q) extra working storage beyond the O(qd+qr) explicit circuit data,
plus four original queries per covariance action. A circuit
value costs q original queries and O(qrd+qd) direct arithmetic after its
centers and coefficients are available. Computing centers b_i(x) is an
explicit part of the enclosing program and its cost must also be counted;
no opaque center-evaluation oracle is supplied. All coefficients are
supplied by the explicit circuit, not fitted. If a sharper Gaussian-source
Lipschitz bound B0<=Bword is PROVED from that circuit, B0 controls Poincare
and ideal law error; Bword controls action randomization. Without a sharper
certificate, use B0=Bword. All claims are conditional on the available x.

3. CANCELING THIRD MOMENTS WITHOUT KNOWING THE MEAN

For an integer n>=2 set
 a_n=1/(n-n^(1/3)), b_n=-n^(1/3)/(n-n^(1/3)),
 kappa_n=(n+n^(2/3))/(n-n^(1/3))^2.                   (8)
From n+1 independent original values form
 U=a_n sum_(i=1)^n g(G_i)+b_n g(G_(n+1)).             (9)
Since n a_n+b_n=1 and n a_n^3+b_n^3=0, V=U-mu satisfies
 E V=0, Cov(V)=K=kappa_n C, E V^tensor3=0.            (10)
As a function of all Gaussian inputs U is B-Lipschitz, B=L sqrt(kappa_n).
Its law has Poincare constant <=B^2. For n>=8, kappa_n<=8/(3n): writing
x=n^(-1/3)<=1/2 gives n kappa_n=(1+x)/(1-x^2)^2<=8/3.

Signed coefficients are ordinary vector arithmetic, not signed probability.
No mean or third moment is measured. These identities also hold for
independent conditional replicas of (6).

IMPLEMENTED WEIGHTS. Choose n=m^3 with an integer m>=2. Then a_n=1/(m^3-m),
b_n=-1/(m^2-1), and kappa_n=1/[(m-1)^2(m+1)] are exactly rational. No cube-root
primitive or approximate moment cancellation is needed. In particular m=2
uses eight weights 1/6 and one weight -1/3, with kappa=1/3.

4. ANALYTIC LEMMA: COVARIANCE COMPLETION AT FOURTH ORDER

Let V be a centered B-Lipschitz Gaussian image in R^d, covariance K, and
E V^tensor3=0. K can be singular and the law need not have a density.
If r^2=S^2-||K||op>0, then
 TV(Law(V+N(0,S^2 I-K)),N(0,S^2 I))
       <=sqrt(24) B^4 sqrt(d)/(8r^4).                 (11)
The added Gaussian is independent. Its covariance is used for analysis only.

PROOF. Write V=H(W)-EH, ||DH||op<=B. The Gaussian integration-by-parts
coefficient is tau=E[K_H DH^T|V], where K_H=int_0^1 P_t DH dt and
delta K_H=H-EH. Each factor has norm <=B. For scalar smooth f,
 R(f):=E[V dot grad f-K:Hess f]=E[T1:Hess f],
 T1=Sym(tau)-K, E T1=0, ||T1||_(L2,HS)<=B^2 sqrt(d). (12)
The last bound follows by centering Sym(tau), whose operator norm is <=B^2.

Poincare applied entrywise to Hess f-E Hess f bounds this functional by
B||T1||||D^3 f||_L2. Riesz representation on the Hilbert closure of symmetric
D^3 f gives a symmetric third-order tensor T2 with norm <=B||T1||. Testing
cubic polynomials, with cutoffs justified by Gaussian-image moments, gives
E T2=0 because EV=0 and EV^tensor3=0. Apply Poincare again to D^3 f and
Riesz on the closure of D^4 f. We obtain a symmetric fourth tensor T3 with
 R(f)=E[T3:D^4 f],  ||T3||_(L2,HS)<=B^4 sqrt(d).      (13)
No K inverse or runtime kernel evaluation is involved.

Interpolate Y_t=sqrt(t)V+Z_t, where Z_t is independent Gaussian with
Q_t=S^2 I-tK, 0<=t<=1. Gaussian differentiation and (13) give
 d/dt E f(Y_t)=(t/2)E[T3:D^4 f(Y_t)].                (14)
Gaussian Hermite orthogonality implies, for any symmetric fourth tensor A,
 int |A:D^4 phi_Q| <=sqrt(24)||A||HS/r^4 when Q>=r^2 I.
Integrating (14) gives sqrt(24)||T3||/(4r^4) for |f|<=1; TV contributes
another factor 1/2. This proves (11). Smooth approximation extends it to
measurable TV tests. Moment matching alone would not prove the conclusion.

For uniform conditional B, prove this separately at every fixed x and
integrate the resulting bound. No measurable-selection assertion for the
analysis-only Riesz witnesses is needed. QED.

5. ACTUAL POSITIVE SAMPLER AND FULL-LAW THEOREM

Choose tau,nu>0 with tau^2+nu^2=sigma^2. Generate U from (9), a fresh standard
Gaussian Z, M independent actions conditional on Z, and fresh Gaussian W.
Return
 O=U+tau Z-(kappa_n/(2tau))Abar_M(Z)+nu W.             (15)
This uses n+1+4M original values on every execution, and has exact mean mu.

Assume B^2=kappa_n L^2<sigma^2 and set r^2=sigma^2-B^2. Then
 TV(Law(O),N(mu,sigma^2 I))
 <=kappa_n^2 L^4 {
   sqrt(d)[sqrt(24)/(8r^4)+1/(8sqrt(2)tau^2 r^2)]
   +phi(1)d/(4tau^2 nu^2 M)},                         (16)
where phi(1)=exp(-1/2)/sqrt(2pi).

PROOF. Replace Abar_M(Z) by CZ solely in an analysis comparison. Its
conditional error has mean zero given U,Z. Taylor-expand only the independent
nu-reserve density. The first term vanishes, and the full-law remainder is
 TV(actual,linear-mean-action comparison)
 <=phi(1)kappa_n^2 E||Abar_M(Z)-CZ||^2/(4tau^2 nu^2)
 <=phi(1)kappa_n^2 L^4 d/(4tau^2 nu^2 M).             (17)
The actual randomized action variance is charged here.

The comparison noise covariance is
 Q1=(tau I-K/(2tau))^2+nu^2 I
   =sigma^2 I-K+K^2/(4tau^2).
The ideal covariance-completed noise has Q0=sigma^2 I-K>=r^2 I.
For Delta=K^2/(4tau^2)>=0, Gaussian KL and Pinsker give
 TV(N(0,Q0+Delta),N(0,Q0))
 <=||Q0^(-1/2)Delta Q0^(-1/2)||F/(2sqrt(2))
 <=B^4 sqrt(d)/(8sqrt(2)tau^2 r^2).                  (18)
Adding the same independent V cannot increase TV. Apply (11) to the ideal
law and add (17),(18). QED.

For tau^2=nu^2=sigma^2/2 and kappa_n L^2<=sigma^2/2,
 TV <=kappa_n^2 (L/sigma)^4
       [(sqrt(6)+1/(2sqrt(2)))sqrt(d)+phi(1)d/M].     (19)
At fixed small incoming scale (L/sigma)^2 sqrt(d), its d/M term is the
square of that scale for M=1. The sqrt(d) terms are smaller still.

6. EVERY-EXECUTION GENERAL QUERY COMPLEXITY

Let rho=L/sigma. Define
 A=(128/9)phi(1)rho^4 d/epsilon,
 D=(128/9)(sqrt(6)+1/(2sqrt(2)))rho^4 sqrt(d)/epsilon,
 n0=max(8,(16/3)rho^2,sqrt(D),(8A)^(1/3)),
 n=m^3, where m>=2 is the least integer with m^3>=n0,
 M=max(1,ceil(A/n^2)).                               (20)
Then each term in (19) is <=epsilon/2, and the deterministic total cap is
 Q=n+1+4M
 =O(1+rho^2+rho^(4/3)(d/epsilon)^(1/3)
             +rho^2 d^(1/4)/sqrt(epsilon)).          (21)

The integer m can be found by doubling and binary search, testing the three
inequalities m^3>=(16/3)rho^2, m^6>=D, m^9>=8A. This uses O(log(n+1)) scalar
arithmetic/comparisons, no cube root. Choosing n this way changes (21) by at
most a fixed factor. Source weights are exactly rational.

Stream all source sums and action sums. Surrounding arithmetic is
O(d(n+M)+log(n+1)), storage O(d), plus reading original whole-vector answers.
Gaussian seeds, real arithmetic and square roots are the explicit primitives.
At most d(n+3+3M)+9M independent scalar standard Gaussians suffice for the
literal implementation, including source replicas, time generation, both
secant actions, and both final Gaussian terms.
A finite-bit theorem still needs primitive-error budgets and a commonly
rounded target; exact real outputs and Gaussian draws are the supplied model.

This is NOT the polylog target. The fixed-accuracy dimension exponent is

8. EXACT NEXT SCIENTIFIC OBLIGATION (NOT A PROVED CONTRACTION)

A precise candidate law-level contract avoids the failed source Sobolev
norm. For an actual represented output X of mean mu and a declared Gaussian
variance a^2, a weak Stein witness D satisfies, for all scalar smooth tests,

 E[(X-mu) dot grad f(X)-a^2 Delta f(X)]
       =E[D:Hess f(X)].                                 (22)

D may live on the program's latent probability space. Define the normalized
defect as an available proved upper bound

 e >= ||D||_(L2,HS)/(a^2 sqrt(d)).                        (23)

The witness is an ANALYSIS object; its existence does not supply a runtime
oracle. Given (22), an independent final reserve nu G yields the full-law
bound

 TV(Law(X+nu G),N(mu,(a^2+nu^2)I))
       <=||D||_(L2,HS)/(2sqrt(2)nu^2).                   (24)

Proof: interpolate sqrt(t)(X-mu)+N(0,(a^2+nu^2-ta^2)I).
Its test derivative is (1/2)E[D:Hess f]. Contract the second Gaussian
Hermite tensor in L1, obtaining sqrt(2)||D||/nu^2, integrate in t, and
divide by two for TV. The same conditional statement can be integrated
over an available retained reference. Thus fixed relative reserve turns
a dimension-normalized defect into O(sqrt(d)e) full-law error.

An original field averaged a CONSTANT number of times at fixed L/sigma,
plus a Gaussian baseline, has e bounded by an arbitrarily small constant:
the original Gaussian-source Stein witness has operator bound L^2/n.
This initialization is acquired and its averaging cost is paid.

What is still missing is a POSITIVE value-only map on an EXPLICIT represented
class that (i) contains that initialization, (ii) returns another member of
the same class, and (iii) improves its proved defect to C e^2, with constants
uniform in dimension and source. Every conditional operation used by that
map must be implemented from original g values. Neither the weak Riesz
witness nor a current-law posterior may be assumed available.

With uniform quadratic improvement, depth O(log log(d/epsilon)) would
suffice in (24). For this to imply the target query theorem, source-query
growth must be at most a fixed factor per depth, or must satisfy an actually
proved subcritical-forest bound of the supplied S3 kind. A generic polylog
factor MULTIPLIED at every depth can become quasipolylogarithmic and is not
enough. Gaussian reserves must fit within the fixed final sigma^2 budget;
conditional fitting, coefficient precision, smoothing and scalar arithmetic
must retain complete deterministic caps.

The fourth-order sampler in this file does NOT establish that self-map.
The shared-time counterexample rejects one tempting uniform contraction,
and the source Sobolev obstructions reject two regularity-based repairs.
The retained-input/anchor law kernel and mean-square input stability are
positive operations available to a different represented class; they do
not yet acquire its full recursive operations. Constructing that class and
its positive update, or obtaining a comparably decisive obstruction to a
specific alternative, is the exact remaining bridge.
