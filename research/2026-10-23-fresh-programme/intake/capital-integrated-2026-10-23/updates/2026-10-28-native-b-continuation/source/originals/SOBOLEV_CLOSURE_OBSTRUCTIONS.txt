SHARP OBSTRUCTIONS TO GAUSSIAN-SOURCE SOBOLEV CLOSURE OF THE HEAT ACTION
27 October 2026. Complements INTEGRATED_ACTION_REGULARITY.txt.

These are obstructions to specified norms of the sampled Gaussian source
representation. They are not Gaussian-mean oracle lower bounds and do not
prove failure of an intrinsic Poincare inequality for the resulting law.
The source functions satisfy the original conservative Lipschitz contract.

1. NATURAL NORMALIZATION

For a Gaussian-source map H with output dimension d, write
 E_G(H)=E||DH||_HS^2.
A globally B-Lipschitz map has E_G(H)<=B^2 d, regardless of its Gaussian
input dimension. Thus E_G(H)/d=O(B^2) is a natural weaker candidate for a
reusable source class. The action below violates that normalization by a
factor of order d, even though its output has covariance at most L^2I by
the existing S4 convex-order receipt.

2. EXACT HIGH-FREQUENCY SECOND-MOMENT LIMITS

Fix d>=2, c in R^d, and s>0. Let
 g_k(x)_i=sin(kx_i)/k, i=1,...,d.
Its potential is -sum_i cos(kx_i)/k^2 and its global Lipschitz constant is
one. It is smooth for every finite k. Use a fresh Gaussian input V, and the
notation r=||V||, u=V/r, P=I-u u^T, q~chi_3, z~N(0,I_d).

The action components are exactly

 J_i=r cos(k[c_i+s(Pz)_i]) sin(ksq u_i)/(ksq).                 (1)

Consequently

 partial_(c_i)J_i=-r sin(k[c_i+s(Pz)_i])sin(ksq u_i)/(sq),
 D_cJ is diagonal,
 D_zJ=-r diag(sin(k[c_i+s(Pz)_i])sin(ksq u_i))P/q.            (2)

For almost every u, every u_i is nonzero and 1-u_i^2>0. Conditional on u,
the Gaussian phase in (2) has positive variance, so its squared sine average
tends to one-half. The radial squared sine likewise averages to one-half
against q^(-2) times the chi_3 density. Since E q^(-2)=1, dominated
convergence applies. The length r is independent of u and E r^2=d.
Therefore

 lim_(k->infinity) E|partial_(c_i)J_i|^2 =d/(4s^2),
 lim_(k->infinity) E||D_cJ||_HS^2 =d^2/(4s^2),
 lim_(k->infinity) E||D_zJ||_HS^2 =d(d-1)/4.                 (3)

The last identity uses ||diag(a_i)P||_HS^2=sum_i a_i^2(1-u_i^2).
These limits also hold after averaging an independent Gaussian center.
Its W derivative gains the corresponding center scale squared, e.g. t
when c=sqrt(t)W. The constants in (3) are one-quarter, not one-half.

In particular the single action has no uniform O(d) Gaussian Dirichlet
energy bound. Even the expected squared operator norm of its Jacobian is
at least its expected squared Hilbert-Schmidt norm divided by d, so that
operator-moment variant also loses a factor of order d.

3. THE OBSTRUCTION SURVIVES THE TWO-ACTION COVARIANCE WORD

Fix positive lambda,eta with lambda+eta<=1, and use

 g_k(x)=lambda x+eta (sin(kx_i)/k)_i.                         (4)

The original Lipschitz constant remains at most one. The inner action is
Q=lambda V+eta J_sine. Its heat Hessian is

 H_s(c)=lambda I+eta exp(-k^2s^2/2)diag(cos(kc_i)),             (5)

which converges uniformly in c to lambda I. Let A=J_2(c;Q) with fresh outer
seeds. For the derivative in the INNER perpendicular seed z1, conditional
unbiasedness of the outer action and weak differentiation give

 E_outer[D_z1 A]=H_s(c)D_z1 Q.                              (6)

This interchange is justified by the finite L2 derivative bounds in the
companion file, and does not evaluate a derivative at runtime. Jensen and
(3)-(6) imply

 liminf_(k->infinity) E||D_z1 A||_HS^2
 >=lambda^2 eta^2 d(d-1)/4.                                 (7)

For example lambda=eta=1/2 gives d(d-1)/64. Thus the second action does not
repair the source-energy scaling. The affine part passes the inner derivative
through the outer mean operator, even though the high-frequency perturbation
of g has amplitude tending to zero.

For center derivatives the conditional mean derivative is
(D_c H_s)Q+H_s D_cQ. The first term has L2 norm bounded by a fixed moment
factor times eta k exp(-k^2s^2/2), hence tends to zero. Therefore

 liminf E||D_c A||_HS^2 >=lambda^2 eta^2 d^2/(4s^2).          (8)

4. POSITIVE AVERAGING DOES NOT CLOSE THIS NORMALIZED SOURCE CLASS CHEAPLY

Take K independent fixed-center/fixed-scale covariance words A_i(V), with
positive weights w_i and the same fresh Gaussian input V. Their inner
perpendicular seeds are distinct. For Abar=sum_i w_i A_i, differentiation
in those distinct blocks gives an exact sum of squared norms. Applying
(7) at each of the finitely many centers/scales yields

 liminf_(k->infinity) E||D_(z1,...,zK) Abar||_HS^2
 >=[lambda^2 eta^2 d(d-1)/4] sum_i w_i^2.                    (9)

The argument also holds for independent Gaussian centers and deterministic
positive time quadrature. Since sum_i w_i^2>=(sum_i w_i)^2/K, restoring a
uniform O(d) bound at the natural raw-action scale requires K=Omega(d)
when the total weight stays bounded away from zero.

This does not lower-bound queries for the full target. A small external
coefficient alpha multiplies both sides by alpha^2, and an unrelated Gaussian
baseline can dominate the total energy. The obstruction concerns preserving
the action's OWN normalized residual-source parameter, which is what a
superlinear source update would need. Another representation or a weaker
law-based norm may avoid it.

5. NO UNIFORM W^{1,p} RECEIPT FOR p>=3, EVEN FOR SMOOTH SCALAR SOURCES

Set d=1, fix t>0,s>0, c=sqrt(t)W with W Gaussian, and keep an independent
Gaussian input V. For g_k(x)=sin(kx)/k the scalar action is

 J=V cos(kc) sinc(ksq),
 partial_W J=-sqrt(t)V sin(kc)sin(ksq)/(sq).                  (10)

For each p>=1 the p-th derivative moment factors into the Gaussian input
moment, the Gaussian phase average, and the radial integral

 sqrt(2/pi) s^(-p)
   integral_0^infinity q^(2-p) exp(-q^2/2)|sin(ksq)|^p dq.    (11)

The phase average tends to the positive mean of |sin|^p over a period.
For p<3 the radial integrand is dominated by an integrable function near
zero. At p=3, (11) grows as a positive constant times s^(-3)log(ks).
For p>3, substitution x=ksq gives growth proportional to

 k^(p-3)s^(-3)
   integral_0^infinity x^(2-p)|sin x|^p dx,                  (12)

whose last integral is finite and strictly positive. Hence no uniform
W^{1,p} bound for p>=3 exists over these smooth, uniformly 1-Lipschitz
conservative sources. At p=2 the limiting moment in (10) is t/(4s^2),
consistent with the positive W^{1,2} bounds.

The scalar word for source (4) has the form
 A=V[lambda+eta cos(kc)sinc(ksq1)]
      [lambda+eta cos(kc)sinc(ksq2)].
Average its W derivative over the second radius. Since
E sinc(ksq2)=exp(-k^2s^2/2), the conditional derivative is lambda*eta times
the derivative in (10), plus terms whose Lp norms are O(k exp(-k^2s^2/2)).
Conditional Jensen therefore proves the same lack of uniform W^{1,p}
bounds for the two-action word whenever p>=3.

6. AN ACTUAL INFINITE FOURTH DERIVATIVE MOMENT IN DIMENSION ONE

The original contract permits a C^{1,1} potential with a Lipschitz gradient.
Choose lambda>eta>0, lambda+eta<=1, and

 g(x)=lambda x+eta|x|,
 F(x)=lambda x^2/2+eta x|x|/2.                               (13)

This F is C^{1,1}; g is globally Lipschitz and even monotone. Put R=sq and
B_R(c)=clip(c/R,-1,1). The exact scalar action is

 J=V[lambda+eta B_R(c)],
 partial_W J=eta sqrt(t)V R^(-1)1_(|c|<R).                  (14)

For small R, the Gaussian center satisfies P(|c|<R) asymptotic to a positive
constant times R. The chi_3 radius density is a positive constant times R^2
near zero. Thus the fourth moment in (14) contains

 integral_0 R^(-4) * R * R^2 dR = integral_0 dR/R,

and is INFINITE. Its second moment is finite. At the fixed deterministic
center c=0, the center derivative already has infinite p-th moment for p>=3.

The two-action word is
 A=V[lambda+eta B_R1(c)][lambda+eta B_R2(c)].
Its center derivative is
 eta V{[lambda+eta B_R2(c)]R1^(-1)1_(|c|<R1)
        +[lambda+eta B_R1(c)]R2^(-1)1_(|c|<R2)}.
The bracketed coefficients are at least lambda-eta>0, so the two terms
cannot cancel. Its Gaussian-center W derivative therefore also has infinite
fourth moment. The radial-seed derivative of the single action has the same
logarithmic fourth-moment divergence: on |c|<R its magnitude is
eta|V||c|/(s q^2), and integrating c^4 over |c|<sq supplies q^5 against
the q^2 radius density and q^(-8) denominator.

Smooth replacements |x| -> sqrt(x^2+epsilon^2) preserve the same uniform
Lipschitz bound and converge in the displayed derivative formulas almost
everywhere. Fatou's lemma therefore gives unbounded fourth derivative norms
even when every individual source is C-infinity.

7. CONSEQUENCE AND PRECISE REMAINING POSSIBILITY

Rare radii do not prevent W^{1,2} acquisition. The complete finite bounds
and the dimension-free input perturbation receipt are proved in the companion
file. But the full source derivative energy has a real d^2 scale, and higher
derivative moments are not uniformly available. Deterministic time nodes,
an endpoint cutoff, and a second fresh action do not remove these facts.

None of this identifies the best Poincare constant of the OUTPUT LAW. A
sampled representation can carry high-frequency derivative energy while
its law is close to a simple Gaussian. In particular, the dimension-free
Gaussian-reserve center-to-law stability bounds in the companion file remain
valid. A successful next construction needs either a closed law-kernel
interface of that kind, a different source representation, or an acquired
intrinsic-law inequality; replacing the existing global Lipschitz assumption
by this sampled map's uniformly normalized Sobolev norm is insufficient.
