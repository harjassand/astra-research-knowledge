SHARED RANDOM TIME CAUSES A REAL FULL-LAW GAP IN THE ACQUIRED SAMPLER
27 October 2026. Analytic counterexample for the actual four-value action.

This is a limitation of a specified correction with a fixed number of shared
random times. It is not an oracle lower bound or a hard Gaussian-mean family.
In particular, the diagnostic source has known zero mean. No numerical tests
are needed for the proof.

1. SOURCE AND EXACT GENERAL-PROGRAM WEIGHTS

For each integer m>=2 put
 n=m^3, q=(m-1)^2(m+1), d=q^2, kappa=1/q=1/sqrt(d).
Use the actual rational replica weights from the general-source construction:
a=1/(m^3-m), repeated n times, and b=-1/(m^2-1), once.
Their sum is one, their cubes sum to zero, and their squares sum to kappa.

Take g_d(x)=(sin x_1,...,sin x_d). It is globally 1-Lipschitz and conservative,
with potential -sum_i cos x_i. Its mean is zero and its covariance is c I,

 c=E sin^2(G)=(1-e^(-2))/2=e^(-1)sinh(1)<1/2.

Let U be the weighted sum of n+1 independent g-values. Its coordinates are
independent and identically distributed, centered, with covariance kappa c I.
As a Gaussian image U has Lipschitz constant sqrt(kappa), so each coordinate
and every linear projection has the corresponding sub-Gaussian proxy.
In particular its coordinate fourth and eighth moments are uniformly bounded
and tend to zero as kappa tends to zero.

The heat Hessian is diagonal, with entries
e^(-(1-t)/2) cos(sqrt(t)W_i). Consequently the expected covariance integrand
is F(t)=f(t)I, where

 f(t)=e^(-1)cosh(t),     integral_0^1 f(t)dt=c.                 (1)

Its time variance is the strictly positive constant

 v=Var_(T uniform[0,1]) f(T)
  =e^(-2)[1/2+sinh(2)/4-sinh^2(1)]>0.                         (2)

2. ACTUAL ACQUIRED ACTION, NOT AN F(T) ORACLE

Let Z be a fresh standard d-Gaussian. Draw one common uniform time T and
run the actual two-secant composition A_T(Z), with a common center for its
inner and outer actions and fresh internal seeds as required by the compiler.
Only its already-proved legal receipts will be used:

 E[A_T(Z)|Z,T]=f(T)Z,       ||A_T(Z)||<=||Z||.                 (3)

These are properties of the four-original-value action, not additional
derivative, conditional-mean, or covariance queries.
Let A=A_T(Z), W another independent standard Gaussian, and

 tau=nu=1/sqrt(2),
 O=U+tau Z-[kappa/(2tau)]A+nu W.                              (4)

The intended target is N(0,I_d). Define the bounded-moment energy statistic

 S_d=(||O||^2-d)/sqrt(2d),    M_d=(Z dot A)/d.

Equations (3) imply

 E M_d=c,    E[M_d|T]=f(T),    Var(M_d)>=v,
 |M_d|<=||Z||^2/d.                                           (5)

Thus M_d has uniformly bounded fourth moments, with limsup E M_d^4<=1.

3. ENERGY EXPANSION AND THE VARIANCE GAP

Put B=U+tau Z+nu W and
S0_d=(||B||^2-d(1+kappa c))/sqrt(2d).
The coordinates of B are independent. Their energy variance tends to two,
and their eighth moments are uniformly bounded. The standard expansion of
the second and fourth moments of a centered independent sum therefore gives

 E S0_d=0,    E S0_d^2 ->1,    E S0_d^4 ->3.                  (6)

Expanding (4), using kappa=1/sqrt(d), gives exactly

 S_d=S0_d+(c-M_d)/sqrt(2)+R_d,                               (7)

where
 R_d=-(U+nu W) dot A/(tau sqrt(2)d)
        +||A||^2/(4tau^2 sqrt(2)d^(3/2)).

The remainder tends to zero in L4. Conditional on A, the W term has fourth
moment 3||A||^4. The U term has fourth moment at most a universal constant
times kappa^2||A||^4, by the source sub-Gaussian proxy. The norm bound in
(3) and Gaussian chi-square moments control the last term. After the displayed
normalizations all three terms vanish in L4.

The possible covariance between S0_d and M_d also vanishes. Condition on
Z and the action's seeds, average U and W, then use E[A|Z]=cZ. This gives
the exact identity

 Cov(S0_d,M_d)=sqrt(2)tau^2 c/sqrt(d) ->0.                    (8)

Combining (5)-(8),

 liminf E S_d^2 >=1+v/2.                                    (9)

This is not just a moment mismatch left without a law conclusion. We next
turn it into a bounded-test TV lower bound using uniform tail control.

4. A STRICTLY POSITIVE FULL-LAW TV LOWER BOUND

Minkowski's inequality in (7), (5), and (6) imply

 limsup E S_d^4
 <=[3^(1/4)+(c+1)/sqrt(2)]^4.

For a target G~N(0,I_d), let T_d=(||G||^2-d)/sqrt(2d). Then
E T_d^2=1 and E T_d^4=3+12/d. Since c<1/2,

 limsup [E S_d^4+E T_d^4]<40.                                (10)

For any random variables X,Y and any A0>0, the bounded test
h(x)=min(x^2,A0)/A0 gives

 TV(Law(X),Law(Y))
 >=[E X^2-E Y^2]/A0-[E X^4+E Y^4]/A0^2.                     (11)

The tail estimate follows from x^2-min(x^2,A0)<=x^4/A0.
Take limits in (11) using (9)-(10), and choose A0=160/v. Data processing
through the energy statistic proves

 liminf_(m->infinity) TV(Law(O),N(0,I_d)) >= v^2/640>0.        (12)

This positive constant is deliberately conservative. The proof concerns the
actual acquired four-value action and uses no fitted law or free F(T) action.
Its extra internal randomness cannot cancel the time variance: (5) forces
that component, and (8) prevents cancellation by the baseline energy noise.

For any fixed M, averaging M independent random-time actions conditional
on the same Z gives the same proof with v replaced by v/M, hence a lower
bound v^2/(640 M^2). This does not preclude letting M grow.

5. WHAT DETERMINISTIC TIME QUADRATURE CHANGES

At fixed positive quadrature times the conditional time means are fixed,
so the Var_T f(T) term in (5) is absent. The separate quadrature audit proves
the actual full-law bound, including stochastic actions and deterministic
covariance bias,

 TV(O_Q,N(0,I_d))
 <=kappa^2[(sqrt(6)+1/(2sqrt(2)))sqrt(d)+phi(1)d S2]
       +kappa xi sqrt(d)/sqrt(2pi).

For this family kappa sqrt(d)=1. Taking J,m both proportional to log(d)
makes xi tend to zero and S2 tend to zero by the elementary positive-weight
bound in that audit. Thus the actual quadrature-based correction has TV
tending to zero along the same dimensions where one random shared time has
the positive gap (12). The extra covariance queries number O(log^2(d)); the
source-replica cost n+1 still has order sqrt(d).

An equally informed alternative is to average O(log^2(d)) independent
random-time actions. Its existing upper bound also tends to zero, and its
worst-case variance receipt can be smaller than the quadrature receipt.
Accordingly this result identifies a genuine shared-time effect and shows
how a fixed representation removes it; it does not establish a universal
query or practical advantage over ordinary averaging or stratification.

The sine example with variable frequency in the quadrature audit separately
shows that deterministic times do not restore a uniform global seed-
Lipschitz constant. Source-class closure remains the next obligation.
