OPERATOR SECOND MOMENT WITH A DOMINATED POSTSELECTION STATE
2026-10-24. luna_truncation. Exact bounded-operator check; no trace or
finite-dimensional assumption is needed for the moment/state step.

Claim

Let Gamma_e be independent Bernoulli(q) variables, 0<q<1. Let T_e be fixed
positive operators in a unital von Neumann algebra, independent of Gamma,
with sum_e T_e<=I and ||T_e||<=h. Define

  W_Gamma=sum_e Gamma_e T_e,   S=sum_e T_e,
  Z_Gamma=W_Gamma-qS.

For any fixed normal state phi,

  E_Gamma Z_Gamma^2=q(1-q)sum_e T_e^2
                         <=q(1-q)h I.                     (1)

If omega_Gamma is any Gamma-dependent normal state and p_Gamma>0 satisfies
the positive-functional domination p_Gamma omega_Gamma<=phi, then

  omega_Gamma(W_Gamma)
       <= q + sqrt(phi(Z_Gamma^2)/p_Gamma).                (2)

Consequently, for a>q and p_*>0,

  Pr_Gamma[omega_Gamma(W_Gamma)>=a, p_Gamma>=p_*]
       <= q(1-q) h/[p_*(a-q)^2].                           (3)

No operators are assumed to commute. The theorem is conditional on the
functional domination premise; it does not assert that every physical
postselection automatically gives p omega<=phi on the same algebra.

Proof of (1)-(3)

Write xi_e=Gamma_e-q. Since the xi_e are independent and centered,

  Z^2=sum_(e,f) xi_e xi_f T_e T_f,
  E Z^2=q(1-q)sum_e T_e^2.

For e!=f, E(xi_e xi_f)=0 multiplies the ordered product T_e T_f, so no
reordering or commutation is used. Each T_e is a positive contraction with
T_e^2<=||T_e||T_e<=hT_e. Summing and using sum T_e<=I proves (1).

For any normal state omega and self-adjoint Z, state Cauchy-Schwarz gives
|omega(Z)|^2<=omega(Z^2). Functional domination, applied to the positive
operator Z^2, gives p omega(Z^2)<=phi(Z^2). Since 0<=S<=I,

  omega(W)=q omega(S)+omega(Z)
      <=q+sqrt(omega(Z^2))
      <=q+sqrt(phi(Z^2)/p),

which is (2). If the event in (3) occurs, (2) forces
phi(Z^2)>=p_*(a-q)^2. Apply scalar Markov and (1), then apply phi:

  E phi(Z^2)=phi(E Z^2)<=q(1-q)h.

This proves (3). Normality is sufficient for the state to be a normal
functional; because the edge sum is finite, no limiting/tracial argument is
used.

BG variable-label coefficient check

For the BG game, assume Alice and Bob use fixed, complete local POVMs before
Gamma, with outcomes (l,s) and (j,t), where s,t are in [k]^r. In a tensor
model or a commuting-operator model, write their effects as A_(l,s)^x and
B_(j,t)^y, so cross-party effects commute. Define the coefficient for the
directed bipartite edge e=(s,t) by

  T_(s,t)=k^-2 sum_(l=1)^r
       A_(l,s)^(s_l) B_(l,t)^(t_l).                         (4)

Each product is positive: the local effects commute and are positive. Each
factor is a contraction, so each product is <=I and
||T_(s,t)||<=r/k^2. Repeated coordinates do not alter this estimate; they
still give at most r label terms.

For the sum bound, regroup by fresh inputs x=s_l and y=t_l:

  sum_(s,t) T_(s,t)
    = k^-2 sum_(x,y) sum_l sum_(s:s_l=x,t:t_l=y)
                         A_(l,s)^x B_(l,t)^y <= I.         (5)

At each fixed x,y, the inner sum is over a subset of the joint outcomes of
the complete local POVMs. Because the cross-party effects commute, these
products form a joint POVM; the selected sub-sum is bounded by I. Averaging
over x,y proves (5). The winning operator is then
W_Gamma=sum_(s,t) Gamma_(s,t)T_(s,t).

The scope matters: positivity of the products and (5) require the usual
commuting local algebras (in particular, a tensor-product strategy). For
noncommuting positive A and B, AB need not be positive. No trace, density
matrix representation, or finite dimension is otherwise used in (1)-(3);
phi can be any normal state on the von Neumann algebra containing these
operators.
