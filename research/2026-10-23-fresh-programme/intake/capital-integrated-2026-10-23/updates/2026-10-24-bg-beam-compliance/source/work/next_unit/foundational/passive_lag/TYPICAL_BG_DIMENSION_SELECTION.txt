TYPICAL BG GRAPHS: A GROWING ENTANGLEMENT-DIMENSION SELECTION OBSTRUCTION
2026-10-24. Actual finite-operator obstruction, not a vector relaxation.
Independently challenged internally at the literal dimension-restricted scope.
External correctness, formal validation, and historical priority UNKNOWN.

The low-information source in Section 5 has zero conditional live loss,
yet no complete one-round extraction with the dimension bound below can
achieve even 1/2 payoff under the original fresh-question law. This is
a quantum-RESOURCE obstruction. The positive gap is dimension-restricted,
not the unrestricted finite-entangled or commuting gap. Those values
of a typical BG graph remain UNKNOWN here. No general quantum repetition
counterexample is inferred.

1. Exact probabilistic statement
---------------------------------
Let k tend to infinity through perfect cubes, r=k^(1/3), N=k^r, and let
Gamma be the bipartite graph on [k]^r by [k]^r in which each edge is
included independently with probability q=k^(-1/5). Let G_Gamma be the
one-round game with independent uniform x,y in [k], answers
 (s,ell),(t,ell') in [k]^r times [r],
and predicate
 ell=ell', s_ell=x, t_ell=y, (s,t) in E(Gamma).             (D1)

Fix any constant 0<zeta<1/6 and put d_k=floor(k^(1/6-zeta)). With
probability tending to one,

                    omega_(<=d_k)(G_Gamma) <=1/2.        (D2)

Here omega_(<=d) optimizes all finite quantum strategies whose two local
Hilbert spaces have dimension at most d, including arbitrary input-
independent shared classical randomness. Equivalently the same bound
holds for arbitrary finite local spaces with a shared state of Schmidt
number at most d: each pure component can be compressed to its Schmidt
supports without changing its table. Classical computation and answer
alphabet size are NOT restricted. No bound on arbitrary commuting states
or unbounded Schmidt number follows from (D2).

This random graph simultaneously has the original BG properties with
probability tending to one:
 |E|/N^2 >=q/2,
 e(S,T)<=k^(2-1/10) for every |S|,|T|<=k.                 (D3)
Their elementary Chernoff/union-bound proof is independent of (D2).

2. Actual finite quantum optimizers have sparse POVMs
------------------------------------------------------
For any FIXED finite graph and local dimensions <=d, the strategy space
is compact. A maximizing shared state can be pure, since the payoff
is linear in its density matrix. There is no state-purification dimension
increase here: select a maximizing pure state on the original d by d
space. An outcome with s_ell!=x can never win. Move its positive effect
to any valid outcome; this cannot decrease payoff. Do the same for Bob.

Fix the state and Bob measurements. The payoff is linear in each Alice
POVM, so a maximizing extreme POVM can be selected independently at
each x. Every extreme POVM in M_d has at most d^2 nonzero effects: if
there were more, their Hermitian matrices would have a real linear
dependence sum_j c_j E_j=0. The distinct complete positive POVMs
 {(1+epsilon c_j)E_j} and {(1-epsilon c_j)E_j}, for sufficiently small
positive epsilon, would average to the original, a contradiction.
Now fix these Alice POVMs and choose extreme maximizing Bob POVMs.
The final value is still the global maximum and BOTH sides have <=d^2
nonzero effects per input. Selecting Bob last does not undo the matrix
extremality or support bound of Alice's already chosen POVMs.

Set L=d^2 and pad each input's support list with zero effects to length
L. Repeated labels in a padded list are harmless, since their effects
can be summed. Ordered outcome-label lists cost at most

        (rN)^(2kL) choices, log count=O(k d^2 r ln k).     (D4)

This is a bound on ACTUAL physical finite POVMs, not a claim that an
arbitrary feasible first-level Gram family has such a representation.

3. A fixed strategy is a weighted independent-edge sum
------------------------------------------------------
Fix its labels, state and measurements BEFORE Gamma is drawn. Group
all accepted-label terms that use the same graph edge (s,t). Its
winning probability is exactly

                Z_Gamma=sum_(s,t) w_(s,t) xi_(s,t),      (D5)

where xi are independent Bernoulli(q), w>=0, and
              sum w<=1,       w_(s,t)<=h=r/k^2.           (D6)
The first fact follows because at each input pair the terms are a
subset of the disjoint joint outcomes of complete POVMs. For the
second, a fixed (s,t) can contribute only at fresh input pairs
 (x,y)=(s_ell,t_ell), ell in [r].
There are at most r distinct such pairs, each of prior weight 1/k^2.
If several ell produce the same pair, their joint events are disjoint,
so that pair still contributes at most 1/k^2. This proves (D6) with
no product-state, coordinatewise-measurement or outcome-uniformity
assumption.

For u>=0, convexity on [0,h] gives
 e^(uw)-1 <=(w/h)(e^(uh)-1).
Consequently
 ln E exp(u Z_Gamma)<= (q/h)(e^(uh)-1).
For any a>q, optimizing at uh=ln(a/q) yields

 P[Z_Gamma>=a]
 <=exp[-(k^2/r){a ln(a/q)-a+q}].                          (D7)

In particular, for a=3/8 and q=k^(-1/5), this is
 exp[-(3/40+o(1)) k^(5/3) ln k].
The weights may depend on every local input and measurement; only
their independence of the sampled graph is used in the fixed-strategy
tail. The following simultaneous net removes that restriction on the
eventual optimizing strategy.

4. Complete positive POVM nets, with the correct sum-of-errors cost
------------------------------------------------------------------
For each fixed ordered label list use a net INSIDE
 P_(d,L)={ (E_1,...,E_L): E_j>=0, sum_j E_j=I_d }.
On Hermitian tuples use max_j ||E_j||_op. This compact set lies in the
unit ball of a real normed space of dimension Ld^2. A maximal delta-
separated subset is a delta-net with cardinality at most
 (1+2/delta)^(Ld^2), by the disjoint-ball volume argument. Its centers
are themselves positive COMPLETE POVMs; arbitrary matrix grids with
an uncharged normalization correction are not used.

Choose delta=1/(128L), and choose a 1/128 Euclidean net of pure unit
state vectors on C^d tensor C^d, of cardinality at most C^(2d^2) for
an absolute C. Each input's total effect error obeys
 sum_j ||E_j-E'_j||_op<=Ldelta=1/128.                     (D8)
This sum, not the error of one effect, controls a measurement change.
For fixed opposite POVMs and any state, the change of winning
probability from altering Alice alone is at most Ldelta: the operator
paired with each Alice effect is a convex average of accepted subsets
of Bob's complete POVM, hence lies between 0 and I. Sum the norms.
The Bob change has the same bound. The pure-state change costs at
most twice its vector norm, so the total payoff error is <=1/32.
All these estimates are independent of the enormous outcome alphabet.

There are 2k such POVMs and one state. Combining their net sizes with
(D4) gives log total size at most

 O(k d^2 r ln k + k d^4 ln(d+1) + d^2).                  (D9)

Every net representative is an actual valid strategy fixed before the
graph. If d<=k^(1/6-zeta), (D9) is o(k^(5/3)ln k): the first two terms
are respectively O(k^(5/3-2zeta)ln k) and
O(k^(5/3-4zeta)ln k). The union of (D7) over the net therefore has
probability tending to zero. With high probability every net strategy
has payoff <3/8. Then every sparse optimal strategy has payoff at
most 3/8+1/32<1/2. Section 2 covers the full finite-dimension optimum,
which proves (D2).

Arbitrary shared classical randomness is an affine mixture of these
strategies. It cannot exceed their common bound. The same observation
applies to any decomposition of a state into pure components of Schmidt
rank <=d, even if different components require different compressions
of the measurement operators. Supplying the component label could
only help, and even that stronger model has value <=1/2.

5. An actual source at vanishing event information per live round
----------------------------------------------------------------
Fix any Gamma for which (D2)-(D3) hold, and use the following completely
classical r-round repeated strategy. On full own question strings
X^r,Y^r, in coordinate i each player outputs its ENTIRE own string and
ell=i. Each coordinate's validity tests in (D1) hold identically. Hence

 W_1=W_2=...=W_r=1{(X^r,Y^r) in E(Gamma)}=:E.             (D10)

For EVERY nonempty proper tested C, its all-win event is EXACTLY E,
with p=|E(Gamma)|/N^2>=q/2, and every remaining conditional live payoff
is one. Since the original questions are uniform on all N^2 pairs,
the selected question density is exactly 1/p on E, giving

 D(Q_full || mu^r)=ln(1/p)<=ln2+(1/5)ln k.               (D11)

If |C|<=r/2 and m=r-|C|, the original event information per live round
is O(ln k/r), tending to zero. For average one-round joint-question
marginals, superadditivity and Pinsker also give mean TV drift at most
sqrt(ln(1/p)/(2m)), tending to zero. These are statements about the
ORIGINAL measured distribution, with no invalid coherent-state entropy
bound or fabricated Bob purification.

Nevertheless any COMPLETE all-input one-round strategy of Schmidt
number <=d_k, with its seed independent of the fresh pair and evaluated
under the original fresh mu, has payoff <=1/2 by (D2). Thus any extraction
that promises payoff tending to one from this source must leave that
resource class. Its required entanglement dimension exceeds
k^(1/6-zeta)=r^(1/2-3zeta), despite L/m tending to zero. Shared classical
seeds of arbitrary size do not repair the obstruction.

The original repeated strategy itself has Schmidt rank one. It uses
full-string classical input access and large answer records, which are
allowed by the usual repeated-game model. It does NOT satisfy the
bounded-total-memory/current-input contract in CAUSAL_MEMORY_CUT_SOURCE.
No conflict with that separate source theorem is asserted.

6. What the obstruction does and does not forbid
-------------------------------------------------
The positive base gap here is 1-omega_(<=d_k)>=1/2. This restricted
resource class is not closed under arbitrary R/H/S reconstruction or
tensor amplification. It is essential NOT to replace omega_(<=d_k) by
omega_* or omega_co. A high-payoff strategy with larger Schmidt rank
may exist; the unrestricted typical-graph values remain UNKNOWN.

Likewise (D2) does not forbid a local strategy with ABORT whose
conditional joint-success table has payoff one at small success mass.
Such a table is not a complete high-payoff strategy under the fresh
law. The lower bound concerns the TOTAL resources of any proposed
repair-plus-complete-extraction protocol, not the mere existence of a
postselected fine table, a common filter, or an abort mask factorization.

It is therefore a real low-L/m obstruction to hiding an unbounded
quantum-representation interface, but not a no-go for unrestricted
payoff-aware selection. The quantum-perfect planted family in the
separate frozen note also does not determine these typical values.

This proof is self-contained apart from elementary finite-dimensional
compactness, convexity, volume nets and scalar Chernoff. The graph/game
template is the primary Braverman--Garg ECCC TR14-095 revision1 Section7:
 https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2014/095/revision/1/download .
No standing S/R/H or finite-TABLE density theorem is used to prove (D2),
(D10), or the resource obstruction. Internal checks do not establish
external correctness, strongest prior, or historical novelty.
