1. Exact commuting branch construction C2--C5

Let A,B be commuting von Neumann algebras, eta a unit vector, and use
chi(F,H)=<eta,FH eta> for positive F in A and H in B. Assume complete
question-independent reference POVMs F0^alpha,H0^gamma and finite
domination radii

  F_u^alpha<=d_A F0^alpha,   H_v^gamma<=d_B H0^gamma.

Cross-algebra commutation is enough: F0^alpha H0^gamma is positive,
chi is monotone in either argument, and
  w0_(alpha,gamma)=||sqrt(F0^alpha)sqrt(H0^gamma)eta||^2.
Thus p0=sum theta w0 normalizes the direct-sum vector

  zeta= direct_sum_(alpha,gamma)
         sqrt(theta(alpha,gamma)/p0)
         sqrt(F0^alpha)sqrt(H0^gamma)eta.

This is a finite direct sum over branches; the summands may remain
infinite-dimensional. For each fine effect G_u^(alpha,a), bounded
Douglas factorization gives a positive C_u^(alpha,a) in A satisfying
  sqrt(F0^alpha) C_u^(alpha,a) sqrt(F0^alpha)=G_u^(alpha,a)/d_A.
The factors can be taken on supp(F0); their sum is <=I because the fine
sum is F_u^alpha<=d_A F0^alpha. Complete by the local failure effect.
Bob's D factors are identical. The shared branch register is added as a
new classical direct-sum register, so its projections lie in both new
local algebras. This does not assert that the old commuting strategy has
an exact finite-tracial representation.

On branch (alpha,gamma), both players' effects are local and commute
across players. Direct substitution yields

  P(1,a;1,b|u,v,c)
    = [sum_(alpha,gamma) theta chi(G_u^(alpha,a),K_v^(gamma,b))]
       /(d_A d_B p0),
  lambda_c=d_A d_B p0.

Summing fine outcomes gives the one-sided identities

  lambda_c P_A(1|u,c)=d_B sum theta chi(F_u^alpha,H0^gamma)<=d_B,
  lambda_c P_B(1|v,c)=d_A sum theta chi(F0^alpha,H_v^gamma)<=d_A.

The inequalities follow by dropping theta<=1, summing both POVMs, and
using that the original coarse sums are <=I. No same-side commutation is
used. If p0=0, every accepted reference weight is zero; domination and
positivity imply the original accepted table is zero. An always-fail
complete instrument with lambda_c=1 handles that branch without division
by zero.

For finitely many c, tensor together these branch resources. Alice can
select the factor for her hypothesized c_A and Bob the factor for c_B.
On a mismatch they act on different tensor factors, so their operators
still commute and define a complete all-input family. The matched-table
identity is not asserted on a mismatched pair; those entries are simply
part of the new legal complete table subsequently supplied to S_actual.

2. Generalized tau and the separate referee drifts

For the complete binary-filter table, the weighted mass definitions give
p<=G_A and p<=G_B because P(1,1)<=P_A(1),P_B(1) pointwise and lambda_c
is nonnegative. Hence B=max(G_A,G_B)>=p and log(B/p)>=0. Q in C7 is a
normalized probability table even if p>1 or a weighted entry
lambda_c P(1,1|c,u,v)>1. Its question marginal obeys

  Q_full(c,u,v)/mu^n(c,u,v)
      =lambda_c P(1,1|c,u,v)/p,

on positive-mu inputs. Consequently it need not be bounded by 1/p.
Retaining D_ref=D(Q_full||mu^n) in C8 is necessary for the general
weighted table; substituting log(1/p) would not be justified.

For an exact two-point illustration, take mu(q_1)=mu(q_2)=1/2, one
conclusive answer, fixed lambda=2, and joint success probabilities
P(1,1|q_1)=3/4 and P(1,1|q_2)=1/4 (complete the local instruments with
failure). Then p=1, but Q_full=(3/4,1/4), so
  D_ref=(3/4)log(3/2)+(1/4)log(1/2)>0=log(1/p).
The density on q_1 is 3/2. Thus the event-only shortcut fails even for
a finite legal filter; C8's D_ref term fixes exactly this issue.

Let the m live coordinates be indexed by i, and write X_i,Y_i for their
two question marginals. Under the product reference mu^n, the X_i
marginals are independent with law mu_X (and similarly for Y). Data
processing and product relative-entropy superadditivity give

  sum_(i live) D(Q_(X_i)||mu_X)
       <= D(Q_(X_live)||mu_X^m)
       <= D_ref,

and likewise for Y. Pinsker and concavity of sqrt then imply

  Delta_X=(1/m)sum_i TV(Q_(X_i),mu_X)
       <=sqrt(D_ref/(2m)),
  Delta_Y=(1/m)sum_i TV(Q_(Y_i),mu_Y)
       <=sqrt(D_ref/(2m)).

This is the H M4 drift term with its two sides kept separate. Together
with C8, both entropy charges obey
  log(B/p)/m<=tau,    D_ref/m<=tau,
so the existing R/H/S_actual composition can use the same tau. No
additional factor is introduced by the max in C8. The condition m>=1 is
implicit in selecting a live coordinate and should be stated wherever
C8 is invoked.

3. Why the fixed-p table-first limit is legitimate

Fix one finite input/output alphabet, fixed finite positive weights
lambda_c, a finite question law mu^n after omitting its zero atoms, and
one target commuting complete binary-filter table with p>0. Apply the
accepted density premise L to the ENTIRE new table, including the
success/failure outputs and every mismatched history-input pair. Let
P_j be finite-tracial approximants converging in table l1. They need not
inherit any operator inequality from the source reference construction:
C7--C10 are scalar statements about any complete table.

For fixed lambda and finite alphabets, p_j,G_A,j,G_B,j and the
unnormalized losing mass are finite linear functionals of P_j, so they
converge to p,G_A,G_B and the original losing mass. Since p>0,
p_j>0 eventually, and normalization gives convergence of the conditional
loss alpha_j and the accepted fine table. Moreover
  Q_full,j(z)=mu^n(z) r_j(z)/p_j
with r_j(z) a fixed finite weighted sum of success entries; hence
Q_full,j converges pointwise to Q_full. On this fixed finite support,

  D_ref,j=sum_z Q_full,j(z) log(Q_full,j(z)/mu^n(z))

converges as well: every reference atom is positive and t log t is
continuous at t=0. This argument does not require Q_full,j/mu^n<=1/p_j;
the approximants may have weighted success entries above one. It only
uses that Q_full,j is normalized, supported on the same finite positive
mu^n support, and converges pointwise. Thus B_j/p_j, D_ref,j, tau_j,
alpha_j, and the right side of C9 converge at this fixed p.

Apply C9 to each finite-tracial approximant. Given any positive final
accuracy a, choose the construction accuracy below a/2 and then choose j
large enough that its scalar right side differs from the limit by less
than a/2. This gives a legal finite-tracial, hence commuting, one-round
strategy within a of the limiting scalar criterion. Equivalently the
commuting game value is bounded by that criterion. The argument does not
produce a convergent sequence of resources or preserve the original
binary-filter table in the final one-round strategy.

For an exact accepted-event reconstruction, C4 implies
  Q_full/mu^n=P_original(E|questions)/p<=1/p
because now lambda_c P(1,1|c,u,v) is the original event probability.
Then D_ref<=log(1/p). C5 also gives
  B<=max{E_c d_A(c),E_c d_B(c)}.
This recovers the conservative reference parameter C11. The operator
identity is used only for the original table; it is neither required nor
claimed for its finite-tracial approximants.

Scope and disposition

No counterexample to the C2--C5 construction, C7--C10 scalar criterion,
or the fixed-p table-first passage was found, conditional on the named
accepted density premise L and the supplied R/H/S_actual interfaces.
The reasoning is fixed-p and finite-table continuity only. It provides
no finite-matrix density claim, no exact tracial representation of an
arbitrary commuting realization, and no uniform dimension, preparation,
time, conditioning, or approximation-cost bound. A useful wording
clarification is to say explicitly that the direct sum is finite over
branches, not necessarily finite-dimensional, and to state m>=1.
