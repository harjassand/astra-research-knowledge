MEASURED HISTORY GATE AND AN EXPLICIT WHOLE-VIEW REPAIR
2026-10-24. Exact finite construction; general useful-selection gate OPEN.

This note gives both sides of a source-specific test of measured-information
selection. At almost every interior half-history, both original measured
opposite-local charges vanish, but the corresponding shared-history fine
table is a PR box. Thus even a MIXED, classical-record version of local
reconstruction cannot be inferred from those charges at each history.
Nevertheless, forgetting that shared history allows an explicit classical
operation to reproduce the WHOLE private-view law with exponentially small
error. This is a concrete payoff-preserving quotient operation for this
source, not an unrestricted positive-gap selection theorem.

The distinction is consequential. A history-by-history obstruction is not
an obstruction to rebuilding the original live law after histories have
been forgotten. Conversely, its omission from the mixed-state entropy
charge cannot be justified just by saying that no pure workspace is needed.

1. One original event and its actual measured local records
----------------------------------------------------------
Let X,Y be independent uniform bit strings of length m, and put

  E={sum_j X_j Y_j=0 in F_2},
  p=Pr(E)=(1+2^(-m))/2.                                  (H1)

The event is a compatible CLASSICAL condition. Therefore its fully
dephased measured posterior Q has D(Q||P)=ln(1/p). No relative entropy of a
coherently postselected pure vector is used anywhere in this note.

At live coordinate i write x=X_i, y=Y_i, and fix the half-history

  r=(a,b)=(X_<i,Y_>i).

The remaining private strings are A=X_>i and B=Y_<i. The event becomes

  a dot B + b dot A + xy=0.                               (H2)

When a,b are both nonzero, define U=b dot A and V=a dot B. Before E these
are independent fair bits. For every fixed x,y, E retains the two equally
likely pairs obeying

  U xor V=xy.                                             (H3)

Thus x,y remain independent uniform bits given r,E, and the (U,V) table
is exactly PR: each winning pair has probability 1/2 and each losing pair
has probability zero.

Both opposite-local measured information charges are nevertheless zero:

  I_Q(x:Y_<=i | r,E)=0,
  I_Q(y:X_>=i | r,E)=0.                                   (H4)

For example, for each fixed x,B,y, exactly half of A completes (H2) when
b!=0, so (B,y) has a law independent of x. The other equality is symmetric.
Any actual local record generated from the corresponding own question
string and an input-independent local resource also obeys this zero
information statement by data processing. The stronger common-seed and
quantum-record conventions must be checked for the chosen original
instrument; they are not inserted into (H4) by assumption.

The selected mass of these histories is

  g_i=Pr_Q(a!=0,b!=0)
     =[(1-2^(-(i-1)))(1-2^(-(m-i)))]/(1+2^(-m)).           (H5)

Hence, for i uniform in [m],

  g_bar=[m-4+(m+2)2^(1-m)]/[m(1+2^(-m))] -> 1.            (H6)

The original question law also changes negligibly at each live coordinate.
For h=2^(-m),

  Q_i(x,y)=[1+2h(-1)^(xy)]/[4(1+h)],
  dTV(Q_i,Unif({0,1}^2))=3h/[4(1+h)].                    (H7)

The total original event information remains O(1), its per-coordinate
budget ln(1/p)/m tends to zero, and almost every interior conditional
history in (H5) has both charges exactly zero.

2. The precise local reconstruction that is impossible
-------------------------------------------------------
Suppose a reconstructor is required, at a FIXED such r, to produce the
whole conditional private-view table on A,B from an input-independent
shared quantum resource and local x-only/y-only measurements. Both players
are allowed the fixed r. Applying their local parity functions b dot A
and a dot B would reproduce (H3).

This is impossible even for commuting strategies. For binary-output
self-adjoint contractions P_0,P_1,Q_0,Q_1, with [P_x,Q_y]=0, the CHSH expression is at
most 2sqrt(2). One direct proof writes it as
  <P_0 eta,(Q_0+Q_1)eta>+<P_1 eta,(Q_0-Q_1)eta>
and uses Cauchy, ||P_x eta||<=1, and
  ||(Q_0+Q_1)eta||^2+||(Q_0-Q_1)eta||^2<=4.
Binary POVM observables are such contractions. Therefore any legal reconstructed
binary table has mean loss at least

  delta_CHSH=(2-sqrt(2))/4.                               (H8)

Its mean total variation from the ideal PR table is at least this amount.
This is a mixed measured-table obstruction, not merely a phase/purifier
obstruction.

It also rules out a zero-cost identification with the state family in
CQ_SELECTION_MIXED_REBUILD_GATE.txt. If that M1 positive-TP factorization
reproduces this PR table and has input ensemble information chi, its
canonical-purification operation M1--M5 gives a legal quantum table with
loss at most chi (the ideal loss is zero). Thus necessarily

  chi >= delta_CHSH in natural logarithm units.             (H9)

In particular, the zero quantities in (H4) cannot simply be substituted
for chi. This conclusion permits positive, non-CP maps: their positive
unital adjoints pull the actual Bob effects back to genuine POVMs. It does
not apply such a map physically to an entangled subsystem. Preserving a
quantum output workspace would require the corresponding CP extension.

Crucial interface scope: U needs Bob's fixed suffix b at Alice, and V
needs Alice's fixed prefix a at Bob. The target of this impossible
reconstruction includes that SAME fixed half-history at both sides.
Originally a,b are private parts of different question strings. A local
protocol has not been given a shared copy of r for free. This failure
therefore diagnoses a history/local-description gate. It does not show
that the original private-view marginal, with r forgotten, is nonlocal.

3. Forget the half-history: a legal finite classical repair
----------------------------------------------------------
Now rebuild only the whole private question-view law, without retaining a
shared duplicate of r. Fix i and put d=m-1>=3 and q=2^d. Concatenate each
party's questions outside i as S,T in F_2^d. Then

  E <=> S dot T=xy.

The FULL selected joint law is particularly simple:

  Q(x,y,S,T)=1/[q(2q+1)] if S dot T=xy, and 0 otherwise.    (H10)

Indeed the number of pairs with dot product zero is q(q+1)/2, and the
number with dot product one is q(q-1)/2. Thus the total number of accepted
quadruples (x,y,S,T) is q(2q+1).

Share one uniform g from the finite group GL(d,F_2). With e_1,e_2,e_3 the
first three standard basis vectors, the local operations are

  S_0=g e_1,          S_1=g e_2,
  T_0=g^(-T) e_3,     T_1=g^(-T)(e_2+e_3).                (H11)

Alice outputs S_x using only x and the seed g. Bob outputs T_y using only
y and the same seed. All four vectors are nonzero, and

  S_x dot T_y=xy for every x,y and every g.                 (H12)

The group action (S,T)->(gS,g^(-T)T) is transitive on nonzero ordered pairs
with a fixed dot product c in {0,1}. For c=1 choose S as a first basis
vector and T as its first dual functional. For c=0 choose S first and T
a nonzero dual functional annihilating it, then complete a basis. These
normal forms prove transitivity. Uniform g therefore gives the uniform
law on the relevant orbit. Their sizes are

  n_0=(q-1)(q-2)/2,     n_1=q(q-1)/2.                     (H13)

Let P' be the resulting genuine local table with fresh x,y independent
uniform. On each nonzero valid pair it has mass 1/(4n_(xy)). This is
strictly larger than the mass 1/[q(2q+1)] in (H10), for BOTH dot products.
Everywhere else P'=0. Consequently its exact total variation distance
from the original selected FULL joint law is just the latter's mass on
zero-vector pairs:

  dTV(P',Q)=3(2q-1)/[q(2q+1)] = O(2^(-m)).                 (H14)

There are 2q-1 such pairs at each of the three fresh inputs with xy=0,
and none at xy=1. Equation (H14) charges the changed fresh-question law
as well; it is not merely a comparison under Q_i.

This is one all-input family with one input-independent finite resource.
It does not invoke a fresh opposite-question oracle, a run-adaptive choice
of i, or a communication step. Its actual state/sample costs are explicit:

* The exact finite shared seed has |GL(d,2)|=prod_(j=0)^(d-1)(2^d-2^j)
  values, requiring ceil(log_2 |GL(d,2)|)<=d^2 register bits. Non-power-of-2
  uniformity is a distribution specification, not a claim of fixed-length
  perfect sampling from fair coins.
* Inversion by Gaussian elimination costs O(d^3) bit operations and the
  local matrix-vector products cost O(d^2). No additional quantum system
  is required by the repair.
* Uniform random d-by-d bit matrices are nonsingular with probability
  prod_(r=1)^d(1-2^(-r))>1/4. Rejection sampling thus uses <4d^2 random bits
  in expectation. To make its resource bound deterministic, cap it at L
  trials and charge the failure probability at most (3/4)^L as additional
  total variation/abort error. No unbounded sampler is hidden in (H14).

4. Actual subsequent fine measurements are preserved
----------------------------------------------------
Suppose the original repeated strategy uses any fixed input-independent
shared resource and arbitrary local instruments depending on its whole
own question string. Suppose E in (H1) is implemented by old checked
answers that copy the relevant question strings, so conditioning on this
part of E changes ONLY their classical law, not the shared resource.

After (H11), insert the fresh x or y at position i, and run the ORIGINAL
local full-string instrument on the ORIGINAL shared resource. The same
conditional local quantum instrument acts in both comparisons, for every
full question pair. Data processing of the block-diagonal classical law
therefore gives at most (H14) error for the entire measured fine table,
and at most (H14) change in any [0,1] live referee payoff. The same argument
holds in trace distance for a retained quantum output if it is generated
by these same actual CP instruments and the questions remain classical.

Thus the earlier shared-history PR obstruction is genuinely escaped by
changing the forgotten history coupling. It is not escaped by claiming an
unavailable local purification or by keeping an uncharged opposite-answer
tag. An arbitrary measurement/instrument-dependent postselection is NOT
covered: E's question-only character is essential in this argument.

5. An arbitrary fresh relation on uniform bilinear private fibers
----------------------------------------------------------------
The whole-view construction is not restricted to the fresh relation xy.
Let Xcal,Ycal be finite, let mu be ANY joint question law, and let
M_xy in F_2 be a fixed finite matrix of rank r over F_2. Compute a rank
factorization

  M_xy=alpha_x dot beta_y,
  u_x=(alpha_x,1,0),  v_y=(beta_y,0,1) in F_2^d,
  d>=r+2.                                                 (H15)

Extra zero coordinates are allowed. Both u_x and v_y are nonzero, even
when the corresponding unpadded vector is zero. The finite lookup tables
alpha,beta and the explicit matrix M are part of the representation cost.

Let S,T be independent uniform vectors in F_2^d, independent of (x,y), and
let the ORIGINAL compatible classical event be

  E_M={S dot T=M_xy}.

Put N=2^d and z=Pr_mu[M_xy=0]. Its exact probability is

  p_M=[N+2z-1]/(2N).                                       (H16)

Share uniform g in GL(d,2), and locally output

  S=g u_x,   T=g^(-T) v_y.                                (H17)

Orbit transitivity gives the uniform nonzero fiber for every fresh input
pair simultaneously. The real fresh question law remains mu. Original
selected mass at each valid point is mu(x,y)/(N^2 p_M); repaired mass at
each nonzero valid point is mu(x,y)/n_(M_xy), with n_0,n_1 as in (H13).
The latter dominates the former because

  N^2 p_M-n_1=Nz>=0,
  N^2 p_M-n_0=N(z+1)-1>0.                                 (H18)

Consequently, the exact full joint-law error is

  dTV(P'_M,P(.|E_M))
      = z(2N-1)/(N^2 p_M)
      = 2z(2N-1)/[N(N+2z-1)].                              (H19)

This includes the original-to-selected question-law difference, for an
arbitrary correlated mu. If z=0 the reconstruction is EXACT. The actual
fine-instrument transfer of Section 4 applies verbatim when this uniform
bilinear private-fiber event is the actual source's question-only event.

The finite shared-seed and execution costs in Section 3 remain valid.
Rank factorization requires the explicit finite M, and storing alpha,beta
requires O(r(|Xcal|+|Ycal|)) bits in addition to the shared matrix. These
costs are not bounded by ln(1/p_M), which is near ln 2 regardless of d.

This is not a generic reduction of an arbitrary event to the theorem.
Writing a Boolean event matrix as a bilinear form does not make its
actual private variables uniform independent vectors, nor preserve its
conditional quantum fine instrument. The large uniform fibers and the
local group orbit are substantive source hypotheses. Their acquisition
at an actual useful greedy-selected coordinate is not proved here.

6. A direct payoff theorem for arbitrary games under this event
--------------------------------------------------------------
The fine-instrument transfer gives an actual payoff-only conclusion, not
just a successful source-table example. Let G be ANY finite two-player
game whose fresh questions are uniform independent bits. Its answer
alphabets and [0,1] payoff are arbitrary. Write v for either its finite
tensor value or its commuting value, using the same model throughout.

Take ANY joint m-copy strategy, with arbitrary dependence of each full
local measurement on the whole own question string. For the ORIGINAL
question-only event E={sum_j X_jY_j=0}, fix ANY coordinate i and put
d=m-1>=3. The procedure (H11), followed by the original whole-string
measurement and retention of the ith answer, is a genuine single-copy
strategy for G. Its resource is the original resource plus the finite
shared classical seed. In the commuting model the blockwise original
measurements still commute; no finite-tracial representation or matrix
density premise is needed. By (H14) and data processing,

  E[payoff_i | E]
     <= v + 3(2q-1)/[q(2q+1)] <= v+3/q,
  q=2^(m-1).                                              (H20)

Equivalently, if the base gap is epsilon=1-v, the original conditional
live loss at EVERY coordinate is at least epsilon-3/q. For q>=6/epsilon
it is at least epsilon/2. No answer-size dependence appears. There is no
selection of a favorable run, and i can be any fixed coordinate before
the fresh questions arrive. Thus for this actual small-ln(1/p)/m source,
a useful payoff control exists even though history-by-history mixed
private-view reconstruction had a constant error obstruction.

The same argument with (H15)--(H19) gives, for ANY game G with fresh law mu
and any actual original private-view source satisfying those uniform
bilinear-fiber hypotheses,

  E[payoff | E_M] <= v + 2z(2N-1)/[N(N+2z-1)].             (H21)

This statement is conditional on the stated SOURCE property, but the
local construction and its consequence are direct and do not invoke
S/R/H or the accepted commuting table-density premise.

It also gives a literal weak event-filter witness. Add one shared classical
Bernoulli flag of probability p_M, independent of the fresh questions and
the GL seed. On success execute the repair; otherwise both parties abort.
The joint event mass is exactly p_M, and both one-sided success masses
are exactly

  G_A=G_B=p_M.                                             (H22)

Its unnormalized accepted fine measure differs from the original one by
at most p_M times (H19) in total variation. Thus its sufficient mass
certificate log(max(G_A,G_B)/p_M) is ZERO, with the finite approximation
error charged separately. This is achieved by an actual finite local
operation, not by assigning zero cost to a hypothetical repair. Since
joint success is bounded by each one-sided success, (H22) is the smallest
possible maximum one-sided mass. If z=0, the entire accepted fine measure
is preserved exactly. The statement is an operation on this whole source
table, not an entrywise ancillary lift of its predicate.

One immediate repeated-question instance is uniform independent
X_j,Y_j in F_2^h with E={sum_j X_j dot Y_j=0}. The fresh matrix has rank h,
the other questions provide d=h(m-1) independent private bits per side,
and d>=h+2 suffices. Here z=(1+2^(-h))/2, p=(1+2^(-hm))/2, and the error
in (H21) is at most 3*2^(-h(m-1)). Thus H20's arbitrary-payoff conclusion
extends to all such finite uniform free question alphabets.

This does not prove a repetition rate for an arbitrary strategy: the
actual greedy checked-win event is generally measurement/answer-dependent,
and no argument here replaces it by a uniform bilinear question event.
It proves a positive, game-unrestricted payoff control under one explicit
event geometry, and makes the remaining acquisition hypothesis exact.

7. Positive-gap and greedy-selection boundaries
-----------------------------------------------
The event is an admissible checked-win source. A game's answer may contain
an auxiliary claimed m-bit string; a tested coordinate can copy each
party's actual live question string into that claim, and a virtual
predicate can test their dot product. This is a local full-string repeated
strategy. The base virtual game has value 1 because both parties can claim
zero. The conditional parity probes above alone therefore do not establish
a positive-gap game whose live winning probability exceeds its base value.

Even a positive base gap can be added without changing that limitation.
Take the AND of real CHSH and the virtual claimed-string parity test.
Its finite-tensor and commuting values equal cos^2(pi/8): the upper bound
comes from real CHSH; constant zero virtual claims attain it. On a checked
coordinate use a separate optimal CHSH resource and copy the live question
strings as the virtual claims. Use independent CHSH resources on live
coordinates, with zero virtual claims there; any desired private-view
payload can be appended to their answers. The checked event has constant
probability cos^2(pi/8) times (H1). The measured-history obstruction persists
in that payload, but live conditional real-CHSH loss stays at its ordinary
base-gap scale (up to the tiny question drift), rather than becoming
near-perfect. Hence this STILL does not refute a theorem restricted to
histories with useful excess live payoff in the actual greedy argument.

The general unresolved selection implication is precisely that additional
payoff-sensitive step. Neither the pointwise mixed-table obstruction nor
the successful parity whole-view repair establishes or refutes it. Any
candidate proof must specify which history coupling can be forgotten,
which actual fine instruments survive that change, and why the original
event information budget acquires that operation at a useful coordinate.
