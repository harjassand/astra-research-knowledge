# Correlated-input falsifier for the free-input factorization

This is a counterexample to extending Sections 3 and 6 of
ONE_SIDED_INSTRUMENT_SELECTION.txt from a free product question law to
correlated within-round inputs. It does not challenge the stated
free-input theorem.

## Smallest useful fixture: two binary rounds

Let each question pair be drawn independently across rounds from
\[
 \mu(0,0)=\mu(1,1)=3/8,\qquad
 \mu(0,1)=\mu(1,0)=1/8.
\]
Thus both one-party marginals are uniform, but within a round
\(\Pr[Y=X]=3/4\). In the two-round game, write the inputs
\((X_1,Y_1),(X_2,Y_2)\). Use a trivial shared state and deterministic local
instruments:

* Alice answers \(A_1=X_2\), and raises her local success flag exactly when
  \(E=\{X_2=X_1\}\).
* Bob answers \(B_1=Y_2\).

Alice's answer and flag are legal functions of her full input \(X^2\);
Bob's answer is a legal function of \(Y^2\). No operation uses the other
party's input. The success probability conditional on the full Alice input
is \(p_{x_1x_2}=\mathbf1[x_2=x_1]\), so \(p=1/2\), and it is not
pointwise constant.

Condition on a live first-round pair \(X_1=x,Y_1=y\). Every such pair has
positive probability. Before selection, the second round is independent,
so \(Y_2\) is uniform given \(X_1=x,Y_1=y\). After selection, \(X_2=x\);
then the original within-round channel gives
\[
 \Pr_E[Y_2=x\mid X_1=x,Y_1=y]=3/4,\qquad
 \Pr_E[Y_2=1-x\mid X_1=x,Y_1=y]=1/4.
\]
In particular, selection changes \(Y_2\mid X_1=x\) from uniform to a
distribution biased toward \(x\). The needed residual-question kernel
depends on Alice's *fresh* first input \(x\); since every \((x,y)\) has
positive mass, it cannot be recovered from \(y\) alone.

For coordinate \(i=1\), the target Alice state in (S10), under
\(Q_X\), has \(X_2=x\) with probability one. Since \(A_1=X_2\), it makes
Alice's selected output \(a=x\) deterministic. But the Bob operator in
(S11) averages \(Y_2\) using the original marginal \(\mu_Y\), which is
uniform. Hence (S12) predicts
\[
 P_{\mathrm{recon}}(A_1=x,B_1=0\mid x,y)
 =P_{\mathrm{recon}}(A_1=x,B_1=1\mid x,y)=1/2.
\]
The actual selected live table is instead
\[
 P_E(A_1=x,B_1=x\mid x,y)=3/4,\qquad
 P_E(A_1=x,B_1=1-x\mid x,y)=1/4.
\]
The Bob-marginal total-variation discrepancy is exactly \(1/4\). For
example at \(x=y=0\), the actual \((a,b)\) table is
\((0,0):3/4,(0,1):1/4\), while the proposed factorization gives
\((0,0):1/2,(0,1):1/2\).

## What conditional kernel would be needed

For this fixture, the exact residual kernel is
\[
 \kappa_{x,y}(v)
   =\Pr_E[Y_2=v\mid X_1=x,Y_1=y]
   =\mu_Y(v\mid X=x).
\]
It is \(3/4\) at \(v=x\), \(1/4\) at \(v=1-x\), and depends on fresh Alice
input \(x\), not just Bob's input \(y\). Replacing the old \(Y_2\sim\mu_Y\)
average by this \(x\)-indexed kernel repairs this particular table, but
it is not the fixed Bob POVM family (S11): Bob's fresh local input is \(y\)
and he does not receive \(x\).

In a general correlated source, the exact conditional live table requires
the joint selected residual-input kernel
\[
 \lambda_{x,y,h}(u,v)
   =\Pr_E[X_{>i}=u,Y_{-i}=v
       \mid X_{<i}=h,X_i=x,Y_i=y],
\]
combined with the actual full-string Alice instrument and Bob POVM.
This kernel can depend on both fresh inputs. Factoring it into the
\(Q_X\)-averaged Alice state of (S10) and an \(x\)-independent Bob
average of (S11) is justified by the free product law, but not by
one-sidedness alone.

## Scope boundary

Because \(E\) is Alice-local, conditioning cannot change
\(Y^m\mid X^m\) when \(X^m\) is the *entire* Alice input: for any full
string with positive selection probability,
\(\Pr(Y^m\mid X^m,E)=\Pr(Y^m\mid X^m)\). The changed kernel here is the
residual \(Y_2\) law after conditioning only on the current coordinate
\(X_1=x\); the selector correlates that fresh coordinate with the
remaining Alice coordinate \(X_2\), which in turn correlates with \(Y_2\).
Thus this is specifically a falsifier for the free-input averaging step
(S11)--(S12), not an assertion that an Alice-only event changes the
conditional law of Bob's questions given the full Alice string.

The pointwise-constant simplification (S7) also cannot be invoked here:
\(p_{x_1x_2}\) is zero off the diagonal and one on it. The example shows
why replacing the input law by an arbitrary correlated \(\mu\), then
using the original unconditional \(Y_{-i}\) average, is invalid. It makes
no claim against the theorem under its stated free-question assumption.
