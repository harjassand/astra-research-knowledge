# Shared-history repair for correlated within-round inputs

This note checks the proposed repair for iid rounds from a possibly
correlated one-round law \(\mu(x,y)=\mu_X(x)\mu(y\mid x)\). It reuses the
two-round counterexample in CORRELATED_INPUT_FACTORISATION_FALSIFIER.txt.
The repair keeps \(R_i=(X_{<i},Y_{>i})\) as an offline shared seed; it does
not average the Bob prefix using the unconditional \(Y\)-marginal.

## The two-round fixture and exact conditional tables

Use
\[
 \mu(0,0)=\mu(1,1)=3/8,\qquad \mu(0,1)=\mu(1,0)=1/8
\]
independently across two rounds, and the same scalar local instruments:
Alice's full answers are \(\mathbf a=(X_2,X_2)\), her success event is
\(E=\{X_2=X_1\}\), and Bob's full answers are \(\mathbf b=(Y_2,Y_2)\).
The original density operator is the scalar state \(1\). The actual
successful fine subinstrument and Bob POVM are
\[
 S_{\mathbf a|X}
   =\mathbf1[X_2=X_1]\mathbf1[\mathbf a=(X_2,X_2)],
 \qquad
 N_Y^{\mathbf b}
   =\mathbf1[\mathbf b=(Y_2,Y_2)].
\]
Thus on each successful full input pair the original fine answer table is
the point mass at these two complete answer strings, before any
coordinate-coarse-graining.

For \(i=1\), \(R=Y_2=v\). Under the selected law,
\[
 Q(R=v)=1/2,\qquad
 q_v(x):=Q(X_1=x\mid R=v,E)
   =\begin{cases}3/4&x=v,\\1/4&x\ne v.\end{cases}
\]
At fixed \(R=v,X_1=x,Y_1=y\), the event fixes \(X_2=x\), while
\(Y_2=v\) is already in \(R\). The full selected answer strings are
\(\mathbf a=(x,x)\) and \(\mathbf b=(v,v)\), deterministically. The
coarsened coordinate table is therefore
\[
  P_E(A_1=a,B_1=b\mid R=v,x,y)=\mathbf1[a=x]\mathbf1[b=v].
\]
The repaired operators give exactly the same table:
\(\tau_a^{x,v}=\mathbf1[a=x]\) on the scalar Bob space, and
\(K_y^{b,v}=\mathbf1[b=v]\). There is no Y-prefix to average for \(i=1\).
The fresh pair law at this history is
\[
 Q(X_1=x,Y_1=y\mid R=v,E)=q_v(x)\mu(y\mid x).
\]

For \(i=2\), \(R=X_1=h\). Now
\[
 Q(R=h)=1/2,\qquad q_h(x):=Q(X_2=x\mid R=h,E)=\mathbf1[x=h].
\]
At fixed \(R=h,X_2=x,Y_2=y\) in the selected support, \(x=h\).
The full selected answer strings are \(\mathbf a=(h,h)\) and
\(\mathbf b=(y,y)\). In the repaired Bob operator, average
\(Y_1=w\) with \(\mu(w\mid h)\), as prescribed by the correlated source;
the retained answer is \(B_2=Y_2=y\), independent of \(w\). Thus
\[
 \tau_a^{x,h}=\mathbf1[a=h],\qquad K_y^{b,h}=\mathbf1[b=y],
\]
and the repaired table equals the actual selected table exactly. For
\(x\ne h\), \(q_h(x)=0\); define any complete Alice POVM there when
constructing a strategy on every input. The selected table never puts
mass on such a pair \((R,x)\).

The exact-rational replay is check_correlated_history_repair.py.

## General operator factorization and locality

Write the repeated input law as
\(\mu^m(X,Y)=\prod_{j=1}^m\mu_X(x_j)\mu(y_j\mid x_j)\). Let
\(p_X=\sum_{\mathbf a}\operatorname{Tr}S_{\mathbf a|X}\) be the success
probability of Alice's local subinstrument on the full input string.
Conditioned on success, the full input law is
\[
 Q(X,Y)=p^{-1}\mu_X^m(X)
        \left(\prod_j\mu(y_j\mid x_j)\right)p_X .
\]
Fix \(R=(h,v)=(X_{<i},Y_{>i})\), and current inputs \(x=X_i,y=Y_i\).
The residual input distribution factors exactly:
\[
 Q(X_{>i}=u,Y_{<i}=w\mid R,x,y,E)
   =Q(X_{>i}=u\mid h,v,x,E)
        \prod_{j<i}\mu(w_j\mid h_j).                 \tag{H1}
\]
The first factor includes the success reweighting \(p_{h,x,u}\) and the
conditioning on \(Y_{>i}=v\). It is independent of current \(y\).
The second factor is the Bob-prefix law given the Alice prefix \(h\);
it is independent of \(x,y,u,v\). The current-pair law at history \(R\)
is
\[
 Q(X_i=x,Y_i=y\mid R,E)=q_R(x)\mu(y\mid x),\qquad
 q_R(x)=Q(X_i=x\mid R,E).                            \tag{H2}
\]
These identities use iid rounds and the fact that Alice's success is
local. They do not require \(X_i\) and \(Y_i\) to be independent.

For completeness, define the selected Alice substate on Bob's original
Hilbert space, retaining Alice's \(i\)-th answer:
\[
 \tau_a^{x,R}
  =\sum_u Q(u\mid h,v,x,E)\,
       \frac{\sum_{\mathbf a:a_i=a}S_{\mathbf a|h,x,u}}
            {p_{h,x,u}}.                              \tag{H3}
\]
Terms with zero \(p_{h,x,u}\) have zero selected weight and are omitted.
It is positive and \(\sum_a\operatorname{Tr}\tau_a^{x,R}=1\).
Define Bob's complete POVM
\[
 K_y^{b,R}
  =\sum_w\left(\prod_{j<i}\mu(w_j\mid h_j)\right)
       \sum_{\mathbf b:b_i=b}N_{(w,y,v)}^{\mathbf b}. \tag{H4}
\]
It depends only on \(R=(h,v)\) and Bob's fresh input \(y\), not on
Alice's fresh \(x\). Completeness follows by summing over \(b\), then
using completeness of every original \(N_{(w,y,v)}\).

The selected fine table uses the original full-string instruments:
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 P_E(A_i=a,B_i=b\mid R,x,y)
  &=\sum_{u,w}Q(u\mid h,v,x,E)
       \left(\prod_{j<i}\mu(w_j\mid h_j)\right)\\
  &\quad\cdot
       \frac{\displaystyle
        \sum_{\mathbf a:a_i=a,\ \mathbf b:b_i=b}
         \operatorname{Tr}\!\left[
          N_{(w,y,v)}^{\mathbf b}S_{\mathbf a|h,x,u}
         \right]}
        {p_{h,x,u}}\\
  &=\operatorname{Tr}[K_y^{b,R}\tau_a^{x,R}].
 \end{aligned}                                                   \tag{H5}
\]
The second equality is exactly (H1); no fine outcome is replaced by a
changed question law. In the scalar replay, \(S_{\mathbf a|X}\) and
\(N_Y^{\mathbf b}\) are the actual deterministic fine-outcome
subinstruments/POVMs, making (H5) an equality of their full induced
answer tables.

## Offline shared seed and question drift

Sample \(R\) from its selected marginal \(Q_R\) before the comparison
strategy receives new questions. For each realized \(R\), prepare one
fixed canonical purification of
\(\bar\rho_R=\sum_xq_R(x)\sum_a\tau_a^{x,R}\); Alice's POVMs are built
from \(\tau^{x,R}\) and depend only on \(R,x\), and Bob uses (H4), which
depends only on \(R,y\). If \(q_R(x)=0\), Alice uses any fixed complete
POVM there. The state and the shared seed law are fixed before the fresh
input draw. Neither player is supplied the other player's realized
current question.

Relative to the original one-round law
\(\mu(x,y)=\mu_X(x)\mu(y\mid x)\), the exact history-conditional drift is
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \operatorname{TV}\big(q_R(x)\mu(y\mid x),\mu(x,y)\big)
  &=\frac12\sum_{x,y}
       |q_R(x)-\mu_X(x)|\,\mu(y\mid x)\\
  &=\operatorname{TV}(q_R,\mu_X).
 \end{aligned}                                                   \tag{H6}
\]
Thus the conditional shared-history repair restores the actual live
table and keeps the Bob operator local; any remaining change to the
original fresh law is precisely the marginal \(X\)-drift in (H6).
