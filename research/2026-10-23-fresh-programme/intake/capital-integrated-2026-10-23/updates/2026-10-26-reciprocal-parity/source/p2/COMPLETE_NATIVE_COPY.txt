## ASTRA — Reciprocal waveguide construction

The latest research identifies a simpler physical alternative to the previous five-mode, chiral-waveguide gate: two localized emitters in an ordinary reciprocal waveguide, supplemented by two passive resonators.

An important restriction comes first. In a reciprocal waveguide, the forward and backward coupling vectors of two emitters separated by distance \\(L\\) have the form

\\[ v\_\pm(\omega)\propto \begin{pmatrix}1\\\e^{\pm i k(\omega)L}\end{pmatrix}. \\]

Their combined radiation matrix has eigenvalues proportional to

\\[ 1+\cos[k(\omega)L], \qquad 1-\cos[k(\omega)L]. \\]

Consequently, the radiation matrix cannot remain proportional to the identity across a continuous frequency interval when the dispersion is nonconstant. The previous exact spectral-frame cancellation therefore cannot simply be transferred to an ordinary bidirectional waveguide.

The stronger alternative is to work directly in the symmetric and antisymmetric emitter sectors. These sectors decouple exactly, and each has its own scalar Fano resolvent. This permits independent passive-resonator compensation without imposing nonreciprocal propagation.

The resulting design is less dependent on the earlier engineered two-port phase pattern, although it still requires separately controlled symmetric and antisymmetric auxiliary modes.

## 2. Certified coherent operation and all-particle capacity

The reciprocal architecture now has a rigorous continuous-band certificate:

\\[ \boxed{\begin{aligned} \\|S\_{\rm full}(E)+I\\|&<0.009072,\\\ \\|S\_{\rm not\ full}(E)-I\\|&<0.009470, \end{aligned}} \\]

for every \\(E/B\in[1.452,1.548]\\).

These are full two-direction photon-state errors, valid for arbitrary normalized wavepackets supported in that interval. The proof uses exact-rational calibration and analytic remainder bounds, not frequency-grid sampling.

The same Hamiltonian satisfies

\\[ \boxed{\inf\operatorname{spec}H_N=-B\min(N,m)} \\]

for every finite particle number \\(N\\), where \\(m\\) is the memory capacity. The result also accommodates the previously constructed interacting, entangled memory states.

### 3. Minimum passive control resources

A further theorem shows that at least two independently coupled passive auxiliary modes are necessary to cancel both scattering slopes in this reciprocal geometry.

Moreover, the antisymmetric channel requires at least one auxiliary resonance inside the photon band. These restrictions follow from the rank of the reactive-response derivative and positivity of passive-resonator residues.

I constructed an explicit realization with two physical side oscillators, real couplings, and no synthetic magnetic flux:

\\[ H\_{\rm side}= \begin{pmatrix} 2.83838&1.15325\\\ 1.15325&2.83838 \end{pmatrix}. \\]

It reproduces the certified parity-resonator Hamiltonian exactly. The remaining cost is demanding: its cross-coupling is approximately 90% of its direct coupling.

## 4. Independent radiation is the decisive obstruction

Adding two independent, unmonitored radiation continua produces a sharp condition.

To achieve a 1% coherent error even at the selected central photon frequency, this architecture requires

\\[ \boxed{\frac{\kappa\_{\rm loss}}{\gamma\_{\rm guided}} <2.3023\times10^{-5}.} \\]

This is a necessary, not sufficient, condition.

The calculation includes every radiation output channel. At an independent-loss spectral ratio of \\(3\times10^{-5}\\), the numerically calculated maximum selected photon-state error already exceeds 1%.

An important distinction survives: the all-particle spectral-capacity theorem remains true even when independent radiation makes the quantum operation insufficiently coherent.

That separates two physical questions often conflated in idealized models: whether matter can stably store a bounded number of excitations, and whether it can perform a useful quantum operation with sufficiently low error.
