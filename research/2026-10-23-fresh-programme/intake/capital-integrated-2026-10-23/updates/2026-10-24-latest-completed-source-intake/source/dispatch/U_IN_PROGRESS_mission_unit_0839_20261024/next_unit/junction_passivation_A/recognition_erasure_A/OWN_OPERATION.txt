The frozen recognition-passivation source and its failures remain unchanged.

Need
The acquired HMSD platform trades recognition strength against product
inhibition. A cis complementary mask has a real pre-admission closure path;
the simplest flexible-linker model even favors that wrong closure. A paid
operation should change actual molecular identity, rather than assign a
favorable effective concentration or a perfect admission cage.

Choice and closest incumbents
Test sacrificial RNA recognition tails, erased by RNase H on the RNA/DNA
hybrid made with a DNA template. The remainder of the product is DNA. This
uses the actual substrate specificity of a named enzyme and spends the RNA
tail; it gives up enzyme-free operation. RNase-H burnt-bridge walkers and
enzyme-driven DNA/RNA circuit turnover are strong incumbents. No new
RNase-H principle, nuclease, or molecular walker is claimed.

Explicit candidate graph, before any kinetic completion is assumed
An internal monomer body M has the acquired alternating DNA assembly ports
plus an orthogonal DNA adapter anchor z, placed outside those ports. A
recognition adapter R contains z* and a dangling RNA recognition segment
t_RNA. Its DNA z* anchor remains on M. The input lock L occupies the
forward DNA assembly port a, as in the acquired TMSD admission step.
The DNA template presents t_DNA* and a*. It first captures t_RNA, then
displaces L from a. This is now an associative/remote-toehold displacement
because recognition is on R and a is on M: its topology and actual rate
cannot be silently inherited from a contiguous source strand.

RNase H cleaves hybridized t_RNA. The DNA template is meant to remain held
by its long DNA a contact while the recognition duplex is digested. The
next monomer's backbone interaction then displaces a* by the source HMSD
mechanism. Template DNA and product DNA are not RNase-H substrates; R's
DNA anchor remains as product decoration. The product is not an unmodified
bare polymer. RNA remnants and actual released locks remain in solution.

Actual differences from the old mask
No second complementary mask must outcompete the retained template tag.
The recognition material is consumed irreversibly, so mask breathing is
replaced by cleavage completeness and fragment binding/inventory. Longer
recognition could in principle be used without leaving the same long tag
on completed products. This is the discriminating prediction to test,
not a derived performance claim.

Decisive gates
1. The enzyme can cut after RNA-tag capture but BEFORE lock admission.
   This can kill an otherwise valid input. Enzyme specificity for hybrid
   alone does not provide an admission AND gate. A finite race or a real
   admission-dependent substrate geometry must be acquired, not assigned.
2. The R-to-M associative admission geometry must permit a invasion without
   threading, and RNase-H access must coexist with that junction.
3. The actual source uses high-salt TAE/EDTA conditions. A named RNase H's
   activity, cleavage products and hybrid minimum in the SAME conditions,
   or an experimentally supported jointly compatible medium, are needed.
4. Cleavage leaves distributions of tethered RNA stubs and free fragments.
   No assumption that they all instantly leave or cannot rebind the
   template is permitted. Late cleavage may clear bound completed product,
   but this needs an explicit state path and readout.
5. The long a duplex still bears processivity, sequence/length fidelity and
   next-growth geometry. Irreversible recognition erasure does not solve
   the old constant-off-rate completion obstruction.

Full-cycle boundary
Per incorporated monomer pay one preassembled RNA/DNA adapter, the ordinary
lock release, RNA-phosphodiester hydrolysis, enzyme inventory/turnover and
any required medium/feed. Free locks and every cleavage fragment are
counted by species/multiplicity. Adapter preparation/regeneration and
fragment disposal are not free; the template should be recovered intact
without adding a complementary protector that simply reintroduces the old
gate. Compare to the unmodified source HMSD architecture, its existing
putative long-template frontier, and real staged synthesis/regeneration.
