# Binary registry model: bounded primary comparison

## Finding

The broad physical combination is already represented in the closest prior: composition-conserving Ar/Kr Monte Carlo on graphite, with species identity exchanges, relaxed particle coordinates, periodic substrate registry, and composition-segregated registry walls. Its companion 1D Frenkel–Kontorova (FK) model explicitly gives species-dependent pair stiffnesses and natural bond lengths plus periodic corrugation. This is strong mechanism ancestry and retires any broad claim that binary composition, elastic mismatch, and substrate registry jointly create domain walls. It does not establish the exact six-site ground-state inequality or the thermodynamic-limit interface-density claim in the present binary Hamiltonian.

## Closest binary-FK primary

Patrykiejew & Sokołowski, “The mechanism of domain-wall structure formation in Ar-Kr submonolayer films on graphite,” *Condensed Matter Physics* 17 (2014), 43601, DOI [10.5488/CMP.17.43601](https://doi.org/10.5488/CMP.17.43601); readable primary text: [arXiv:1501.02566 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/1501.02566).

In the 2D model (Sec. 2), truncated Ar/Kr Lennard–Jones pair interactions act with a species-dependent three-wave periodic graphite potential. The canonical Metropolis simulation translates atoms and attempts identity exchanges at fixed composition (Sec. 2, lines 66–86). It reports Kr-rich commensurate domains, Ar/Kr walls, and an Ar-composition-dependent commensurate–incommensurate transition in finite clusters (Sec. 3, lines 105–126, 139–148). Thus species are annealed together with registry in the 2D simulation; this is more than a static binary FK analogy. The paper expressly says solid-like mixed Ar–Kr/graphite had not been experimentally studied (Sec. 1, lines 53–57): these are simulations, not a demonstrated material operation.

The 1D model (Sec. 4, Eqs. 4.1–4.7) has harmonic nearest-neighbour terms with pair-specific (K_{AA},K_{AB},K_{BB}) and natural lengths (b_{AA},b_{AB},b_{BB}), alongside a periodic cosine whose amplitude depends on species. It sets (K_{AB}=\sqrt{K_{AA}K_{BB}}), (b_{AB}=(b_{AA}+b_{BB})/2), and uses reduced values (b_{AA}=1.9a), (b_{BB}=1.8a), (K_{BB}=0.9K_{AA}), (v_A=0.0055), (v_B=0.003). But its wall location is a chosen coordinate (N_1) to be minimized over, and wall composition is specified through the Ar fraction; this is not the proposed fully local annealed-spin Hamiltonian with an independent explicit (-J\sigma_i\sigma_{i+1}). The displayed wall-rest-length equations (4.4, 4.6) have apparent malformed equality chains, so they should not be used as unambiguous mixing laws. Their 2D identity-exchange simulations supply the stronger annealing evidence.

## Elastic-Ising comparator and resource boundary

Fratzl & Penrose, “Ising model for phase separation in alloys with anisotropic elastic interaction—I. Theory,” *Acta Metallurgica et Materialia* 43 (1995), 2921–2930, DOI [10.1016/0956-7151(95)00005-G](https://doi.org/10.1016/0956-7151(95)00005-G), is the decisive conventional compressible/elastic-Ising comparator. Its primary abstract specifies binary A/B occupancy on a 2D periodic square lattice, longitudinal/transverse springs between nearest and next-nearest neighbours, a nearest-neighbour chemical term preferring like pairs, and Kawasaki dynamics. Its mean-field result is coherent phase separation. It therefore already owns annealed binary chemistry + elastic relaxation + attractive interfaces, but it has no periodic substrate registry term; its stated theory concerns coherent phase separation, not the same substrate-pinned FK operation.

For the candidate’s stated nearest-neighbour ferromagnetic (-J\sigma_i\sigma_{i+1}) alone, at fixed 50:50 composition the periodic 1D minimum has only two unlike bonds, so interface density tends to zero as chain length grows. A conventional finite-period Ising pattern requires a further competing interaction (for example a longer-range term of the opposite sign); that is a different explicit energetic resource, not an implication of the stated ferromagnetic term. This elementary comparison does not clear priority for the mechanically generated registry mechanism.

## Decision boundary

- **Already conventional:** binary segregation at registry walls; species-dependent natural pair spacing coupled to a periodic corrugation; Kawasaki/identity-swap equilibration with mechanical coordinates.
- **Not the exact same specification:** these papers do not state the proposed six-site construction’s explicit pair-mismatch rule, independent NN (-J), and claimed zero-temperature positive interface-density result as an exact theorem.
- **Not acquired:** the Ar/Kr work is computational and the elastic-alloy source is theory/model. No experimental acquisition/control/measurement workflow or practical material operation is established here.
- **No novelty conclusion:** the source check only sets the closest prior boundary. Any narrower distinction requires exact Hamiltonian comparison and broader priority review.
