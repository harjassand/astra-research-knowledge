# RNase H recognition-erasure source gate

## Result

Recognition erasure by RNase-H-cleavable chimeric oligonucleotides is an established incumbent. The closest operational precedent is Duck et al.'s cycling-probe system: its primary abstract describes a DNA–RNA–DNA probe and a DNA target catalytically converting successive probes into detectable products at constant temperature ([PubMed record, PMID 2400595](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2400595/)). This establishes target reuse after probe conversion, not the proposed product-tethered passivation architecture.

For substrate specificity, Hogrefe et al. directly studied *E. coli* RNase H on DNA–RNA–DNA/DNA heteroduplexes ([JBC 1990, DOI 10.1016/S0021-9258(19)39397-4](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0021-9258(19)39397-4)). The accessible primary record reports cleavage restricted to the short RNA tract in the DNA/RNA duplex. Their chimeric substrate was cut at one site into two DNA–RNA-containing fragments; maximal turnover required at least four contiguous ribonucleotides, while the three-ribonucleotide analogue had turnover reduced by over two orders of magnitude. This is direct evidence for RNA-segment consumption inside a DNA-flanked chimera, and against assuming cleavage leaves a clean DNA product.

## What this does and does not establish

The two sources support an incumbent operation that can cleave a hybridized RNA segment while reusing the DNA target. They do **not** show the proposed DNA adapter anchor plus RNA recognition tail attached to a growing molecular product. The JBC substrate is a soluble chimera paired to a complementary DNA strand, not a tethered product; fragment identities show that RNA residues can remain on cleaved DNA-containing fragments, but do not establish which fragment would stay attached to the proposed anchor, whether that residual stub retains template affinity, or whether all recognition contacts are erased.

The accessible Duck record is its abstract, not the full article. It does not expose enzyme preparation, substrate lengths, reaction medium, cleavage-product distribution, or tethered-substrate behavior. The JBC abstract likewise does not provide a matched operating medium. Accordingly, RNase-H activity in the inherited Cabello-Garcia 1 M NaCl TAE/EDTA, 30 °C setting is **unknown**; no source acquired here tests that compatibility. EDTA in the incumbent medium is an explicit compatibility concern, not proof of failure.

The recognition-erasure operation therefore loses novelty as a general principle, while the specific completion-gated, product-anchored implementation remains unverified. The decisive missing interface is a matched substrate/product measurement showing that cleavage occurs after the required assembly event, leaves the intended DNA anchor on product, and removes enough RNA-mediated template binding in the actual operating medium. Nothing in these sources supplies that result.
