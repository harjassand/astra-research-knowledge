SPECTRAL-COAREA TRACIAL RECONSTRUCTION CHECK
2026-10-24. Bounded check of the direct construction for near-synchronous
finite tensor pure strategies. No implication from low loss to
near-synchrony is asserted.

Setup and exact mismatch identity
---------------------------------
Write |psi>=vec(S), S>=0, Tr(S^2)=1, in matched Schmidt bases. For a
question z, let (A_z^a)_a and (B_z^a)_a be complete local PVMs and put
C_z^a=(B_z^a)^T. Then C_z is also a PVM. The same-label agreement
probability is sum_a Tr(S A_z^a S C_z^a). Expanding the Hilbert-Schmidt
norm gives exactly
  delta_z = 1/2 sum_a ||A_z^a S-S C_z^a||_2^2.

There is a useful pointwise bridge from this mismatch to Bob's spectral
commutators. For any projections A,C and S>=0, set
X=S^(1/2) A S^(1/2), Y=S^(1/2) C S^(1/2). Direct expansion yields
  2||A S-S C||_2^2-||[S,C]||_2^2
    = 2||X-Y||_2^2 + 2||A S(I-A)||_2^2 >= 0.
Here Tr(X^2)=Tr(A S A S), while
Tr(A S^2)-Tr(A S A S)=||A S(I-A)||_2^2; retaining this term is
necessary for the exact identity. Thus for every z,
  g_z := sum_a ||[S,C_z^a]||_2^2 <= 4 delta_z.
This is pointwise and uses only the matching of outcome labels and the
PVM property.

One common input-independent max-ent mixture
--------------------------------------------
For t>0 let P_t=1_[t,infinity)(S), r_t=rank(P_t), and
|Phi_t>=vec(P_t)/sqrt(r_t) when r_t>0. Use shared randomness with measure
  d w(t)=2t r_t dt.
It is normalized because integral 2t r_t dt=Tr(S^2)=1. In finite
dimension P_t is piecewise constant, so this is actually a finite convex
mixture after grouping intervals with the same P_t. Each branch is
maximally entangled on P_t H. The same original full-space PVMs are legal
on each branch; equivalently their compressions P_t A_x^a P_t and
P_t B_y^b P_t are complete POVMs on the support. The mixture and
measurements are chosen independently of the input pair.

For settings x,y and outcomes a,b, set C_y^b=(B_y^b)^T. The original
outcome amplitude matrix is T_ab=A_x^a S C_y^b. The mixture's amplitude
in its t-branch, with the mixture weight absorbed, is
K_ab(t)=sqrt(2t) A_x^a P_t C_y^b. These amplitudes give the actual
probabilities by squared Hilbert-Schmidt norms.

Comparison isometry and the two error terms
-------------------------------------------
Define a mathematical isometry on matrices M with right support in
supp(S) by
  (V M)(t)=sqrt(2t) M S^+ P_t,
where S^+ is the inverse on supp(S). Indeed
  integral 2t S^+ P_t S^+ dt = P_supp(S),
so ||V M||_{L2(t;HS)}=||M||_2. This is only a comparison isometry, not a
local physical operation. For U_ab=A_x^a C_y^b S, one has
  (V U_ab)(t)=sqrt(2t) A_x^a C_y^b P_t.

First compare T_ab to U_ab. Summing over Alice's complete PVM eliminates
the A_x^a factors, giving
  sum_ab ||T_ab-U_ab||_2^2
    = sum_b ||[S,C_y^b]||_2^2 = g_y.

Second, the squared candidate-to-VU error summed over outcomes is
  h_y=integral 2t sum_b ||[P_t,C_y^b]||_2^2 dt.
In an eigenbasis of S with eigenvalues s_i, this is
  h_y=sum_{i,j,b}|(C_y^b)_{ij}|^2 |s_i^2-s_j^2|.
By Cauchy-Schwarz it is at most sqrt(g_y) times
  sqrt(sum_{i,j,b}|(C_y^b)_{ij}|^2(s_i+s_j)^2).
The latter squared sum is at most 4: the row and column sums of
sum_b |(C_y^b)_{ij}|^2 are one, and the cross term is at most one by
Cauchy-Schwarz and Tr(S^2)=1. Hence
  h_y <= 2 sqrt(g_y).

Payoff consequence and scope
----------------------------
Let pi(x,y) be any fixed pair-input law, with Bob marginal nu, and let
L(x,y,a,b) be any [0,1]-valued loss/payoff weight. The square-root
probabilities are norms of the corresponding weighted outcome vectors.
The two comparisons above, the fact that V is a common isometry, and
Minkowski give the two-sided bound
  |sqrt(E_candidate L)-sqrt(E_original L)|
       <= sqrt(g) + sqrt(2) (E_{y~nu} sqrt(g_y))^(1/2),
  g=E_{y~nu} g_y.
Using Jensen and g_y<=4 delta_y, if delta=E_{z~nu}delta_z then the
right side is at most 2 sqrt(delta)+2 delta^(1/4). Thus the candidate
tracial mixture approximates the fixed-law payoff as delta->0. Since
the question set is finite, a bound on each delta_z also gives a uniform
all-pairs table bound; an average delta alone controls arbitrary pair
laws only when the Bob marginal is the one used in that average (or with
a finite-set factor such as 1/min_z nu(z)).

No positivity or completeness defect occurs: the candidate uses actual
local PVMs, or complete compressed POVMs on each P_t support. The proof
does not physically transport the original state, preserve its residual
workspace, establish a relative-entropy bound, or show that small game
loss forces small delta. It only gives a payoff reconstruction from an
independently established near-synchronous condition.
