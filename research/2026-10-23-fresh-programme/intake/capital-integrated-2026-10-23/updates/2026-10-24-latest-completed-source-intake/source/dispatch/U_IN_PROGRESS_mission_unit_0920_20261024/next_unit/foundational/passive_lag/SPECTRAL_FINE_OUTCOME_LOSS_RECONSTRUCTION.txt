Spectral reconstruction of the actual classical fine-outcome law
================================================================

STATUS: Internally derived and checked by the parent and one bounded
algebraic challenger. These are internal checks, not external validation.
No novelty, unrestricted selection, or efficient-acquisition claim.
The old common-phase and tracial sources remain frozen and unchanged.

Governing prior. Vidick, Almost synchronous quantum correlations,
arXiv:2103.02468v3, Theorem 3.1 and Corollary 3.3, already establish a
dimension-independent finite-tensor approximation by an input-independent
ensemble of projective maximally-entangled strategies. The derivation below
uses the original fine PVMs, permits their spectral compressions to be POVMs,
and explicitly couples classical fine records in a loss-sensitive norm. It
does not establish a new priority claim or retain the original quantum
workspace. Primary: https://arxiv.org/html/2103.02468v3 .

1. Literal finite-tensor interface
---------------------------------

Use a pure shared state eta=vec(S), with S>=0 and Tr(S^2)=1. A Schmidt-basis
change and local padding give this form. Let {A_x^a} and {C_y^b} be PVMs on
the common finite Schmidt ambient space, where C_y^b=(B_y^b)^T is Bob's
matrix-side effect. Padding can be completed arbitrarily outside the state
support. The physical Bob PVM is the transpose of C in this fixed basis.

For a fixed original input-pair law mu, write

  P(x,y,a,b) = mu(x,y) || A_x^a S C_y^b ||_2^2.                (S1)

All a,b are the actual recorded outcomes of these PVMs. A bounded losing
payoff is an arbitrary prescribed ell(x,y,a,b) in [0,1]. It may depend on
the full classical fine record; it may not secretly measure a retained
quantum output that is absent from a,b.

General finite POVMs can first be dilated, using fixed local ancillas and
one common dilation for the finite family. This preserves their classical
table. It does not assert that an arbitrary supplied quantum-output
instrument is replaced by its Lüders realization without a cost.

Define the actual Schmidt-commutation certificate

  g_y = sum_b || [S,C_y^b] ||_2^2,
  g   = E_(y~mu_Y) g_y.                                     (S2)

This is a state-and-operator certificate; its value is not inferred from
an event probability or from a measured marginal of Alice's output.

2. One fixed legal pre-input spectral ensemble
----------------------------------------------

Put P_t=1_[t,infinity)(S), r_t=Tr P_t. The measure

  dnu(t)=2t r_t dt                                           (S3)

has total mass Tr S^2=1. Zero-rank intervals have zero weight. Independently
of both fresh inputs, share t from this measure, use the maximally entangled
state vec(P_t)/sqrt(r_t), and run the ORIGINAL physical PVMs. This is one
all-input strategy. Its full fine-outcome law is

  Q(x,y,a,b)=mu(x,y) int_0^infinity
                    2t || A_x^a P_t C_y^b ||_2^2 dt.          (S4)

In finite dimension the implementation is a finite ensemble. If the distinct
positive eigenvalues are s_1>...>s_m>0, set s_(m+1)=0 and P_j=1_[s_j,infty)(S).
Use branch j with probability

  pi_j=(s_j^2-s_(j+1)^2) Tr P_j.                              (S5)

These probabilities sum to one. Each branch needs a shared classical flag
and a maximally-entangled state of rank at most dim(S). One may keep the
original ambient physical spaces and original PVM instruments. There is no
online spectral measurement whose result is assumed to be known on both
sides, and no branch may depend on x or y. This is a specified replacement
resource; it is not a deterministic LOCC conversion of the supplied eta.

Its CLASSICAL table is also a tracial strategy: on P_j use the complete
POVMs P_j A_x^a P_j and P_j C_y^b P_j. Indeed, projectivity of the ambient
original effects gives

  ||A P_j C||_2^2 = Tr(P_j A P_j C)
                 = Tr((P_j A P_j)(P_j C P_j)).                (S6)

The compressed effects need not be projections. Their static table equals
the physical original-PVM table on the embedded ME support. Their compressed
Lüders instruments need not give the same quantum-output instrument.

3. A fixed comparison isometry; no local-recovery inference
----------------------------------------------------------

Let P=supp S. On the Hilbert-Schmidt subspace {M: M=MP}, define

  (V M)(t)=sqrt(2t) M S^+ P_t.                               (S7)

For the jth positive-eigenvalue column, the integrated squared multiplier is
int_0^(s_j) 2t/s_j^2 dt=1. Thus V is an isometry on this subspace. Extend it
isometrically on {M: M=M(I-P)} into an extra orthogonal Hilbert space. It acts
identically on x,y,a,b record labels. V is only a mathematical comparison
isometry; it is not asserted to factor into physical local maps.

Consider the direct sums over the original input and outcome labels

  T_xyab=sqrt(mu_xy) A_x^a S C_y^b,
  U_xyab=sqrt(mu_xy) A_x^a C_y^b S,
  R_xyab(t)=sqrt(mu_xy) sqrt(2t) A_x^a P_t C_y^b.              (S8)

T and R each have norm one by both PVM completeness relations. U also has
norm one: summing a first gives sum_b ||C_y^b S||_2^2=1. Since U=UP on its
matrix domain,

  (V U)_xyab(t)=sqrt(mu_xy) sqrt(2t) A_x^a C_y^b P_t.         (S9)

Completeness of Alice's original PVM gives the EXACT first defect

  ||T-U||^2 = E_y sum_b ||[S,C_y^b]||_2^2 = g.              (S10)

The second defect, again summing a, is exactly

  ||R-VU||^2 = h,
  h=E_y int_0^infty 2t sum_b ||[P_t,C_y^b]||_2^2 dt.        (S11)

No input-pair product assumption is used: the two calculations depend only
on the original Bob marginal mu_Y. The same isometry V serves ALL inputs.
The error statements are averaged under the declared mu: changing Bob
marginal requires its corresponding g; uniform conditional/per-pair control
needs full-support weights or the corresponding finite-set factor.

4. Exact coarea and a universal loss-aware bound
-----------------------------------------------

In an eigenbasis of S with eigenvalues s_i>=0, for every Hermitian C,

  int 2t ||[P_t,C]||_2^2 dt
       = sum_(i,j) |s_i^2-s_j^2| |C_ij|^2.                 (S12)

Apply Cauchy-Schwarz across (y,b,i,j), with the original marginal weights:

  h <= sqrt(g) sqrt(E_y sum_(b,i,j) (s_i+s_j)^2 |C_yb,ij|^2).

Each of the two squared terms contributes Tr S^2=1 after PVM completeness;
the cross term is at most 2 by Cauchy-Schwarz. Hence

  h <= 2 sqrt(g),
  ||VT-R|| <= kappa(g):=sqrt(g)+sqrt(2) g^(1/4).             (S13)

This is an all-fine-record comparison. The reverse triangle inequality on
each fixed classical block gives

  H(P,Q):=[sum_(x,y,a,b) (sqrt(P)-sqrt(Q))^2]^(1/2)
       <= kappa(g).                                        (S14)

The total variation bound TV(P,Q)<=H(P,Q) also follows. More directly, the
losing-record multiplication diag(sqrt(ell)) commutes with V and contracts
norms. If alpha=E_P ell and beta=E_Q ell, then

  |sqrt(beta)-sqrt(alpha)| <= kappa(g),
  beta <= (sqrt(alpha)+kappa(g))^2.                          (S15)

Thus a perfectly winning original strategy is replaced by a legal tracial
fine table with loss at most kappa(g)^2. More generally the error couples
to the existing loss through its square root. There is no answer-count or
input-count coefficient in S13--S15.

The same argument controls arbitrary classical postprocessings of the full
fine record by data processing. It does not prove closeness of the original
and replacement shared density operators, nor of their residual quantum
workspaces: V was not a local comparison map on those workspaces.

5. An observable same-question mirror bounds g
----------------------------------------------

Suppose Alice has an actual PVM {D_y^b} for each Bob question y. It may be
the same-setting family of a synchronous game; no fresh Bob question is
provided to Alice in an ordinary off-diagonal run. The certificate is the
separately defined same-question test

  delta=E_y [1-sum_b Tr(D_y^b S C_y^b S)].                   (S16)

PVM completeness implies

  E_y sum_b ||D_y^b S-S C_y^b||_2^2=2delta.                 (S17)

For arbitrary Hermitian D,C and positive S,

  ||[S,C]||_2^2 <= 2 ||D S-S C||_2^2.                     (S18)

For completeness, for an unordered index pair with S eigenvalues u,v and
matrix entries d,c, the two residual entries have squared sum

  |v d-u c|^2+|u d-v c|^2.

Minimizing over the complex number d gives

  [(u^2-v^2)^2/(u^2+v^2)] |c|^2
    >= (u-v)^2 |c|^2,

where the zero denominator case is zero. The corresponding commutator pair
is 2(u-v)^2|c|^2. Diagonal residual terms are nonnegative, proving S18.
Summing S18 and applying S17 gives

  g<=4delta,
  kappa(g)<=2 sqrt(delta)+2 delta^(1/4).                   (S19)

This is acquired compatibility ONLY if the actual counterfactual mirror
test is specified/calibrated on this state. A small original checked-event
relative entropy, a coarse outcome marginal, or a postselected success
probability does not supply S16.

6. The FMS synchronous completion: a literal consequence, not a repair
--------------------------------------------------------------------

Let G be the frozen FMS game with imported strict tracial gap
epsilon_tr=1-omega_tr(G)>0. Make H on common setting set Z=X disjoint-union Y,
|Z|=10. Use the original predicate on XxY, its transpose on YxX, equal-answer
consistency on the ten diagonal cells, and accept the other forty cells.
The pair law is uniform on all 100 cells. Typed answers at the same setting
are identified in the equality test.

Suppose a finite-tensor H strategy has total loss eta. Its original XxY
FMS block has loss alpha<=4eta. Its mean diagonal mismatch on Z is at most
10eta. Restrict S16 to the five Y settings, so delta<=20eta and g<=80eta.
The SAME spectral ensemble is a legal tracial G strategy. Therefore

  sqrt(epsilon_tr)
    <= 2sqrt(eta)+sqrt(80eta)+sqrt(2)(80eta)^(1/4)
    <=16 eta^(1/4),                 0<=eta<=1,

and hence

  eta >= 2^(-16) epsilon_tr^2.                              (S20)

This explicit finite-tensor/closure gap is not needed to prove a qualitative
commuting gap: exact synchrony already transfers each Alice generator to
its commuting Bob partner on the state, making the Alice state tracial.
A hypothetical perfect H strategy would therefore give a perfect tracial
G strategy, contradiction; compactness of the finite commuting correlation
set gives omega_co(H)<1. The finite spectral proof above is not silently
asserted in a type-III commuting algebra.

The completion CHANGES the original game. The frozen common-phase source
does not satisfy its extra consistency premise for free. With the actual
original FMS bases, a bridge diagonal loses

  (3-2sqrt(2)cos(theta))/18,

already positive at theta=0. The exact specified product-source H success
is h(theta)=[443+(4+2sqrt(2))cos(theta)]/450, whose maximum is below one.
Consequently that particular common-phase product-instrument source wins
all n H rounds with probability int h(theta)^n dtheta/(2pi), bounded by
max(h)^n. This is not an unrestricted repeated-value bound.

7. What this construction does and what remains missing
-------------------------------------------------------

The positive operation is an explicit one-family, pre-input spectral ME
replacement with the actual original fine outcome labels and a quantified
loss bound. It uses supplied state/measurement descriptions and can have
large preparation/calibration costs. It does not need a fresh-question
oracle, input-dependent sector selection, or an assumed tracial original
state.

The remaining unrestricted selection gate is not closed. Its selected
target need not be one fixed state with one all-input local instrument
family, and the original checked event need not acquire any actual mirror
consistency. Neither S2 nor S16 has been bounded by L/m for unrestricted
full-string strategies. Coarea supplies a useful conversion only AFTER
that real operator compatibility has been supplied or forced. More entropy
notation does not create the missing counterfactual mirror operation.
