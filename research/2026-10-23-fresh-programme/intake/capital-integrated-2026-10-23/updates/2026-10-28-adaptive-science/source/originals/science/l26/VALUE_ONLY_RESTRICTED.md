# An actual g-value implementation on the conservative stress family

This is a validation of the law-update mechanism, not a universal sampler or an advantage claim. An equally informed ordinary competitor uses g(0) to learn the mean of this known family and adds Gaussian noise, with one query.

Let the supplied family parameters kappa,eta,k be positive, kappa+eta<1, and let an unknown constant m enter the genuine conservative source

    g(x)=m+kappa x+eta sin(kx)/k,
    F(x)=m x+kappa x²/2-eta cos(kx)/k².

Its mean is m. Put a=sqrt(1-kappa²) and X=g(G)+aZ. The actual Stein residual is the R_k from CORE_THEOREM.md (11), with delta=eta. Define

    C_omega(x)=exp[kappa² omega²/(2a²)] cos(omega x/a),
    w(t)=exp[-k²(1-t²)/2].

Because P_a C_omega=cos(omega x), the exact source preimage R0 is

    R0(x)=kappa eta C_k(x)
       + integral_0^1 w(t){kappa eta C_(kt)(x)
             +eta²[C_(k(1+t))(x)+C_(k(1-t))(x)]/2}dt.   (1)

This preimage is constructed, not assumed. Draw a single uniform T in [0,1] and replace the integral in (1) by its integrand at T, obtaining Rhat0(x). It is conditionally unbiased and has deterministic magnitude bound

    |Rhat0(x)|<=b=2kappa eta E_k+eta² E_(2k),
    E_v=exp[kappa²v²/(2a²)].                           (2)

No derivative or potential value is needed to evaluate a cosine. For any v,

    cos(kv)=k{g(v+pi/(2k))-g(v-pi/(2k))-kappa pi/k}
                                                          /(2eta).  (3)

There are four cosines in Rhat0 and hence eight original g-value calls. Generate Y_G,V as in CORE_THEOREM.md and return

    g(G)+aZ-pi/(2sqrt(2)a) Rhat0(Y_G)V+tau N.           (4)

The total is nine g queries on every execution, with no stochastic loop. The uniform T is generated by the first coordinate of a three-dimensional Gaussian divided by its radius and mapped affinely to [0,1]. All unknown constant offsets cancel in (3).

For exact scalar formulas the theorem of CORE_THEOREM.md Section 8 and the actual second-moment bound (2) give

    TV(Law(output),N(m,(1+tau²)))
      <= H(tau)[A eta b+B b²]+phi(1)c² b²/tau²,        (5)

with the constants defined there. Thus for fixed kappa,k,tau this is a positive, original-value-only nonlinear update with error O(eta²), whereas its coefficient perturbation and initial Stein residual are O(eta). The entire implemented randomness, including the random residual action, is included in the final b² term. The bound may be numerically loose or vacuous away from the perturbative regime.

The exponential functions in (1)–(4) are bounded-interval scalar computations. For a literal Gaussian-and-arithmetic program, uniformly approximate them by fixed truncated Taylor polynomials; approximate pi, for example by its Machin arctangent series. For any requested additional error zeta, the degree is O_(kappa,k)(log(1/zeta)); no extra g queries are introduced. Formula (3)'s endpoint error is controlled by the known Lipschitz constant. A coupling plus the fresh tau reserve charges the resulting scalar implementation error in TV. The NumPy script uses floating exp and pi only to check the displayed formulas; it does not certify unrounded continuous-law TV.

The actual scientific boundary is visible in (2): the acquired source preimage costs exp(O(kappa² k²/a²)). The generic C^{1,1} class permits unbounded k. Consequently this finite family construction does not give a uniform residual compiler, and setting those exponential constants to one would discard the central obstruction.

For this known family g(0)=m, so the one-query ordinary Gaussian sampler is exact and strictly stronger as a sampler. The nine-query construction is retained only as an end-to-end validation of the acquired Newton step and its source-reserve cost. It is not offered as a practical or asymptotic advantage over that comparator.
