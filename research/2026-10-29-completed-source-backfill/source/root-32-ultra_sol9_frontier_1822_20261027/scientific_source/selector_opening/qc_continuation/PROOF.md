# Why transporting the known isotropic QC cone cannot resolve the even target

**QC of the newly accepted even-Hopf energies remains UNKNOWN.** This note
completes a distinct attempted QC operation: positive decomposition into the
entire known isotropic QC quartic cone, transported by arbitrary invertible
real input/output maps and then symmetrized. The operation has a rigorous
class-wide obstruction, not a failed numerical fit or an SOS-degree cutoff.

The full isotropic QC theorem receives full ordinary-comparator credit.
The theorem below is a projection/normal-form result, not a new claim that
isotropic rank-one convexity implies QC. External priority of this projection
result has not been established.

## 1. Exact symmetry projection and the class being tested

For A=[[a,b],[c,d]], put

    T=|A|^2, q=detA,
    h=(x,y,z)=(a^2+c^2-b^2-d^2,2(ab+cd),2q).

Then |h|=T. Let J be the real linear map

    J(A)=[[-b,a],[d,-c]].

It preserves T and sends h to -h. It is a composition of a left reflection
and a right rotation, hence preserves rank-one directions, PC, and QC.
Define the linear projection on homogeneous quartics

    Pi f(A)=(1/2) average_(R in SO(2)) [f(RA)+f(RJ(A))].       (1)

This is an exact finite operation on quartics. The row-rotation dependence
has only harmonics 0,+/-2,+/-4, because f is homogeneous of degree four.
Its Haar average is therefore exactly the average at angles
0, pi/4, pi/2, 3pi/4. Including J gives eight evaluations. The image of Pi
is precisely the six-parameter even-Hopf space h^TKh. In particular Pi
fixes every new target in the accepted even-Hopf family.

The fully informed known-generator attempt allows every bi-SO(2)-invariant
homogeneous ROC quartic W and all invertible real P,S, with generators

    A -> W(PAS).

These are QC by Bruno--Pasqualotto's full invariant-cone theorem and the
standard linear change of independent/dependent variables. Transposed
generators W(P A^T S) are included as well, since W(B^T)=W(B) turns them
into W(S^T A P^T). The attempted cone also allows arbitrary PC quartic
summands, nonnegative coefficients, and coefficient limits.

## 2. Projection-collapse theorem

**Theorem.** For every bi-SO(2)-invariant homogeneous ROC quartic W and
every invertible real P,S, Pi[W(PAS)] has the form

    C(A)+gamma q(A)^2,              gamma>=0,               (2)

where C is a globally convex homogeneous quartic polynomial. Thus it has
the explicit smooth convex polynomial lift C(B)+gamma s^2 and is PC.

Consequently, no left-row-SO(2)-invariant, spin-flip-even non-PC quartic
can lie in the coefficient closure of the positive cone generated by all
these transported isotropic ROC/QC quartics together with arbitrary PC
quartics.

The conclusion covers the fixed accepted

    T^2+(252/25)q^2+(99/25)qx

and the whole accepted family

    T^2+12q^2+4 beta_m qx,  beta_m=(m^2-1)/(m^2+1), m>=197.

No conclusion that these targets are or are not QC follows from this
restriction on one sufficient construction.

## 3. Absorbing every output map into the full isotropic cone

Write

    H(B)=average_(R in SO(2)) W(PRB).

It is a homogeneous quartic. Right rotational invariance follows from that
of W, and left rotational invariance follows by translating the integration
variable R. ROC is preserved by each invertible left map PR and averaging.
Therefore H is another member of the full bi-isotropic ROC cone.

The exact characterization, already established in Appendix A of
[Bruno--Pasqualotto](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.06367v1#A1), says

    H=a T^2+b Tq+c q^2,

with either a=b=0,c>=0 or

    a>0, |b|<=4a, c>=-2a+3b^2/(8a).

Equivalently,

    H=a W_t+mu q^2,
    W_t=T^2+4tTq+(6t^2-2)q^2,
    |t|<=1, mu>=0.                                      (3)

This uses the entire isotropic ROC/QC cone, including its non-PC members,
not merely a smaller DM subfamily. Since row averaging and J projection
give exactly

    Pi[W(PAS)](A)=[H(AS)+H(J(A)S)]/2,

it remains to analyze the spin average of H after an arbitrary input map.

There is also a direct quantitative check of the absorption step. After
proper rotations and absorbing a possible reflection into b, take
P=diag(p1,p2), p1,p2>0, and put

    c0=(p1^2+p2^2)/2, c1=(p1^2-p2^2)/2,
    A0=c0^2+c1^2/2, dP=p1p2.

For an input W=a0 T^2+b0 Tq+c0_iso q^2, its row-average coefficients are

    a=a0 A0,
    b=b0 c0 dP,
    c=c0_iso dP^2-2a0 c1^2.                            (4)

If a0>0 and mu0=c0_iso+2a0-3b0^2/(8a0), then the new mu in (3) is

    mu=mu0 dP^2+a0 c1^2
         +3b0^2 c1^2 dP^2/(16 a0 A0)>=0.

Also |b|/(4a)<=1, since c0 dP<=c0^2<=A0. These identities independently
show that no hidden output-map restriction enters the argument. The case
a0=b0=0 is just a nonnegative multiple of q^2.

## 4. An explicit convex normal form after any input map

If a=0 in (3), then H=cq^2 with c>=0 and its spin average is simply
c det(S)^2 q^2. This case is already of the required form. Hence assume
a>0 and use the parameter t in (3).

Diagonalize SS^T by a proper column rotation. For some equatorial unit
vector e, a scalar d0>0, and real lambda,delta,

    |AS|^2=d0[T+lambda (e dot (x,y))],
    det(AS)=d0 delta q,
    lambda^2+delta^2=1,       |lambda|<1.               (5)

The sign of delta is the orientation of S. J fixes T and reverses all
three h coordinates, so it changes the sign of the anisotropic part in
the first equation and of the determinant in the second. Let x_e=e dot
(x,y). The average of the two W_t terms in (3), divided by d0^2, is

    G=T^2+lambda^2 x_e^2+4t lambda delta q x_e
         +(6t^2-2)delta^2 q^2.                         (6)

Set

    v=(lambda e1,lambda e2,t delta),
    rho^2=|v|^2=lambda^2+t^2 delta^2<=1.

Since z=2q, direct completion gives the exact identity

    G=(1-rho^2)[T^2-z^2/2]+rho^2 T^2+(v dot h)^2.      (7)

Every term in (7) has a concrete convex explanation:

* S4(A)=T^2-z^2/2=T^2-2q^2=tr[(A^TA)^2] is convex. A self-contained
  Hessian identity is

      D2S4(A)[X,X]
         =2|A^TX+X^TA|_F^2+4|X A^T|_F^2>=0.

* Since |h|=T, the two quadratic forms rho T+v dot h and rho T-v dot h
  are nonnegative. Their squares are convex: composition of a nonnegative
  convex quadratic with the convex nondecreasing square function is convex.
  Their average is exactly rho^2 T^2+(v dot h)^2.

This includes rho=0 without dividing by zero. Thus G is a convex quartic
for every allowed t,lambda,delta. Restoring the coefficient a d0^2 and
the determinant term in (3) yields

    Pi[W(PAS)] = a d0^2 G + mu det(S)^2 q^2.

This proves (2), with its explicit convex lift.

For an implementation without eigenvectors or normalization divisions, set
D=SS^T, d0=tr(D)/2, d1=(D11-D22)/2, d2=D12, and

    v_d=(d1,d2,t detS),        r_d=|v_d|^2.

Then the same convex polynomial is exactly

    d0^2 G=(1-t^2)det(S)^2 S4+r_d T^2+(v_d dot h)^2.       (8)

All coefficients in (8) are rational for rational t,S. The sign of detS
is retained in v_d. Formula (8) also covers singular and zero S directly.
Similarly (4), with c1^2=c0^2-det(P)^2, uses only rational arithmetic on
the entries of P. If P is zero, the projected generator is zero; otherwise
a>0 when the original a0>0. The separate a0=0 case is the nonnegative
determinant-square generator. Thus all zero and singular cases can also
be handled explicitly, without relying on a limiting argument.

## 5. Why arbitrary positive mixtures and PC remainders do not escape

The projection Pi preserves PC, because every linear transformation in
(1) acts linearly on the five minors and averaging preserves convex lifts.
Let an even left-invariant target F be a positive combination of transported
isotropic generators plus a PC remainder. Applying Pi leaves F unchanged
and turns every summand into a PC energy by the theorem. Therefore F would
be PC, a contradiction for the newly accepted targets.

The same conclusion holds for coefficient limits. One can either use
closedness of the finite-dimensional PC cone, or directly apply the already
accepted finite minors-Jensen witness: it is nonnegative on every projected
summand and PC remainder and is continuous in the 35 quartic coefficients.
It cannot converge to its strictly negative value on the target.

The closure statement also permits singular coordinate maps obtained as
limits of invertible ones. Allowing arbitrarily many directions, worse
condition numbers, all isotropic boundary parameters, or polynomial degrees
in a downstream representation does not remove this particular obstruction.

## 6. Constructive replay, sources, and remaining operation

`verify_projection.py` reconstructs the row-average and spin-average
identities from literal matrix substitutions, the output-map mu identity,
and the convex S4 Hessian identity. It uses no producer imports, numerical
optimization, or SOS feasibility status. Eight rotations/reflections suffice
for the exact projection. For rational W,P,S the projected coefficients
and the polynomial lift coefficients are rational; the square root used
to explain convexity cancels from those final coefficients.

The full isotropic QC implication is prior:
[Bruno--Pasqualotto, Theorem 2 and Appendix A](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.06367v1#A1).
The source's heat correction is already an ordinary complete method for
that invariant cone. The present result explains why arbitrary linear
transport and positive symmetry combinations of that method still cannot
reach the even non-PC target.

[Guerra--Kristensen, Theorems 1.2--1.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2112.10563v1)
requires both left and right rotational isotropy for its automatic results.
The qx term fails the right invariance. Its non-PC behavior at a conformal
matrix therefore does not contradict that theorem. This reading supplies
no QC implication for the new target.

The missing operation is now precise: a proof of QC for the new target
cannot be a positive decomposition into all these transported isotropic
quartic QC generators, even with arbitrary PC remainders and limits. It
would need a genuinely anisotropic QC generator or a coupled nonlocal/
nonlinear compatibility argument that does not factor through that cone.
No compatible-gradient negative witness, new sufficient anisotropic
inequality, or QC resolution has been obtained in this continuation.
