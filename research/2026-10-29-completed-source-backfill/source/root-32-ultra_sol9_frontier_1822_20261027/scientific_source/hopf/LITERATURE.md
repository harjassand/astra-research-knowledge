# Primary-source comparison, checked 28 October 2026

The bounded source check below does not establish world priority. General
ROC/non-PC examples in two dimensions are classical. The present exact
result is the failure within the six-parameter even-Hopf polynomial
sector, with strict ROC and a full six-entry open neighborhood. The
infinite family additionally fails PC at a conformal matrix. QC remains
UNKNOWN throughout the new families.

## Aubert: the support and three-atom interfaces are prior

G. Aubert, *Quelques remarques sur les notions de 1-rang convexité et
de polyconvexité en dimensions 2 et 3*, M2AN 22(1), 1988, pp.5–28.
[Primary article and PDF](https://www.numdam.org/item/M2AN_1988__22_1_5_0/).
Downloaded full PDF to sources/Aubert1988.pdf; extracted all pages and
read the decisive printed pp.8–11 and16–18.

Theorem1.3 already characterizes planar PC by the upper/lower support
intervals obtained from positive/negative determinant increments.
The remarks following Corollary1.4 also give a three-matrix minors-Jensen
characterization. Neither the opposite-determinant principle nor the
use of three actual matrices should be claimed as new here. Section3
reproduces Aubert's earlier example: an isotropic quartic on det>0 with
negative conformal values, extended by positive infinity outside that
domain. Its formula and domain differ from the new positive polynomial
on all real matrices. The 1987 Cambridge article's public abstract was
also checked, but its gated full text was not acquired separately:
[DOI10.1017/S0308210500018382](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0308210500018382).

## Bandeira–Pedregal: quartic acoustic polarization is prior

L. Bandeira and P. Pedregal, *Finding new families of rank-one convex
polynomials*, Ann.IHP AN26, 2009, pp.1621–1634.
[Primary PDF](https://www.numdam.org/item/10.1016/j.anihpc.2008.08.002.pdf).
Read the local retained primary text's introduction, fourth-degree
reduction, and Examples1–3. Their established method swaps the two
quadratic slots in a quartic Hessian and reduces ROC to eigenvalue
optimization at rank-one states. That method is used here. Example2
contains the beta=0 determinant-square subfamily. The inspected source
does not give the nonzero-beta ROC boundary above or classify PC of
the six-parameter even Hopf sector. This is a read-scope statement.

## Guerra–Kristensen: nonnegative homogeneous examples already exist

A. Guerra and J. Kristensen, *Automatic quasiconvexity of homogeneous
isotropic rank-one convex integrands*, arXiv2112.10563v1, 20Dec2021;
related published DOI10.1007/s00205-022-01792-2.
[Primary v1 full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2112.10563v1).
Read Theorem1.3, Remarks1.4 and3.6, and Proposition3.7 with its proof.

Theorem1.3 supplies PC at conformal matrices for nonnegative p-homogeneous
ROC integrands with p>=n, requiring BOTH left and right SO(n) invariance.
Our qx term violates right rotational invariance. Proposition3.7 already
gives nonnegative p-homogeneous ROC/non-PC examples, including p=4,
using positive-part functions of two entries. Those examples are neither
polynomials nor left-row-SO(2)-invariant. Thus positivity, homogeneity,
and non-PC alone are not the novelty boundary; the even Hopf polynomial
scope and its exact certificates are the actual present claims.

## Bruno–Pasqualotto: modern DM QC comparator has stronger symmetry

G. Bruno and F. Pasqualotto, *Quasiconvexity for the Dacorogna–Marcellini
Energy*, arXiv2608.06367v1, 06Aug2026.
[Primary v1 full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.06367v1).
Read the introduction, Theorems1–2 and the invariant-cone statement.
The paper proves full-ROC-range QC of the DM family and of homogeneous
quartics invariant under BOTH left and right planar rotations. The
new qx term has only left rotational invariance, so that theorem does
not certify QC of the present energy. The classical DM family contains
the odd Hopf term Tz, while the present energies are even in h.

## Fantuzzi et al.: the earlier polynomial-selector gap is distinct

The parent task's retained source is *Polyconvexity with Moments and
Sums of Squares*, arXiv2604.11124v1, particularly Definition2.5 and
Remark2.1. The earlier accepted rational-selector example showed PC
outside a polynomial-global-selector cone. The present example fails
actual PC and therefore every support representation at one point.
It must not be described merely as failure of a finite SOS hierarchy.
