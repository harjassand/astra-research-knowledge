# The actual three-atom witness is not a bounded gradient Young measure

This is a new application of an established theorem to the accepted literal
witness. It does not decide global quasiconvexity of the energy. It rules out
using these exact three matrices and weights as a homogeneous bounded-gradient
microstructure violating quasiconvexity.

## 1. Primary theorem and its exact scope

Stefan Müller, *Rank-one convexity implies quasiconvexity on diagonal matrices*,
International Mathematics Research Notices 1999(20), 1087–1095,
[DOI](https://doi.org/10.1155/S1073792899000598).
The author's [primary preprint, printed p.3, Theorem 2](https://files-www.mis.mpg.de/mpi-typo3/preprints/1999/preprint1999_29.pdf)
states:

> Every gradient Young measure supported on diagonal 2×2 matrices is a laminate.

The definition immediately before this theorem uses sequences bounded in
W^{1,infinity}. The local source and a rendered visual check of the statement
are retained as sources/Muller1999.pdf and Muller1999_Theorem2.png. The result
is not an assertion about arbitrary unbounded sequences with concentration.

## 2. Exact diagonalization of the actual support plane

Let A, X0, X1, X2 and the three weights be exactly those in hopf/PROOF.md,
so the support matrices are Bi=A+Xi and their mean is A. In particular,

    X1 = [[0, 11/20], [-1/25, 0]],
    X2 = diag(12/5, -24/5).

The exact mean identity puts X0 in Gamma=span{X1,X2}. Its determinant form is

    det(s X1+t X2)=(11/500)s²-(288/25)t².

Set

    alpha=sqrt(11/5760)=sqrt(110)/240,
    Rplus=X1+alpha X2, Rminus=X1-alpha X2.

Both Rplus and Rminus have rank one and are linearly independent. With
b=11/20, c=-1/25 and d=12/5, define

    U = [[alpha d, -alpha d], [c,c]],
    V = [[1, b/(alpha d)], [1, -b/(alpha d)]].

Both U and V are invertible. Direct exact multiplication gives

    U^{-1} Rplus V^{-1}=E11,
    U^{-1} Rminus V^{-1}=E22,
    U^{-1} X1 V^{-1}=diag(1/2,1/2),
    U^{-1} X2 V^{-1}=diag(1/(2 alpha),-1/(2 alpha)).

Thus the affine rank-one-preserving transformation

    L(B)=U^{-1}(B-A)V^{-1}

maps all three support matrices to the diagonal plane and maps the barycenter
to zero. The exact algebra is replayed in verify_completion.py without
importing producer code.

## 3. Consequence for these atoms and weights

Suppose the actual three-atom measure mu=sum_i lambda_i delta_{Bi} were a
homogeneous gradient Young measure generated by bounded W^{1,infinity}
gradients. Translation, invertible changes of dependent variables and an
invertible linear change of the spatial coordinates preserve this property.
Therefore L#mu would be a bounded gradient Young measure supported on diagonal
2×2 matrices. Müller's theorem makes it a laminate.

Laminate Jensen inequalities are preserved by invertible affine rank-one
maps: compose any rank-one convex test function with the inverse map. Hence
mu itself would be a laminate. But the accepted globally rank-one convex
quartic F has the exact strict Jensen violation

    sum_i lambda_i F(Bi)-F(A)
       = -13534664264487807/296890944920312500 <0.

This contradicts laminate Jensen. Consequently **this mu is not a homogeneous
bounded-gradient Young measure**. No approximation sequence with uniformly
bounded gradients can realize these exact limiting atoms and weights.

Right-normalization by A0^{-1} in PHYSICAL_DOMAIN.md is another invertible
rank-one-preserving linear change, so it does not remove this exclusion.
The energy completion still has an exact finite minors-Jensen obstruction to
polyconvexity; it has acquired no compatible gradient microstructure.

This theorem does not exclude a different measure or a different spatial
construction from proving failure of quasiconvexity. Global QC of the original
quartic, aligned family and completed energies remains UNKNOWN. This is an
application of established prior science, not a new general Young-measure
theorem or an audit of Müller's proof.
