# The appropriate next operation: a complete strict-support hierarchy

This is a scoped consequence of established matrix Positivstellensatz
machinery, derived here to decide how to proceed after GAP_PROOF.md. It is
not a new general polyconvexity hierarchy or a full-PC completeness claim.

Let H be any homogeneous quartic on R4 and k any homogeneous quadratic.
Write

    P(A,X)=H(A+X)-H(A)-DH(A):X-k(A)detX.

Assume that, for some a,b>0, this GLOBAL support actually satisfies

    P(A,X)>=a|A|²|X|²+b|X|⁴                 for all A,X.    (1)

Theorem. For some finite integer r>=0, |X|^(2r) P(A,X) is a polynomial SOS.
No bound on r or efficient acquisition is asserted here. At a chosen r the
SOS search is one SDP, linear jointly in the quadratic k and the Gram.

This supports enumeration of a useful sufficient certificate class: every
quartic possessing a strict polynomial support of(1) is eventually found in
exact mathematics. It does NOT say every strict polyconvex quartic possesses
such a quadratic selector, every quasiconvex quartic is polyconvex, or every
weakly nonnegative support can use this fixed norm denominator.

## Proof

Quartic polarization makes P quadratic in A:

    P(A,X)=A^T H2(X) A+2 b3(X).A+c4(X),

where the coefficient degrees in X are2,3,4 respectively. Put

    M(X)=[[H2(X),b3(X)],[b3(X)^T,c4(X)]].

For |u|=1, homogenizing(1) in the independent affine scalar y gives
(A,y)^T M(u)(A,y)>=a|A|²+b y². This is first obtained for y!=0 by multiplying
(1), with A/y, by y², and then for y=0 by continuity. Therefore M is
uniformly positive definite on the X-unit sphere.

The matrix quadratic module generated by (1-|X|²)I and (|X|²-1)I is
Archimedean; its positivity set is exactly that sphere. The established
matrix Scherer--Hol theorem gives

    M(X)=sum_j f_j(X)^T f_j(X)+(1-|X|²)N(X),             (2)

with polynomial rows f_j and a symmetric polynomial N. The cited source
states the matrix Archimedean theorem and the sphere/equality-ideal step:
Trung Hoa Dinh, Toan Minh Ho and Cong Trinh Le,
*Some applications of Scherer-Hol's theorem for polynomial
matrices*,1904.00206v1, Theorem1.1 and Section4:
https://arxiv.org/html/1904.00206v1 . The underlying theorem is existing
Scherer--Hol2006 machinery, not an invention in this programme.

It remains to remove the sphere relation without introducing a denominator
in A. Let D=diag(-I4,1). The mixed degrees give M(-u)=D M(u) D. On the sphere,
average the SOS rows in(2) under this involution. Splitting each row into
[f(u)+f(-u)D]/2 and [f(u)-f(-u)D]/2 yields two types: its first four entries
are odd and last entry even, or vice versa. For a row of the second type,
replace it by the four rows u_i times that row. Its sum of row squares is
unchanged on the sphere, while its parity switches. We may therefore assume
all rows have odd first four entries and even last entry.

Choose an odd integer k large enough to exceed all first-four-entry degrees
and to have k+1 exceed every last-entry degree. Homogenize the first entries
to degree k and the last to degree k+1 by multiplying each monomial by the
appropriate integer power of |X|². Parity ensures every power is integral.
Let those homogeneous entries be g_j(X),h_j(X). On |u|=1 they equal the rows
just obtained, so

    sum_j [g_j(u).A+h_j(u)]²=P(A,u).

For X=s u with s=|X|>0, homogeneity now gives

    sum_j [g_j(X).A+h_j(X)]²
        =s^(2k+2) P(A/s,u)
        =s^(2k-2) P(A,X).

Both sides are polynomials and agree off X=0, so agree everywhere. This is
the claimed SOS with r=k-1. Multiplication by |X|² preserves SOS, so every
larger r is also feasible once this r is. This completes the proof.

## Implementable interface and its actual evidence

At level r, squared features are all A_i X^alpha with |alpha|=r+1 and all
X^beta with |beta|=r+2. Their number is

    nr=4*C(r+4,3)+C(r+5,3).

All Gram monomials have bidegrees(A²,X^(2r+2)),(A,X^(2r+3)),X^(2r+4).
Their count is

    mr=10*C(2r+5,3)+4*C(2r+6,3)+C(2r+7,3).

The k selector has ten unrestricted coefficients. A fixed-level coefficient
identity is linear in those ten and in a nr-by-nr PSD Gram. Levels0,1,2 use
(nr,mr)=(26,215),(60,658),(115,1485). There is no universal runtime promise.
The actual candidate from GAP_PROOF.md has an exact strict level0 separator
and a positive exact level1 Gram; the strict-support theorem explains a
sound route beyond its failed level0, rather than promising that increasing
selector degree repairs the same class.

For rational H the existence statement can be strengthened to a rational
selector and a positive-definite rational Gram at some finite level. Choose
a small positive rational delta<min(a,b), apply the proof to
P-delta*(|A|²|X|²+|X|⁴), then add back
delta*[|A|²*(|X|²)^(r+1)+(|X|²)^(r+2)]. The latter has a strictly positive
diagonal Gram on EVERY level-r feature, with ordinary multinomial
coefficients. The resulting feasible Gram is PD. Its identity is an affine
system with rational coefficients in the ten selector coefficients and Gram
entries. Any nonempty rational affine space has a rational particular point
and rational nullspace basis, so its rational points are dense. A sufficiently
near rational point retains PD. This proves existence, without an acquired
bound on its bit size. It also yields the qualitative exact semi-decision
route of enumerating levels and rational affine candidates with PD replay.

The displayed candidate separately has acquired rational coefficients and
independent exact replay. No universal degree/bit bound, efficient search,
automatic reconstruction on singular faces, or finite stopping criterion
for negative instances follows from this theorem.
