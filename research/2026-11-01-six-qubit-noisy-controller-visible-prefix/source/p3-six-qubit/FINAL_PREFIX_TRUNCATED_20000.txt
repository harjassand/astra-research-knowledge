## 1. The question I chose

I concentrated on this:

> **Can the correction dynamics preserve the logical quantum information throughout its actual recovery paths, including disturbances of the controller, while removing error entropy quickly enough to prevent accumulating failures?**

That question proved more discriminating than asking whether the device has an energy gap or eventually returns to its ground space.

The operational target was the channel

\[
\Phi_T
=
\mathcal D_{\mathrm{out}}\,
e^{T\mathcal L}\,
\mathcal E,
\]

where \(\mathcal E\) supplies encoded data and the specified controller state, \(\mathcal L\) is the continuously operating physical generator, and \(\mathcal D_{\mathrm{out}}\) is one fixed final decoder.

I measure its error by

\[
\epsilon(T)=\frac12\|\Phi_T-\mathrm{id}\|_\diamond.
\]

This controls preservation of **every unknown input qubit and its correlations with any reference**. It prevents a calculation that merely stabilizes a known state or retains the classical labels \(0\) and \(1\) from being mistaken for a quantum memory.

No ideal correction is inserted between physical steps.

## 2. What the strongest existing routes already provide

The literature comparison changed the construction early.

**The compact Ising memory is already known.** Kapit, Chalker and Simon explicitly give three primary qubits with pairwise Ising interactions, each coupled to a rapidly decaying shadow qubit. Up to local basis conventions, this is the six-qubit architecture below. Its physical mechanism cannot be claimed as a new invention. :chatgpt-content-reference{index="0"}

There are also strong general theories. Pastawski–Clemente–Cirac construct memories using engineered dissipative recovery, while Lebreuilly and collaborators give autonomous error-correction conditions and protection orders. Those results establish substantial recovery machinery; realizing their engineered operations from the requested qubit primitives remains a separate problem. :chatgpt-content-reference{index="1"}

For a noisy controller, the strongest route I found is the hierarchical cellular-automaton construction of Dünnweber–Styliaris–Trivedi. Its synchronous construction has a nonzero noise threshold. Its version-2 text explicitly describes the continuous-time threshold argument as an outline, with a detailed proof and general nonunitary Lindbladian perturbations left for future work. Its local cells and conditional channels also do not directly supply a static two-qubit-Hamiltonian, one-qubit-loss device. :chatgpt-content-reference{index="2"}

The October 2026 framework of Drechsler–Oreg–Aharonov supplies another important ingredient: it relates a class of CSS quantum memories to classical stochastic processes and proves a four-dimensional Toom-memory result for synchronous and Floquet dynamics. The general time-independent lift remains open in that work. :chatgpt-content-reference{index="3"}

These comparisons locate the opening precisely: **the missing result is a useful physical realization whose complete noisy correction histories satisfy the logical protection conditions.**

## 3. The actual six-qubit device

### Physical components

There are three data qubits \(d_1,d_2,d_3\) and three controller qubits \(a_1,a_2,a_3\).

The Hamiltonian is

\[
H=
-J\sum_{i<j}Z_iZ_j
+
4J\sum_i n_{a_i}
+
g\sum_i X_iX_{a_i}.
\]

Its terms are:

- Three data–data \(ZZ\) interactions.
- Three data–controller \(XX\) interactions.
- Three one-qubit controller energy terms.

The continuously active dissipative and noise processes are

\[
\sqrt{\kappa}\,\sigma^-_{a_i},
\qquad
\sqrt{\gamma}\,X_i,
\]

and, on every controller,

\[
\sqrt{\eta_x}\,X_{a_i},
\qquad
\sqrt{\eta_y}\,Y_{a_i},
\qquad
\sqrt{\eta_z}\,Z_{a_i}.
\]

Every constituent is a qubit. Every Hamiltonian interaction has support at most two. Every dissipator has support one.

There is no measurement, correction schedule, or classical processing during storage.

### How it corrects

The code is

\[
|0_L\rangle=|000\rangle,\qquad
|1_L\rangle=|111\rangle.
\]

A single data bit flip raises the Ising energy by \(4J\). The corresponding controller has that excitation energy, so the error can transfer coherently into it. Controller decay then removes the energy and entropy.

The two logical branches have matching correction amplitudes. The controller therefore need not learn whether the stored state was \(|0_L\rangle\), \(|1_L\rangle\), or an unknown superposition.

The noise bias matters: this corrects data bit flips. A physical data \(Z_i\) already acts as a logical phase error.

### An operating point in the full physical model

In units of \(g\), I used

\[
J=8g,\qquad \kappa=3g,
\]

\[
\gamma=\eta_x=\eta_y=\eta_z=\frac{g}{1024},
\qquad T=\frac{100}{g}.
\]

Thus each controller has **three** fault channels, each as strong as the data bit-flip channel. The controller is not assigned a quieter environment.

The reset is still a substantial declared resource:

\[
\frac{\kappa}{\gamma}=3072.
\]

The full physical generator gives

\[
\boxed{
\epsilon(T)=0.00587302023874
}
\]

and entanglement fidelity

\[
\boxed{
F_e(T)=0.99412697976126.
}
\]

An auditable floating-point forward-error analysis gives a conservative allowance of \(1.3\times10^{-7}\) on either quantity. Consequently,

\[
\boxed{\epsilon(100/g)<0.005874.}
\]

This calculation includes finite response, continuing controller faults, finite controller occupancy, and all the off-resonant terms in the Hamiltonian.

### The fair comparison

All rows use the same data-noise rate and storage time.

| Route | Decoded half-diamond error |
|---|---:|
| One bare qubit | 0.08871122 |
| Three idle encoded qubits, with the same final decoder | 0.02221278 |
| Six-qubit autonomous device with noisy controllers | **0.00587302** |
| Ideal instantaneous correction every \(1/g\) | 0.00028528 |

The autonomous device improves on **idle encoded storage with the same final decoder by about \(3.78\times\)**.

The ideal recurring correction service is substantially better. That row is retained as a strong benchmark, though it receives an additional perfect service that the autonomous device does not use.

### Why this preserves quantum information

The calculation has an exact reduction to the logical channel, not merely a population-survival statistic.

In the error basis, each physical data flip decomposes as

\[
X_i=I_L\otimes A_i+X_L\otimes B_i.
\]

The full generator preserves logical-\(X\) populations. A cross-sector matrix retaining the complete syndrome and controller dynamics determines a coherence \(z(T)\), with

\[
F_e=\frac{1+\operatorname{Re}z}{2},
\qquad
\epsilon=\frac{|1-z|}{2}.
\]

An additional symmetry makes the decoded channel a logical bit-flip channel in this specified model. These identities give the reference-preserving interpretation of the numerical result.

## 4. The general result: a theorem over complete recovery histories

The most reusable theorem concerns the competition between a real recovery process and the next fault.

Let faults of type \(f\) occur as independent, state-independent Poisson events at rates \(\lambda_f\), and let

\[
\Lambda=\sum_f\lambda_f.
\]

Suppose an isolated fault initiates a physical recovery excursion satisfying three conditions:

1. The logical qubit and its reference remain unchanged throughout the excursion.
2. A physical reset returns the device to the same clean internal state after a random time \(\tau_f\), independent of the logical input.
3. The fixed final decoder works even if storage ends before that reset.

Then

\[
\boxed{
\frac12\|\Phi_T-\mathrm{id}\|_\diamond
\le
\min\!\left\{
1,\,
T\sum_f\lambda_f
\left[1-\mathbb E e^{-\Lambda\tau_f}\right]
\right\}.
}
\]

A simpler consequence is

\[
\boxed{
\frac12\|\Phi_T-\mathrm{id}\|_\diamond
\le
\min\!\left\{
1,\,
T\Lambda\sum_f\lambda_f\mathbb E\tau_f
\right\}.
}
\]

### What makes this a rare-history guarantee

Conditional on an isolated recovery lasting \(\tau_f\), the probability that another fault arrives before recovery is

\[
1-e^{-\Lambda\tau_f}.
\]

Averaging this expression uses the **whole recovery-time distribution**, including its rare long histories. There is no assumed maximum number of faults or maximum recovery time.

The first-moment version follows only afterward, from

\[
1-e^{-x}\le x.
\]

The mean is therefore bounding a derived competing-event probability. It is not being used to stand in for an unproved tail estimate.

### Why the quantum argument is valid

Partition the unobserved Lindblad trajectories according to whether a second fault arrives during an unfinished isolated recovery.

Every admitted good trajectory, followed by the same fixed decoder, produces a scalar multiple of the identity logical channel. That includes histories still recovering at the final time.

Their sum is

\[
\mathcal G_T=(1-p_{\mathrm{bad}})\,\mathrm{id}.
\]

The remaining histories form a completely positive map with input-independent trace weight \(p_{\mathrm{bad}}\). After normalization, it is a quantum channel. The half-diamond error is therefore at most \(p_{\mathrm{bad}}\).

This decomposition is used in the proof. **The device does not observe the trajectory record or postselect on it.**

### Finite response of the physical controller block

For the resonant two-state correction block, retain controller phase noise continuously and define

\[
\alpha=\frac{\kappa}{2}+2\eta_z.
\]

With detuning \(\delta\), the exact mean reset times are

\[
\mathbb E\tau_d
=
\frac2\kappa+
\frac{\alpha^2+\delta^2}{2\alpha g^2},
\]

for a data error, and

\[
\mathbb E\tau_a=\frac2\kappa
\]

for a controller excitation, when \(g\ne0\).

At zero detuning and without phase noise,

\[
\mathbb E\tau_d
=
\frac2\kappa+\frac{\kappa}{4g^2}.
\]

This exposes a real design tradeoff: increasing reset indefinitely eventually slows correction through the quantum Zeno effect.

The report also derives the exact Laplace transforms of the reset-time distributions and uses them directly in the stronger bound. These formulas apply to the explicitly defined resonant analysis model. The useful six-qubit operating point was evaluated from the complete physical Hamiltonian separately.

## 5. The attempt to protect against every single-qubit Pauli error

I next used a nine-data-qubit Bacon–Shor subsystem code. Its gauge Hamiltonian uses only pairwise \(XX\) interactions within rows and pairwise \(ZZ\) interactions within columns.

This is a serious conventional starting point. Noncommuting two-local gauge Hamiltonians already have an error-suppression literature, and autonomous Bacon–Shor correction using optical coherent feedback also predates this construction. :chatgpt-content-reference{index="4"}

Two failures were decisive.

### Failure A: perfect cooling can corrupt the qubit

I first gave the code an idealized cold bath with every strictly downward single-Pauli spectral transition, excluding upward and zero-frequency transitions.

After one central \(X\) fault, the equal-strength nearest-neighbor model returned to the ground space with logical probabilities

\[
(p_I,p_X,p_Z,p_Y)
=
(0.3091204,\;0.6089948,\;0.0321299,\;0.0497549).
\]

The system reached its ground space with probability one, but the logical qubit was wrong with probability

\[
\boxed{69.09\%.}
\]

The calculation retains density matrices within degenerate energy shells and the logical Pauli labels.

**Ground-space recovery is not quantum-information recovery.** The cooling path itself can execute a logical error.

### Failure B: an exact resonance survives infinitely sharp filtering

The simpler nearest-neighbor gauge Hamiltonian has a Pauli symmetry that sends

\[
H_D\longmapsto-H_D.
\]

This duplicates desired ground-return frequencies elsewhere in the spectrum. A single central-\(Y\) fault can populate a state from which two perfectly resonant drainage steps implement a logical \(Y\).

For the retained example, a perfectly sharp drain network still produces approximately \(9.74\times10^{-4}\) logical failure after that one fault.

Narrowing the linewidth cannot fix an exact resonance.

Adding all pairs within each row and column—18 gauge interactions—breaks this particular symmetry. That architectural change has a concrete reason. It does not, by itself, prove that the remaining spectrum is suitable.

## 6. The strongest general ingredient: match excitation to recovery

The selective construction tunes controller qubits to return error-syndrome eigenmodes directly to the code ground state.

In a fixed correction frame, each desired return acts identically on the logical qubit. The key issue is then how long physically produced errors occupy these modes.

Define

\[
w_{i,a}
=
|\langle a|E_i|g\rangle|^2,
\qquad
W_a=\sum_j w_{j,a}.
\]

Here \(w_{i,a}\) is the probability that physical error \(E_i\) populates mode \(a\), while the matched collection of drains couples that mode with strength

\[
G_a=g\sqrt{W_a}.
\]

The exact finite-response calculation gives

\[
\mathbb E\tau_i
=
\frac2\kappa+
\frac{\kappa}{4g^2}
\sum_a\frac{w_{i,a}}{W_a}.
\]

For this nine-qubit code,

\[
\sum_a\frac{w_{i,a}}{W_a}\le16,
\]

and, summing over all 27 physical single-qubit Pauli errors,

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{i=1}^{27}\sum_a\frac{w_{i,a}}{W_a}=240.
}
\]

### Why this matters

A tiny recovery matrix element appears disastrous if one prices the entire device using the slowest mode.

But that same mode may be extremely unlikely to be populated by the physical noise. The matched construction retains both facts. Its weak population probability cancels the corresponding inverse-overlap response penalty.

The identity is elementary once formulated. Its useful contribution is identifying **which physical response quantity should be bounded**, rather than asserting mathematical novelty for the sum itself.

### What the noisy controller does in this model

In the ideal selective model, every controller operation acts as the identity on the logical factor. Even overlapping controller faults alone cannot directly change the logical information. They can heat the error syndromes, where a later data fault may become harmful.

There is an exact finite-temperature statement, without eliminating the controller dynamics. If each controller has raising/lowering ratio \(r\), the stationary data factor is proportional to

\[
|g\rangle\langle g|+rP_{\mathrm{error}}.
\]

With the stated thermal controller initialization and no data faults,

\[
\Pr(\text{data occupy an error mode at time }t)\le240r
\]

for every \(t\).

This separates **controller-induced syndrome heating** from direct logical damage. It does not yet solve simultaneous continuing data noise in the full physical implementation.

### The resource count

The complete selective construction uses:

- Nine data qubits.
- Eighteen two-qubit gauge interactions.
- **432 reset qubits.**
- Individually specified controller frequencies and couplings.

Those resources are explicit. They are not hidden inside a large local dimension.

## 7. Where the full physical admission fails

The desired selective Hamiltonian contains spectral projectors. The actual two-qubit coupling contains **all** its other matrix elements too.

I derived a complete-channel averaging bound that retains the continuing noise. It is valid, but its sufficient costs are prohibitive.

For the retained spectrum, granting a forbidden frequency separation of order \(5\times10^{-5}J\), a conservative admission to error \(0.01\) at time \(1/\Gamma\) requires approximately

\[
\frac g\Gamma=1.92\times10^7,
\qquad
\frac Jg=1.52\times10^{24}.
\]

Here \(\Gamma\) is the total Pauli-event rate per physical qubit.

**These are costs of a loose sufficient proof, not physical lower bounds.** They do not show that the device requires such parameters. They show that this argument does not earn a useful operating regime. I rejected enlarging the construction to satisfy it.

A less pessimistic finite-linewidth calculation produced a smaller candidate:

| Quantity | Retained candidate |
|---|---:|
| Data qubits | 9 |
| Reset qubits | 148 |
| Targeted spectral modes | 80 |
| Controller linewidth | \(0.01J\) |
| Mean isolated-fault logical failure | 0.9923% |
| Worst isolated-fault logical failure | 1.8636% |

Those results come from a **weak-coupling, secular model with Lorentzian tails**. They do not establish the corresponding 157-qubit physical memory under continuing data and controller noise.

The distinction is consequential: an accurate description of isolated recovery does not automatically admit a device over a long sequence of noisy recovery events.
