## Strongest available result

I constructed a **scheduled quantum architecture whose encoded protection improves systematically at fixed, nonzero physical noise and control ratios**, despite a retained, interacting, noisy entropy apparatus.

The central contribution is a **joint process certificate**. It derives bounds on simultaneous reset failures and damage to live quantum data from their actual coupled dynamics, then converts those bounds into the complete channel condition needed for fault tolerance. The controller and drains can retain syndrome history; they need not be restored to fresh states between cycles.

The strongest physical version uses **three modeled qubits per working wire, before error-correction and routing overhead**. It resets retired wires directly, includes finite switching ramps, and permits equal gate and cooling strengths.

This is a complete mathematical construction within an explicit open-system model. Its maintained finite-temperature dissipative ports and prescribed pulse schedule remain supplied physical resources. It does not yet establish an autonomous device, a competitive threshold, or an advantage over conventional fault tolerance equipped with independent local reset.

## 1. The physical construction

At each working-wire site, place three qubits:

| Component | Role | Noise retained in the model |
|---|---|---|
| \(D_i\) | Data, syndrome, routing or coherent-feedback wire | Bit and phase noise, control errors, residual contacts |
| \(B_i\) | Drain used during reset | Continuous loss, positive heating, contact errors |
| \(C_i\) | Part of a retained entropy-control network | Continuing noise and arbitrary retained correlations |

The layout is a slab of fixed thickness. Intended Hamiltonian terms act on one or two qubits. The operational graph has degree at most five; an enlarged graph allowing generic two-qubit faults within neighboring columns has degree at most fourteen.

The reset interaction is

\[
H_i(t)=
u_i(t)\bigl(\sigma^+_{D_i}\sigma^-_{B_i}+\mathrm{h.c.}\bigr)
+n_{B_i}F_i(C)+K_i.
\]

The drain and controller dynamics continue throughout operation. Every working and drain qubit experiences Pauli noise. The drain’s population dynamics therefore includes both downward and upward transitions:

\[
\left(\kappa_i+\frac{\gamma}{2}\right)\mathcal D[\sigma^-_{B_i}]
+\frac{\gamma}{2}\mathcal D[\sigma^+_{B_i}]
+\frac{\gamma}{4}\mathcal D[Z_{B_i}].
\]

The entropy-removal apparatus is consequently **heated and imperfect**, with a strictly positive upward rate.

### Direct reset changes the interface

When the correction circuit has finished using a wire, it explicitly retires it. Its exchange with the drain then:

1. Ramps up for time \(1/g\).
2. Holds for finite time \(T\).
3. Ramps down for time \(1/g\).

The retired wire’s previous state and correlations may enter the drain, controller and emitted field. That is legitimate for a reset.

Other wires remain live throughout this interval. Their unknown states and reference correlations must survive. Their actual off-state interactions have a bounded **nonzero** strength \(v\), and those interactions are included in the same evolution.

This removes the separate cooled source and transfer operation of the preceding construction:

| Resource | Direct reset | Preserved shuttle alternative |
|---|---|---|
| Modeled qubits per working wire | \(D,B,C\): three | \(D,A,B,C\): four |
| Prepared output | Retired \(D\) itself | Source \(A\), followed by transfer |
| Gate/cooling scale | \(J=g\) is allowed | Sufficient proof needs small fixed \(g/J\) |
| Transfer operation | None | Finite noisy SWAP |
| Main control requirement | Finite exchange ramps and bounded off-state residual | Continuously coupled source plus fast transfer |

The direct version needs genuinely tunable coupling. Declaring a large detuned interaction to be “off” would not establish the required bound.

Extensive heating is handled by **continuing entropy removal at fixed density**: one finite-rate drain per working wire. The construction does not rely on an initially cold buffer remaining useful indefinitely.

## 2. The central theorem protects the quantum action

Cooling alone is insufficient. For example,

\[
\mathcal T(\rho_D)
=
Z_D\rho_DZ_D\otimes |0\rangle\langle0|_{\rm source}
\]

has a perfectly cold source and a wrong data channel.

The construction therefore uses two targets within the same physical evolution:

- At a reset site: the pure output \(|0\rangle_D\).
- At a live site: the identity channel, represented mathematically by a Bell state between \(D\) and a reference.

These references are proof variables—a partial Choi representation of the channel. They are not additional physical preparations, and they do not restrict the user’s unknown input.

For every selected set of sites \(S\), the derived physical bound is

\[
\widehat{\mathcal T}_S^{\,*}
\left(\prod_{i\in S}Q_i\right)
\le p^{|S|}I,
\]

where \(Q_i\) rejects the appropriate reset or live-data target. The bound is uniform over arbitrary old reset inputs, unselected data, drains and controller states.

**The all-subset condition is essential.** Small individual error probabilities can conceal a single controller event that corrupts every source together.

### From joint defects to complete channel faults

Define a mathematical good-operation supermap at each site:

- A reset supermap replaces that output by \(|0\rangle\), retaining the actual old-input dependence in the remaining apparatus map.
- A live-data supermap bypasses the true input through the intended identity operation.

These commuting supermaps are diamond contractions. If \(\Delta_i\) denotes the difference from the corresponding good-operation supermap, then

\[
\boxed{
\left\|\Delta_S[\mathcal T]\right\|_\diamond
\le
4^{\,|S_{\rm live}|}
\bigl(2\sqrt p+2p\bigr)^{|S|}.
}
\]

The factor four per selected live qubit comes from normalized partial-Choi conversion. It depends on the marked inputs, not the total apparatus size.

This produces an **exact fault expansion of the actual physical channel**. Because the estimates are complete channel norms, successive batches compose while retaining the same correlated apparatus—even after earlier signed fault components.

No replacement map, Bell preparation or ideal correction is physically inserted between steps.

The direct-reset architecture also removes an earlier counting penalty. Each working wire receives one ordinary operation, either `RESET` or `IDLE`. There is no separate live idle and prepared source that must be charged as two locations.

## 3. Finite response is derived from the physical generator

For the reset pair \(D,B\), use

\[
V=d\Gamma
\begin{pmatrix}
3/2&-i/2\\
i/2&1/2
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
N=n_D+n_B.
\]

Here \(d\Gamma\) is the explicit two-spin quadratic operator. The calculation includes the doubly excited state.

At nominal exchange \(g\) and loss \(4g\),

\[
\mathcal L_0^*V=-gN.
\]

The witness obeys

\[
V\ge n_D,\qquad N\le4V,\qquad V\le2N.
\]

Allowing 10% exchange and loss calibration errors, controller fields of norm up to \(g/5\), continuing heating and generic source-contact errors of norm at most \(\epsilon g\), the derived inequality is

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal L^*V
\le
-\frac g7 V+
\left(\frac98\gamma+28\epsilon^2g\right)I,
\qquad
\epsilon\le\frac1{224}.
}
\]

The generic contact errors need not conserve excitation or lie in an exact syndrome algebra.

The controller may have noncommuting interactions and retained correlations. The proof does not assume that it mixes or forgets. Each local inequality is uniform over its state; products of local witnesses then obey a cooperative hierarchy.

### Finite ramps

During switching, \(N\) is the useful observable. It commutes with any intended exchange envelope and with the intended drain-controller interaction. Including generic defects,

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal L_{\rm ramp}^*N
\le
2gN+\left(\gamma+2\epsilon^2g\right)I.
}
\]

A ramp of duration \(1/g\) therefore incurs an explicit finite response factor and noise addition. It requires neither an exactly empty drain nor instantaneous release.

Time-dependent source witnesses and live-data Bell witnesses connect ramp-up, hold and ramp-down. Generic cross-column faults enter through their **incident generator norm per column**, rather than an extensive whole-apparatus norm.

## 6. Comparisons, failures and unresolved work

### What the construction adds

Conventional fault tolerance with independently available local noisy reset already supports fixed-ratio scaling. It remains the simpler route when that service is physically available.

Retained bath memory is also compatible with established non-Markovian threshold results under their specified conditions. Terhal–Burkard, for example, permit long bath memory while explicitly supplying fresh exact ancillas. 

The service constructed here is a **uniform joint channel guarantee for a retained entropy network**, including strong intended source/controller coupling, finite response, generic weak contacts and continuing live-data damage. Its value is that this service is derived from the specified apparatus rather than assumed as a fresh-ancilla interface.

This crosses the mathematical boundary from a finite protected cell to a fixed-ratio family within the declared model. It does **not** yet demonstrate a capability beyond strong conventional fault tolerance equipped with suitable local reset, or establish historical novelty.

### Substantive failures preserved

1. **Cold-capacity scaling fails under extensive heating.** More initially cold buffer spins do not supply indefinite entropy removal.
2. **Marginal coldness is insufficient.** A common event can corrupt every source while each marginal error probability remains small.
3. **Cold outputs do not certify logical preservation.** A source can be cold while the data undergoes a logical \(Z\).
4. **Data and source contact faults cannot generally be multiplied as independent events.** One physical contact can damage both at first order.
5. **Finite amplitude damping is not exact renewal.** Loss-only amplitude damping has no positive exact pure-reset component at finite time.
6. **A detuned large interaction is not automatically a small physical contact.** That reduction requires its own complete dynamical argument.

### What remains unresolved

The current arXiv:2601.20818v3 construction has stronger autonomous discrete-control scope. Its continuous-time section still leaves a full detailed proof and broader perturbations open; the present result does not establish that transfer. :chatgpt-content-reference{index="3"}

Two physical services remain essential assumptions:

- **Maintained finite-temperature Markov drains.** Their noisy relay dynamics and composition are constructed here. Their ultimate realization by a finite Hamiltonian reservoir, including arbitrary returns, is not.
- **The bounded-error pulse program and actuators.** Finite ramps, local timing errors, pulse errors and off-state residuals are charged. A robust autonomous clock and arbitrary array-wide program failures are not covered.

Additional actuator modes or returning fields must be included in the same operational model. The “three-qubit” description is not permission to hide a larger quantum controller or reservoir.
