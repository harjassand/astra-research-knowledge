\section*{Result and scope}

This investigation constructs a finite noisy acquisition method for a whole
family of passive responses, derives the asymptotically necessary number of
controls that must be activated together, realizes the obstruction with ordinary
resistor networks, and implements a retained-record decision certificate. The
goal is to make experiments produce reusable knowledge for later interventions,
including combinations absent from calibration.

The strongest result is mathematical. It closes a specific finite-noise gap in
the pinned Astra determinant representation. The generic experimental budget
can be enormous, the numerical demonstration has three controls, and historical
priority and a practical advantage over the best equally informed methods are
unestablished. The new contribution is a source-specific acquisition and decision
guarantee. Rational fitting, linear programming, network energy reduction, and
convex optimization are established methods and are credited below.

All prior Astra premises are bound to repository
\texttt{harjassand/astra-research-knowledge}, revision
\begin{center}\small\texttt{51ce47ce39f976517d60e2f87bbe280b651f6056}.\end{center}
The complete ordinary proofs, source copies, runnable programs, exact
certificates, comparison notes and informative failures accompany this report.

\section{The consequential question}

An unknown passive system has many possible interventions. Can a finite bank of
imperfect scalar experiments support reliable choices after prices, budgets or
available actuators change? A complete microscopic model is one possible route.
The present route acquires the response law directly, retaining uncertainty in
every model consistent with the observations.

The scientific difficulty is operational. A control with zero individual effect
can matter in a combination. Small signals can make direct internal-parameter
identification unstable. A numerical fit at measured settings does not alone
bound an untested combination, and accurate prediction does not automatically
supply an efficient optimizer. All three issues are addressed, under a specific
fixed-source contract.

Alternative openings were screened before concentrating here. Reversible
rare-event lifetime prediction has strong existing potential-theoretic and
spectral methods. Broad no-reset quantum claims already have strong subsystem
and correlated-noise constructions, including relevant Astra premises. The
selected response problem had a concrete missing bridge: N1152 already gave a
positive determinant representation and proposed a linear-programming outer
family, but left finite noisy convergence unproved. No prior result is reopened
or downgraded by this choice.

\section{Source and physical operation}

There are $n$ finite controls. The same fixed source returns gain
\begin{equation}
 F(u)=g^\top P(I+PBP)^{-1}Pg,\quad P=\operatorname{diag}(\sqrt u),\quad
 B\succeq0,
 \label{eq:source}
\end{equation}
for $u_i\ge0$, $\sum_i u_i\le\delta$. Warranted bounds are
$|g_i|\le G$ and $B_{ii}\le\kappa$. The learner receives neither $B$ nor $g$.
Put $L=\delta G^2$, $q=\delta\kappa$, $x=u/\delta$ and
$f(x)=F(\delta x)/L$ on $\X=\{x\ge0:\sum x_i\le1\}$. Then $0\le f\le1$.
Zero $L$ is trivial. Singular $B$ and exactly dark $g_i=0$ are permitted.

This contract has a literal passive-network supplier. Clamp two terminals of a
resistor network at unit and zero voltage. Complete the unknown baseline
Dirichlet energy around its equilibrium voltages. Adding nonnegative conductance
$u_i$ across actuator edge $i$ gives terminal conductance
\begin{equation}
 J(u)=c_0+\min_z\left\{\norm z^2+\sum_i u_i(g_i+b_i^\top z)^2\right\},
 \qquad B_{ij}=b_i^\top b_j.
 \label{eq:energy}
\end{equation}
At unit voltage, terminal current equals the numerical value of $J(u)$.
Eliminating $z$ gives \eqref{eq:source}. The maximum principle supplies $G=1$.
A known baseline parallel conductance at least $a>0$ across each controlled
edge supplies $B_{ii}\le1/a$, hence $q\le\delta/a$, without reconstructing the
hidden network. Those baseline elements have wiring, power and size costs.

For gain tasks, $F(u)=R(u)-R(0)$ for the actual scalar report $R$; separate
unknown offsets need not be identified. The noisy zero reading and its shared
error remain in the retained record. The source class, fixedness, available
positive doses, calibration, settling and error bounds are premises. A finite
fit does not certify them. General chemistry, changing devices, new controls
and retained dynamical memory are outside this theorem.

\section{Finite noisy acquisition of the entire law}

The inherited N1152 determinant identity is
\begin{equation}
 f(x)=\frac{N(x)}{D(x)},\quad
 D(x)=\det(I+C\operatorname{diag}x),\quad
 N(x)=\det(I+(C+hh^\top)\operatorname{diag}x)-D(x),
\end{equation}
where $C=\delta B$ and $h=g/G$. Both polynomials are square-free with
nonnegative coefficients. For $s=|S|\ge1$,
\begin{equation}
 d_\varnothing=1,\quad b_\varnothing=0,\qquad
 0\le d_S\le q^s,\qquad 0\le b_S\le s^2q^{s-1}.
 \label{eq:boxes}
\end{equation}
At $q=0$, the first-degree numerator bound is one and all higher terms vanish.

Choose a retained degree $r\ge1$ and let $\rho=\min(r,n)$. The inherited
one-sided truncation bound is
\begin{equation}
 0\le f-\frac{N_r}{D_r}\le e_r,\quad
 A_r=\sum_{j=0}^{\rho}\frac{q^j}{j!},\quad
 e_r=\begin{cases}0,&\rho=n,\\q^\rho/(\rho! A_r),&\rho<n.\end{cases}
 \label{eq:tail}
\end{equation}
The same truncated coefficients work for the entire simplex.

Independently choose a lattice degree $k\ge1$. Acquire intervals
$f(x_\alpha)\in[y_\alpha-\xi_\alpha,y_\alpha+\xi_\alpha]$,
$\xi_\alpha\le\xi$, at
\begin{equation}
 x_\alpha=(\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_n)/k,\quad
 \alpha_i\in\mathbb N_0,\quad\sum_{i=0}^n\alpha_i=k.
\end{equation}
There are $M=\binom{n+k}{k}$ settings; each activates at most $k$ controls.
The interval errors may be correlated. A single simultaneous coverage event
is required.

Form a rational coefficient polytope $\Pset$ from \eqref{eq:boxes}, retaining
degrees at most $\rho$, and impose
\begin{equation}
 (y_\alpha-\xi_\alpha-e_\alpha)D(x_\alpha)
 \le N(x_\alpha)
 \le(y_\alpha+\xi_\alpha)D(x_\alpha).
 \label{eq:LP}
\end{equation}
Here $e_\alpha=e_r$ is valid, or use the sharper actual-dose tail in the full
proof. All constraints are linear in the coefficients. The true truncated pair
is feasible. Every fitted denominator is at least one. A feasible pair need
not itself be physically realizable or concave; that relaxation is explicit.

Define the rational finite cross-tail and the simplex norming constant
\begin{align}
 B_{r,k}(q)&=\sum_{\substack{1\le a\le\rho,\ 0\le b\le\rho\\a+b>k}}
 \frac{a q^{a+b-1}}{(a-1)!b!},
 & C_k&=\binom{2k-1}{k}.
\end{align}

\begin{theorem}[Uniform finite-noise acquisition]
On the simultaneous node-coverage event, every pair of feasible responses
satisfies
\begin{equation}
 \sup_{x\in\X}\left|\frac ND-\frac{N'}{D'}\right|
 \le \Delta_{r,k}:=C_k A_r^2(2\xi+e_r)+(C_k+1)B_{r,k}.
 \label{eq:main}
\end{equation}
Consequently
\begin{equation}
 f(x)\in\left[\min_{\Pset}\frac{N(x)}{D(x)},\
 \max_{\Pset}\frac{N(x)}{D(x)}+e_x\right]\cap[0,1]
 \label{eq:service}
\end{equation}
has width at most $\min(1,\Delta_{r,k}+e_r)$, simultaneously for all future
commands, including adaptively chosen commands.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For two feasible pairs let $H=ND'-N'D$. At each lattice node, both ratios
belong to the same interval of width at most $2\xi+e_r$. The coefficient boxes
and the elementary-symmetric bound $\sum_{|S|=j}x_S\le1/j!$ give
$1\le D,D'\le A_r$. Thus $|H(x_\alpha)|\le A_r^2(2\xi+e_r)$.

The positive degree-$a$ numerator and degree-$b$ denominator terms obey
$N_a(x)\le a q^{a-1}/(a-1)!$ and $D_b(x)\le q^b/b!$. Each positive product
$ND'$ and $N'D$ therefore has total-degree-$>k$ tail in $[0,B_{r,k}]$.
Their signed difference has absolute tail at most $B_{r,k}$, with no extra
factor two.

Write $H=H_{\le k}+H_{>k}$. The inherited degree-$k$ simplex interpolation
formula has cardinal functions
$L_\alpha(x)=\prod_{i=0}^n\binom{k\lambda_i(x)}{\alpha_i}$, with
$\lambda_0=1-\sum x_i$ and $\lambda_i=x_i$. Absolute falling factorials are
bounded by rising factorials. Their generating functions show
$\sum_\alpha|L_\alpha(x)|\le C_k$, independently of $n$. Hence
\[
 |H_{\le k}(x)|\le C_k\{A_r^2(2\xi+e_r)+B_{r,k}\}.
\]
Add the tail and divide by $DD'\ge1$. The true truncated pair is feasible, and
\eqref{eq:tail} adds one one-sided remainder to the physical interval.
\end{proof}

Every concrete quantity here is rational for rational specifications. The
interval extrema are ordinary linear-fractional programs, transformed to LPs
by Charnes--Cooper. Small individual coefficients are retained as uncertainty;
they are not recovered by unstable division.

\section{A sharp law for simultaneous control order}

Taking $r=k<n$ gives
\begin{equation}
 B_{k,k}(q)\le\sum_{j\ge k}\frac{(2q)^j}{j!}
       +q\sum_{j\ge k-1}\frac{(2q)^j}{j!}.
\end{equation}
For fixed $q>0$, factorial decay defeats $C_k<4^k$. A target normalized width
$\tau\to0$ is attained with
\begin{equation}
 k=(1+o(1))\frac{\log(1/\tau)}{\log\log(1/\tau)},
 \qquad \xi=\tau^{1+o(1)}.
 \label{eq:order}
\end{equation}
For fixed $n$, the full-degree alternative $r=n,k=2n$ eventually suffices
without a truncation tail. The order in \eqref{eq:order} concerns the largest
number of controls activated simultaneously, not the total number of settings.

\subsection*{A matching obstruction built from ordinary resistors}

Use $n+1$ hidden nodes indexed $0,\ldots,n$, $n\ge2$. Each is anchored to the
two boundary rails with total conductance $s=2/\kappa$. Its baseline voltage is
$a_j$, implemented by conductances $sa_j$ to the high rail and $s(1-a_j)$ to
the low rail. Choose
\[
 a_0=0,\quad a_1=\cdots=a_{n-1}=1/2,
 \qquad a_n=0\ \text{in A},\quad a_n=1\ \text{in B}.
\]
Actuator $i$ adds conductance between adjacent hidden nodes $i-1,i$.
All conductances are nonnegative. Both baseline currents are
$c_0=(n-1)/(2\kappa)$, and the grounded baseline matrix is $sI$.

If any actuator is off, its missing edge separates the final endpoint from the
first. Reflect the voltages of the final component through $v\mapsto1-v$.
The middle-node anchors are symmetric, while the final anchor switches rails.
Dissipation is unchanged. Thus \emph{every proper-support terminal-current
experiment agrees exactly}, at every amplitude, including adaptive experiments.

For equal full dose $t=\delta/n$, the reduced $B$ has diagonal $\kappa$ and
neighbor entries $-\kappa/2$. The endpoint source entries are
$(1/2,\ldots,-1/2)$ or $(1/2,\ldots,1/2)$. A tridiagonal cofactor gives
\begin{equation}
 \Delta_n=J_B(t\mathbf1)-J_A(t\mathbf1)
  =\frac{(\kappa/2)^{n-1}t^n}{D_n}
  \ge\frac{4L}{n}e^{-q}\left(\frac{q}{2n}\right)^{n-1},
 \quad L=\delta/4,
 \label{eq:lower}
\end{equation}
because $D_n=\det(I+tB)\le e^q$.

\begin{theorem}[Necessary interaction order]
For fixed $q,L>0$, any method that uniformly predicts every admitted source to
error $\eps$, in every control dimension, with success probability greater than
$1/2$, requires maximal simultaneous support
\[
 k\ge(1-o(1))\frac{\log(L/\eps)}{\log\log(L/\eps)}.
\]
This already holds for the resistor networks above.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Use $n=k+1$. All permitted transcripts have the same law for A and B. If
$\Delta_n>2\eps$, the two sets of successful predictors are disjoint, so both
cannot have success probability greater than $1/2$. The negative logarithm of
the lower bound in \eqref{eq:lower} is $n\log n+O_q(n)$, yielding the result.
\end{proof}

The abstract dark-path construction is inherited from Astra. The quantified
accuracy consequence, its matching to the acquired upper bound, and the literal
two-rail realization are developments of this investigation. The lower bound
does not prove that $\binom{n+k}{k}$ total settings are necessary. A dense
background counts as part of an experiment's actual support. Internal voltage
probes or extra forcings would change the information interface.

\section{Turning the record into an actual decision}

Equation \eqref{eq:energy} makes the true response concave. It does not make
every relaxed rational fit concave. The consumer must preserve this distinction.
The full proof supplies two routes.

\textbf{Uniform-value route.} A single retained $\widehat f$ with
$\norm{\widehat f-f}_\infty\le\zeta$ supports any later objective
$f(x)-c^\top x$ over any nonempty compact convex $K\subseteq\X$ with a paid
linear optimization oracle. The generic source has directional curvature
$\mathcal C=8q(1+\sqrt q)^2$. Finite differences toward the $n+1$ simplex
vertices give an approximate Frank--Wolfe direction with loss
$\nu=\mathcal C h/2+4\zeta/h$. After $T$ updates,
\[
 \mathrm{regret}\le\nu+\frac{2\mathcal C}{T+2}.
\]
Choosing $h=\min(1,\eps/(2\mathcal C))$,
$\zeta\le\eps h/16$, $T\ge\max(1,\lceil4\mathcal C/\eps\rceil)$ gives
regret at most $\eps$. The cost is at most $(n+2)T$ offline evaluations and
$T$ linear-oracle calls, with no new source measurements. The $q=0$ affine
case uses one linear optimization. Numerical errors must be paid explicitly.

\textbf{Sharper task certificates at full retained degree.} When $r=n$, a
proposed action $x$ has LP-certified value interval $[f_-,f_+]$. Since
$D_i(x)\ge0$,
\begin{equation}
 \frac{N_i-f_+D_i}{D}\le\partial_i f(x)
                     \le\frac{N_i-f_-D_i}{D}.
\end{equation}
Extremizing these linear-fractional quantities over the same coefficient
polytope gives $g_-\le\nabla f(x)\le g_+$. True concavity and nonnegative
coordinates imply
\begin{equation}
 \mathrm{regret}(x)\le
 \max_{y\in K}(g_+-c)^\top y-(g_--c)^\top x.
 \label{eq:regret}
\end{equation}
An ordinary nonlinear optimizer may propose $x$. Its output is accepted by
\eqref{eq:regret}, even if its surrogate is nonconcave or its solve is local.

Floating LP duals are proposals only. For transformed constraints $Az\le0$,
$e^\top z=1$, $0\le z_j\le U_j$, any rational $\lambda\ge0,\nu$ proves
\[
 c^\top z\le\nu+\sum_j U_j\max\{c_j-(A^\top\lambda)_j-\nu e_j,0\}.
\]
Every reported bound is checked with exact fractions. The independent checker
needs only the scalar records, public bounds and saved duals.

\section{Executed example and informative failure}

The main demonstration emulates the four-node network above with the last node
attached to the high rail, $\kappa=4$, $\delta=1$, $G=1/2$, $L=1/4$.
Its normalized model has $q=4$, $h=(1,0,1)$ and tridiagonal $C$ with diagonal
4 and neighbor entries $-2$.

168 nonzero setting writes, 84 resets and corresponding clearing are charged.
The assumed calibrated raw-current radius is $1/8{,}000{,}000$ per setting.
The shared baseline subtraction yields normalized radius $10^{-6}$. The

\section{Costs and the strongest conventional alternatives}

The settings count is $\binom{n+k}{k}$ and coefficient storage is
$2\sum_{j=1}^{\rho}\binom nj$. Exact rational LP has polynomial cost in this
explicit input size and bit length; that statement does not make the input
small. Every setting, clearing, settling interval, repeated read, calibration,
stored coefficient and future optimization call is part of the bill.

The sufficient generic bounds are already severe at moderate effective dose.
For normalized width $0.01$, the exact conservative construction with $r=k$
gives the following budgets; these are not physical lower bounds.
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{rrr}
\toprule
$q$ & Sufficient $k$ & Sufficient per-node radius\\
\midrule
0.1 & 5 & $1.62\times10^{-5}$\\
1 & 25 & $5.35\times10^{-18}$\\
3 & 68 & $2.08\times10^{-45}$\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
At $n=1000,q=1,k=25$, the setting count is approximately $8.90\times10^{49}$.
The actual LP envelopes can be much narrower, as the task example shows. A
scalable large-control acquisition advantage has not been earned.

With a bounded conditionally unbiased sensor of range width $W$, a sufficient
repeat count per raw setting is
$2W^2\log(2M/\alpha)/(L^2\xi^2)$, including baseline subtraction. This
requires an appropriate fresh-trial or martingale model. Fixed calibration
bias, drift and settling error do not disappear under averaging. A scalar
two-point family already gives a worst-case statistical lower bound of order
$\eps^{-2}\log(1/\alpha)$; no such lower bound is claimed for a noiseless
or calibrated analog interface.

\textbf{Rational fitting is established.} Multivariate interval-rational
approximation was developed by Salazar Celis, Cuyt and Verdonk [4]. Cuyt and
Yang [5] already derived rational error formulas through polynomial
interpolation of a denominator-multiplied discrepancy. Modern positive
denominator fits [6] are strong ordinary alternatives. Neither multiplying
denominators nor solving a rational LP is a new general algorithm here.

\textbf{Known-model network optimization is established.} Effective-resistance
design and related network allocation problems have strong convex methods [7].
For one task, direct noisy convex optimization may require far less calibration
than acquiring an entire law. If internal probes or a well-conditioned
parametric source model are available, identification can also win. Giving
those extra observations to only one comparator would change the problem.

The defensible increment is the explicit uniform finite-noise guarantee for
this positive determinant class, including dark and nearly dark controls,
its matching simultaneous-support law, and the complete retained-record
decision construction. An ordinary rational fitting or design method can adopt
these ingredients. No exclusive computational advantage is implied by the name
of the learned object.

\section*{Scientific conclusion}

There is a sharp relationship between required precision and how many controls
must be calibrated together, even in simple passive networks. The retained
response can be acquired with finite noisy data and used for later certified
decisions without identifying weak internal couplings. This is a completed
foundational result at the declared interface. The unresolved consequential
step is reducing total experimental and precision costs on large, physically
qualified systems; the present work does not turn that remaining burden into
an assumed service or a civilization-changing claim.
