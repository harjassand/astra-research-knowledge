Result: predicting a nonlinear network without following its fast reactions

I derived and implemented a way to estimate stationary observables and control responses in a class of interacting stochastic networks, with computational work that does not grow with the population or the magnitudes of the fast interaction rates.

The strongest concrete case is a binary activation network with spontaneous activation rate 
ℎ
=
3
h=3, decay rate 
𝛿
=
1
δ=1, and cooperative activation involving pairs of parents. Ordinary transmission and cooperative activation can have arbitrarily large finite rates. For a local query, the construction needs at most 4.5 computational events per unbiased sample in expectation. It also has an explicit bound on the work needed to inspect and update its representation.

That is a sample-cost bound, not the cost of an arbitrarily accurate answer. Accuracy still requires repeated samples, and reading local rules, compiling gates, and processing rate encodings remain charged.

The central principle is:

A bound on the correlations that the physical dynamics can sustain can make their stationary equations computationally contractive, even when the underlying interactions are arbitrarily fast.

Here that correlation bound is derived from the model’s activation and decay dynamics. A stationary distribution, compressed state, favorable sign problem, or known response coefficient is not supplied.

The complete construction, proofs, implementations, numerical comparisons, and retained failures are in:

The broader objective remains open for weakly seeded collective transitions, general inhibitory dynamics, and relative prediction of rare events. The work below earns a concrete regime in which extensive nonlinear interactions cease to impose a fast-event simulation bill.

Following every reaction throughout the network can be an expensive route to that answer. Replacing the network by a small effective model might help, but then the difficult operation is acquiring and justifying the reduction.

The genuinely different challenger was to work backward from the requested observable, retaining only its relevant ancestors. Cooperative interactions make those ancestors branch into Boolean alternatives. That led to a positive construction with an explicit storage-cost bound—but its sufficient condition deteriorated as cooperation became stronger.

The surviving construction emerged by changing the normalization of those backward equations. Instead of bounding every intermediate observable pointwise, I bounded its expectation under the laws the physical dynamics can actually produce.

That distinction is what allows strong cooperation to survive the analysis.

9. What existing research already supplies—and what this derivation adds

The generic ingredients have strong precedents.

Latz and Swart explicitly treat cooperative activation and backward Boolean-function evolution. Their antichain representation already supplies the positive certificate framework. I used it as a serious challenger, rather than treating backward ancestry as a new invention. 
arxiv.org

Völlering gives a general signed stochastic representation for generators with negative coefficients, including the Feynman–Kac compensation. Converting negative potential into killing is established machinery. 
arxiv.org

Głuchowski and Menz develop product-basis coefficient criteria for ergodicity and covariance decay. Their continuous-time theorem requires bounded single-site basis values. The present normalized vacancy basis exceeds that pointwise bound, so I supplied a separate proof that its moments are bounded on a physically acquired class of laws, together with a finite entry time from arbitrary starts. I have not proved that every alternative application of their framework fails to recover this regime. 
arxiv.org

Modern sparse linear solvers also already achieve dimension-independent operations when given suitable matrix-access and contraction conditions. Weare and Webber’s analysis makes those conditions explicit. An informed conventional solver could use the representation constructed here, and may improve on the elementary sampling implementation. 
arxiv.org

Astra supplied a closely relevant warning. At revision db0969a8586afaa38137a4371b3457c5576fadea, the 
N807 governing proof already gives bounded nonlinear correction samples, conditional on a certified predictor and global enclosure. Its acquisition limitation remains explicit. That pushed this construction toward obtaining the useful bound directly from the physical rules.

The candidate contribution is therefore specific:

Acquire a locally evaluable, contracting correlation representation directly from activation/decay dynamics, and use it to obtain prediction and response costs independent of arbitrarily large birth-interaction rates.

Historical priority for that complete consequence is not established. Nor has a wall-clock advantage over the strongest suitably informed conventional solver been established. The acquired representation is the main result; generic signed Monte Carlo is not.

10. The decisive unresolved obstruction

The construction works because physical activation and decay supply a correlation envelope strong enough to absorb the absolute coefficients created by nonlinear interactions.

That operation is earned in the stated regime. It is still missing in the regimes most likely to contain difficult collective phenomena.

Three boundaries matter most.

Weak seeding and collective transitions. When the computable continuation bound reaches one, the present cost guarantee fails. That could reflect a poor representation, actual slow dynamics, or multiple infinite-system stationary laws. This work does not distinguish all those possibilities or cross the general transition barrier.

Inhibition and nonmonotone control. General inhibitory rules can leave a constant term in the restricted vacancy polynomial. The weighted coefficient mass may then refuse to fall below one. The preserved unweighted signed construction handles some such gates at limited rates, but it does not provide the arbitrary-strength consequence.

Rare-event relative accuracy. Bounded additive variance does not make an exponentially rare probability cheap to resolve relatively. The retained birth–death counterexample shows how a small local-rate error can induce an exponentially large probability ratio. Nothing here supplies a general rare-lifetime oracle.

The next substantive opening is to acquire a stronger correlation envelope—one that retains enough interacting structure to handle weak fields or inhibition—while keeping its own construction and evaluation affordable. Assuming such an envelope would simply relocate the original many-body problem.

What survives now is a complete theoretical and executable capability: local stationary prediction, late-time prediction from arbitrary starts, and control response for an extensive nonlinear class, with the fast-event cost removed and the remaining computational costs exposed.
