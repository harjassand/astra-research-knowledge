## 3. Record-preserving service twirl
Expand each complete-port Kraus operator as A_lambda=sum_P P tensor B_lambda,P, with B_lambda,P=Tr_L[(P† tensor I)A_lambda]/k. The M' factor includes all allowed classical records. Averaging a genuinely fresh uniform conjugating Pauli pad and permanently discarding the key removes cross terms, giving sum_P Ad_P tensor B_P on arbitrary joint inputs. This is a superoperator identity, not a product-state approximation. The aggregate nonidentity effect equals the Bell-loss effect from the same port.

Each retained data-dependent record may be refined into its own instrument branch before this expansion. Pad averaging then still yields Pauli-on-data tensor CP-memory branches with that record retained. Future control conditioned on those records is memory-side processing. There is no need to erase the records or assume their probabilities were originally independent of the payload. Conditioning on a retained pad key is entirely different and invalidates the identity. Keys must remain excluded from all assessed final outputs and future source/controller access, including late disclosure.

## 5. Privacy, clock and resource obligations
The same complete port must be exposed to the source on both roles. Current role, test setting and pad must not leak through pulses, routing, timing, abort selection, or decoder interactions. Preparation can reveal old permitted history before the fresh coin. If the bath remains coupled during decoder work or waiting, that coupling belongs to the same complete port or requires a separately paid error bound; it cannot be omitted because it is not called a source query.
