# Bounded scalar and fixed4×4 algebraic arithmetic
2026-10-29. Supporting implementation contract for BOX_MULLER_FINITE_COMPILER_DRAFT.md; theoretical algorithm, not an executed interval library.

Let B denote the midpoint-input bit length plus requested accuracy bits. All operations below have an a priori polynomial(B) number of rational bit operations; the matrix dimension is fixed4.

1. Logarithm. Write each positive rational midpoint u=2^j v with1<=v<2 by integer comparisons (|j|=O(B)). Evaluate log v using2*sum_{l>=0} z^(2l+1)/(2l+1), z=(v-1)/(v+1) in[0,1/3]. The tail is bounded geometrically. Evaluate log2 by the same series at z=1/3. O(B+log B) terms give the requested absolute error after multiplication by j. Using the unscaled series near u=0 would be exponentially slow and is NOT the algorithm.
2. Square root. Rational interval bisection on the nonnegative log interval gives a rigorous enclosure. The derivative bound is not needed for bisection; choose output interval directly. The public midpoint range bounds all magnitudes by polynomial(B).
3. Trigonometric functions. Compute pi from the Machin identity16 arctan(1/5)-4 arctan(1/239), with geometric alternating tails. The angle lies in[0,2pi]. Taylor sine/cosine at a rational angle enclosure have factorial tails on this fixed compact interval, and their derivatives are bounded by1. A fixed polynomial(B) truncation bound suffices. Rational interval composition and final dyadic rounding produce the declared component error.
4. Minimum singular value. Form the exact rational Hermitian H=Ghat†Ghat. Its degree4 characteristic polynomial has polynomial(B)-bit coefficients. Remove repeated polynomial factors by rational gcd; Sturm isolation gives certified real eigenvalue intervals. Bisection to absolute eigenvalue error O(a²) followed by square-root enclosures gives a singular-value interval of width a/16. Exact zero roots are detected algebraically. Close eigenvalues do not require resolving eigenvectors. This gives a public polynomial(B) budget using fixed-degree coefficient-height and root-separation bounds.
5. Polar matrix and synthesis. On accepted matrices the spectrum is bounded below by a²/4. One exact algebraic description is H^(-1/2)=sum_i lambda_i^(-1/2) P_i, where the sum is over distinct positive eigenvalues and P_i=product_{j!=i}(H-lambda_j I)/(lambda_i-lambda_j). Repeated eigenvalues use one projector, not division by zero. Fixed degree and polynomial coefficient height imply polynomial(B) representation and certified approximation cost, even if some distinct roots are close. Exact-zero pivot tests and Givens elimination compile the resulting known4×4 unitary into a fixed number of known one-/two-qubit controls; required angle precisions are polynomial(B), with a declared summed operator-error budget. This is algebraic processing of known rational data, never an unknown-unitary inverse oracle.
