# Primary predecessors and the actual comparison

**Read during continuation6, 2026-10-29.** This file separates the known mechanism from the constructive receiving calculation in this folder. No priority claim is made for the behavioral method, task-directed learning, or the elementary hidden-delay obstruction.

## 1. Willems, Rapisarda, Markovsky and De Moor: spanning the behavior

J. C. Willems, P. Rapisarda, I. Markovsky and B. De Moor, *A note on persistency of excitation*, Systems & Control Letters 54 (2005), 325–329, DOI [10.1016/j.sysconle.2004.09.003](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.sysconle.2004.09.003).

Primary author page: <https://ftp.esat.kuleuven.be/stadius/markovsky/abstracts/04-101.html>.
Original repository PDF: <https://eprints.soton.ac.uk/262195/1/PersistencyExcitation.pdf>.

The original repository PDF was denied by the web reader. The author abstract and bibliographic entry were read, and the theorem statement was checked through its explicit primary-paper restatement as Lemma 4.2 in Coulson et al. below. For a controllable LTI behavior, excitation of order window length plus minimal order supplies the full trajectory span. This is precisely the completeness supplier used in Section 3 of our construction. It is not a new operation-algebra theorem.

## 2. Markovsky and Rapisarda: direct simulation and control

I. Markovsky and P. Rapisarda, *Data-driven simulation and control*, International Journal of Control 81 (2008), 1946–1959, DOI [10.1080/00207170801942170](https://doi.org/10.1080/00207170801942170).

Primary repository metadata: <https://eprints.soton.ac.uk/263423/>.
Repository manuscript: <https://eprints.soton.ac.uk/263423/1/ddctr.pdf>.

The manuscript reader returned a 403; the primary publisher/repository abstract was read. Its stated task is direct computation of the response to a new input and initial condition from an existing trajectory, without an explicit identified realization. Coulson et al.'s Lemma 4.1 and discussion explicitly use this prior for past-based initialization. Our act of designing an unseen waveform from past trajectories therefore has a direct predecessor earlier than DeePC.

## 3. Coulson, Lygeros and Dörfler: the full constructive comparator

J. Coulson, J. Lygeros and F. Dörfler, *Data-Enabled Predictive Control: In the Shallows of the DeePC*, ECC 2019.

Primary manuscript: <https://arxiv.org/pdf/1811.05890>.

Read Sections IV–V, including the past-state uniqueness statement, trajectory-spanning statement, Hankel constraints, algorithm, and proof of feasible-set equivalence. The past window fixes the hidden minimal state; future controls and outputs are generated by a common data coefficient vector. A quadratic optimization then synthesizes constrained controls, with exact deterministic LTI equivalence to MPC.

This is the closest ordinary comparator. Our exact finite-data theorem and no-reset interpretation deliberately instantiate its mechanism. The measured square-inverse error certificate is an elementary perturbation specialization for a finite task, not a claim to replace the established theory. No interpretation of unstructured noisy Hankel fitting as an exact physical trajectory is allowed.

## 4. van Waarde, Eising, Trentelman and Camlibel: information for control is task dependent

H. J. van Waarde, J. Eising, H. L. Trentelman and M. K. Camlibel, *Data informativity: a new perspective on data-driven analysis and control*, IEEE TAC 65 (2020), 4753–4768.

Primary manuscript: <https://arxiv.org/pdf/1908.00468>.

Read the model-class definition of informativity, Theorems 16–17 and their proofs, and Example 19. All models compatible with the data must share the requested control property. Their state-feedback stabilization condition uses a right inverse of the measured state matrix giving a stable closed-loop map. This can hold even when the plant is not uniquely identified. The paper also shows that other tasks can require identification-level information.

This defeats any generic novelty claim for the slogan “learn only what control needs.” Its particular state-feedback result uses state measurements, so it is not silently transferred to hidden-state output-only sensing. Our port construction uses the separate behavioral past-observability interface instead.

## 5. Oymak and Ozay: finite-sample hidden-state input–output identification

S. Oymak and N. Ozay, *Non-asymptotic Identification of LTI Systems from a Single Trajectory*.

Primary manuscript: <https://arxiv.org/pdf/1806.05722>.

Read the observation model, Theorems 3.1–3.2, the output-prediction lemma, and the identification discussion. They estimate Markov parameters from one input–output trajectory with explicit finite-sample bounds before any balanced realization. Stability and a quantified residual from older hidden state enter the error budget; the input/process/sensor noise and initial-state assumptions are explicit.

This is a stronger statistical comparator than pretending all Hankel entries are independent. Our elementary bounded-error fixture does not claim their rates or assume their stochastic theorem holds for arbitrary correlated physical disturbance. In particular, a single noisy trajectory is an established setting, not a new source of predictive information by itself.

## 6. Wagenmaker, Simchowitz and Jamieson: optimal task-directed exploration

A. J. Wagenmaker, M. Simchowitz and K. Jamieson, *Task-Optimal Exploration in Linear Dynamical Systems*, ICML/PMLR 139 (2021), 10641–10652.

Primary paper: <https://proceedings.mlr.press/v139/wagenmaker21a/wagenmaker21a.pdf>.

Read the interaction protocol, model/task Hessian, Theorems 2.1–2.2 and proof sketches, and the LQR corollary. The exploration criterion weights information by how parameter errors affect the requested decision. Under its assumptions, certainty-equivalent synthesis and an adaptive experiment-design algorithm achieve matching instance/task-dependent performance to leading order.

The principal linear-dynamics setting observes states, begins at a specified initial state, and has an explicit noise model. It therefore does not directly supply an arbitrary hidden microscopic output interface. It does establish that task-dependent excitation and precision allocation are mature theoretical goals. A new physical principle must provide additional structure or access, rather than rename this goal.

## 7. Berberich, Köhler, Müller and Allgöwer: robustness and all-time control are established separate steps

J. Berberich, J. Köhler, M. A. Müller and F. Allgöwer, *Data-Driven Model Predictive Control With Stability and Robustness Guarantees*, IEEE TAC 66 (2021), 1702–1717.

Primary manuscript: <https://arxiv.org/pdf/1906.04679>.

Read the noisy-data prediction-error argument (Lemma 2), the main practical exponential stability theorem, and its discussion of excitation, noise amplitude, regularization and order bounds. This directly addresses amplification of measurement error through trajectory coefficients and the distinction between a predicted and actual future trajectory. Its all-time stability proof has further design assumptions and quantitative restrictions.

Companion primary paper: *Robust Constraint Satisfaction in Data-Driven MPC*, <https://arxiv.org/pdf/2003.06808>; introduction and problem scope were read, not its full proof. Our finite-horizon certificate stops before recursive feasibility and stability. It therefore cannot claim a new all-time result merely by repeatedly executing its one-horizon program.

## 8. Selection conclusion

The strongest ordinary route already provides causal excitation, useful untested waveform synthesis, a hidden-state continuation rule, finite measurement-error accounting, and—in stronger established formulations—robust feedback guarantees. It applies because a linear port law and finite relevant memory are warranted. The passive hidden-delay pair in our main file isolates what finite data do not supply: a certificate ruling out all additional task-relevant hidden directions in a larger unbounded-order class.

The parent role should therefore demand a physical supplier of that missing information, a justified wider response class, or a useful invariant objective which remains controllable despite the unresolved alternatives. These are selection criteria, not evidence that a new candidate has already met them.
