# Molecular construction selection: genotype-expressed abiotic material phenotypes
**3 November 2026 | independent candidate | theoretical mechanism, not experimental or novelty certification**

## Selection contract and failed candidates

The target is **a physically heritable instruction-to-material interface**: in a common reagent bath, a population of different DNA programs must produce different, spatially specified nonbiological molecular architectures. Each architecture must remain associated with the DNA genotype that encoded it. Direct measurement of the resulting *physical function*, selection and re-expression of the recovered genotype must preferentially reproduce that function rather than a rare fabrication accident. This creates an evolutionary search capability for nonbiological nanoscale architectures. The larger ambition is converting selected programs to stable, load-bearing, multicomponent 3D matter, without asserting that this conversion is solved.

**Rejected:** Peptide docking/handoff solves some retention and cleanup operations but cannot certify wrong permanent alpha-amides; independent true chemistry is missing (preserved P2 source and Astra N1020). **Rejected as a distinctive opening:** equilibrium DNA computation alone (Stérin et al. 2026) does not manufacture an irreversible chemical product; generic DNA-origami inorganic templating, covalent guest addition and DNA nanostructure replication are already published. **Rejected after removal test:** merely copying a *known static pattern* from a parent can be done by parallel batch assembly with the same digital recipe and starting oligo pool. There is no need for a physical parent copier; the proposed copying apparatus would be cosmetic. The task is instead **expressing a population of distinct, evolvable, not individually provisioned spatial instructions through common chemistry**.

Important strong incumbents: programmable origami/PDI photoproducts (11 investigated dimers, 2024), 3D origami plasmonic crystals (2025), origami-derived inorganic ALD frameworks (2025), 2026 scaffolded equilibrium DNA programs, modern microdroplet functional selection (including a 2026 clonal DNA-nanoflower workflow), and ordinary high-throughput optical screens. The actual novelty, throughput and advantage of the *integrated interface* are unknown.

## Candidate physical operation: common-bath genotype-to-phenotype translation

**Restricted first material:** an optically active **DNA-supported dye array** containing either donor-like or acceptor-like PDI-type cargo (more specific, separately qualified chemistries are needed). The actual useful output is a **measured optical response** (spectrum, lifetime, or selected energy-transfer channel) under a fixed input illumination. This avoids treating a barcode as proof of an arbitrary chemical bond: photons are the physical performance being selected. The restriction is not yet a stable industrial material.

**DNA address mechanics.** A fixed DNA-origami chassis exposes `n` unique registering sites `a_1,...,a_n` along an accessible, ordered path. A single-stranded genotype tape `g` carries modules `[complement(a_i), b_{g_i}]`, where `g_i∈{0,1}` and `b_0,b_1` are mutually discriminable hybridization domains. The first portion pins the program to physical position `i`; the second remains exposed to recruit a `0`- or `1`-cargo conjugate. Only **two cargo chemical types** are needed across all positions, plus the fixed family of address strands. Different `g` give different dye arrangements using the same shared cargo solutions. The smallest pilot can use `n=8` (256 nominal programs); `n=16` offers 65,536 nominal programs. The DNA tape requires O(n) nucleotide count, not O(2^n) individual cargo species. A nominal 12 nt position domain plus 12 nt symbol domain is ~24 n nt, but secondary structures, cross-hybridization, spacing and exact duplex energetics must be designed and measured, not inferred from that count.

**Population expression.** Single input genotypes are clonally isolated before materialization, e.g., one DNA molecule per droplet followed by clonal amplification. Every droplet receives the *same* rigid chassis, tile solutions, exchange reagent, ions, buffer and optical substrate. Reversible cargo occupancy equilibrates before optional locking. To select functional phenotypes, measure photons or spectra *in the same hydrated structure actually used*, recover the still-associated DNA code, amplify with controlled mutation and re-express in a fresh common bath. The 2026 scaffolded DNA computer establishes relevant reversible scaffolded tile competition, not this proposed genotype-to-chemical-phenotype transfer. The 2026 DNA-nanoflower droplet assay demonstrates genotype-linked selection for **proteins**, not for abiotic dye arrays.

**Chemistry and protection:** Start with hybridized cargo only; it is itself a genuine supported functional assembly, but has finite exchange lifetime. If long-term durability is required, add a separately evaluated covalent handle between guest and structure (e.g., an existing DNA-origami click or photo-crosslinking class) **after** the reversible occupancy stage. Avoid reactive promiscuous cargo during the recognition stage. Each cargo type must be chemically characterized beforehand; a mislabelled dye carrying an otherwise valid DNA tag is invisible to the DNA selector. Stabilizing the DNA chassis with silica/ALD is a *distinct* operation that may bury active groups, destroy genotype reading and alter the optical physics. Do not call stabilized product successful until direct optical remeasurement on that very product.

**Physical accessibility:** The first structure is a 1D path on a planar or open 3D DNA scaffold so both the read domains and any crosslinking/ligation sites face bulk solvent. Do not put the reaction between two tightly sealed DNA sheets, where enzymes and donor escape become unearned. Cross-droplet mixing, DNA aggregation, off-scaffold attachment, optical quenching, dye photodamage and salt-mediated folding changes have to be included in the actual process. A true 3D extension will need accessible channels and a load-bearing framework independent of DNA; no method for the full extension has been demonstrated by this construction.

## Exact central single-site operation

Let E be a vacant cargo site, C/W correct/wrong reversibly docked species, and C*/W* permanent correct/wrong attachment. In a fixed local environment:

- E→C at `a_C`, E→W at `a_W`, C→E at `b_C`, W→E at `b_W`.
- C→C* at `l_C`, W→W* at `l_W`.
- E→W* at `d` captures *off-template permanent chemical leak* (nonspecific lock or other wrong stable bridge) while the site is vacant.

The exact renewed hazard weights per return to E are

`S_C = a_C*l_C/(b_C+l_C)`, `S_W = a_W*l_W/(b_W+l_W)`.

From E, for the above exponential CTMC,

`P(C*)=S_C/(S_C+S_W+d)`, `P(W*)=(S_W+d)/(S_C+S_W+d)`,

`E[T] = [1+a_C/(b_C+l_C)+a_W/(b_W+l_W)]/(S_C+S_W+d)`.

This was independently checked against direct inversion of the transient CTMC. The chemical error **odds** are

`P(W*)/P(C*) = (S_W+d)/S_C`.

Fast nonspecific locking `l_W >> b_W` destroys dissociation-mediated recognition. Slower locking can improve discrimination but exact mean time and incomplete conversion matter; nonzero `d` creates an independent permanent wrong-product floor. No DNA label, fluorescence barcode or post-wash topology witness resolves W* after it occurs. These are general CTMC observations, not priority-claimed new laws; the physically new operation would be engineering their rates and chemistry in the mixed-genotype apparatus.

**Numerical falsifier (invented rate inputs, not measurements):** `a_C=a_W=1/s`, `b_C=.01/s`, `b_W=10/s`, `l_C=l_W=.1/s`, `d=0` gives `P(W*)=.0107737512` and `E[T]=11.0881s`. For 10,000 independent identical sites, all-correct probability is `9.04e-48`, in spite of a thousand-fold difference in off-rates. A 99% all-correct 10,000-site object requires error per site `<=1-0.99^(1/10000)=1.0050e-6` under the independent identical case; the union-bound sufficient condition does **not** require independence. In the same baseline, the error odds need >10,800-fold further suppression. The terms `S_W` **and** `d` must pay this; adding more readout DNA cannot eliminate chemical leak.

An *illustrative* set `a_W=.001/s`, `l_W=.0001/s`, `d=1e-7/s` with other parameters unchanged yields `P(W*)≈1.21e-7`; this only proves that a numerical parameter window exists, NOT that DNA contact geometry or covalent chemistry supplies those rates. The decisive chemistry task is to derive or measure those protections in the *full operating cycle*, including mixed-genotype crosstalk and buffer incompatibility. The chosen chemical class will be abandoned if its reproducible leak remains above required scale.

## Functional heredity, selection and the real resource accounting

Suppose a droplet contains `K` independent new assemblies expressing genotype `g` (a nontrivial condition), illumination time `t`, photon-detection efficiency `eta`, assembly brightness `r(g)`, and background `B`. An optimistic shot-noise model gives `Y|g ~ Poisson(K*t*eta*r(g)+B)`. Under equal-prior discrimination of two ideal Poisson brightnesses `lambda_1>lambda_0`, the Bhattacharyya bound is

`P_Bayes(error) <= 0.5 exp[-(sqrt(lambda_1)-sqrt(lambda_0))**2/2]`.

For a 10% brightness difference and `B=0`, about `3.28e3` detected photons from the dimmer object/droplet suffice for this **upper bound** to be 1%. That count is not acquired; photobleaching, variable K, assembly failures, wavelength crosstalk, nonlinear optical interactions and biological/chemical background can dominate Poisson variance. Unlike hypothetical complete bond assay, **optical output is the specified physical output**. Re-express each selected `g` from newly amplified DNA in fresh material to rule out accidentally bright, chemically incorrect one-off constructs. The actual selected quantity is expected reproducible function `E[R|g]`, not the correct molecular structure of an arbitrarily picked particle.

For `D` screened genotypes and `n` sites, the minimum physical inputs remain Omega(D*n) bits/monomers to instantiate the D architectures, Omega(D) isolated samples/readouts, photon/illumination cost and a complete DNA and waste budget. The claimed potential advantage is **not sublinear material cost**. It is avoiding D individually engineered spatial reagent recipes: `O(n)` distinct shared address supplies and two shared cargo species can, *if genotype expression works*, manufacture many differently encoded assemblies in parallel. A conventional robot using the **same new physical expression interface** can tie any controller; novelty would reside in that interface, not in selection algorithms. An incumbent with comparably cheap pooled code-directed assembly also defeats the proposed advantage.

## What does and does not survive the tests

The physical ingredient to remove is **co-localized, genotype-specific recognition on a common chassis with genotype-linked recovery**. Remove it and a mixture of the same dyes gives unregistered disordered material, or one must individually supply and address genotype-specific component recipes. Merely removing parent copying does not defeat batch assembly: that earlier candidate was rejected.

A 256-program, 8-site pilot could establish a genuine *mixed-library genotype-to-optical-phenotype mapping*, not revolutionary 3D materials. Requirements in the same actual cycle: (1) genotype-specific occupancy and independently measured covalent guest chemistry when locking is used, including wrong-tag standards; (2) retained genotype–phenotype association at sample level; (3) direct optical output from newly rebuilt copies of selected genotypes; (4) matched optical search using conventional separate-recipe origami and droplet screening, including reagents, time and sorting; (5) a quantified lock error/time matrix rather than guessed rate selectivity.

**Decisive residual:** demonstrate a common-bath physical translation of many different spatial instruction tapes into *reproducibly distinct* abiotic functional architectures, with no hidden per-genotype reagent preparation or perfect sensor and with credible chemistry/bit and cost accounting. The next major residual is that a selected hydrated DNA photonic structure may **lose its function** after mineralization/ALD or in a 3D conducting load-bearing structure. A selection result in the hydrated prototype does not supply that transfer.

**Consequence if solved:** a DNA-addressed abiotic materials search and manufacturing platform that exploits the heritability, mutation and selection normally available to biomolecules but searches spatial arrangements of inorganic, photonic or catalytic components. It could be transformative if it substantially exceeds equal-resource automated materials optimisation and yields durable high-performance matter. Neither effect has been earned here.

## Additional incumbent and two sharp physical obstructions (same selection stage)

**A stronger optical incumbent:** Gopinath et al., *Nature* 2016, reported 65,536 independently programmed photonic cavities on one chip: https://www.nature.com/articles/nature18287 . **A stronger selection incumbent:** Rajwar et al., *Advanced Materials*, published 2026, selected amplifiable single-stranded DNA 'structure genomes' for cellular uptake: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42670682/ . **A stronger common-component programmable assembly incumbent:** Moerman et al., *PNAS* 2026, controlled DNA-encoded kinetic reaction timing to build distinct colloidal architectures from the same components: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13012110/ . **A direct prior long-range proposal:** Accounts of Chemical Research 2017 https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.accounts.7b00280 explicitly identifies DNA-templated molecular evolution for light-harvesting and CO2-fixing materials as a potentially revolutionary goal, and observes mutation/resynthesis gaps in the then-published systems. Therefore the broad idea of applying directed molecular evolution to abiotic materials is prior art. What remains is a physically integrated and advantageous genotype-expressed *spatial functional-material phenotype*; novelty and practical advantage remain unknown. The 2016 throughput is a warning that merely making 65,536 distinct optical devices is not a breakthrough.

**Equal-lock chemistry ceiling (new derivation of this model, not priority-claimed):** If correct and wrong guests have *the same* irradiation-activated lock hazard `ell>0` and wrong guest unbinding `b_W>b_C>0`, then the exact wrong/correct odds are

`R(ell)=(a_W/a_C)*(b_C+ell)/(b_W+ell)+(d/a_C)*(1+b_C/ell)`.

With `d=0`, reducing lock rate can improve selectivity only up to the equilibrium binding ratio `R_min=(a_W*b_C)/(a_C*b_W)`, approached as `ell->0`, when the mean waiting time diverges as `1/ell`. This bound is independent of the DNA instruction length; simply delaying the chemical lock does not erase wrong binding. With `d>0` fixed independently of `ell`, `R(ell)->infinity` as `ell->0`, so a finite optimum is compulsory. Set `q=sqrt(d*b_C/[a_W*(b_W-b_C)])`. For `0<q<1`, the unique minimum is at `ell_star=q*b_W/(1-q)`; for `q>=1` odds decrease monotonically with ell and the infimum occurs at infinite locking rate. For the illustrative baseline `a_C=a_W=1 s^-1`, `b_C=.01`, `b_W=10`, even `d=0` leaves at least `p_wrong=.000999001`; with fixed `d=1e-6 s^-1` the best identical-lock choice has `ell_star≈.000316396 s^-1` and `p_wrong_min≈.00106308`. Both miss the 10,000-site 99% fidelity goal by >1000-fold, despite arbitrarily clever scheduling. To overcome the floor, the apparatus needs independently justified `a_W/a_C` suppression, `b_W/b_C` enhancement, and/or true *chemically gated* `ell_W/ell_C` suppression, and separate irreversible-leak control. These terms can be coupled: if the leak scales with irradiation or reagent concentration, the fixed-`d` optimum is not transferable. No external chemical 'proofreader' has been supplied.

**Clonality/resource ceiling:** A common-bath library must retain genotype/phenotype ownership. If genotypes are loaded randomly into compartments as a Poisson variable of mean `lambda`, the occupied fraction is `1-exp(-lambda)` and the fraction of occupied droplets containing >=2 initial DNA molecules is `1-lambda/(exp(lambda)-1)`; if molecules have distinct genotypes these are invalid multi-genotype compartments. At `lambda=.1`, occupancy is `9.516%` with `4.917%` multiple-template among occupied. Making this conditional fraction <=0.1% by mere dilution requires `lambda≈.002`, with only `0.1998%` occupied compartments, or `~500.5` total droplets per occupied compartment. This can eliminate the throughput advantage. Alternative clonal capture, sorting, imaging or bead templating costs equipment, assay, energy and handling and is not freely assumed. Ordinary genotype-linked FADS systems have the same issue, so their published input/output/cost is the correct source-matched comparator. Also measure a per-particle optical ratio/lifetime or count a separately calibrated reference fluorophore: otherwise high absolute photon output could merely reflect an accidentally high `K` copies per droplet rather than a high-function genotype.

**Refined decision gate:** 8-site/256-variant genotype expression is a narrow experimentally tractable discriminator, *not* a serious competitor to the 2016 65,536 independently programmed photonic cavities. A material revolution would require (i) pooled physical genotype->nonbiological phenotype expression at a large and diverse design count, with directly measured reproducible functional output and no private per-design reagent synthesis; (ii) a physical discrimination/locking law with measured joint rates and acceptable defect tolerance, including leak and heterogeneous positions; (iii) a cost-matched advantage over best existing photonic origami printing, high-throughput sorters, and genotype-encoded colloid assembly; and (iv) preservation of the selected **function** after transfer into a durable, stable three-dimensional material, or a compelling alternate durable functional use. If (i)-(iii) fail, select a different physical operation; do not call a conventional algorithm or a trivial linker an invention.
