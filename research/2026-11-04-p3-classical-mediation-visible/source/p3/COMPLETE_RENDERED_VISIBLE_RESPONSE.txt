Result: an exact boundary for classical mediation, with an acquired discovery test

I preserved the endpoint/checkpoint work and its limitations unchanged. I selected a different question with stronger scientific leverage:

When can noisy many-body dynamics be implemented entirely through local measurements, classical messages and local control—and how can an experiment discover that this is impossible without reconstructing every microscopic rate or calibrating its detector?

For a precisely defined class of commuting open-system dynamics, I established a necessary-and-sufficient criterion, constructed its physical implementation on the feasible side, and constructed observable entanglement certificates on the infeasible side.

Two explicit consequences make this more than another access theorem:

A three-qubit, purely dissipative evolution creates entanglement while remaining positive under every partial transpose at every time.
A connected family of arbitrarily large kagome systems has a fixed noise interval in which the state remains separable across every bipartition, yet ceases to be fully separable.

I also constructed and executed a discovery procedure that learns a witness from uncalibrated detector records after a preparation that earns initial separability without requiring a pure reset. Its held-out synthetic data give an exact statistical rejection of the stated conditional separability null.

These are mathematical results, physical constructions under explicit control assumptions, and executable numerical experiments. A laboratory demonstration, historical-priority claim and computational speedup have not been established.

8. Exact scope of the completed result

The main scientific result is the equivalence, within the stated commuting Markovian class, between a compact covariance feasibility condition, preservation of full separability, and an explicit local classical implementation. Its failure produces a constructive observable obstruction. The pure-bath and kagome examples show that every partial-transpose test—and, in the kagome case, separability across every cut—can miss that obstruction.

The acquired capability is a conditional entanglement-discovery test that works from raw records without microscopic-rate recovery, detector calibration or a pure reset. It has been executed on a specified synthetic apparatus and independently replayed from the packaged records.

The theorem does not cover arbitrary Lindbladians, multiple noncommuting local coupling operators, non-Markovian mediators or unbounded target observables. The detector protocol does not guarantee visibility, and its operational null must retain the corrected source–detector conditions. The physical constructions require trusted controls and paid preparation time.

The old endpoint/checkpoint theorem, signed-versus-positive limitation, finite intervention certificate, simulator interface and matched-baseline findings remain unchanged. The archive records their identities and hashes.

The new result therefore changes what can be decided and discovered in a concrete physical class: it supplies a complete boundary where previous cut diagnostics leave ambiguity, and a way to test the relevant collective property without solving the larger identification problem. The remaining technological obstruction is achieving sufficient signal and control accuracy at an acceptable preparation cost; the explicit kagome accounting shows how substantial that obstruction still is.
