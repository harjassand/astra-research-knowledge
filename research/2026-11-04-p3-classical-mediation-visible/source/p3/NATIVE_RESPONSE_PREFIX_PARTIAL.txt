## 1. The reformulation that made the problem tractable

A fully separable state has one representation of the form

\[
\rho=\sum_\lambda p_\lambda\,
\rho_1^{(\lambda)}\otimes\cdots\otimes\rho_m^{(\lambda)}.
\]

Checking each bipartition asks a different question. Each cut can admit its own separable decomposition without those decompositions assembling into a single decomposition over all individual parties. Positivity under partial transpose is weaker still.

That distinction matters operationally. A mediator implemented only through local operations and classical communication cannot create entanglement from a fully separable input. The relevant question is therefore whether **one consistent local implementation exists for the entire dynamics**.

The existing dephasing literature provides powerful criteria based on partial transpose and measurement–feedforward constructions. Wang’s dissertation explicitly identifies the unresolved obstruction posed by entanglement that a partial-transpose test could miss in the general, multiple-dissipator setting. That became a concrete target for the construction. :chatgpt-content-reference{index="0"}

I gave a collaborator a separate starting context: the broad mission, available tools and Astra access, without the endpoint mechanism. It independently explored dissipative simulation and found that the sufficient Gaussian construction substantially overlapped Astra N1201. We therefore concentrated on the harder question: **is that construction complete, or merely one successful ansatz?**

A separate non-quantum branch constructed diagnostic-bus tomography for sparse passive networks. It required new physical couplings to the hidden nodes, and its advantage over strong fitting methods with the same hardware remained unresolved. The nearby positive-clock extension of the endpoint programme also met an algebraically equivalent resolvent baseline. Those findings are retained in the archive; neither was promoted into an advantage claim.

The selected route succeeds by deriving a necessary condition directly from separable states, before introducing any stochastic trajectory representation.

## 2. A complete theorem for commuting correlated dephasing

### The model

There are \(m\) finite-dimensional parties, with one nonconstant local Hermitian observable \(A_i\) per party. Let

\[
U=U^{\mathsf T}\in\mathbb R^{m\times m},
\qquad
C=R+iJ\succeq0,
\]

where \(R=R^{\mathsf T}\) is real and \(J=-J^{\mathsf T}\) is real. Consider

\[
\mathcal L(\rho)=
-i\left[\frac12\sum_{ij}U_{ij}A_iA_j,\rho\right]
+
\sum_{ij}C_{ij}
\left(
A_i\rho A_j-\frac12\{A_iA_j,\rho\}
\right).
\]

This includes commuting bilinear interactions and arbitrary correlated Markovian dephasing within the stated operator family. Commuting local Hamiltonians can also be included.

Here, “classical mediation” means local operations and classical communication, without a pre-existing shared entangled resource.

### The criterion

The semigroup \(e^{t\mathcal L}\) preserves every fully separable input for every \(t\ge0\) **if and only if** there are real diagonal matrices \(D=\operatorname{diag}(d)\) and \(S=\operatorname{diag}(s)\) such that

\[
\boxed{
\Sigma(D,s)=
\begin{pmatrix}
D &-\frac12(U+S+J)\\[2mm]
-\frac12(U+S-J)&R-D
\end{pmatrix}
\succeq0 .
}
\]

This has three consequences.

1. **It gives an exact structural decision problem.** There is one \(2m\times2m\) positive-semidefinite constraint with \(2m\) real diagonal variables. Strict feasibility or infeasibility margins can be verified with rational certificates.

2. **Feasibility supplies a physical implementation.** The channel at any finite time is implemented by local Gaussian-pointer measurements, classical communication and local phase feedback.

3. **Infeasibility supplies an experiment.** A product of local two-eigenlevel superpositions becomes non-fully-separable at every sufficiently small positive time. A dual certificate supplies a two-body witness, and a second-derivative bound supplies a certified finite time at which its expectation is negative.

The formulation is polynomial in matrix size. It is not a claim of exact polynomial-bit decision arbitrarily close to an ill-conditioned boundary.

### Why necessity holds beyond Gaussian trajectories

For qubits, choose \(A_i=n_i=(I-Z_i)/2\), start in \(|+\rangle^{\otimes m}\), and define

\[
a_i=|+\rangle\langle-|=\frac{Z_i-iY_i}{2}.
\]

Let \(M\) contain the normal second moments, and let \(N\) contain the anomalous second moments, with its diagonal left free. Every fully separable state admits a completion satisfying

\[
\begin{pmatrix}
M&N\\
N^*&M^*
\end{pmatrix}\succeq0.
\]

This follows from a mixture of product-state means and the local Bloch-ball inequality. It does not assume a Gaussian decomposition or a particular simulation algorithm.

At the initial product state, direct differentiation gives

\[
4M'(0)=C,\qquad
4N'_{ij}(0)=-R_{ij}-iU_{ij}\quad(i\ne j).
\]

If separable outputs existed along times approaching zero, the normalized completed moment matrices would have a positive limit. Converting that complex covariance to a real covariance gives exactly \(\Sigma(D,s)\).

Thus failure of the matrix condition excludes **every** fully separable decomposition near zero, including non-Gaussian ones. Restricting each general \(A_i\) to two distinct eigenvalues extends the argument to the stated finite-dimensional class.

### Why sufficiency is an actual operation

For a feasible \(\Sigma\), take a Gaussian reference variable \((x,y)\) with covariance \(h\Sigma\), and define the product Kraus operator

\[
K_{x,y}
=
\exp\!\left[
\sum_i(x_i+iy_i)A_i
-h\sum_i d_iA_i^2
\right].
\]

The implementation measures \(x_i\) locally with a Gaussian pointer, broadcasts those results, and samples the conditional phase variable \(y\). A local correction handles the diagonal Hamiltonian gauge.

Crucially, the pointer outcomes have their **Born-weighted distribution**. Drawing an unweighted Gaussian trajectory would not implement the same instrument. The construction includes the actual outcome law and covers singular \(D\) through the generalized Schur complement.

Astra N1201 already supplied important positive-Gaussian machinery. The added result here is the necessity argument and its equivalence to full-separability preservation for the general complex correlated-noise model. The source ledger pins the exact Astra revision and records that distinction.

## 3. An explicit purely dissipative counterexample to all partial-transpose diagnostics

Set the coherent interaction to zero, \(U=0\), and use three qubits with

\[
C=LL^\dagger+\frac{19}{10}I,
\qquad
L=
\begin{pmatrix}
1+2i&1+2i\\
-1&-2\\
2i&-2+i
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

The expression makes complete positivity immediate. There is no coherent interparty Hamiltonian.

For the evolution from \(|+\rangle^{\otimes3}\), I established both of the following:

- **Every partial transpose remains positive for every time.** Each transformed generator has an exact rational positive-semidefinite certificate.
- **The state becomes entangled.** An exact rational dual witness has negative initial slope and, at
  \[
  t=\frac1{35727},
  \]
  obeys
  \[
  \langle W(t)\rangle
  < -1.1912394\times10^{-7}.
  \]

The finite-time statement uses an exact Taylor remainder bound. It does not depend on interpreting a small negative floating-point eigenvalue.

This directly addresses the multiple-dissipator, PPT-invisible obstruction described in Wang’s discussion. It also clarifies the relationship with the later measurement–feedforward negativity transition: the published negativity result remains valid, while absence of negativity does not determine the complete full-separability boundary. :chatgpt-content-reference{index="1"}

For this example, I claim positivity under every partial transpose. I do **not** claim separability across every cut; that stronger statement belongs to the kagome construction below.

### A physical channel construction

The formal Lindblad notation is backed by a concrete quantum-bus implementation.

For each column \(v\) of \(L\), use a fresh vacuum oscillator and the controlled displacement associated with

\[
L_v=\sum_i v_i n_i.
\]

Tracing out the displaced oscillator produces exactly the dephasing channel generated by \(\mathcal D[L_v]\), at a finite abstract channel time.

A naive sequence of local controlled displacements creates unwanted coherent geometric phases. I eliminated those phases with a symmetric sequence of local half-displacements:

\[
e^{B_1}\cdots e^{B_m}e^{B_m}\cdots e^{B_1},
\qquad
B_i=\frac{\sqrt h}{2}
n_i(v_i b^\dagger-v_i^*b).
\]

The commutators are central, so the unwanted phases cancel exactly. Two independent buses and local classical phase noise realize the displayed \(C\).

For the three-qubit example, one block uses two vacuum modes, twelve local controlled-displacement kicks and three local Gaussian phases. The numerical oscillator implementation converged to the analytic channel; at the tested cutoff of 32 oscillator levels, the maximum density-matrix discrepancy was about \(3.6\times10^{-16}\). That is a numerical convergence check, not a hardware error estimate.

The resource requirements remain substantive: fresh uncorrelated vacuum modes, calibrated local controlled displacements, oscillator reset, and paid laboratory time. Abstract channel time \(h\) is not pulse duration. Controlled-force architectures and trapped-ion Ising experiments provide relevant physical antecedents and primitive evidence, but they do not establish the performance of this particular construction. :chatgpt-content-reference{index="2"}

## 4. A scalable interval where every cut is separable but the whole state is not

The stronger many-body example uses a periodic kagome graph with

\[
n=3L^2,\qquad L\ge4,
\]

unit nearest-neighbour interactions, and generator

\[
H=\frac14\sum_{i<j}V_{ij}Z_iZ_j,
\qquad
\mathcal L_{\rm noise}
=
\gamma\sum_i\mathcal D[n_i].
\]

The two exact thresholds differ:

| Noise strength | Established behaviour |
|---|---|
| \(\gamma<2\sqrt2\) | Some bipartition can become entangled. |
| \(2\sqrt2\le\gamma<3\) | Every bipartition preserves separability for all times, while a product input becomes non-fully-separable at sufficiently small positive time. |
| \(\gamma\ge3\) | An explicit implementation using only local operations and classical communication exists. |

The interval

\[
\boxed{2\sqrt2\le\gamma<3}
\]

does not shrink with system size.

The proof uses two different graph structures. The cut threshold follows from the crossing subgraphs formed by kagome triangles; a suitable cut attains \(2\sqrt2\). The full-separability threshold follows from the uniform mode and alternating hexagonal-loop modes, which provide matching primal and dual certificates at \(3\).

This is not genuine multipartite entanglement: the state is separable across each bipartition. The consequence is the incompatibility of the different cut decompositions with a single fully local decomposition.

### An observable and a finite time

I constructed a witness measured using only three global local-analysis axes:

\[
X,\qquad Q_\pm=\frac{Z\pm Y}{\sqrt2}.
\]

One score contains a squared global sum. Its raw range would grow with \(n\), so I proved a clipping correction: clipping that contribution at \(16\) and adding \(6\times10^{-6}\) preserves the fully separable bound uniformly in \(n\). Processing each shot takes \(O(n)\) work.

At

\[
\gamma=\frac{57}{20}=2.85,
\qquad
t=\frac1{64},
\]

an exact interval calculation gives

\[
\langle W(t)\rangle
\approx-0.0003025181034151,
\]

and the corrected clipped observable satisfies

\[
\langle W_{\rm clipped}(t)\rangle
\le-0.0002965181034151.
\]

The channel also has an exact circuit construction: \(2n\) commuting two-qubit phase gates in six nonconflicting layers, together with independent local dephasing. There is no Trotter approximation in that implementation.

### The measurement cost remains severe

The bounded score prevents a sample-range penalty that grows with system size. It does not make the signal large.

Using the conservative margin \(g=0.00029\), the derived guarantee for both type-I and type-II error at most \(0.01\) requires

\[
\boxed{37{,}482{,}033{,}203\ \text{preparations}.}
\]

Each preparation still uses \(n\) qubits and \(O(n)\) operations, measurements and processing. Control errors must fit within the small witness margin.

The scalable result is therefore a scientific classification and an observable construction. It is not yet a practical large-system detection protocol.

## 5. Earning a discovery interface without a pure reset or detector calibration

The mathematical criterion consumes specified model coefficients. The experimental learner below consumes detector records and private control labels. Those are separate access contracts.

### Preparation that earns the relevant initial separability

Apply independent uniformly random local \(Z\) phases and permanently discard their keys. For an arbitrary joint source–detector state,

\[
\Delta_Z(\rho_{SD})
=
\sum_x |x\rangle\langle x|\otimes D_x,
\qquad D_x\succeq0.
\]

The source is then a mixture of product basis states, including conditional on the resulting classical label. Trusted local rotations preserve that property.

This operation does not create polarization or a pure state. A maximally mixed source can remain entirely uninformative. Its purpose is to earn the initial separability required for the null hypothesis.

The discarded keys must remain unavailable to the later apparatus. If they are retained and used to undo the randomization, the averaged preparation argument no longer applies.

This architecture also avoids assuming that switching off a controller restores an unchanged unknown native law. The tested operation is the actual subsequent evolution under the stated resource contract.

### Private controls around an unknown joint detector

For each trial:

1. Choose the local analysis basis.
2. Apply independent private random Pauli frames \(X^rZ^s\).
3. Read the detector.
4. Decode its bit string by XOR with \(r\).

The detector can be an arbitrary joint POVM, including coherent and correlated readout errors. Its response can depend on previous history. It must not receive the current private choices through a separate side channel.

Phase averaging removes the coherent measurement terms. Bit averaging makes the effective classical response an unknown convolution,

\[
\overline M(z\mid x)=p(z\oplus x).
\]

There is no need to estimate or invert \(p\).

The key mathematical observation is that the resulting witness statistics can be represented by

\[
\Psi_p=\sum_e p(e)\bigotimes_i T_{e_i},
\]

where

\[
T_0=\mathrm{id},
\qquad
T_1(A)=\operatorname{Tr}(A)I-A.
\]

For a qubit, \(T_1\) reverses the Bloch vector. It is positive but not completely positive. Consequently, \(\Psi_p\) preserves the fully separable set, which is sufficient to preserve the witness null.

This is not a physical channel reconstruction or a generally valid density-matrix estimate. On entangled inputs the formal map can fail to be positive.

Positive maps that preserve entanglement verification already have an important antecedent in the squashing-model literature. Bit-flip averaging and twirled readout mitigation are also established methods. The construction here uses these ideas to preserve a null hypothesis under a general joint detector, eliminating the calibration-and-division step for this particular testing task. :chatgpt-content-reference{index="3"}

### A source–detector loophole that had to be corrected

An independent audit found that “no initial source–detector entanglement” is insufficient for a claim about the reduced source state.

A detector can hold a classical label identifying an entangled component of an otherwise separable reduced mixture. That label can make a decoded witness negative even when the reduced source is maximally mixed. The archive includes an explicit four-Bell-state counterexample with decoded witness value \(-1/8\).

The corrected contract is:

- For a claim about the reduced source state, require source–detector independence conditional on the observed past.
- With shared classical flags, test the stronger operational null that the source is fully separable conditional on every such flag.

The discarded-phase preparation earns the required initial conditional separability. The subsequent null excludes shared entangled resources and nonlocal quantum operations.

Unknown detector response can still erase all statistical power. Trusted local controls, known qubit structure, private randomness and the stated correlation conditions remain necessary resources.
