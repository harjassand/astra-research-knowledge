# A global normal-fluid operation, with its physical ceiling

29 October 2026. The proof has passed root's whole analytic review. This is a mathematical sampler/normalizer construction; no new Langevin execution or physical data acquisition is claimed.

For the native stable conjugate-flavor model

`H=b†Tb+c†conjugate(T)c+sum_i U_i(nb_i-nc_i)^2`, `T>=0`, `U_i>=0`,

the actual finite-slice auxiliary-field potential has a topology-, bandwidth- and slice-independent Hessian perturbation bound

`||Hess log|det(I-r exp(i phi)B)|^(-2)|| <=4 beta Umax r/(1-r)^2 =chi`.

When chi<1, this supplies the missing global mixing interface on ANY hopping graph: native matrix products acquire the entire gradient in O(L M^3) arithmetic, and an explicit Euler/Girsanov receiver gives a polynomial TV guarantee. Positive bounded-ratio annealing acquires the field normalizer without a pressure oracle. Strong-convexity sampling and annealing are established ordinary methods; the contribution is their qualified native admission, not a new generic MCMC algorithm.

The same representation exposes why this does not yet supply the sought ordered-matter capability. At fixed fugacity r<1 the EXACT physical full opposite-flavor pair density matrix has norm at most `[r/(1-r)]^2`. The finite-slice insertion is generally complex and is not called a positive density matrix: the proof passes fixed-sector insertions and uniformly summable number tails to exact Gibbs first. At fixed beta Umax, a divergent pair eigenvalue would force the present curvature gate to close. Large beta times hopping bandwidth alone does not repair that gate.

For a canonical count of grand mass b, the inherited ceiling is only `[r/(1-r)]^2/b`; b remains a paid input. No arbitrary prescribed-density or ordered-phase conclusion follows.
