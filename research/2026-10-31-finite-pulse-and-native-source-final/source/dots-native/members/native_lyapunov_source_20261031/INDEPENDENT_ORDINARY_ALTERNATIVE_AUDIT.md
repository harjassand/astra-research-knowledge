## 1. Gauge improvement is a real comparator, not a universal removal theorem

Shang and França, *Hamiltonian dynamics from pure dissipation*, arXiv2604.18533v2, explicitly discuss lowering simulation norms by shifting and mixing jumps while compensating the Hamiltonian. Their Section 4, Eqs. (19)–(20), is the relevant operation; the main Hamiltonian-from-dissipation result has a different approximation/time tradeoff. This is ordinary prior support for optimizing a representation before simulation, not a supplied native Lyapunov/first-event compiler. [Primary full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.18533)

An elementary independent check limits what a norm-only gauge argument can promise. Take one source qubit, L=sqrt(2)|0><1|, H=|1><1|, psi=|0>, and physical jumps sqrt(lambda)L. This source satisfies the native energy hypotheses. In any isometrically mixed/identity-shifted jump representation, write M_a=u_a sqrt(2lambda)|0><1|+c_a I with sum|u_a|²=1. Acting on |1>, the source |0> and |1> components are orthogonal, so

sum_a ||M_a|1>||²=2lambda+sum_a |c_a|².

The acquired stack normalization squared cannot be smaller than this. Thus this gauge family cannot make the ordinary stack-action upper bound independent of lambda. Compensation in the Hamiltonian cannot make a sum of nonnegative normalization costs cancel.

This is NOT a simulation lower bound. The same qubit channel has exact Kraus operators diag(1,exp(-lambda T)) and sqrt(1-exp(-2lambda T))|0><1|. One ancilla and stable scalar arithmetic give a cheap direct implementation. It is an explicit example where both the new general receiver and a rate-normalized generic bound are inferior to the strongest instance-specific ordinary solution.

## 2. The trajectory theorem has a different actual clock interface

Borras and Marvian, *Quantum simulation algorithms based on quantum trajectories*, arXiv2509.10425v2, require sum L_a†L_a=Gamma I in Eq. (19). Eq. (20) then gives state-independent exponential holding times and unitary normalized no-jump evolution. Their construction already uses oblivious amplification of isometries; that ingredient is not new here. Lemma 7 gives amplitude damping as an example outside their class even after gauge transformation. [Primary Sections 2–4](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.10425)

For the native dark stack, sum L†L=2H vanishes on psi but is positive on Q. It is not a positive scalar identity. In this representation a positive scalar identity would contradict darkness. A uniform native physical jump cap also does not turn the conditional holding law into a state-independent distribution: the hazard depends on the unknown joint source state.

One can certainly write the familiar first-event identity for arbitrary generators. What still needs an acquired quantum operation is the entire operator-valued instrument, not a numerical CDF evaluated on an unknown input state. The native receiver supplies that instrument through an integrable proposal envelope, endpoint/path LCUs and complete amplification. The state-dependent versus state-independent clock distinction is a genuine missing hypothesis in a direct application of the cited trajectory theorem. It does not establish that no later or differently formulated ordinary trajectory method supplies the same operation.

## 3. Strong general stack simulation already handles channels and controls

Chen, Gao, Wang and Zhou, *Query-Optimal and Gate-Efficient Lindbladian Simulation*, arXiv2609.18757v1, Section 1.1 and Theorem 1.2, use a Hamiltonian encoding and a projected unitary encoding of the rectangular jump stack. Their normalized action is (alpha_Ham+alpha_stack²)T, with an additive time/precision query bound and near-linear additional gates up to polylogarithmic factors. This is a strong full-channel comparator, not merely expectation estimation. [Primary theorem and access model](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.18757)

Substitution of the present supplied physical encodings gives Hs+sqrt(mu B)+mu alpha_L². The theorem does not state that a physical jump-count estimate replaces this normalization. Its arbitrary-channel diamond guarantee already permits prescribed controls, references and retained registers by ordinary segment composition. Therefore retained-control correctness alone is not an exclusive capability of the native construction.

Applying the general simulator to the low-rate branch is a legitimate and often simpler ordinary alternative; the final source instead constructs that branch explicitly by Taylor LCU and a complete first-event isometry. In the high-rate branch, importing the general theorem directly does not give the claimed logarithmic switching-rate cost. To improve its bound structurally one must actually construct new weak cells/encodings or prove a stronger restricted-access history estimate; a smaller physical emission count is not by itself that construction.

## 4. Causal query compression is the closest abstract alternative

Kitchen, arXiv2609.31312, defines action using supplied normalization bounds uniformly over histories, not expected physical occupation. Lemma 6.3 has an occupied-input query compiler, but requires weak active-entry, actual query-input, absorbed clean-return and inactive-column estimates. It also lists transducer/coefficient/input-reflection operations as free operations of that query statement; generic query bounds alone do not price their gate implementation. [Primary Sections 2, 5 and 6](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.31312)

The native count MGF concerns the particular physical source jump labels. Oracle calls in a coherent source kernel, a block encoding's failure branches, repeated adjoint calls and QQ coupling insertions are not those physical jumps. The count effect therefore cannot simply replace the primitive-query history generating function. In particular, PKP=0 does not mean K annihilates P: the P-to-Q part creates excursions and must be implemented.

A possible independent alternative would build a causal weak-cell program with a Lyapunov weight controlling ALL these primitive ports, acquire its source/input reflections, and prove a gate-efficient compressed routing scheme. That is a concrete program to test, not a theorem instantiated by the count inequality alone. Conversely, once the native receiver has already constructed finite first-event cells with polynomial per-cell work, an abstract causal compiler might reorganize or improve them. Such a reformulation would use the already-earned operation and would not show that it was free from the original inputs.

The finite ordinary LCHS interface is already explicitly credited and paid in the native source: on Q, exp(-sH)=2exp(-s/2)F(s), with F acquired to uniform operator error and replaced by zero only above a certified exponentially small tail. The earlier independent kernel receipt shows why this is a stronger ordinary alternative than demanding a free normalized no-jump oracle from a generic channel simulator.

But this block is the SOURCE-ONLY free propagator. The actual no-jump generator also contains noncommuting sqrt(lambda)K. Directly applying LCHS to the entire matrix uses the large acquired source/coherent action unless additional structure is exploited. Whether an existing more abstract theorem or a simpler high-order elimination method already implies this conjunction is not resolved by the finite source search above. ## 6. Strong instance-specific and classical alternatives

For explicit small total Hilbert dimension, dense generator scaling/squaring and CPTP repair already give a deterministic channel approximation with polynomial dependence on full dimension and logarithmic dependence on numerical rate scales/precision. Prescribed controls are then ordinary channel composition. That method can dominate the present high-degree asymptotic circuit construction on every feasible small demonstration.

Event-driven classical quantum trajectories likewise avoid a fine physical time mesh when the actual state and no-jump matrix can be stored and evaluated. Radaelli, Landi and Binder's *Gillespie algorithm for quantum jump trajectories* explicitly targets repeated trajectories of relatively small systems, including partially monitored dynamics. Only the accessible abstract/search-visible primary description was used here; no gated full text was fetched. [Primary abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.15405)

For succinct many-qubit systems, those alternatives may require exponentially large matrices/vectors or many samples for a chosen statistical task. That observation is not a separation theorem. Matrix-product, symmetry, Fourier/moment closure and higher-order homogenization can be substantially better on structured instances. Their acquired representation sizes and error/control interfaces must be compared on a concrete model, not dismissed because the full Hilbert space is large.

## 7. Consequence and stopping boundary

No direct black-box instantiation of the four inspected primary algorithms was found that replaces the present native structural receiving proof while keeping the same acquired-access and logarithmic-rate bound. The exact reasons are their stated normalization/clock/primitive-port hypotheses, not a vague difference in terminology.

At the same time, the result should be described as an explicit structure-dependent composition of established trajectory, functional-calculus and amplification tools. The concrete scientific delta presently supported is the full paid native first-event interface under the energy/centering certificates. Originality, optimal resource exponents and practical advantage remain unestablished.

The next consequential test is not another formal source-class extension. It is a concrete succinct source/system instance, with acquired encodings and a controlled observable, on which the strongest exact dense/symmetry/tensor or elimination route has a priced bottleneck and the new receiver has an actual credible resource estimate. No such winner is established by this audit.
