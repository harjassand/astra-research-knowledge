## 1. Main chronological identity and completeness

For chronological function operator \(P_w\), the actual density is
\(P_w^*f\). Thus

\[
\langle P_u^*f,P_v^*f\rangle
=\langle f,P_uP_v^*f\rangle.
\]

The function-operator order is the real chronological order: forward u,
then reversed v. Every reversed factor is a fresh forward stochastic call
under the common-equilibrium premise. There is no microscopic noise replay
or pair of conditional futures. The report bound \(B^2\), three-entry
squared norm and confidence coefficient \(4r_n\) are correct.

Total variation contraction controls an arbitrary later common Markov
receiver, including an unmeasured intervention. This conclusion uses equality
of the conditional future apparatus law, not an inference about the kinetics
of that intervention from equilibrium weights. Full physical state and
accessible residual records must be included or have a supplied isolation
interface. In particular, a record identifying which preparation word was
executed cannot be ignored if the consumer can read it.

The randomized test has conditional response
\(1/2+(\rho_u-\rho_v)/(2B)\). Integrating it against the density difference
gives \(D/(2B)\) exactly. Hence zero acquired distance is equivalent both to
equal full ensembles and to indistinguishability within the adjoint-closed
menu on this reachable family. No corresponding assertion for arbitrary
hidden inputs follows.

## 2. Retained records: valid normalization and the visibility gap

For the same actual record distribution p on both sides,

\[
\operatorname{TV}_{R,X}
=\frac12\sum_rp_r\|\rho_r-\tau_r\|_1
\le\frac12\sqrt{\sum_rp_r\|\rho_r-\tau_r\|_2^2}.
\]

This is an average guarantee under the actual p. It does not promise
the same tolerance conditional on every rare r. The stated change-of-context
bound under \(dq/dp\le L\) follows by positivity.

The unnormalized alphabet example checks exactly: a deterministic hidden
bit conditional on each of M equally likely labels versus an independent
uniform bit has joint TV \(1/2\), unweighted joint squared error \(1/M\),
and normalized conditional squared error one. An alphabet factor cannot
be deleted from the unweighted objective.

The direct acquisition paragraph writes \(f_R(Y_0)f_R(Y_{\rm end})\).
That literal report requires a known visible \(f_r\). For an actual prefix
instrument, \(f_r=A_r^*f/p_r\) can instead depend on hidden state.
The instrument identity alone does not turn it into an endpoint observable
or a conditional specimen sampler.

The companion construction retains the whole actual prefix. With
\(a_r=A_r^*f\),
\(b_r=P_{u_r}^*a_r\), \(c_r=P_{v_r}^*a_r\), and fixed positive design
weights q summing to one on replaced branches, it acquires

\[
\mathcal D_q=\sum_r\frac{\|b_r-c_r\|_2^2}{q_r},
\qquad \operatorname{TV}_{R,X,\mathrm{later}}\le\frac12\sqrt{\mathcal D_q}.
\]

The real calibration chronology is:
baseline mu, initial f reading, actual prefix and observed r, u_r,
reversed v_r, reversed instrument branch with its actual label checks
and selector coefficients, final f reading, division by q_r.
The real forward branch probability is already present in the observed
prefix event. Multiplying it again would be wrong. Its reversed adjoint
coefficient is separately required.

This method neither learns every p_r nor fabricates a conditional copy.
The weights q are numerical design weights. For q_r=0 the reference is
executed unchanged on that branch, yielding exactly zero branch error.
Literal common-equilibrium reverse-path reports are bounded by B,
so the joint witness constant is \(C=B/q_{\min}\), its probability
gap is \(\mathcal D_q/(2C)\), and the echo report range is
\([0,B^2/q_{\min}]\). Actual costs can therefore still grow with rare
design weights and record complexity.

If p-normalization is chosen for efficiency, p must be acquired from
actual f-mu prefix trials or weighted baseline reports
\(f(Y_0)1_{\{R=r\}}\). Unweighted baseline prefix frequencies are not
deployment probabilities. The simplest counterexample is a uniform
visible bit, f equal to twice the indicator of zero, and a prefix that
only reads that bit: the baseline record probability is \(1/2\), while
the deployment probability of zero is one.

## 3. Rejection history and selected calibration

For fixed-label rejection from genuinely independent baseline specimens,
the rejected specimens, their labels and the failure count are independent
of the accepted hidden state conditional on the fixed accepted label.
This follows by factoring the joint independent trial law at each possible
first success. Its stated mean preparation count is correct.

That factorization does not automatically include an accepted-trial
duration or internal preparation transcript that depends on its hidden
state. Shared device/fabrication parameters can also invalidate unconditional
independent-specimen reasoning. The root files correctly require the full
conditional source interface; the clean rejection claim must keep that
scope.

For each fixed complete calibration history C, the next deployment must
actually possess its declared conditional input law and future source law.
On a simultaneous success event, conditioning fixes the selected candidate.
Integrating its conditional TV bound pays the calibration failure once.
The candidate-acceptance margin \(D\le4\epsilon^2-8r\) is correct.

Arbitrary pilot selection followed by fresh conditional validation is valid.
For the new instrument extension, freeze q, the active branches and the
branch programs before that validation. A fixed finite collection can instead
be validated simultaneously. Neither approach conditions an already
correlated specimen into the needed baseline.

## 4. Exposed selection keys and repeat composition

The identity/flip example has uniform marginal output but joint
state/key TV \(1/2\) against a key-independent reference. For m fresh uses,
the graph \(x_j=(-1)^{J_j}\) has actual probability one and reference
probability \(2^{-m}\); the exact joint TV is \(1-2^{-m}\).

The Hilbert identity

\[
D_{\rm key}-D_{\rm marg}
=\sum_iw_i\|\rho_i-\bar\rho\|_2^2
\]

is correct. With a retained key, classical mixing cannot improve this
linear fidelity objective below its best component merely by cancellation.
Changing the accessible-key interface needs its actual physical operation.

The fresh-repeat hybrid bound is also correct. One simultaneous calibration
event pays its failure probability once, then the conditional preparation
errors add. Fresh conditional initialization is required on each use.
Recursive substitution requires qualified actual intermediate inputs.
Changing later operations on only one side cannot be justified by the
one-preparation common-receiver theorem.

## 5. Weighted adjoints, raw normalization and precision

Reversibility with respect to \(\mu w_j\) gives

\[
P_j^*=M_{w_j}P_jM_{1/w_j}.
\]

The physical endpoint multiplier is therefore
\(w_j(Y_{\rm start})/w_j(Y_{\rm end})\), as stated. Reversing operation
order supplies the word adjoint as a positive weighted report; it does
not implement a stochastic inverse. The directional echo range
\(B^2W_v\), density bound \(BW_w\), telescoping under a common weight,
and witness constant \(B\max(W_u,W_v)\) are correct. Unknown fixed
kinetic prefactors remain permitted; drift between acquisition and
deployment is not implied away by sharing an equilibrium.

For raw \(0\le g\le G\), \(Z=\mu g\), normalized squared error is the
raw three-entry numerator divided by \(Z^2\). On a joint confidence
event, its nonnegative numerator upper bound and positive lower bound
on Z give the stated valid certificate. One may use the same calibration
data with a valid joint event; independence is not needed just for
that final interval operation. In contrast, inserting data-selected
weights into new empirical reports needs a protected selection argument.

The raw witness gain \(ZD/[2G\max W]\) is correct. It does not require
exact Z in the implemented randomization. The finite-instrument extension
likewise needs only one global Z, not every conditional p_r.

Uniform test-probability error at most zeta changes a response difference
by at most \(2\zeta\). Report bias bounded by report range times a
coupled TV budget is valid. The stated rejection perturbation bound
\(B\eta/(1-B\eta)\) follows by normalization and is marginal, as declared.
Actual sensing, randomization, endpoint weights and operation clocks still
need their supplied precision and cost budgets.
