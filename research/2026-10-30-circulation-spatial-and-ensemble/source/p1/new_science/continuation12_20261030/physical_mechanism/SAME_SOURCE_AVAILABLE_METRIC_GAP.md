# A same-source gap between hidden-state distance and all available future records

30 October 2026. Existing physical-mechanism role. Constructive continuation
of the receiving attack on root's four-state information-cost family.
This is a proof for that actual menu; it does not extend the menu or claim
that a reversed feedback controller is physically available.

## 1. Source, initial laws and actual future interface

The state is (a,b) in {+1,-1}^2. Only a is observed. The two generators
are Q_0 and Q_epsilon from root's
ADAPTIVE_INFORMATION_COST_OF_HIDDEN_ENSEMBLE_VALIDATION.md:

\[
 (a,b)\longrightarrow(a,-b)\quad\hbox{at rate }1/4,
\]
\[
 (a,b)\longrightarrow(-a,b)\quad\hbox{at rate }1/4,
\]
\[
 (a,b)\longrightarrow(-a,-b)
 \quad\hbox{at rate }1/4-q\,ab/2,\quad
 q\in\{0,\epsilon\},\quad0<\epsilon\le1/4.          \tag{1}
\]

The comparison in this note uses the same source epsilon in both
experiments. Only the initial prepared state law differs:

\[
 \rho_\epsilon=1+e^{-1}
     [\cosh(\epsilon)a+\sinh(\epsilon)b],\qquad
 \rho_0=1+e^{-1}a,                                 \tag{2}
\]

relative to the uniform law mu. These are the one-time-unit active and
baseline preparations from the supplied input 1+a.

The future controller may switch generators predictably using its
complete visible history, all visible jump times and independent
randomness. It may retain all of those future records. It has no b
sensor, hidden transition mark, swap of a and b, or access to a
preparation-identity record that differs between (2).

An additional fresh initializer, if used during the continuation, must
have the same complete conditional output/record/timing law in both
experiments and erase dependence on the previous hidden state. Such an
initializer cannot reveal an old b seed before replacement. Its real
cost remains. Independent additional specimens are permitted under
independent native noises and the same complete initializer contract.
Those specimens do not supply extra fresh copies of the unknown
initial preparation in (2) for free.

## 2. The needed forward filter invariant

For a known current a, let h be the conditional mean of b. On an
interval without a visible flip and with a bounded supplied q,

\[
 \dot h=-h/2+(qa/2)(1-h^2).                        \tag{3}
\]

At a visible flip whose pre-jump control is q, with a denoting the
pre-jump label,

\[
 h_{\rm after}=\frac{qa}{1-qah_{\rm before}}.       \tag{4}
\]

For 0<=q<=epsilon, every update preserves [-2epsilon,2epsilon].
At the upper endpoint the drift is at most -epsilon/2, and at the
lower endpoint at least epsilon/2. The jump magnitude is at most
epsilon/(1-2epsilon^2)<2epsilon.

This argument is stronger than a probabilistic almost-sure statement:
the normalized matrix recursion preserves this interval for every
finite positive-likelihood label transcript, every bounded waiting
control and every allowed jump control value. The waiting and jump
values need not be related by a particular feedback rule for this
algebraic invariant to hold.

## 3. Fixed visible transcripts and their matrices

Order the hidden states as b=+1,-1. When the visible label is a, the
subgenerator for waiting without a visible flip is

\[
 K_a(q)=
 \begin{pmatrix}
 -3/4+qa/2 & 1/4\\
 1/4 & -3/4-qa/2
 \end{pmatrix}.                                   \tag{5}
\]

It is symmetric. Its row sums are minus the visible flip intensity
conditional on b. The density matrix for a visible jump a to -a is

\[
 J_a(q)=
 \begin{pmatrix}
 1/4 & 1/4-qa/2\\
 1/4+qa/2 & 1/4
 \end{pmatrix},\qquad J_a(q)^T=J_{-a}(q).          \tag{6}
\]

Let a fixed record over [0,T] contain labels, visible jump times, the
controller seed and the resulting control schedule. Conditional on
that record, every hidden trajectory has the same prescribed q(s).
The hidden transition matrix associated with the observed path is

\[
 A=S_0J_{a_0}(q_1)S_1\cdots
       J_{a_{m-1}}(q_m)S_m,                       \tag{7}
\]

where S_i is the killed waiting propagator generated by (5) on the
corresponding visible-constant interval. Each q_i is the original
predictable control value at that visible jump.

Fixing the controller seed makes policy and stopping consistency
indicators functions of the visible record alone. They do not depend
on hidden b trajectories, so they cancel in the conditional hidden
posterior. This is why adaptation does not add an unobserved factor
to (7).

The transpose of each waiting propagator is its time-reversed matrix
product, with the same visible label and time-reversed interval values
of q. Transposing (7), then using (6), gives the reversed-label sequence
of killed waiting and visible-jump matrices.

There is an important qualification. If the forward policy switches
q immediately after a visible flip, the transposed jump must retain
the original pre-jump q_i. It is not legitimate simply to declare the
reversed feedback rule causal, or silently use the original post-jump
value instead. No such assertion is needed: Section 2's invariant
holds for every allowed q_i separately from the waiting schedule.
The transpose is used as a positive matrix calculation, not as a
claimed implementable reversal of the feedback experiment.

For merely bounded measurable waiting schedules, the propagator
statement follows by step approximation in L1 and continuity of finite
matrix evolutions. Finite T has finitely many visible jumps almost
surely because the actual intensities are bounded.

## 4. A uniform posterior bound on the initial hidden bit

Use mu as the reference initial law for this calculation. Conditional
on a_0, the initial b distribution is uniform. Put 1=(1,1)^T and
b=(1,-1)^T. The posterior initial hidden mean from (7) is

\[
 E_\mu[b_0\mid R_T]
 =\frac{b^T A1}{1^T A1}
 =\frac{1^T A^T b}{1^T A^T1}.                     \tag{8}
\]

The last ratio is the final hidden mean of the normalized transposed
matrix recursion started from a uniform hidden distribution. By
Sections 2 and 3,

\[
 |E_\mu[b_0\mid R_T]|\le2\epsilon.                 \tag{9}
\]

This proves the required backwards-information bound. It does not
follow merely by identifying b_0 with the terminal filtered bit of the
original run; those are different random variables.

If a controller stops before deterministic T, one can extend it
mathematically by Q_0 and reveal the further visible record. The
stopped record is a measurable subrecord of the enlarged record, so
the bound follows by conditional expectation. As T increases,
bounded martingale convergence gives (9) for the complete well-defined
infinite future record. Coarsening or withholding controller seeds also
preserves the bound.

A true reset destroys dependence on the original b_0. Apply the argument
up to its first such reset, then use the common conditional law of the
post-reset record. Independent auxiliary specimens can change the
realized schedule, but after their records are fixed their likelihood
factors are independent of this specimen's b_0. Their availability does
not change (9).

## 5. Full future-record total variation

Let M denote the common reference law of the complete future record
from initial mu. Both initial laws in (2) are absolutely continuous
with respect to it after applying the same controller, with record
density equal to the conditional expectation of the initial density.
Set

\[
 A_\epsilon=\cosh\epsilon-1,\qquad
 B_\epsilon=\sinh\epsilon.
\]

Since |E_mu[a_0|R]|<=1 and (9) holds,

\[
\begin{split}
 {\rm TV}(P_{\rho_\epsilon}^{R},P_{\rho_0}^{R})
 &=\tfrac12 E_M\left|
 e^{-1}\{A_\epsilon E_\mu[a_0\mid R]
       +B_\epsilon E_\mu[b_0\mid R]\}\right|\\
 &\le\frac{e^{-1}}2
       [\cosh\epsilon-1+2\epsilon\sinh\epsilon].
\end{split}                                      \tag{10}
\]

The estimate is uniform over the entire permitted adaptive controller
class, all finite stopping times and complete well-defined histories.
It is O(epsilon^2).

The immediate visible a read gives the lower bound

\[
 d_{\rm available}(\rho_\epsilon,\rho_0)
 \ge \frac{e^{-1}}2(\cosh\epsilon-1).               \tag{11}
\]

Consequently the same-source available operational distance is
Theta(epsilon^2), although the full-state distance is exactly

\[
 {\rm TV}(\rho_\epsilon\mu,\rho_0\mu)
   =\frac{e^{-1}}2\sinh\epsilon
   =\Theta(\epsilon).                             \tag{12}
\]

At these positive epsilon, the sign of the full density difference
is exactly b. Therefore adding the actual a/b swap followed by an
a read attains (12), not merely its order. It changes the interface.

## 6. A useful likelihood qualification

If the initial a read is included in R, the reference record density
from rho_0 is 1+e^{-1}a_0, bounded below by 1-e^{-1}.
Equation (9) gives the pointwise likelihood-ratio bound

\[
 \left|\frac{dP_{\rho_\epsilon}^R}{dP_{\rho_0}^R}-1\right|
 \le \frac{e^{-1}
       [\cosh\epsilon-1+2\epsilon\sinh\epsilon]}
             {1-e^{-1}}.                          \tag{13}
\]

Thus arbitrarily rare future-history selection does not uncover a
first-order signal from this single prepared state at the supplied
menu. This is a separate same-source, restricted-initial-family result;
it does not grant universal postselection guarantees to arbitrary
L2 preparation certificates.

Retained records from the preparation itself, especially the identity
of the preparation word, are excluded unless their actual joint laws
are separately matched. The theorem concerns the states in (2) fed to
the same future controller, not a new oracle that repeatedly supplies
fresh copies of whichever unknown preparation was used.

## 7. Meaning for the cost comparison

The source has a uniform spectral gap and bounded positive rates.
The difficulty is the access geometry: a hidden discrepancy of order
epsilon induces only order-epsilon^2 effects throughout the old
available future interface. Qualifying against an eventual new hidden
readout can therefore be a stronger task than optimizing current
visible use.

This sharpens the intended meaning of the ensemble certificate and
its fourth-order worst-case acquisition cost. It does not make the
old menu fully observable on arbitrary hidden initial states, and it
is not a universal claim about physical sensors or reversible systems.
