## Constructive result

The initial question was whether an unknown nonlinear material can supply
useful training information through actual thermal operations without
reconstructing hidden forces or copying microscopic states.

The strong ordinary answer is yes for a precise transient task. For a
finite word of actual kernels P_(k,h) preserving known visible Gibbs tilts
exp(h B_k)mu, a warranted equilibrium episode gives every pulse derivative
of a bounded endpoint loss through

\[
 \partial_{h_k}E_hF(X_n)|_0
 =E_0[F(X_n)(B_k(X_k)-B_k(X_{k-1}))].
\]

The same visible record supplies the entire gradient, without a small
finite-difference denominator. Neither hidden-state reconstruction nor
reversibility is required. The proof is differentiation of stationarity,
and the basic principle is directly preempted by ordinary Markov FDT,
especially Dembo--Deuschel (2010).

FINITE_WORD_RESPONSE_LEARNING.md supplies the actual interface, proof,
finite-confidence bound, optimal first-order pulse selection and a
finite-amplitude line search that accepts only a statistically validated
physical improvement. A constant field telescopes to two endpoint reads;
waveform costs depend on total variation as well as actual elapsed time.
Baseline preparations, finite clock calls, noninvasive sensing, thermal
freshness, control calibration, memory and switching remain paid.

## Exact limitations that changed the scope

For the reversible two-state family
q_h(x,-x)=c(h)exp(-hx), all choices of c(h) with the same c(0) have the
same complete baseline paths and the same static tilted equilibria.
The stationary-start endpoint derivative is fixed by FDT. Nevertheless,
the two-time path-loss derivative and second-order endpoint response
depend on c'(0), as does the endpoint derivative conditional on X_0.
Bounded rates do not bound c'(0) or the usable linear-response radius.

This is why the result is not a complete-record learner or a general
finite-amplitude nonlinear predictor. It also shows why an accepted
unconditional training objective cannot simply be conditioned on every
retained sensor record.

Static Gibbs covariance learning and Agnostic EqProp are stronger ordinary
alternatives when their actual preparation, observable control derivatives,
floating parameters, clamping and loss-nudging ports exist. Their ports
and relaxation costs are retained. Finite-time nudging has an explicit
lag-covariance bias; a frozen hidden component can prevent that bias
from vanishing.
