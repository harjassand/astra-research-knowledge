Claim: For the first-order model xdot=-Kx-diag(alpha)x^{circ3}+e_1u, y=x_1, with real symmetric SPD K, positive nodewise alpha_i and known dimension, the source derives an exact H1/H3 contraction. On a generic subclass with simple K eigenvalues, nonzero port modal overlaps, and nonzero pairwise-distinct hidden weights beta_i=alpha_i K_{i1}^2, the contraction B(s,t)=sum_{i>1} beta_i q_i(s)q_i(t), q(s)=(sI+K)^-1e_1, forms a symmetric matrix in the H1 modal basis whose eigenvectors recover the physical coordinates up to hidden signed permutation. The argument targets the needed contraction and does not reconstruct the full ambient quartic tensor. This remains an internally derived source candidate with an unverified acquisition interface.
Interface: Exact linear response H1 and cubic Volterra response H3 for the known first-order constitutive model, zero initial state and a single driven/measured coordinate. The original coefficient is recovered using a high-opposite-frequency limit or a finite-R extrapolation; H1 supplies modal poles and port weights. This is an exact kernel identity, not an assumption that hidden states or the full H3 tensor are directly measured at no cost.
Proof spine: The high-opposite-frequency pair separates the port contribution from hidden-node terms in H3. After subtracting the port term, the coefficient is a bilinear sum over hidden resolvent coordinates weighted by beta_i. In the H1 modal basis this is U^T diag(0,beta_2,...,beta_n)U; distinct eigenvalues of that matrix expose the physical mode rows, after which K and the nodewise nonlinear coefficients follow.
Costs: The source describes finite low-frequency evaluations after the asymptotic identity, but the first-order high-frequency route is noise-sensitive and the inverse can be exponentially ill-conditioned. No end-to-end acquisition, stable finite-noise bound, or advantage over nonlinear identification is established. The O(n^3) bounded-frequency query count belongs to a separate second-order oscillator model recorded in N597, not this first-order card.
Failure: Zero direct port coupling, repeated hidden beta values, repeated K eigenvalues or zero modal overlap defeat this particular reconstruction step but are not impossibility theorems for other kernels. High-frequency cancellation can suppress the hidden signal severely. The source’s focused prior-art review identifies parallel Wiener–Hammerstein/Volterra branch separation as a strong structural overlap; the exact physical-coordinate recovery distinction and priority remain unresolved.
Closest comparator: The source compares the cubic branch sum with parallel Wiener–Hammerstein identification and cites established Volterra/tensor methods, nonlinear frequency-response identification, and Vanelli–Hendrickx arXiv:2412.08472v4. These precedents prevent claims of novelty for branch separation or nonlinear network identifiability in general.
Open verification: Independently derive the H1/H3 contraction and the physical-coordinate recovery under every nondegeneracy condition; reconcile it with the source’s acquisition audit and compare with parallel Wiener–Hammerstein physical-parameter recovery.
