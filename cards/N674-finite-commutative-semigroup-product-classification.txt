N674-finite-commutative-semigroup-product-classification | Finite commutative semigroup products admit a transport classification and a single-exception normal form

INTERFACE
Let S be a finite commutative semigroup, m=|S|, with no identity assumed. For x in S let e_x be its unique idempotent power, and define x R z iff e_x z=z. Let μ be a full-support probability law on S, α=min_x μ(x)>0. Let T(μ) be the laws ν for which there is a transport γ(x,z) with source marginal μ, target marginal ν, and γ(x,z)=0 unless x R z. Let P_n(μ) be the product laws of n arbitrarily dependent variables, each with marginal μ.

SOURCE-REPORTED CLASSIFICATION
Set n0=max{3,ceil(1/α)}. For every ν in T(μ) and n≥n0, the report claims there is an exchangeable coupling with each coordinate marginal μ and product law ν. Its normal form uses n−1 copies of a random B and one exceptional E, placed uniformly among n positions. At most m² weighted atoms (B,E,product) are needed.

Conversely, for every ν in P_n(μ),

    dist_TV(ν,T(μ)) ≤ c(S)/n,
    c(S)=min{m−1, Σ_x(t_x−1)},
    t_x=min{r≥1 : e_x x^r=x^r}.

Thus T(μ) is the set of limiting product laws and is exactly the set of fixed output laws attainable at all sufficiently large replica counts. If e_x x=x for every x, P_n(μ)=T(μ) for n≥n0. The threshold is a uniform sufficient bound, not necessarily the optimal threshold for an instance. The report supplies a three-element example at distance 1/[2(n−1)], so the general error order cannot be improved to o(1/n).

CONSTRUCTION SPINE
For a transport-supported pair x R z, multiplication by x is periodic on the orbit beginning at z. Choose r in the appropriate residue class modulo that orbit period so x^(n−1)(x^r z)=z; the reported exceptional value is φ_n(x,z)=x^r z, with the r=0 case requiring no identity. The construction then balances the exceptional-entry law against the repeated-entry law by a stochastic-kernel fixed point on idempotent fibres. The report argues contraction for n≥3 and gives an explicit finite linear-system expression. Uniformly placing the exception yields exchangeability and the exact product law. See the source proof ranges for the complete argument and assumptions.

For any real f on S, the report also gives an O(c(S)/n) finite-size bound on the optimal expectation of f from a pointwise optimization over targets z with e_x z=z. For a finite abelian group the eventual transport set is the whole probability simplex; for a finite join-semilattice it is the set of laws stochastically dominating μ. These are dependent-copy statements.

LIMITS AND CORRECTIONS
Full support is essential to this criterion: the report gives μ=δ_0 in Z/2Z, where every product is 0 although R is universal. Commutativity is essential to the stated extension: in a right-zero semigroup xy=y, R is universal but the product is X_n and P_n(μ)={μ}. The report also gives a separate rational LP of O(m² log n) variables for finite-n feasibility, including some zero-support inputs. Therefore an exponential joint-table comparison is not a defensible computational-power claim; the candidate contribution is the transport classification, eventual exact realization, single-exception form, and finite-size bound.
