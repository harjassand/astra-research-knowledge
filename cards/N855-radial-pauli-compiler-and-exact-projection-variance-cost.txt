SCOPE AND INPUT. For a qubit, let a normalized rotation-covariant POVM on R^3 have density M(x)=u(r)I+v(r)n·sigma with r=|x|, n=x/r, u(r)>=|v(r)|, and 4 pi integral_0^infty r^2 u(r)dr=1. On zero-u radii set a(r) arbitrarily; elsewhere put w(r)=4 pi r^2u(r) and a(r)=v(r)/u(r). These are the exact hypotheses for the compiler; no gamma form or all-linear Fourier identity is needed for the postprocessing statement. The all-linear identity is an additional exact-law condition used in the variance result below.

DIRECT COMPILER. Draw r~w and n uniformly on S^2 independently of the state rho=(I+s·sigma)/2. Make one projective measurement of n·sigma with result b in {+1,-1}, where P(b|n,rho)=(1+b s·n)/2. Set n0=bn; draw an independent sign epsilon with E[epsilon|r]=a(r), i.e. P(epsilon=-1|r)=(1-a(r))/2; return X=r epsilon n0. The pushforward direction n0 has density (1+s·n0)/(4 pi), and after the sign channel the direction at radius r has density (1+a(r)s·n0)/(4 pi). Thus the volume density is w(r)(1+a(r)s·n)/(4 pi r^2)=u(r)+v(r)s·n=Tr[rho M(x)]. This realizes exactly the given POVM in the ideal continuous-sampling model with one projective qubit shot.

STATISTICAL DOMINATION. Let Y be the outcome of the ordinary covariant spin POVM with density (I+y·sigma)dOmega/(4 pi); its state law is (1+s·y)dOmega/(4 pi). The compiler's output is a state-independent Markov postprocessing K of Y: independently draw r~w and epsilon with mean a(r), then output x=r epsilon y. Hence P_rho^X=P_rho^Y K for every qubit state rho. The covariant spin experiment Blackwell-dominates the radial output for every state-inference decision problem using this measurement interface. This is stronger than the variance comparison: the radial format cannot add state information, even if a downstream estimator is nonlinear. This statement is limited to the stated measurement experiment; it does not compare distinct physical access interfaces or claim a general estimator lower bound.

For Z~p, let I2=integral_0^infty t^2/Gamma(2+t)dt and I4=integral_0^infty t^4/Gamma(2+t)dt. EXACT-LAW VARIANCE CONSTRAINT. For every radial mean-zero scalar noise Z on R^3 with finite coordinate variance s^2 and every normalized positive qubit POVM M satisfying the operator identity integral_R3 exp(i t·x)M(dx)=phi_Z(t)exp(i t·sigma) for all t in R^3, one has

    s^2 >= 2 + 64/(27 pi^4) = 2.02433418015925... .

This is a necessary noise-variance bound for exact common scalar additive noise in every real projection. Proof spine: differentiate the identity at zero to obtain first operator moments sigma_j and E_{I/2}|X|^2=3(1+s^2). Positivity with |x|I-x·sigma positive gives 3<=E_{I/2}|X|, whence s^2>=2 and E(|X|-3)^2<=3(s^2-2). On I/2 the radial characteristic is phi_Z(t)cos(t); at t0=pi/2 it vanishes, while isotropy gives E[sinc(t0|X|)]=0. Since sinc(3t0) has magnitude 2/(3 pi) and |sinc'|<=1/2, Cauchy-Schwarz yields 2/(3 pi)<= (pi/4)sqrt(3(s^2-2)), giving the displayed bound. The covariant 3Y estimator has effective coordinate noise variance 2 but fails this exact projection-law condition (its characteristic is sinc(3t), nonzero at pi/2).

BOUNDARY. The displayed lower bound is nonoptimal. It is not a minimizer proof, a universal variance optimum, or a sample-complexity lower bound for arbitrary nonlinear estimators. It applies only to radial, mean-zero finite-coordinate-variance noise and positive normalized qubit POVMs satisfying the stated exact all-t operator identity. The gamma variance gap is for the specific supplied density and the specified one-copy vector estimators; do not promote it to a universal gap for all estimators or all exact kernels. No arbitrary-tuple or higher-dimensional compiler is established.

IMPLEMENTATION GATES. The compiler assumes exact draws from w, uniform continuous axes, evaluation of a(r) in [-1,1], and ideal projective rotations/readout. No certified CDF, inverse-CDF or rejection sampler, bit-complexity bound, finite-resolution POVM error bound, or hardware calibration is supplied. The compiler does not remove state preparation, random-axis control, readout, radial preprocessing, or precision costs. Numerical radial quadrature gives CDF about 0.99655446 by r=12 and 0.99999887 by r=20; these are diagnostics only, not certified tail bounds. A finite digital implementation must state its output bins and bound the induced POVM error; a discrete grid is not automatically close in total variation to the continuous law.
