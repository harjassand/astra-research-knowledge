Claim: For each integer d>=1, let Q_d identify U and U P for P in Sym(d), with q_d([U],[V])=min_P ||U-VP||_F/sqrt(d). Define Delta_copy e_i=e_i tensor e_i and Phi_d([U])=(U tensor U)Delta_copy U*/sqrt(d), viewed in the complex Hilbert space Hom(C^d,C^d tensor C^d) with Frobenius norm. The source proves q_d([U],[V]) <= ||Phi_d([U])-Phi_d([V])||_F <= 3 q_d([U],[V]). Thus this quotient metric has a Hilbert embedding with distortion at most 3 uniformly in d. For a finite irreducible reversible Markov kernel with spectral gap gamma, this yields the stated squared-distance Poincare bound with factor 9/gamma; a separate conductance bound controls unsquared distances with factor 3/h.
Interface: The quotient is by the right action U~UP of permutation matrices only; it is not a quotient by monomial matrices or phases. q_d is normalized Frobenius distance after optimal column matching, not its square. Delta_copy is the copying map and is local notation. The Poincare consequences assume a finite irreducible reversible kernel and its stated spectral-gap or conductance convention.
Proof spine: The copying map is invariant under simultaneous permutation of its basis labels. For the lower distortion bound, expand the mixed tensor difference; |U_ij|^2 is doubly stochastic, so Birkhoff averaging bounds its assignment score by the nearest permutation trace score. For the upper bound, telescope the three unitary factors and bound each term by ||U-P||_F. Apply the ordinary Hilbert-valued Poincare inequality to Phi for the squared-distance consequence; use scalar level sets and Gaussian projections for the unsquared version.

Failure: The theorem uses exact orthonormal bases and permutation matching; it does not extend as stated to arbitrary matrices modulo column permutations.
