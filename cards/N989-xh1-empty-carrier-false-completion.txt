N989-xh1-empty-carrier-false-completion | An empty-carrier signal aliases productive transfer and hydrolysis in the stated assembly states

STATUS | accepted internally for the exact stated pair of reaction-state descriptions, at the version bound by knowledge/reviews/A989-p1-xh1-selected-scope.json. This does not assert how often hydrolysis occurs in an apparatus.

EXACT CLAIM
For upstream 3'-O-acylated DNA donor U-O-CO-P_n, the listed productive transfer to a downstream aminated adaptor and hydrolysis of the acyl linkage both leave the upstream carrier in state U-OH. A downstream polymerase that senses only U-OH cannot distinguish the two outcomes. Thus donor-empty is not, by itself, a completion certificate. The failure is observational: an adequate next-address rule must also read a product-qualified receiving state, or the hydrolysis leakage must be separately bounded for the intended use.

SOURCE-REPORTED INTERFACE
Only the empty upstream donor is observed under the two supplied reaction-state alternatives; no receiving-adaptor occupancy or free-product observation is supplied.

RIBOSOME CONTRAST
For the source's ribosomal two-state comparison, peptidyl transfer leaves tRNA_P empty and places the chain on A-site tRNA_A, whereas donor hydrolysis leaves tRNA_P empty, releases free chain, and leaves tRNA_A unchanged. Checking the next carrier's product occupancy distinguishes these two listed outcomes. This does not imply the ribosome independently senses perfect chemical identity or that correct transfer certifies the later cyclization step.

BOUNDARIES
The source does not claim that the described DNA apparatus experiences substantial hydrolysis; it establishes only that the single empty-donor observation cannot identify which of the two transitions occurred. The same aliasing pattern is reported for PKS ACP-SH after productive translocation or failed hydrolysis. Physical reaction rates, sensor performance, product fidelity and integrated assembly remain unacquired.

COST AND INFORMATION BOUNDARY
This is exact non-identifiability in a supplied two-transition observation model, not measured chemistry, hydrolysis prevalence or sensor performance.
