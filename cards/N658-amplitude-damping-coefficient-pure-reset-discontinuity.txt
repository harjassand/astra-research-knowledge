Claim (source-reported obstruction). For qubit amplitude damping \(\mathcal A_\gamma\), with \(K_0=\operatorname{diag}(1,\sqrt{1-\gamma})\), \(K_1=\sqrt\gamma\,|0\rangle\langle1|\), the manuscript reports \(\eta=1\) for every \(0<\gamma<1\) and \(\eta=0\) at complete reset \(\gamma=1\). It states this for the regular Petz, fixed interior-order Petz Rényi, Chernoff and N402 KL/CMI coefficients. The lower bound is claimed from an explicit rare-sector local-Hessian construction; data processing supplies the upper bound. The manuscript further reports \(\|\mathcal A_\gamma-\mathcal A_1\|_\diamond\to0\) as \(\gamma\uparrow1\).

Operational implication claimed: no estimator using a uniformly bounded worst-case number of black-box channel uses can estimate this coefficient to additive error \(\varepsilon<1/2\) with success probability at least \(2/3\) for every channel. The manuscript gives \(\|\mathcal A_\gamma-\mathcal A_1\|_\diamond\le2\sqrt{2(1-\sqrt\gamma)}\to0\) and bounds the output trace distance of any adaptive \(N\)-use strategy by \(N\|\mathcal A_\gamma-\mathcal A_1\|_\diamond/2\), reducing estimation to distinguishing the near-reset channel from the reset.

Boundary: this is an information-access obstruction for unrestricted black-box channels. It does not apply to exact supplied Choi matrices, and does not rule out estimation under a promised support or spectral margin.
