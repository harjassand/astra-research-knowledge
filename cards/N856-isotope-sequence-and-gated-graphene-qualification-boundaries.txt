Three distinct subclaims are preserved, with primary-author credit and no transfer between apparatuses.

**Cu-ZIF-gis isotope sequence.** Loading, closing, cooling, and thermally releasing hydrogen isotopes is experimentally studied prior art (Jung et al., 2025, Nature Communications, DOI 10.1038/s41467-025-56649-5). A proposed additional feed-free purge must satisfy a quantitative bound: under the two-species, no-re-adsorption/no-exchange model, for positive Λ_D, doubling retained D/H odds while retaining at least 90% of captured D requires cumulative unwanted/desired escape exposure ratio Λ_H/Λ_D >= 7.578813. Zero D loss with positive H escape is the infinite-ratio limit. The existing mixed-feed breakthrough and single-gas data do not supply a same-bed mixed-feed fractional-release/receiver balance or full-cycle recovery/energy advantage. This is a stop/measure boundary, not a newly qualified separator.

**Graphene hydron capture/release.** Soong et al. (2025, Nature Communications, DOI 10.1038/s41467-025-65771-3) demonstrate reversible hydrogenation/deuteration, isotope-dependent potential shift, and about 10^14 hydrons/cm^2. Their isotope substitutions use separate proton and deuteron electrolytes, not a mixed-feed separator. At the reported density, even granting all-D occupation, the upper capacity is 3.3445 micrograms D/m^2 per fill; product collection, mixture selectivity and turnover are not established.

**Independent gated-graphene active-control precedent.** J. Tong et al. (2026, Nature Communications 17, 4873, DOI 10.1038/s41467-026-71147-y) report double-gated/twisted-bilayer graphene with signed-field and carrier control that selects the reacting layer while controlling proton transport in that device. Tong et al. (2024, Nature 630, 619–624, DOI 10.1038/s41586-024-07435-8) established independent field/density control in double-gated monolayer graphene. These are established experiments and original-author contributions, not Astra results. They support active control in the reported graphene apparatus; they provide no transferred coefficient or demonstrated operation in hBN, a battery, or the isotope-separator routes above. The tBLG report also describes substantially lower proton current than its monolayer comparator; this is cross-paper, not a matched head-to-head result.
