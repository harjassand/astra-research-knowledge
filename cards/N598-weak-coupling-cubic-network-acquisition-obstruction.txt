N598-weak-coupling-cubic-network-acquisition-obstruction | Weak coupling hides O(1) network differences behind fourth-order port signals
status=source_derived_unreviewed; The proof and diagnostic/forecast record are source-reported. Intake did not reproduce them. External validation and physical acquisition remain absent; no practical advantage or foundational breakthrough is established.
Symbols and resource contracts LOCAL to this card.

Claim: For the source’s bounded-input weak-coupling family K_epsilon=[[a,epsilon b^T],[epsilon b,B]] and a hidden orthogonal rotation of the unobserved block, the one-port linear transfer is identical and the hidden matrices can remain O(1) apart while the port-output difference is O(epsilon^4) over the stated finite-horizon/control class. With independent Gaussian scalar readout noise of scale sigma, the source derives KL(P_K,P_Kprime)<=N C^2 epsilon^8/(2 sigma^2), hence N=Omega(sigma^2 epsilon^-8/C^2) samples for fixed-success discrimination; continuous white-noise observation charges total time in the same way. This rules out a uniform robust finite-noise recovery bound over the unrestricted generic SPD family under that interface, but does not refute exact identifiability. Separately, the source reports an n=8 conditional forecast with 145.7 million scalar samples and 32.9% K RMS error, versus a 2.59% local-oracle likelihood benchmark; these are not theorem bounds or executed global estimators.
Interface: First-order zero-initial cubic model, scalar bounded adaptive input, single scalar output, hidden-block orthogonal alternatives with equal linear transfer, stability/hidden-block lower bounds as stated in reports/s45_realization.txt, and independent additive Gaussian sample noise. The continuous-time white-noise analogue charges observation time. The finite n=8 forecast assumes supplied H1, common alpha and a specified perturbative acquisition protocol; reset/calibration and residual nonlinear costs remain unresolved.
Proof spine: Dissipativity bounds the state and makes hidden-state amplitudes O(epsilon). Rotating the hidden cubic coordinates changes the port dynamics only at O(epsilon^4). The Gaussian KL chain rule then makes the n-sample information O(N epsilon^8/sigma^2), and a two-point testing argument yields the sample lower bound. This acquisition obstruction is independent of a particular estimator within the stated observation model.
Costs: The lower bound diverges as coupling epsilon tends to zero even for O(1)-separated hidden matrices. A separate source forecast for n=8, gamma=.03, common known alpha reports 1.457e8 scalar samples and 0.32901 relative K RMS error; its local likelihood comparator reports 0.02587. This is a conditional numerical forecast, not an acquired result or universal performance guarantee. The tested n=3 first-order full nonlinear fit outperformed the cubic-truncated fit; n=5 recovery failed for both in the recorded test.
Failure: The obstruction is not a structural nonidentifiability theorem: exact germs can still distinguish the models at every positive coupling. It does not rule out stronger input/output interfaces, instance-dependent costs, or uniformly conditioned subclasses. The n=8 forecast and synthetic fits are finite/model-specific diagnostics, and the likelihood comparator is a local oracle benchmark rather than an executed globally convergent estimator.
Closest comparator: The packet compares the cubic route with full nonlinear ODE grey-box system identification, a competent matched local-likelihood benchmark, and established Volterra/PWH methods. It reports no checked practical advantage. This is a scoped operational barrier, not a claim that every nonlinear identification method requires this cost on every subclass.
Open verification: Reconstruct the uniform output-contrast and KL bounds with the exact adaptive-control/noise assumptions; preserve the distinction from structural identifiability. Then demonstrate a physically justified, globally reliable code or estimator that beats a matched nonlinear baseline after charging H1 calibration, excitation, reset, time, noise and computation.
