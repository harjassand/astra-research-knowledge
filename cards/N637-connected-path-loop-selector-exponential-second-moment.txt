Claim: For each k>=1, an explicit connected path instance on 2k qubits with 2k-1 gates and positive rational weights has exact partition function Z=26^k(1+epsilon)^(k-1), epsilon=1/(100k), computable in O(k) rational arithmetic, while the specified iid I/C/D loop-selector estimator satisfies E[Y^2]/Z^2 >= exp(-0.02)(189/169)^k.
Interface: The pair gates are F(4,4,1) and bridges F(1,epsilon,epsilon); one selector is drawn independently per gate with probabilities proportional to a,c,d and Y is the product normalizer times the exact selected-word trace. The claim is estimator- and family-specific.
Proof spine: Leaf partial traces give Z=2 product_e(2a_e+c_e+d_e). Condition on all bridges selecting I; pair-gate relative second moments multiply to (189/169)^k, while the bridge probability/normalization factor is bounded below by exp(-0.02). A parity check is required when evaluating selector loops.
Costs: The exact partition function takes O(k) rational arithmetic with polynomial bit lengths; one selector draw and exact path trace are polynomial in k. The second moment grows exponentially in k.
Failure: This refutes the usual variance-based FPRAS argument for this iid estimator on this connected family only. The family itself has an elementary exact algorithm. It proves no counting hardness, no lower bound for all estimators, and no general tensor-network variance barrier. Finite selector checks are source-reported and not rerun here.
