EXACT CLAIM
Under the supplied CIR model, thermal initialization and complete aggregate count-time interface, a positive acquired gamma-root filter pays explicit Bernoulli-to-Poisson reset errors. Finite-horizon pruning bounds the joint count-record/terminal-radius law with logarithmic expected retained-factor count. A different summable anytime schedule bounds the entire count-record law with logarithmically growing expected factor count. Neither result bounds worst-case bits or conditions uniformly on rare histories.

SOURCE-REPORTED INTERFACE
Let S follow dS = kappa(m nu - S)dt + sqrt(2 kappa nu S)dW from the supplied thermal Gamma(m, rate 1/nu) law. The observer receives every fine aggregate-count time with known predictable efficiency 0 <= c_t <= c_max and intensity c_t S. It does not receive the latent radial path. For a Gamma-mixture posterior with common rate beta and index PGF P(z), beta' = kappa beta - kappa nu beta^2 + c_t; an observed count updates P_+(z) = z[mP(z)+zP'(z)]/(m+P'(1)). The exact finite-polynomial filter and its CIR/gamma-mixture ingredients are established prior art.

ACQUIRED ROOT OPERATION
Represent P(z) = exp(lambda(z-1)) product_j(1-p_j+p_j z), with lambda >= 0 and 0 <= p_j <= 1. The stored state is beta, lambda and the finite Bernoulli-factor list. A count is processed from these known parameters by a real-rooted secular equation with monotone intervals; no unknown posterior is queried. Count interlacing creates at most two fresh lifetime tags, while every inherited p-factor decays at least as exp(-kappa elapsed_time). Replacing a known Bernoulli(p) factor by Poisson(p) preserves positivity and the represented conditional first moment and pays TV(Bernoulli(p),Poisson(p)) = p(1-exp(-p)) <= p^2.

FINITE-HORIZON GUARANTEE
For the same predictable control functional in the exact and shadow processes, pruning factors at tag age L = kappa^-1 log(1/tau) gives
  TV(law(Y_[0,T], S_T), shadow law) <= 2 c_max m nu T tau^2,
  E[M_t] <= (2 c_max m nu/kappa) log(1/tau).
The expected factor-count bound also holds when the algorithm is driven by the true physical count record. For 0 < epsilon < 2 c_max m nu T and T,c_max > 0, choosing tau = sqrt(epsilon/(2 c_max m nu T)) in (0,1) makes the joint full-count-record and terminal-radius error at most epsilon and gives E[M_t] <= (c_max m nu/kappa) log(2 c_max m nu T/epsilon). These are bounds on expected stored real parameters, not total bits or worst-case work; an external long-history controller's memory is separate.

ANYTIME GUARANTEE
With a per-tag threshold tau(b)^2 = [epsilon/(2 c_max m nu t0)](1+b/t0)^(-2), where t0,c_max > 0 and 0 < epsilon < 2 c_max m nu t0, every finite record horizon jointly with its terminal radius has TV error <= epsilon, and the count-record laws on the full infinite time axis also have TV <= epsilon. The expected stored roots grow only logarithmically in time:
  E[M_t] <= (2 c_max m nu/kappa)[(1/2)log(2 c_max m nu t0/epsilon) + log(1+t/t0)].
This differs from fixed tau: fixed tau has time-uniform expected size but a finite-horizon error growing with T. Neither theorem gives constant worst-case memory or rare-history conditional error.
