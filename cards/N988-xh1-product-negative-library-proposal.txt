N988-xh1-product-negative-library-proposal | Chemical-state-negative filtering proposes a product-qualified ribosomal display library

STATUS | mechanism proposal from the selected XH1 source; chemical capture/display compatibility and the integrated library remain unacquired. Source-preservation and arithmetic checks do not establish a working assembler, chemical performance, novelty, or advantage.

EXACT CLAIM
The source proposes encoding distinct nonstandard peptide products on separate mRNA templates, using established ribosomal elongation and mRNA-display linkage, then applying a downstream chemical-state-negative filter to a completed genotype-linked library. In its example, an uncyclized hydrazinoester product retains an exposed hydrazinyl nitrogen that can react with aldehyde, while the desired cyclic product has consumed that nitrogen. Marking/removing the linear products could qualify library members before functional selection. The source proposes adapting known hydrazone chemistry to intact mRNA fusions; selective capture in an RNA-compatible environment, fusion survival, side-reaction rates, and false tagging have not been demonstrated. The immobilized aldehyde scavenger is speculative.

SOURCE-REPORTED INTERFACE
Proposed completed genotype-linked library, credited ribosomal cyclic-backbone synthesis and puromycin display; selective removal must distinguish the exact linear/cyclic products while preserving their attached nucleic acid. This combined selective capture/display interface remains unacquired.

DECODING AND REUSE CONDITIONS
Two different chemical instructions in one common pool need distinguishable codon/charged-tRNA decoding registers. Mixing distinct monomers on the same tRNA species or codon is not individually addressable merely because there are two mRNA programs. Lee's established AUG/GAC/CGU assignments demonstrate prescribed placement, not arbitrary late per-chain rewriting. The puromycin-linked mRNA/product fusion is a consumed library object; translation catalysts can be reused, but the fusion is not a reusable assembly catalyst.

BOUNDARIES
The proposed purge happens after synthesis of the full product. It does not make each ribosomal elongation step wait for a product-state certificate, guarantee chemical identity, or correct ring closure on the ribosome. The donor-empty aliasing failure is recorded separately in N989. Correct transfer onto the next tRNA distinguishes transfer from hydrolysis for the stated two-state comparison; it still does not certify the later intramolecular ring-closing reaction. Chemical-state-negative library selection is therefore a distinct mechanism from product-gated progression at every assembly step.

SUBSTRATE-SPECIFIC PURITY ARITHMETIC
The cited approximately 22% cyclic / 78% linear result is for one tested six-membered-ring substrate, not the different Lee et al. Figure 8 double-ring experiment. If p=0.22 and a later filter retains fractions t and w of correct and incorrect products, respectively, then q=pt/[pt+(1-p)w]. Reaching q>=0.99 requires w/t<=0.002849, or at least 351-fold relative discrimination. If t=0.9, at least 99.744% of incorrect molecules must be removed. The source's multi-motif p^2 calculation assumes independence that was not measured; do not apply the 22% example to the double-ring substrate. The companion Python performs Bayesian and sampling arithmetic only; it was not executed during this intake.

COST AND INFORMATION BOUNDARY
Credit the existing ribosomal cyclic-backbone chemistry of Lee et al. (2025), established mRNA display linkage from Roberts and Szostak (1997), and the 2026 genetically reprogrammed cyclic glycopeptide mRNA-display result of Johansen-Leete et al. These sources already demonstrate substantial chemistry and genotype-linked selection; this intake did not independently audit their literature. Lee's example is substrate-dependent, and other substrates reportedly cyclize much more efficiently, so those are stronger comparators before adding a filter. Specialized monomers, charged tRNA, flexizyme, GTP-driven translation, puromycin linkage, chemistry-compatible purification, and product recovery remain costs. No integrated synthesis/display/filter machine, measured RNA-compatible capture, or practical advantage is established.

NEXT OBLIGATION
Test whether selective aldehyde marking/removal works on the exact uncyclized and cyclized products while intact puromycin-linked mRNA survives; control false tagging at other nucleophiles and identify truncations by product mass. Then test two separately encoded products in one common translation pool with correct mRNA/product identity and quantify the retained library. The source's stated comparison is high-diversity discovery against matched automated synthesis and current mRNA-display/ribosomal methods; no such matched experiment has been run.
