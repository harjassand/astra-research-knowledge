INPUT CONTRACT
Let M be an arbitrary matroid on n elements, accessed through an independence oracle. Let A∈Q^{m×n} have squared Euclidean column norms at most one, x∈P(M) be rational, and c∈Q^n be any rational cost vector. The response claims a deterministic algorithm returning a basis B such that

    cᵀ1_B ≤ cᵀx,                    ||A(1_B−x)||∞ ≤ C,

with C=262336. It claims polynomially many independence-oracle queries and ordinary bit operations in n, m, and the total binary input length; the output basis and all intermediate rational data have polynomial encoding length.

EXACT-MARGINAL CONSTRUCTION
For fixed A,x, the objective-aware basis procedure is used as a separation response in an ellipsoid metarounding argument. The response claims a deterministic polynomial-time construction of rational probabilities λₗ and at most n+1 bases Bₗ satisfying

    Σₗ λₗ=1,   Σₗ λₗ1_{Bₗ}=x,   ||A(1_{Bₗ}−x)||∞≤C for every support point.

The cost guarantee belongs to the one-objective basis procedure; the report does not claim every atom of the unbiased distribution is cost-nonincreasing. The probability law is constructed deterministically. Exact sampling by rational-probability rejection is claimed to take polynomial expected time; no uniformly bounded fair-random-bit sampler is claimed.

REPORTED CONSTRUCTION
The proposed algorithm combines inherited spectral-barrier/face-descent machinery with two computational controls:

- **Face-preserving precision reset.** After an exact local move reaches a matroid face, a rational linear program finds a nearby rational point inside that face, a dyadic box, and the retained exact input-defined affine equations. Determinant bounds reset coordinate bit length independently of the previous denominator. Moving spectral constraints are allowed a bounded numerical-error budget rather than being preserved exactly.
- **Quantitative negative-curvature progress.** The proof claims a feasible rational direction with inverse-polynomial negative curvature while retaining objective and protected-row equations. A full step decreases the potential by a fixed amount; a short step reaches a proper matroid face. At most n short face losses occur. Rational approximation is used for spectral derivatives; exact transcendental operations are not assumed.
- **Independence-oracle interface.** Matroid separation is reduced to submodular minimization of r(S)−y(S), with rank evaluations from independence queries. The report also describes recovery of the minimal face, exact line endpoints, and the linear precision-reset problems through this separator.

The source gives conservative fixed parameters (including K=64) and a reported local-update count O(n⁹(m+n)¹²). It states that the full algorithm has not been implemented end to end and is far from practical. Its broader transfer principle is limited to Boolean-vertex polytopes with bounded exchange width, polynomial separation, and controlled rational description; it is not a theorem for arbitrary polytope intersections.

CONSEQUENCES AND LIMITS
For a Boolean matrix W with at most Δ nonzeros in each column, scaling by q=⌈√Δ⌉ gives the source-claimed discrepancy bound C⌈√Δ⌉ for arbitrary matroid bases, with the stated one-objective or exact-marginal interface. The long-edge obstruction remains for arbitrary intersections of two matroids; no general intersection algorithm is claimed. A fixed finite-step lower bound is not assumed, and independent dyadic coordinate rounding is explicitly rejected as an exact feasibility repair.

The response reports exact checks of dimension/progress inequalities, one precision-reset example, and 80 face-recovery and terminal-cost cases over five small matroid families. These diagnostics are source-reported only: their code and receipts were unavailable and were not rerun.
