## Source claims and our mathematical deductions

The manuscript's Theorem 1.1 claims one integer polynomial for every recursively
enumerable natural-tuple predicate, with exactly one complete natural witness
tuple on members and none on nonmembers. Internal rational codes, slacks,
branch selectors and inactive variables all count. Proposition 4.2 supplies
an intermediate-growth function through a square-index height window;
Sections 5–7 install a Pell power graph and accepting counter computation.
These are full-read author claims. No decisive manuscript defect was found
in this scoped reading, but that is not a complete independent composition or
kernel proof.

Our first deduction is exact hidden-index addition and multiplication from
the same bounded-error height interface. For positive canonical point codes
Q=C(k), R=C(l), S=C(r), write

`A=max(W(Q+R),W(Q−R))=W_(k+l)`.

Then addition is `W(S)=A`. For multiplication use

`L=A W(S) W1`,
`D=W(Q) W(R) max(W(S+P),W(S−P))`.

The exact identity is `log(L/D)=2a(kl−r)+E`, with `|E|≤6c`.
Under `6c<logκ<2a−6c`, the two polynomial inequalities
`L≤κD` and `D≤κL` hold exactly when r=kl. Every auxiliary is uniquely
fixed after the output; zero and inactive branches are explicitly gated and
zeroed. The interface is an arithmetic graph on natural-coded points, not a
Diophantine model over Q or an acquired graph from an ordinary integer index
to its representative. Complete derivation and charged representation sizes
are in `notes/logic_gate14/cycle19/HEIGHT_WINDOW_ARITHMETIC.md`.

The six-height method needs bounded additive error. The monotone integer
sequence `W_j=2^(8j²+floor(j^(3/2)))` has quadratic leading growth and vanishing
relative error, yet rejects true products for every fixed multiplicative
window eventually. For k=l=10, r=100, its perturbation is already 13 in base-2
logarithms, exceeding the test window 7. This identifies why Poonen's older
relative denominator estimate alone cannot replace the full-coordinate height
in this mechanism; it does not refute his theorem or all denominator methods.

Our second universal deduction is a search barrier. For any fixed nonrecursive
RE set and any single-fold polynomial representing it, every total recursive
witness-coordinate or bit-length bound is exceeded on infinitely many members.
Otherwise finite-box evaluation decides membership; finitely many exceptions
can be hard-coded. Ordinary DPRM representations already have this barrier.
Uniqueness therefore enables auditable certificates, not generic bounded
certificate acquisition. Conversely, existential fixed numerical constants
do not imply that no recursive compiler exists: one valid finite tuple can
be hard-coded. The missing interface was supplied constants, not an abstract
noncomputability theorem for the compiler.
