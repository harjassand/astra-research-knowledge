Claim: There is a polynomial-time Turing reduction from the 0/1 permanent to exact evaluation of E[p(G)^2] for integer-coefficient arithmetic circuits p and independent standard real Gaussian inputs G. The proof builds 0/1 Gram factorizations B_t B_t^T=tI+A, uses the complex Wick identity to recover perm(tI+A) from the real and imaginary circuit norms, interpolates at N+1 positive integer t values, and takes an exact integer square root of perm(M)^2.
Interface: The oracle returns the exact rational/integer expectation with all output bits. The reduction makes polynomially many queries to circuits of polynomial size and degree over independent standard real Gaussians. It is a Turing reduction, not one-query or approximate evaluation.
Proof spine: For a bipartite adjacency matrix A=[[0,M],[M^T,0]], construct B_t from edge columns e_i+e_j and t-d_i singleton columns so B_tB_t^T=tI+A. Complex Gaussian moments give E|P_t|^2=2^N perm(B_tB_t^T); split into real/imaginary integer circuits. Interpolation recovers q(0)=perm(A)=perm(M)^2.
Costs: The construction uses N+1 integer parameter points and polynomial-size, degree-N circuits with O(N^2) real Gaussian inputs; outputs and interpolation operands have polynomial bit length.
Failure: No implication is claimed for approximate/additive norm evaluation, norm-zero testing, randomized polynomial identity testing, or general identity testing. The reported finite exact checker is not run by this intake.
Closest comparator: Uses the standard #P-completeness of the 0/1 permanent (Valiant, 1979); the source cites this antecedent. It does not make a new approximate-counting or identity-testing result.
